## The Arts in Early Childhood: Social and Emotional Benefits of Arts Participation

This report by Melissa Menzer, commissioned by the National Endowment for the Arts (NEA), examines the social and emotional benefits of arts participation in early childhood from 2000 to 2015. The study focuses on music-based activities, drama/theater, visual arts, and crafts but excludes storytelling and literary arts or electronic media engagement. Key findings highlight that arts participation positively influences social skills development and emotion regulation ability among typically developing children as well as special populations like toddlers from low socioeconomic families and those with autism or Asperger syndrome. The report suggests there is a need for further research to understand the mechanisms behind these benefits and how they can be effectively integrated into educational programs.
