## Effects of Training and Using an Audio-Tactile Sensory Substitution Device on Speech-in-Noise Understanding

This study explores the effects of training and using an audio-tactile sensory substitution device (SSD) on speech-in-noise understanding for non-native English speakers. The device delivers low-frequency tactile vibrations corresponding to auditory inputs, aiming to improve comprehension in noisy environments. Two groups underwent different types of training: unisensory auditory and multisensory audio-tactile. Both groups showed significant improvements in Speech Reception Threshold (SRT), with comparable performance regardless of the type of training. The study suggests that both training methods can enhance speech-in-noise understanding, though participants performed better when using matching vibrations during testing.
