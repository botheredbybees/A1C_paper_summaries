## Understanding Suffering: Utermohlen’s Self-portraits and Alzheimer’s Disease

The article 'Understanding Suffering: Utermohlen’s Self-portraits and Alzheimer’s Disease' by Elizabeth M. Harrison explores the complexities of human suffering, particularly in the context of Alzheimer’s disease. Harrison uses the self-portraits of artist William Utermohlen, who was diagnosed with Alzheimer’s, as a teaching tool for nursing students to understand the psychological and physical suffering experienced by patients with this condition. Utermohlen’s series of self-portraits from his diagnosis to his death provide a visual narrative of his decline, making them a powerful educational resource. The article discusses how these portraits can help students develop observational skills and empathy towards patients experiencing chronic and debilitating conditions. This creative teaching method aims to address the challenge of teaching students to diagnose and care for suffering clients, especially given the constraints of clinical settings and limited exposure to patients with Alzheimer’s disease.
