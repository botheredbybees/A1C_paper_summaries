## Ethical conduct in research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and communities: Guidelines for researchers and stakeholders

These guidelines, published by the National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC), outline ethical considerations for conducting research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples in Australia. The document emphasizes the importance of ethical research practices that enhance the wellbeing and rights of Indigenous communities. It highlights the historical context of research with Indigenous Peoples, noting that not all past research has been beneficial and may have been ethically questionable. The guidelines aim to improve the way researchers engage with Indigenous communities, build research capabilities, and respect the unique cultural protocols and values of each group. They stress the need for cultural competency and the importance of obtaining informed consent, respecting intellectual property, and ensuring equitable partnerships in research projects. The guidelines are essential for researchers and stakeholders aiming to conduct respectful and beneficial research with Indigenous communities, contributing to the broader goals of health equity and social justice in the creative arts and health fields.
