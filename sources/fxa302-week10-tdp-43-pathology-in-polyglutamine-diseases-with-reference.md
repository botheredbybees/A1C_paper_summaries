## TDP-43 pathology in polyglutamine diseases: With reference to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

This article discusses the role of TDP-43 in polyglutamine (polyQ) diseases and its relationship to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Initially identified as a marker specific to ALS and frontotemporal lobar degeneration, TDP-43 pathology is now recognized in several neurodegenerative diseases, including tauopathies and synucleinopathies. However, in polyQ diseases such as Huntington's disease (HD), spinocerebellar ataxia type 3 (SCA3)/Machado-Joseph disease (MJD), and spinocerebellar ataxia type 2 (SCA2), TDP-43 appears to play a significant pathogenic role. Intermediate-length polyQ expansions in ataxin 2 (ATX2) have been linked to a genetic risk factor for ALS, suggesting a direct interaction between TDP-43 and polyQ pathology. The study highlights the importance of TDP-43 in the pathogenesis of polyQ diseases, particularly ALS, and suggests that TDP-43 may influence polyQ pathology through distinct mechanisms.
