## The Effects of Acute Exercise on Mood, Cognition, Neurophysiology, and Neurochemical Pathways: A Review

This review article examines the effects of acute exercise on mood, cognition, and neurophysiological changes in both humans and animal models. The authors summarize a range of studies indicating that single bouts of physical activity can positively impact executive functions such as attention, working memory, decision-making, and inhibitory control, as well as mood states and stress levels. Additionally, the review highlights improvements in various aspects of cognitive function linked to different brain regions like the prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, striatum, and amygdala. The authors discuss the variability in study results due to differences in exercise protocols and suggest a standardized approach for future research to better understand the mechanisms underlying these effects. This work has implications for understanding how acute exercise can serve as an intervention tool for mental health and cognitive enhancement in both healthy individuals and those with neurological disorders.
