## Comics in the Health Humanities: A New Approach to Sex and Gender Education

Susan M. Squier's chapter in the health humanities explores the emergence of comics as a medium for sex and gender education, contrasting traditional medical approaches with underground comic books. Squier discusses early medical lectures on human sexuality at Indiana University in the late 1960s, noting their heteronormative and normative focus. She highlights the failure of these courses to significantly change students' attitudes and knowledge about sexuality. In contrast, Squier points to underground comics from the 1960s and 1970s, such as Justin Green's 'Binky Brown Meets the Holy Virgin Mary,' as alternative educational tools. These comics openly depicted various aspects of human sexuality, challenging societal norms and taboos. Squier argues that comics offer a unique platform for sex education, providing personal and diverse perspectives on sexual health and experiences, thus enriching the field of health humanities.
