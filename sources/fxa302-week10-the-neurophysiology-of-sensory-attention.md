## THE NEUROPHYSIOLOGY OF SENSORY ATTENTION

The document discusses the neurophysiology of sensory attention, focusing on the role of attention in cognitive processes and sensory perception. It explores how attention can selectively amplify sensory signals, such as increasing the magnitude of auditory evoked potentials (AEP) and visual-evoked potentials (VEP) when subjects focus on specific stimuli. The text also delves into the distinction between attending to an object versus attending to a location, and how this relates to the neural mechanisms underlying attention. The author uses the metaphor of a spotlight to illustrate how attention directs resources to prioritize one out of many possible targets. This selective attention is crucial for cognitive functions like memory, perception, and behavior. The text highlights the complexity of understanding attention and its neural bases, emphasizing the importance of this phenomenon in various aspects of neuroscience.
