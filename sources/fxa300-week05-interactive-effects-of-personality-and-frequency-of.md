## Interactive effects of personality and frequency of exposure on liking for music

The study by Hunter and Schellenberg explores how personality traits, particularly Openness-to-Experience, influence individuals' preferences for music as a function of exposure frequency. Through experiments involving undergraduate participants completing the Big Five Inventory and providing liking ratings for musical excerpts heard at different frequencies (novel, 2 times, 8 times, or 32 times), they reveal that while group-level data show an inverted-U pattern of preference increase followed by decrease with over-exposure, individual variations are significant. Higher Openness-to-Experience is associated with increased liking initially but decreased preference for highly exposed music, indicating a complex interplay between personality and exposure effects on musical preferences. These findings underscore the importance of considering individual differences in understanding how people's attitudes toward music evolve over time.
