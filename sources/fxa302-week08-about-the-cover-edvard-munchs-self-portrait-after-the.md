## About the Cover: Edvard Munch's Self-Portrait After the Spanish Flu

Edvard Munch's self-portrait painted after the Spanish flu outbreak of 1918-1919 is examined for its artistic and historical significance. The work reflects Munch's lifelong struggle with illness and despair, which heavily influenced his art. Munch's painting captures the somber mood and despair of a pandemic victim, paralleling the artist's previous work that focused on psychological and physical suffering. The article places Munch's self-portrait in the context of his broader artistic output and the cultural climate of the early 20th century, emphasizing the connection between personal and societal anxieties during the Spanish flu. Munch's work continues to resonate with contemporary issues in infectious disease, highlighting the enduring relevance of his artistic exploration of illness and despair.
