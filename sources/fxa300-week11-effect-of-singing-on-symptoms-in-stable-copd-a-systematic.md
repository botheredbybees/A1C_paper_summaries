## Effect of Singing on Symptoms in Stable COPD: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis investigates the impact of singing on symptoms in patients with stable Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD). The study, which includes five randomized controlled trials involving 333 patients, evaluates the effects of singing on various health-related outcomes. The authors conclude that singing can improve the physical component summary (PCS) of quality of life as measured by the Short Form 36 (SF-36) and maximal expiratory pressure (PEmax) in patients with COPD. However, singing had limited effects on mental health outcomes, lung function, exercise capacity, and adverse mental states. The findings suggest that singing may be a beneficial, cost-effective, and person-centred intervention for managing COPD symptoms, particularly in enhancing physical quality of life and respiratory muscle function. This study contributes to the understanding of non-pharmacological therapies in pulmonary rehabilitation and highlights the need for further research to explore the long-term benefits of singing therapy in COPD patients.
