## This Weird, Incurable Disease: Competing Diagnoses in the Rhetoric of Morgellons

The chapter explores the rhetorical aspects of the Morgellons disease, a condition characterized by skin sensations and symptoms that are often dismissed as delusional parasitosis by healthcare professionals. Patients describe mysterious fibers, black residue, and various systemic symptoms. The disease's diagnosis is contested between medical professionals who see it as a psychological condition and patients who believe it to be a physical, infectious disorder. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) initiated an investigation under pressure from patient advocates and members of Congress, highlighting the societal impact and the influence of the internet in spreading health concerns. The chapter argues that the rhetorical framing used by patients and medical professionals shapes public perception, research direction, and treatment approaches. It highlights the role of rhetoric in medical discourse and patient advocacy, emphasizing how symbolic patterns structure meaning and action in health contexts. This case study underscores the complexities in defining and treating conditions that bridge biological and cultural realms.
