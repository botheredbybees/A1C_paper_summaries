## Music and Movement for Health: an arts-based Intervention to promote health and wellbeing of community dwelling older adults in Ireland

The 'Music and Movement for Health' study is a pragmatic, cluster-randomized controlled feasibility trial designed to assess the viability of an innovative music and dance intervention aimed at enhancing health and well-being among community-dwelling older adults in Ireland. The research involved recruiting one hundred participants aged 65 years or older from seven clusters in the Mid-West region of Ireland, with four clusters assigned to the intervention group and three to the control group. The intervention consisted of a weekly 1.5-hour session followed by a 1-hour home-based program over twelve weeks. Based on the Medical Research Council Framework, this complex intervention was developed through stakeholder co-design using research evidence and theoretical guidance to enhance engagement. The study achieved its feasibility objectives regarding recruitment rates, retention, attendance, and participation, with no safety issues identified. Outcome measures were deemed suitable for the target population. Preliminary results indicate that the Music and Movement for Health initiative is feasible, acceptable, and safe for older adults in Ireland's Mid-West region, marking a pioneering evidence-based arts intervention combining music therapy with dance to improve health and well-being.
