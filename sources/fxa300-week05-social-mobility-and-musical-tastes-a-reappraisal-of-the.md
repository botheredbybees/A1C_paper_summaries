## Social mobility and musical tastes: A reappraisal of the social meaning of taste eclecticism

Philippe Coulangeon’s article examines the relationship between social mobility and musical tastes, arguing that secondary socialization influences taste more than primary socialization does. The study finds that selective omnivorousness (a preference for highbrow music alongside mass-produced pop) is common among higher-status groups, often associated with upward mobility. Extensive omnivorousness, less selective in cultural preferences and not necessarily tied to social mobility, occurs among intermediate status individuals. Another pattern, prevalent among lower-status groups and also linked to social mobility, exhibits little preference for highbrow genres. These findings suggest that taste eclecticism reflects complex social trajectories and settings. The research challenges the simplistic view of cultural distinction and highlights the nuances in understanding how social mobility impacts cultural tastes.
