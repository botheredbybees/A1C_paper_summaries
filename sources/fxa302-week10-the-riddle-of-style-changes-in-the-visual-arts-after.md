## The riddle of style changes in the visual arts after interference with the right brain

This article investigates the impact of visuo-spatial hemineglect, a neuropsychological condition caused by right-brain damage, on the visual arts. The authors analyze the post-stroke works of Lovis Corinth and Luchino Visconti, noting significant changes in their artistic styles following this condition. Key aspects observed include omissions, alterations in color usage, perseveration, and deformation in the artwork. Despite the diverse backgrounds and time periods of these artists, their post-stroke oeuvre exhibits similar stylistic changes, suggesting a potential relationship between visuo-spatial hemineglect and artistic production. The study proposes that neuropsychological investigations of artists with such conditions can provide insights into the brain-art relationship, offering a novel approach to understanding the influence of neurological damage on artistic creativity and expression.
