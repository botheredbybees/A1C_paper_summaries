## Art, nature and mental health: assessing the biopsychosocial effects of a ‘creative green prescription’ museum programme involving horticulture, artmaking and collections

This paper evaluates a unique museum-based intervention combining horticulture, artmaking, and engagement with museum collections to assess its biopsychosocial effects on mental health service users. The study utilized an exploratory sequential mixed methods design involving qualitative interviews, diary entries, and quantitative psychological wellbeing measures. It found that participants experienced increased feelings of well-being, improved self-esteem, decreased social isolation, and the formation of community bonds. Quantitative data showed a significant increase in psychological well-being post-intervention. The research highlights the potential benefits of combined arts- and nature-based activities for mental health improvement.
