## Cascading Reminiscence Bumps in Popular Music

This study explores the phenomenon of cascading reminiscence bumps in music-evoked autobiographical memories, suggesting that music transmitted from generation to generation shapes personal recollections and emotional responses more significantly than other cultural influences. The research identifies a peak in the recall of autobiographical memories for music from one’s late adolescence and early adulthood, but also uncovers an earlier peak for music from participants' parents’ generation during their childhood years. This finding indicates that music played during formative years has a lasting impact on individuals throughout their lives due to its strong personal and emotional significance. The study's results highlight the unique role of music in cultural transmission and social development, suggesting that it evokes more vivid, specific memories and stronger emotions compared to other forms of cultural content.
