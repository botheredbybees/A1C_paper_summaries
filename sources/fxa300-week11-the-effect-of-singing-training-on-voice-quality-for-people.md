## The Effect of Singing Training on Voice Quality for People With Quadriplegia

This study examines the impact of singing training on voice quality for individuals with quadriplegia. The research was conducted as a randomized controlled trial, involving 24 participants divided into a singing intervention group and an active music therapy control group. The intervention group received a 12-week program of respiratory-targeted singing exercises, while the control group engaged in music therapy activities. The study utilized objective voice assessments, including sound pressure levels and voice quality measurements, as well as subjective assessments through the Multidimensional Voice Profile and Perceptual Voice Profile. Results indicated that individuals with quadriplegia often experience voice impairments such as breathiness and roughness. Although the singing intervention showed potential for improving voice quality in terms of jitter, shimmer, and noise-to-harmonic ratio, the improvements did not reach statistical significance. The findings suggest that singing training may offer some benefits in ameliorating voice impairments in individuals with quadriplegia, contributing to better vocal performance and quality of life in the context of creative arts and health.
