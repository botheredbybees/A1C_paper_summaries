## Open Tension Pneumothorax in “The Dying Niobid”

The article discusses the depiction of an open tension pneumothorax in a marble statue known as “The Dying Niobid” from ancient Greece, housed at the Uffizi Gallery in Florence. The authors provide evidence suggesting that one of the statues represents a young man suffering from extreme asphyxia due to a penetrating arrow wound on his right side, leading to an open tension pneumothorax. This condition is characterized by a disruption of pleural structures and air accumulation within the pleural space, causing severe respiratory impairment and cardiovascular instability. The visual representation offers insight into ancient understanding of trauma and thoracic surgery, potentially serving as a teaching tool for medical students in icono-diagnosis to enhance their skills in physical examination.
