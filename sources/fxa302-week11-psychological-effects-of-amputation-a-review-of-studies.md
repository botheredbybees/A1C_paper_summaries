## Psychological effects of amputation: A review of studies from India

This review article discusses the psychological impacts of amputation in India, focusing on the prevalence of psychiatric disorders among amputees. The study synthesizes findings from 12 observational studies, involving samples ranging from 16 to 190 individuals, predominantly male and primarily due to traumatic causes such as motor vehicle accidents and machinery injuries. The review highlights a significant range in the prevalence of psychiatric disorders, with reported rates of depression ranging from 10.4% to 63%, and PTSD ranging from 3.3% to 56.3%. The article underscores the need for psychological support and the involvement of mental health professionals in the treatment and rehabilitation of amputees to address these distressing conditions effectively. This research is crucial for understanding the unique challenges faced by amputees in the Indian context and informs the direction of further research and service delivery.
