## Does Singing Promote Well-Being?: An Empirical Study of Professional and Amateur Singers during a Singing Lesson

This study explores the potential beneficial effects of singing on well-being by comparing professional and amateur singers during a singing lesson. Eight amateurs and eight professionals participated, with physiological and psychological measures taken before and after the lesson. The results showed that both groups experienced increased heart rate variability and serum concentrations of oxytocin. However, amateurs reported increased joy and relaxation, whereas professionals experienced more arousal and less well-being. The study suggests that singing may promote well-being more for amateurs than for professionals, possibly due to the different motivations and experiences of the two groups.
