## Rapid and dynamic processing of face pareidolia in the human brain

This study explores the neural mechanisms underlying face pareidolia, which refers to the perception of illusory faces in non-face objects. Utilizing functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and magnetoencephalography (MEG), researchers examined both spatial and temporal aspects of how the brain processes these illusory perceptions. They discovered that initial processing is more similar to real face detection, but within 250 milliseconds, this representation shifts, making illusory faces indistinguishable from regular objects in subsequent neural activity. This suggests an initially broad, sensitive mechanism for detecting facial features, which quickly resolves into a more object-based perceptual framework. The research highlights the brain's capacity to rapidly integrate and reinterpret visual stimuli, providing insights relevant to creative arts and health through understanding cognitive flexibility and perceptual biases.
