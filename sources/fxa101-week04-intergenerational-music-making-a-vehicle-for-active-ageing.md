## Intergenerational music-making: a vehicle for active ageing for children and older people

The chapter explores the benefits of intergenerational music-making in fostering active ageing for children and older adults. Active ageing is defined as a comprehensive strategy to maximize participation and well-being across different stages of life, emphasizing individual, organizational, and societal levels. Intergenerational practices aim to facilitate meaningful collaborations among various age groups through purposeful activities that support mutual learning and interaction.

Research highlights the positive impacts of such activities on social and personal development, identity negotiation, physical health, cognitive skills, and emotional well-being for both children and older adults. Music-making promotes cognitive skills related to listening, concentration, attention, and idea sharing, leading to creative expressions. Intergenerational music programs also foster diverse populations' integration and interpersonal attachments.

Benefits specifically experienced by older adults include improved health and reduced isolation through social interactions. Generativity, the concept of contributing positively to society, enhances their sense of worth and productivity. Younger participants benefit from the dissolution of negative stereotypes about ageing and the creation of positive relationships that boost self-esteem.

Case studies illustrate how intergenerational music-making can serve as a valuable resource for communities, fostering connections between older adults and children through shared musical interests and collaborative projects.
