## Music to My Senses: Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging Evidence of Music Analgesia Across Connectivity Networks Spanning the Brain and Brainstem

This study investigates the neural mechanisms underlying music analgesia using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) in 20 healthy individuals. Participants underwent noxious thermal stimulation with and without concurrent pleasurable music. The study aimed to explore how music alters connectivity across brain networks involved in pain perception, cognition, emotion, and autonomic function. Results indicate that music modifies connectivity between key brain regions such as the insula, thalamus, hypothalamus, amygdala, and hippocampus. These findings suggest that music analgesia involves complex interactions across cognitive, emotional, and sensory domains, with implications for pain management strategies. However, the effects are variable across individuals, highlighting the importance of considering individual pain sensitivity in therapeutic approaches involving music.
