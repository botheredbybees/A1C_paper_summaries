## The Virtues of the Imperfect Body

Rosemarie Tong explores the ethical implications of genetic interventions in human reproduction, focusing on whether parents have a right or duty to use genetic technologies to create 'perfect' offspring. Drawing on the film Gattaca, Tong questions the societal pressures and moral implications of designing children without imperfections. She contrasts the views of John A. Robertson, who supports parents' rights to enhance their children's genetic traits, with Dena Davis, who argues against genetic interventions that limit children's future opportunities. Tong's central argument revolves around the virtues and moral value of living with imperfections and the ethical considerations of parental choices in shaping their children's genetic makeup.
