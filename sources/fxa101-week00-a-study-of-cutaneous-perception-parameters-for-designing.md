## A Study of Cutaneous Perception Parameters for Designing Haptic Symbols towards Information Transfer

This paper examines the cutaneous perception parameters essential for designing haptic symbols to enhance information transfer through vibrotactile displays. The study addresses challenges in converting auditory and visual concepts into tactile patterns due to differences in spatial and temporal resolutions between sensory modalities. It explores how manipulating various tactile perception parameters, such as intensity, loci, frequency, duration, illusions, and combinations thereof, can create perceivable haptic symbols for effective information transfer. The authors also discuss the limitations of current vibrotactile communication systems and offer recommendations on defining which cutaneous perception parameters to engage based on specific tasks or patterns.
