## Late-life creativity

This review discusses the book 'Four Last Songs' by Linda and Michael Hutcheon, which explores late-life creativity through the lens of four composers: Giuseppe Verdi, Richard Strauss, Olivier Messiaen, and Benjamin Britten. The authors provide a narrative that highlights how these artists maintained or even enhanced their creative capacities in later life despite health challenges. Although the inclusion of Britten is questioned due to equating his illness with aging, the book overall presents a valuable counterpoint to negative stereotypes about aging by emphasizing the potential for continued artistic productivity and innovation. The review concludes that the Hutcheons' work underscores the importance of reframing old age in terms of creative potential rather than decline, contributing significantly to discussions on longevity and creativity within the context of gerontology.
