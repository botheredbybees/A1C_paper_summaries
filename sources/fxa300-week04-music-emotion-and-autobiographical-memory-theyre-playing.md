## Music, Emotion, and Autobiographical Memory: They're Playing Your Song

This study investigates very long-term memory for popular music across different age groups and the relationship between musical memories and emotional responses. Older adults (ages 66-71) and younger adults (ages 18-21) listened to excerpts of songs from various decades, rating their emotions and preferences while trying to recall details about each song. The results showed that older participants had stronger emotional connections and better recollection for music from their youth compared to more recent years. However, the study found limited cued autobiographical memories specific to events recalled by these musical stimuli. Additionally, older adults were less likely than younger ones to retrieve multiple attributes of a song together (like title and artist). There was also a positive correlation between emotionality ratings and memory performance, particularly for older participants. The findings contribute to the understanding of long-term memory retention and the interplay between emotional responses and memory retrieval in the context of music.
