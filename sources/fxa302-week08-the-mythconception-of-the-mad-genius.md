## The mythconception of the mad genius

Arne Dietrich challenges the widely held belief that there is a strong link between creativity and mental illness, often referred to as the 'mad genius' concept. He argues that this belief is a myth based on anecdotal evidence and selective reporting of data, rather than on robust scientific research. Dietrich emphasizes that while there are many famous historical figures who suffered from mental illness, these cases are not representative of the general population. He cites statistical data showing that mental illness is relatively common, occurring in about 10% of the population for mood disorders and similar prevalence for anxiety disorders. In contrast, the incidence of exceptional creativity and genius is likely much lower. Therefore, the claim that mental illness and genius are inherently linked is statistically invalid. Dietrich highlights cognitive biases such as the base rate fallacy and availability heuristic as contributing to the persistence of this myth. The article underscores the importance of empirical evidence in understanding the relationship between creativity and mental health, rather than relying on anecdotal stories and selective data. This perspective is crucial for creative arts and health studies, as it encourages a more nuanced and evidence-based approach to understanding the complexities of creativity and mental health.
