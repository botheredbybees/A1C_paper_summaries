## Neuromuscular and Musculoskeletal Problems in Instrumental Musicians

This article by Richard J. Lederman reviews the medical issues faced by instrumental musicians, focusing on neuromuscular and musculoskeletal disorders. Over a 20-year period, there has been growing interest in the health of performing artists, with particular emphasis on musicians. Among 1353 musicians evaluated, 64% had musculoskeletal disorders, 20% had peripheral nerve problems, and 8% suffered from focal dystonia. Women were more commonly affected than men, especially for musculoskeletal issues. The article highlights the prevalence and types of disorders, noting specific conditions like regional muscle pain, tendinitis, ligament sprain, thoracic outlet syndrome, ulnar neuropathy, and carpal tunnel syndrome. Each instrument type is associated with distinct patterns of symptoms linked to the instrument's static and dynamic stresses. Electrodiagnostic studies are crucial for diagnosing nerve entrapment syndromes, and successful treatment outcomes are highest for these conditions, followed by musculoskeletal pain syndromes. Despite recent advances, focal dystonia remains difficult to treat. The author emphasizes the importance of understanding these conditions to ensure musicians can continue their careers without pain or disability, and underscores the need for further research into the causes and treatments of these disorders.
