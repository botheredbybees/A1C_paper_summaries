## Disability in Two Doctor Stories

Martha Stoddard Holmes' chapter 'Disability in Two Doctor Stories' explores the portrayal of disability in Mary Shelley's 'Frankenstein.' Holmes argues that the novel, while often interpreted through a biomedical ethics lens, can also be read as a narrative about the social construction of disability. The creature created by Victor Frankenstein, despite being physically capable, is socially ostracized due to his appearance, illustrating the impact of societal perceptions on disability. Holmes contrasts the medical model of disability, which views it as a personal tragedy and focuses on individual impairment, with the social model, which considers disability as a product of societal attitudes and environments. The chapter highlights the relevance of disability studies in understanding narratives of marginalization and the importance of considering the perspectives of disabled individuals in academic discourse.
