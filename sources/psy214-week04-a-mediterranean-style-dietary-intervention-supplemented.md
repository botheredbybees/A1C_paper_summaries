## A Mediterranean-style dietary intervention supplemented with fish oil improves diet quality and mental health in people with depression: A randomized controlled trial (HELFIMED)

The study investigated the impact of a Mediterranean-style dietary intervention supplemented with fish oil on mental health and diet quality in individuals suffering from depression. Participants were randomized into two groups: one receiving food hampers, cooking workshops following a Mediterranean diet (MedDiet) for three months, and fish oil supplements for six months; the other attending social activities as a control group. Assessments at baseline, three, and six months included mental health evaluations, quality of life questionnaires, and dietary surveys. The MedDiet intervention led to significant improvements in both diet quality and mental health outcomes compared to the control group. Key findings showed higher consumption of vegetables, fruits, nuts, legumes, whole grains, and reduced intake of unhealthy snacks and red meat/chicken among those on the MedDiet. Mental health metrics such as depression severity and quality-of-life scores also improved significantly for the intervention group. The study highlights that dietary changes are achievable and can positively influence mental health in people with depression.
