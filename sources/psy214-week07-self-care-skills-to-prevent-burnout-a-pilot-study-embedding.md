## Self-Care Skills to Prevent Burnout: A Pilot Study Embedding Mindfulness in an Undergraduate Nursing Course

The study explores the effects of mindfulness education and self-care skills training on stress and self-care among undergraduate nursing students. The four-week pilot program included in-person mindfulness sessions for first-year nursing students, with a follow-up assessment four weeks later. Key findings indicate that practicing mindfulness outside of class significantly increased self-care scores by 9.5% from pre- to post-intervention (p = .01), although this improvement was not sustained at the follow-up. Stress levels did not change significantly over the intervention period or during the follow-up, possibly due to immediate clinical placements after the program's conclusion. The study concludes that incorporating mindfulness education can enhance self-care skills in nursing students, potentially reducing burnout risk upon entering professional practice.
