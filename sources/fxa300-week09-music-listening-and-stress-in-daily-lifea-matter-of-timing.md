## Music Listening and Stress in Daily Life—a Matter of Timing

This study investigates the relationship between music listening and stress in daily life, focusing on the temporal dynamics of this association. The research, conducted through an Ambulatory Assessment study with 60 participants aged 18 to 34, uses both subjective self-reports and objective data to track music listening and stress levels over a week. The findings suggest that self-reported music listening is associated with lower stress levels, whereas objective data did not show a significant correlation. However, when assessing the duration of music listening, both subjective and objective data indicate that at least 20 minutes of music listening is linked to lower stress reports. The study also revealed a time-delayed effect of music listening on stress reduction, indicating that the benefits of music listening may take some time to manifest. The research highlights the importance of combining both subjective and objective measures to understand the full impact of music listening on stress reduction, and underscores the need for further experimental research to confirm these findings.
