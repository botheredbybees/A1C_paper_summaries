## Emotion Processing and Mental Health

This excerpt from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle discusses the role of emotion processing in mental health, particularly in the context of depression and anxiety. It highlights that these conditions are characterized by exaggerated implicit emotional reactions due to compromised prefrontal function, as noted by affective neuroscientists Hariri and Whalen. The text explores the neural circuitry underlying emotional responses, the control mechanisms involved in regulating these responses, and the distinction between explicit and implicit emotional processing. A key focus is the amygdala, which plays a critical role in processing trustworthiness and is implicated in judgments of approachability and trustworthiness, as demonstrated in a seminal study by Adolphs, Tranel, and Damasio. The chapter underscores the relevance of affective neuroscience to understanding cognitive processes influenced by emotional context, providing valuable insights into the neural bases of emotions and their relationship to mental health.
