## Aspirational Ethics: Considerations for Art Therapy Practice in a Health-Care Setting for Adults With Dementia

Sarah Anacleto’s article explores the ethical principles guiding art therapy practice for adults with dementia, focusing on autonomy, nonmaleficence, beneficence, fidelity, justice, and creativity as outlined by the American Art Therapy Association (AATA). The author discusses the complexities of decision-making in health-care settings where clients may have diminished capacity due to their condition. She emphasizes the importance of respecting client autonomy, even when it requires flexibility and creativity from therapists. An example is provided about a client who refused to allow her artwork to be photographed despite prior consent from a personal representative, highlighting the need for art therapists to prioritize client choices over their own research goals. The article also addresses nonmaleficence by discussing how art therapists must consider physical safety, emotional support, and cognitive disorientation in their practice. Furthermore, Anacleto highlights beneficence through promoting well-being not only within individual therapy sessions but also within the broader health-care community, advocating for respectful interactions with clients' artworks and creative processes.
