## Gratitude: Interpersonal, Circumstantial, Transcendental, and Interventions

The lesson discusses the psychological concept of gratitude, distinguishing between interpersonal gratitude (gratitude towards a person), circumstantial gratitude (appreciation for life circumstances), and transcendental gratitude (spiritual appreciation). Gratitude can be both a state (temporary feeling) and a trait (dispositional tendency). It also explores gratitude interventions, primarily through journalling and letter-writing. Journalling involves recording daily things one is grateful for, often three per day, to enhance well-being. Letter-writing interventions involve writing and delivering a letter of thanks. Additionally, the lesson introduces mental subtraction as a way to appreciate life by considering how life might be different without certain positive events. The lesson includes references to studies by Emmons and McCullough (2003), Seligman et al. (2005), Jackowska et al. (2016), O’Connell et al. (2017), and Koo et al. (2008).
