## Homo faber Revisited: Postphenomenology and Material Engagement Theory

This paper integrates perspectives from postphenomenology and Material Engagement Theory (MET) to explore the notion of Homo faber, emphasizing the human predisposition for technological embodiment and creativity. The authors argue that humans co-constitute themselves through their engagement with material forms and technologies, which not only shape but also transform human consciousness and behavior over time. This continuous dialectic is central to human evolution and development, highlighting a unique interactivity between people and things that distinguishes the human species from others. By bridging long-term archaeological views and contemporary technological perspectives, the paper aims to redefine Homo faber as a concept beyond mere tool-making, focusing on the creative material engagement inherent in human life.
