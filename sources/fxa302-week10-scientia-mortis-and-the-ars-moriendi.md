## Scientia Mortis and the Ars Moriendi

Jeffrey P. Bishop critiques the concept of scientia mortis, a science aimed at upholding the humanity of dying patients through spiritual care assessments. The article discusses how the medical humanities and palliative care often seek to integrate spirituality and humanism into healthcare, aiming to provide a more humane and compassionate approach. However, Bishop argues that the spiritual assessments, often grounded in social scientific methodologies, fail to capture the essential and irreducible aspects of spiritual and religious life at the end of life. He highlights the tension between the desire to measure and standardize spiritual care and the inherently unmeasurable nature of spirituality. Bishop contrasts this with an ars moriendi, an art of dying grounded in a lived and storied experience, which resists the standardization and objectification of spiritual care. The article emphasizes the importance of recognizing the limitations of scientific approaches in understanding and addressing the spiritual dimensions of end-of-life care, advocating for a more nuanced and locally specific approach to palliative care.
