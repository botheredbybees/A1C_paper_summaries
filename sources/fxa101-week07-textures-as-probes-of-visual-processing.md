## Textures as Probes of Visual Processing

The article explores the utility of visual textures as probes for understanding visual processing mechanisms at both the behavioral and neural levels. Visual textures are defined as stimuli that have structure across multiple spatial scales and are well-suited for addressing general questions about visual function. The authors argue that textures are effective probes because they bridge the gap between analytically convenient stimuli and complex naturalistic stimuli, offering insights into perceptual spaces, the modulation of early visual processing by task, and the transformation of sensory stimuli into patterns of neural activity. Key questions addressed include the nature and structure of perceptual spaces, modulation of early visual processing by task, and the transformation of sensory stimuli into relevant patterns of neural activity. The paper also highlights historical contributions to the field and discusses how visual textures can be used to investigate various aspects of visual processing, including the role of top-down influences. The relevance to creative arts and health lies in the understanding of visual perception, which can inform the design and appreciation of visual art and aid in developing visual rehabilitation techniques for health applications.
