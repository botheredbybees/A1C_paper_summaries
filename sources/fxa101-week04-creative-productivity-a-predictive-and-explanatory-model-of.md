## Creative Productivity: A Predictive and Explanatory Model of Career Trajectories and Landmarks

This article presents a comprehensive model to explain and predict variations in creative productivity over time and across individuals, focusing on major and minor creative achievements. The author develops a theoretical framework that incorporates the Darwinian concept of variation-selection processes in creativity, suggesting that creativity involves spontaneous generation and selection of conceptual combinations without prior knowledge of their utility. The model accounts for empirical age curves of creative output, explaining both longitudinal and cross-sectional variations in career trajectories. It proposes two parameters—initial creative potential and age at career onset—as well as information-processing rates to derive predictions about individual differences in productivity and the placement of career landmarks such as first, best, and last contributions. The article highlights how this model can explain diverse patterns of creative success and decline observed across different domains and individuals, offering a parsimonious yet powerful explanation for the complex dynamics of creativity.
