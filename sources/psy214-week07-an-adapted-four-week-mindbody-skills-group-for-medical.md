## An Adapted, Four-Week Mind–Body Skills Group for Medical Students: Reducing Stress, Increasing Mindfulness, and Enhancing Self-Care

This study evaluates the feasibility, acceptability, and effectiveness of a four-week mind–body skills workshop for medical students designed to reduce stress, increase mindfulness, and enhance self-care behaviors. The objective was to address the high-stress environment in medical school by providing a scientifically grounded intervention that fits within the demanding schedule of medical students. Participants included 44 medical and physician-scientist (MD/PhD) students from Duke University School of Medicine who completed pretest and posttest evaluations using validated self-report surveys measuring stress and mindfulness levels. Results indicated high feasibility, with enrollment increasing over three years to 23 participants per workshop in 2011. Participants reported the workshop was valuable for coping with medical school challenges, leading to a significant decrease in perceived stress (P < .001; d = 1.38) and an increase in mindfulness (P < .001; d = 0.92). These findings suggest that brief, voluntary mind–body skills workshops can be effective for enhancing medical students' self-care and resilience.
