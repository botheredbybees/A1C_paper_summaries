## Roald Dahl and the complete locked-in syndrome: “Cold dead body, living brain”

This paper discusses Roald Dahl's depiction of the complete locked-in syndrome in his short story 'William and Mary'. It describes a scenario where a patient's brain is kept alive and functioning outside the body, lacking all motor output, including eye movements, and communicates through a brain-computer interface. The author highlights how Dahl's 1959 story was ahead of its time compared to the medical community's recognition of this condition in 2006. The paper also reviews the clinical implications and ethical considerations of the complete locked-in syndrome, including the use of pupil responses as a diagnostic tool for consciousness.
