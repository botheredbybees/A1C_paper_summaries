## Week 8 Lecture on Arts Therapy and Dementia Care

The Week 8 lecture in the course FXA100 focuses on the application of arts therapy and facilitation techniques for individuals with dementia. It distinguishes between an Arts Therapist, who uses therapeutic models like CBT and ACT through arts activities, and an Arts Facilitator, whose role is more about providing ideas and coordinating projects while being responsive to participants' creative expressions.

The lecture emphasizes the importance of understanding two modes of communication in art-making: internal dialogue, where the artwork serves as a form of self-reflection, and outward communication, aimed at sharing with others. It highlights challenges faced by adults with dementia regarding preconceptions about art creation, advocating for an unfolding story approach over focusing on end products.

Key methods introduced include guided initiation to assist individuals in overcoming blocks due to brain damage and facilitating participation without overwhelming them. The lecture also discusses the importance of safety measures and cultural considerations when designing arts programs for diverse groups affected by dementia. Additionally, it addresses specific challenges associated with different types of dementias, such as Alzheimer’s disease and Fronto-Temporal Dementia.

The discussion on participatory visual arts in dementia care encourages students to consider the modes of communication identified by Killick & Craig (2011), strategies for stimulating creativity, and safety measures for engaging participants. The lecture concludes with reminders about upcoming assessments and a reflection on designing programs that prioritize process over outcome.
