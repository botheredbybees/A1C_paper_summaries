## Examining arts and creativity in later life and its impact on older people’s health and wellbeing: a systematic review of the evidence

The study by McQuade and O’Sullivan (2024) conducts a systematic review to explore the impact of arts and creativity on the health and wellbeing of older adults. The authors examined ninety-three empirical studies published between 2013 and 2020, focusing on group-based interventions involving dance, music, singing, visual and creative arts, theatre, and drama. Key findings indicate that participation in these activities is associated with various positive health outcomes: improved physical balance and lower body strength from dancing; enhanced cognitive function, quality of life, and affective states through regular engagement in music and singing; reduced loneliness and increased social connectedness via visual and creative arts; and emotional wellbeing gains from theatre and drama. The review concludes that group-based arts and creativity interventions can significantly benefit older adults by promoting physical, mental, and social health, thus supporting public health policies aimed at healthy aging.
