## Character Strengths

The document explores the concept of character strengths, emphasizing their importance in positive psychology. The rationale behind studying character strengths is that they offer a framework that contrasts with traditional diagnoses found in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) by focusing on capacities for thinking, feeling, and behaving in ways that benefit both individuals and society. The Values in Action (VIA) framework was developed by Christopher Peterson, Martin Seligman, and a team of 55 social scientists over a 3-year project, incorporating insights from world religions, philosophers, historical and fictional figures, and psychologists. This framework identifies 24 character strengths organized into six overarching virtue categories: wisdom, courage, humanity, justice, temperance, and transcendence. However, recent research suggests that the empirical support for the structure of these virtues is lacking, and newer approaches propose a more simplified framework of intellectual, social/interpersonal, and self-regulatory/intrapersonal virtues. The document highlights the uniqueness of each individual's constellation of character strengths, with no one possessing all or none of the strengths. The VIA Character Strengths Survey is recommended for individuals to understand their own unique strengths.
