## The Brain of Opera Singers: Experience-Dependent Changes in Functional Activation

This study investigates the neural correlates of professional singing training and its impact on brain activation patterns. Researchers used fMRI to examine brain activity in professional opera singers, conservatory-level vocal students, and laymen during the singing of an Italian aria. The results showed that extensive vocal training is associated with increased functional activation in the bilateral primary somatosensory cortex, right primary sensorimotor cortex, inferior parietal lobe, and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex. At the subcortical level, expert singers demonstrated higher activation in the basal ganglia, thalamus, and cerebellum. These findings suggest that professional singing training leads to neural reorganization, enhancing kinesthetic motor control and sensorimotor guidance. The study's implications extend to voice rehabilitation and the acquisition of implicit motor skills requiring interoception. Understanding these adaptations provides insights into the neurobiological underpinnings of vocal expertise and could inform therapeutic interventions for voice disorders.
