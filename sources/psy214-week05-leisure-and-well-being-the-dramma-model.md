## Leisure and well-being: the DRAMMA model

The DRAMMA model, proposed by Newman, Tay and Diener (2014), provides a framework for understanding the role of leisure activities in promoting well-being. This model emphasizes five key components: Detachment-recovery, Autonomy, Mastery, Meaning, and Affiliation. The central argument is that each component contributes uniquely to an individual's ability to derive satisfaction from leisure activities and enhance their overall sense of well-being. Key findings suggest that individuals who experience detachment-recovery through leisure activities, such as disengaging from work responsibilities, tend to report higher levels of psychological recovery and well-being. Autonomy in choosing one’s own leisure pursuits is also linked to greater life satisfaction. Mastery refers to the challenge and skill balance necessary for engaging in enjoyable activities, which can lead to a sense of accomplishment and personal growth. Meaning involves finding purpose and significance in one's leisure choices, contributing to a deeper connection with self and others. Affiliation highlights the importance of social connections during leisure time, fostering support networks that enhance emotional well-being. The DRAMMA model is highly relevant to creative arts and health as it encourages individuals to integrate meaningful, fulfilling activities into their lives, thereby promoting holistic wellness.
