## Factory Music: How the Industrial Geography and Working-Class Environment of Post-War Birmingham Fostered the Birth of Heavy Metal

Leigh Michael Harrison's article explores the origins of heavy metal music in the industrial city of Birmingham, England, during the post-war period. Harrison argues that the sounds and visual landscapes of Birmingham's heavy industry, along with the city's working-class conditions, significantly influenced the development of heavy metal music. The article details how Black Sabbath and Judas Priest, both from Birmingham, drew inspiration from the city's industrial environment and the daily struggles of working-class life. The author discusses how the city's industrial geography and social conditions are reflected in the lyrics and sound of heavy metal, which emerged as a new genre in the late 1960s. Harrison's study highlights the importance of environmental and social factors in the creation of musical genres, emphasizing the role of the city's industrial landscape in shaping heavy metal's aggressive and dark sound. The article contributes to the field of geomusicology, examining the relationship between geographic settings and musical development.
