## Aesthetic Music Therapy

Colin Andrew Lee's chapter on Aesthetic Music Therapy (AeMT) explores the therapeutic potential of music, particularly through the lens of Mozart's Requiem. Lee discusses how the composition reflects the human condition and its profound influence on therapeutic practice. He details a case study involving a client with AIDS, where Mozart's music served as a foundational element for an improvisational session, demonstrating the therapeutic relationship and emotional release. Lee's work questions the relationship between musical assurance and therapeutic outcomes, proposing that great works of art transcend reality and enhance clinical improvisation. The chapter outlines the historical development of Lee's practice, from his initial training in Nordoff-Robbins music therapy to his research and teaching in North America. It highlights the importance of music-centered thinking and the compositional process in music therapy, leading to the conception of AeMT as a balance between music-centered practice and therapeutic aims.
