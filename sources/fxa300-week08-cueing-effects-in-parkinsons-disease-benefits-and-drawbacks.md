## Cueing effects in Parkinson’s disease: Benefits and drawbacks

This study explores the use of cueing, including auditory and rhythmic signals, to improve gait and balance in patients with Parkinson’s disease (PD). Despite the effectiveness of cueing in enhancing motor performance, the response varies among patients due to factors such as cognitive overload and perceptual rhythmicity. The CuPiD project, funded by the European Union, investigated the efficacy of an intelligent cueing system, CuPiD, which incorporates sensor technology to provide real-time feedback based on walking performance. The study involved 40 PD patients who underwent a six-week training program outside their homes. Results indicated that while the CuPiD group trained less than the control group, they showed significant improvements in balance. Both groups improved on single and dual task gait speed, highlighting the complexities involved in delivering cueing in a clinically meaningful way.
