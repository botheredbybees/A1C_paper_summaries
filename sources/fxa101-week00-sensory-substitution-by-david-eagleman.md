## Sensory Substitution by David Eagleman

The webpage discusses sensory substitution, a technique for circumventing the loss of one sense by feeding its information through another channel. Neuroscientist David Eagleman developed this method to help people with hearing impairments perceive auditory information via small vibrations on their skin, initially using a vest and later scaling down the technology to a wristband device. This non-invasive approach was commercialized under Neosensory and expanded beyond deafness to address high-frequency loss, tinnitus, and balance issues. The broader aim is to understand how sensory streams can be re-packaged into atypical sensory channels for rehabilitation or new perceptions. Underlying the work are principles of neural plasticity.
