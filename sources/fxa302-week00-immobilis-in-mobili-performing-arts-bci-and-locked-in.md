## Immobilis in mobili: Performing Arts, BCI, and Locked-In Syndrome

This paper explores the intersection of brain-computer interfaces (BCI) and locked-in syndrome (LIS) in the context of performing arts. It discusses how BCI technology can provide people with LIS, who have severe motor impairments but intact cognitive function, the means to engage in artistic expression. The author outlines three conceptual models to guide the design of BCI systems for this purpose, emphasizing the potential of BCI to serve as a medium for artistic expression that overcomes physical limitations. The paper also examines the broader implications of BCI technology in the context of disability and the creative process, proposing that technology can redefine the boundaries of artistic creation and enhance the accessibility of artistic expression.
