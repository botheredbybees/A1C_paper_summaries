## Technology-enhanced writing therapy for people with aphasia: results of a quasi-randomized waitlist controlled study

This study evaluates the benefits of technology-enhanced writing therapy for individuals with aphasia, focusing on acquired writing impairment or dysgraphia. The research utilized a quasi-randomized waitlist controlled design, where participants received 12 hours of therapy either immediately or after a 6-week delay. The primary outcome was measured through a functional assessment of writing, which was conducted both manually and using assistive technology. The study revealed significant improvements in writing performance when technology was employed, indicating a compensatory rather than remedial effect. Secondary measures, such as written naming, functional communication, mood, and quality of life, did not show significant changes. The findings suggest that digital technologies, including speech-to-text software, can be effectively used in therapy to support individuals with aphasia in achieving functional writing tasks.
