## Tracing the Dynamic Changes in Perceived Tonal Organization in a Spatial Representation of Musical Keys

This study investigates the cognitive representation of harmonic and tonal structure in Western music using a tone-profile technique, where listeners rate how well single tones fit into scales, chords, or cadences. The research reveals stable rating profiles for major and minor keys that are spatially mapped on a four-dimensional torus, reflecting relationships like the circle of fifths and parallel/relative key relations. Single chords (major, minor, diminished, dominant seventh) are closely associated with their corresponding tonal contexts. Modulations between chord sequences demonstrate varying rates of perceived key changes based on proximity in musical space. The findings suggest that listeners integrate harmonic functions across multiple chords to develop an evolving sense of key, which supports the notion of hierarchical cognitive processes involved in music perception and structure.
