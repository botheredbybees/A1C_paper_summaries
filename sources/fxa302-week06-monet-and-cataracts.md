## Monet and Cataracts

The article 'Monet and Cataracts' explores how Claude Monet's progressive cataract disease impacted his artwork. Known as the father of Impressionism, Monet experienced significant visual impairment from cataracts that began in 1912. This condition led to a notable change in style and color palette in his later works compared to earlier pieces like 'Waterlily Pond and Japanese Footbridge' painted before his eye problems. Monet's famous series of 'Water Lilies', depicting his garden at Giverny, shows a stark contrast with the murkier colors and less clarity characteristic of cataract-affected vision. The article also mentions other historical painters who have been affected by visual disorders, suggesting that such conditions can significantly alter an artist’s perspective and technique in their later works.
