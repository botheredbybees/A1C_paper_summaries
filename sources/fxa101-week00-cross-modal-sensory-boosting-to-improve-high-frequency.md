## Cross-Modal Sensory Boosting to Improve High-Frequency Hearing Loss: Device Development and Validation

The study explores a vibrotactile sensory substitution wristband designed to assist individuals with high-frequency hearing loss in understanding speech communication better. The device converts specific high-frequency phonemes into haptic vibrations, allowing users to perceive sounds they might otherwise miss due to their hearing impairment. Over the course of six weeks, 16 participants wore this wristband and showed significant improvement in self-perceived ability to understand conversations, particularly in noisy environments or reverberating spaces. The average APHAB (Abbreviated Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit) benefit score improved from a baseline of 40.32 to 27.93 at the end of the study period. Participants without hearing aids experienced more pronounced benefits compared to those using traditional hearing devices, highlighting the potential for this technology to complement existing assistive technologies.
