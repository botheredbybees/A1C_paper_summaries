## Introduction to Section I: The Neurobiology of Thinking

The document introduces the neurobiology of thinking, a field that examines the biological foundations of mental phenomena, particularly consciousness and cognition. It emphasizes the interdisciplinary nature of cognitive neuroscience, which integrates methods and theories from various disciplines such as philosophy, biology, and psychology. The author discusses how cognitive neuroscience distinguishes itself by focusing on the biological mechanisms underlying mental processes, while acknowledging the challenges of defining its boundaries due to overlapping interests with other fields. The text also explores the historical context of cognitive neuroscience, highlighting its emergence alongside studies of human behavior and cognitive psychology. It concludes by asserting the importance of considering both human and nonhuman animal research in understanding neural functions, underscoring the need for a broad yet precise definition of cognitive neuroscience to encompass its interdisciplinary scope.
