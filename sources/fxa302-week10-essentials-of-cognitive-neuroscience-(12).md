## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience

The text excerpt is from the book 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle, discussing the role of the Fusiform Face Area (FFA) in face processing. It highlights a study by Parvizi et al. (2012) demonstrating the causal role of the FFA in face perception through direct electrical stimulation in the human brain. The study involved a patient who experienced perceptual metamorphoses when focusing on faces but not on other objects, such as 'Get Well Soon' helium balloons. This finding supports the idea that the FFA is necessary for normal face processing, despite previous evidence suggesting it is not specialized for face representation. The text also touches on the reconciliation of seemingly incompatible perspectives on cognitive neuroscience, illustrating how cutting-edge techniques can provide evidence for both views. The relevance to creative arts and health lies in understanding the neural basis of perception and cognition, which can inform therapeutic approaches for disorders affecting perception and recognition, such as prosopagnosia.
