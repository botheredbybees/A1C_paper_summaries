## Decision Making - Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience

The chapter explores the neurological basis of decision-making, particularly focusing on perceptual, value-based, and foraging decisions. Perceptual decisions are those based on sensory inputs, such as judging the direction of motion in a visual field. Microstimulation experiments by Newsome, Britten, and Movshon demonstrated the causal relationship between neural activity in the middle temporal (MT) area and a monkey's decision-making process about motion direction. Key findings include the role of MT neurons in perceptual decisions and the influence of motor plans on value-based decisions. The chapter also discusses the role of the orbitofrontal cortex in representing a common currency for decision-making. The relevance to creative arts and health lies in understanding the neural mechanisms underlying cognitive processes, which can inform creative interventions and therapeutic approaches.
