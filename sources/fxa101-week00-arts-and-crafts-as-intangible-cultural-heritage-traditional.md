## Arts and Crafts as Intangible Cultural Heritage: Traditional Embroidery in Georgia

The paper discusses the importance of arts and crafts, specifically traditional embroidery (naqaroba) in Georgia, as a form of intangible cultural heritage. It highlights how traditional naqaroba, such as Achara embroidery, is deeply connected to local customs, rituals, beliefs, and knowledge systems. The author examines the historical context of naqaroba, emphasizing its significance in social status determination, especially among women who create it. Another focus is on 'Blue Table's' lurji sufra (blue tablecloth) production, which combines traditional patterns with modern technology to produce innovative products for the tourism market and has been recognized as an intangible cultural heritage object by Georgian authorities since 2017.
