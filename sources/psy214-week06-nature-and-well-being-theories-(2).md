## Nature and Well-being: Theories

This document outlines several theories linking nature to human health and well-being: Attention Restoration Theory (ART), Stress Reduction Theory, the Biophilia Hypothesis, and Nature Connectedness and Well-Being. ART posits that exposure to natural settings can restore attentional resources depleted by daily activities through four key mechanisms—being away, fascination, extent, and compatibility. Evidence for this theory includes both subjective reports and objective measurements but also highlights limitations such as varying definitions and individual differences.

Stress Reduction Theory focuses on the physiological and affective benefits of nature exposure, rooted in an evolutionary perspective that suggests natural environments can reduce stress due to their calming effects and ability to promote positive emotions. The Biophilia Hypothesis proposes humans have a deep-seated need for connection with nature, which is integral to our psychological well-being.

Nature Connectedness and Well-Being explores the relationship between individuals' sense of connectedness with nature and various aspects of mental health, distinguishing between hedonic (pleasure-based) and eudaimonic (meaningful pursuit-based) well-being. These theories collectively underscore the importance of natural environments for enhancing psychological health and reducing stress in modern society.
