## Beyond “do not touch”: the experience of a three-dimensional printed artifacts museum as an alternative to traditional museums for visitors who are blind and partially sighted

This study explores the use of three-dimensional (3D) printing technology to make museums accessible for individuals who are blind and partially sighted (BPS). It examines the perceptions and experiences of BPS individuals regarding traditional museum visits, which often prohibit touching due to concerns about preservation. The researchers created a 3D printed artifacts museum (3D-PAM), where replicas of famous museum artifacts were available for tactile exploration by visitors who are BPS. Through semi-structured interviews with eleven participants, the study found that BPS individuals generally have negative perceptions of traditional museums due to restrictions on touching and limited sensory engagement opportunities. However, the 3D-PAM offered a positive experience by allowing full tactile interaction with replicas, enhancing accessibility and understanding of cultural artifacts. The findings suggest that 3D-PAMs can serve as an alternative or supplementary resource for increasing museum accessibility for BPS individuals.
