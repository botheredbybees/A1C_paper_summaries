## Skeletomotor Control

The chapter 'Skeletomotor Control' in 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle explores the neurological mechanisms governing human movement. It highlights three principal motor control circuits: the corticospinal system, the cortico-cerebellar system, and the cortico-basal ganglia system. Each circuit is responsible for sending and processing motor commands, with feedback loops that refine and enhance future actions. Motor cortex, located in the frontal lobe, plans and generates commands in abstract coordinates which are translated into muscle tensions by the spinal cord. The cerebellum plays a critical role in motor learning and fine-tuning movements, while the basal ganglia gate cortical activity, integrating information from various cortical territories. The chapter also discusses the discovery of mirror neurons in premotor cortex and their implications for understanding social cognition and neurological disorders. These motor control principles are not limited to physical movement but extend to cognitive functions and behavior, suggesting a broader application of motor control principles across different domains of human activity.
