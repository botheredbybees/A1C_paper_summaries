## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience

The text from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle delves into the foundational aspects of cognitive neuroscience, specifically focusing on the brain's structure and function. The central nervous system is organized into four primary parts: the spinal cord, brainstem, cerebellum, and cerebrum. The computational elements of the brain are neurons, which are distributed across cortical and subcortical areas. Neurons consist of a cell body, dendrites, and an axon, which enable them to receive and transmit signals through electrical and chemical processes. Electrical action potentials are converted to chemical signals, such as neurotransmitters, at synapses. Different types of neurotransmitters can have excitatory or inhibitory effects on postsynaptic neurons, leading to membrane potential oscillations. These oscillations are crucial for controlling the flow of information in the brain. The chapter emphasizes the importance of understanding the neuron's physiology to establish a foundational knowledge base for further explorations into higher cognitive functions such as thinking, consciousness, decision making, and language.
