## The distribution of memories for popular songs in old age: An individual differences approach

This study examines the memory retention of popular songs in older adults, focusing on individual differences and musical preferences during adolescence and early adulthood. The research aimed to replicate findings that music from one's youth is remembered best and to investigate how personal identity formation influences these memories. Participants aged between 58 and 86 years listened to song excerpts from the German charts between 1954 and 1997, rating their preference for and frequency of listening to each song. Results showed that songs popular during participants' youth (ages 10–30) were recognized more often than those from other life periods. The study concludes that musical preferences and self-concept development in young adulthood contribute significantly to the formation of long-term memory for music, emphasizing the importance of individual differences in understanding this phenomenon.
