## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience: Chapter 11 - DECLARATIVE LONG-TERM MEMORY

Chapter 11 of 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle delves into the neuroscientific understanding of declarative long-term memory (LTM). The chapter highlights challenges in early neuroimaging studies of LTM due to difficulties in differentiating hippocampal activity across varying conditions and separating novelty-driven effects from actual encoding processes. Postle discusses how advancements in fMRI techniques, such as fast event-related methods and multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA), have facilitated the identification of brain regions associated with successful memory encoding, including the prefrontal cortex (PFC) and medial temporal lobe (MTL). The chapter also explores the impact of intensive memorization on brain structures, exemplified by London taxi drivers whose posterior hippocampus expands in response to their spatial memory demands. Postle emphasizes the significance of contextual reinstatement in memory retrieval, suggesting that it plays a critical role in recalling stored information. The chapter concludes with discussions on familiarity versus recollection and the implications for understanding memory retrieval processes, highlighting the hippocampus's pivotal role in recollection. This chapter offers valuable insights into the cognitive and neural mechanisms underlying memory formation and retrieval, contributing to the broader field of cognitive neuroscience and its relevance to health and wellbeing.
