## Heads Shoulders Knees and Toes! Puppetry as a Therapeutic Tool

Dr. Caroline Astell-Burt discusses the use of puppetry as a therapeutic tool for children with various disabilities and special educational needs. The article highlights that puppets can engage children's imaginations, motivating them to improve their physical abilities through play. For instance, a child with diplegia was inspired to sit up straighter and cross her midline while operating a puppet named Hilary the Witch to learn letters. Similarly, infants with diplegia were encouraged by watching a puppet called Tubby jump, prompting them to become more mobile themselves. Astell-Burt also describes an intervention for boys with dyspraxia using shadow puppets, which boosted their confidence and excitement about their creations. Additionally, she illustrates how a young girl with hemiplegia used a sock-puppet as a swan during a performance, demonstrating significant movement of her affected arm in front of an audience, underscoring the cathartic effect of puppetry on the child's self-esteem and physical engagement. The article emphasizes that puppetry can stimulate both gross and fine motor movements, build confidence through public demonstration, and inspire kinaesthetic experiences. Dr. Astell-Burt encourages therapists to explore puppetry as a motivational tool in their practice.
