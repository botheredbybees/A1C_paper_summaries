## Transforming the Literary “I” Into the Cinematic Eye in Julian Schnabel's Adaptation of Le Scaphandre et le Papillon (The Diving Bell and the Butterfly)

This paper examines Julian Schnabel's film adaptation of Jean-Dominique Bauby's memoir, 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly'. It explores how Schnabel uses cinematographic techniques to convey Bauby's experience of locked-in syndrome and the process of regaining his voice through blinking letters. The film emphasizes subjective camera techniques, creating a perspective that mimics Bauby's limited vision and interaction with the world. Through this method, Schnabel illustrates the fusion of Bauby's narrative 'I' with the cinematic 'eye', underscoring the challenge of reconstructing identity and communication. The paper also discusses the role of the viewer as an active participant in constructing meaning, reflecting the post-structuralist view of subjectivity shaped by discourse.
