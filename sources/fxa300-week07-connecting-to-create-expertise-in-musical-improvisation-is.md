## Connecting to Create: Expertise in Musical Improvisation Is Associated with Increased Functional Connectivity between Premotor and Prefrontal Areas

This study explores the neural correlates of musical improvisation expertise by examining brain activity and functional connectivity in pianists with varying experience in improvisation. The research found that greater improvisation experience is associated with reduced activity in frontoparietal executive cortical areas and enhanced functional connectivity between bilateral dorsolateral prefrontal cortices, dorsal premotor cortices, and presupplementary motor areas. This suggests that creative behaviors can be automated through training, leading to more efficient information exchange within neural networks critical for creativity. The findings highlight the potential for improvisational training to influence brain function at a network level, contributing to our understanding of the neural basis of creativity in the context of musical performance.
