## “It gave me something big in my life to wonder and think about which took over the space… and not MS”: managing well-being in multiple sclerosis through art-making

This study investigates the role of art-making as a leisure activity for individuals with multiple sclerosis (MS) and its impact on their well-being. The research, using Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA), involved single semi-structured interviews with five adults living with MS who engaged in visual art-making as a leisure occupation. The study revealed that art-making provided a significant source of meaning and fulfillment, enabling participants to manage the physical and psychological challenges of MS. Key themes included the use of art as a means to fill occupational voids left by illness-related changes, the provision of social camaraderie and learning opportunities, and the affirmation of emotional well-being and self-worth through creative expression. The research underscores the importance of creative leisure activities in helping individuals with MS to maintain a sense of self and identity, despite the progressive nature of the disease. The findings contribute to the understanding of how art-making can be a multifaceted support for well-being, offering a counterbalance to the limitations imposed by MS.
