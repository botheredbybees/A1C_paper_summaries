## Keeping research on track II: A companion document to Ethical conduct in research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and communities: Guidelines for researchers and stakeholders

The document 'Keeping research on track II' is a companion to the ethical guidelines for research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples. Published by the National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC) in 2018, it emphasizes the importance of ethical research practices that respect the cultural sovereignty, values, and diversity of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities. The guideline underscores the need for research to be led and driven by these communities, ensuring that methodologies and practices are culturally appropriate and beneficial. Key aspects include building respectful relationships, developing research ideas that align with community priorities, and ensuring equitable participation and benefit. The document also highlights the importance of recognizing and respecting the diversity within and between Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander groups, advocating for a research approach that upholds mutual responsibility and ethical conduct. This guideline is crucial for fostering meaningful and ethical research collaborations that contribute positively to the health and well-being of Indigenous communities in Australia.
