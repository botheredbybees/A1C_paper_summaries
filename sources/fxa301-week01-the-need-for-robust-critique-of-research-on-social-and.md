## The need for robust critique of research on social and health impacts of the arts

This paper discusses the growth of interest in the social and health impacts of the arts in the UK since the late 1990s, highlighting early critiques by Belﬁore and Mirza regarding exaggerated claims about these benefits. The authors review recent commissioned reports on arts and health research by WHO Europe and DCMS which assert positive roles for the arts in promoting health and reducing inequalities but also identify substantial limitations within them. They argue that broad scoping reviews are insufficient to guide practice and policy development due to their methodological shortcomings, emphasizing instead the necessity of rigorous, systematic approaches ensuring trustworthiness in review outcomes. The paper underscores that while engaging with the arts may contribute positively to well-being, its potential role in population health and reducing social inequalities remains unproven.

The critique includes observations such as the possibility for any participatory activity to be empowering regardless of whether it is arts-based, limited attention given to cost-effectiveness relative to established practices within health services, and a focus on short-term effects rather than long-term outcomes. Additionally, there is concern over overlooking negative impacts from arts engagement and insufficient consideration of artistic quality in assessments of health initiatives.
