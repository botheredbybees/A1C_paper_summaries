## Critical thinking about mindfulness

The document discusses critical perspectives on the practice of mindfulness in the context of stress, self-care, and mental health. It highlights several concerns including overprescribing of mindfulness, commercialization, and unethical applications. The overprescribing issue is examined through the lens of growing evidence of efficacy while also cautioning against the hype that can overshadow this evidence. The commercialization aspect is discussed in terms of the influence of big business and the lack of rigor in some commercial products, such as mobile apps, which often lack adequate testing and may have privacy concerns. Unethical applications of mindfulness are presented, suggesting that mindfulness might be used to encourage acceptance of unsatisfactory conditions rather than addressing the root causes. Finally, the concept of 'McMindfulness' is introduced, describing it as a superficial and commodified version of mindfulness that lacks the philosophical depth and is used primarily for feel-good outcomes without deeper psychological, social, or cultural transformation. This document is particularly relevant to the creative arts and health by emphasizing the need for critical evaluation of mindfulness practices to ensure their ethical and effective application in therapeutic and social contexts.
