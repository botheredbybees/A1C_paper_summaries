## Musical Performance Anxiety: A Review of Literature

Christopher Taborsky’s article reviews research literature on musical performance anxiety, examining its psychological causes, outside influences, and treatments. He highlights studies by researchers such as Brotons (1994), Hamann (1982), and LeBlanc et al. (1997), which find that the presence of an audience increases anxiety levels, and that female musicians tend to report higher anxiety and heart rates. The review also discusses the impact of timing on anxiety levels and the role of social phobia. It suggests that extensive formal study may help mitigate anxiety, and advises music educators to prepare students for the anxiety of performing in front of an audience. Overall, the article underscores the importance of understanding and addressing musical performance anxiety to enhance the quality of the performance experience.
