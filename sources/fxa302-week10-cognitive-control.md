## Cognitive Control

Cognitive control, as described in the text by Postle (2015), is the brain's ability to guide behavior and prioritize information processing in accordance with internal goals. This process is critical for managing complex behaviors and preventing interference from irrelevant stimuli, making our daily lives more efficient and effective. The text discusses several key aspects of cognitive control, including environmental dependency syndrome, which results in behavioral disinhibition following lateral prefrontal cortex (PFC) damage. Perseverative responding, as seen in the Wisconsin Card-Sorting Test, is highlighted as a cardinal sign of impaired cognitive control. Additionally, electrophysiological studies reveal distinct patterns in patients with PFC lesions, indicating impaired attentional selection and filtering of task-irrelevant information. The text also explores the unique properties of the PFC, such as its ability to transition between active and inactive states, and its extensive connectivity and dopamine innervation. These properties are crucial for implementing control and managing performance-related information. Overall, the chapter underscores the profound impact of cognitive control on human behavior and its importance in maintaining mental health and well-being.
