## Visual Object Recognition and Knowledge

Chapter 9 of 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' explores the neurological syndrome of visual agnosia and its implications for visual object recognition and knowledge representation. The chapter highlights the challenges posed by visual agnosia, particularly prosopagnosia, which affects the ability to recognize individual faces despite intact visual perception. The author critiques traditional cognitive neuropsychology models and introduces parallel distributed processing (PDP) models as a more neurologically plausible explanation. The discussion includes debates surrounding the fusiform face area (FFA) and its role in face recognition, challenging the notion of strong modularity in the ventral stream. Key themes also include the influence of perceptual expertise and the use of multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) to challenge category-specificity claims. The chapter underscores the importance of reconciling conflicting evidence to advance our understanding of visual cognition and its neural correlates.
