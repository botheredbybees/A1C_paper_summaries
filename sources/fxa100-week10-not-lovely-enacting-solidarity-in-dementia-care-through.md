## (not) lovely: Enacting solidarity in dementia care through participatory Arts

This article examines how participatory arts can foster solidarity in dementia care, particularly by validating negative emotions such as sadness and anger rather than suppressing them. The author focuses on Entelechy Arts’ Walking Through Walls programme, which engages care home residents living with dementia in co-creating improvised art to promote relational and embodied practices of care. By attuning to the full range of emotional expressions, artists can challenge oppressive institutional systems and reframe negative emotions as important acts of communication rather than symptoms requiring medication or isolation. The study argues that validating these emotions through kinaesthetic attunement creates moments of embodied solidarity among residents, staff, and practitioners, fostering a sense of agency and connection. This approach is crucial in dementia care settings where negative feelings are often pathologized, highlighting the need for more inclusive and dignified care environments.
