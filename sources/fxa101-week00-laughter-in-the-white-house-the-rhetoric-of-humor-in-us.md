## Laughter in the White House: The Rhetoric of Humor in US-American State Dinner Speeches

The study investigates the role of humor in US presidential state dinner speeches aimed at strengthening international relations. Analyzing a dataset of 87 speeches from seven presidents between 1986 and 2025, the research combines political discourse analysis with the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH). It identifies that humor is primarily self-deprecating or affiliative, contributing to positive rapport and emotional bonding. The article highlights how humorous rhetoric can be speaker-, hearer-, or other-oriented, emphasizing its strategic role in diplomatic settings.
