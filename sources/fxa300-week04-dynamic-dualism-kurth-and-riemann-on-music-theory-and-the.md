## Dynamic Dualism: Kurth and Riemann on Music Theory and the Mind

This article explores Ernst Kurth's engagement with Hugo Riemann's ideas and his reformulation of harmonic dualism into 'dynamic dualism'. The piece delves into the psychological underpinnings of music theory, particularly focusing on how Kurth refined his perspective in response to Riemann’s theories. It discusses the broader context within which Kurth's work emerged, emphasizing the significance of Riemann as an unavoidable interlocutor for scholars working in music theory during the early 20th century. The author argues that dynamic dualism encapsulates Kurth's critique and expansion on harmonic dualism, presenting it as a principle deeply rooted in musical listening experiences. Through detailed analysis of Wolf’s “An den Schlaf,” Kurth illustrates how chromatic alterations can create complex tension dynamics within seemingly simple harmonic progressions, highlighting the interplay between individual voices and performers to define Romantic style.
