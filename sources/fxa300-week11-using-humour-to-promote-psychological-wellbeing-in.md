## Using humour to promote psychological wellbeing in residential homes for older people

The study evaluates the impact of a structured humorous activity on the psychological wellbeing of older individuals residing in care homes. Participants were divided into an intervention group that engaged in an 'old-time sing-a-long' activity and a control group receiving standard care. The research employed two standardized instruments—the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ-28) and the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS)—to assess the psychological wellbeing of participants. Results indicated that those who participated in the humorous activity experienced significantly reduced levels of anxiety and depression compared to the control group. This research underscores the potential benefits of humor and laughter as therapeutic tools in enhancing mental health outcomes for older adults, contributing to the broader field of creative arts and health by highlighting the positive influence of humor on psychological wellbeing.
