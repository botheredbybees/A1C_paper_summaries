## The emergence of the creative ager – On subject cultures of late-life creativity

The paper explores the concept of creativity in later life through a theoretical framework that views both age and creativity as social practices, rather than intrinsic human capacities. It argues that recent interest in late-life creativity is linked to the commodification of creativity itself within neoliberal paradigms emphasizing productivity and self-realization. The study presents qualitative data from interviews with older adults engaged regularly in creative activities to examine how these practices shape their self-image and sense of aging. Findings suggest that creative engagement fosters a self-concept centered on productivity, anti-aging attitudes, and the maintenance of social roles despite aging. By critically evaluating current modes of creativity, the research highlights the normative positions embedded within such practices and proposes a praxeological perspective to better understand late-life creativity's role in shaping aging narratives.
