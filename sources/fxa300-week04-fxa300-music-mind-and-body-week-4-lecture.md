## FXA300 Music, Mind and Body Week 4 Lecture

The lecture covers fundamental aspects of sensation and perception in music, focusing on how the brain processes auditory information to form perceptions of rhythm, pitch, contour, and tonality. Key concepts include bottom-up processing, where sensory input builds an understanding of individual elements like drums or a harpsichord, and top-down processing, which uses pre-existing knowledge to interpret stimuli efficiently. The lecture delves into neural pathways responsible for various musical features, such as the hierarchical structures in pitch processing and how different brain regions collaborate to process intervals, contours, melodies, and tonalities. It also discusses rhythmic entrainment stages, from neuronal phase locking to social synchronization among group members. The material highlights the significance of brain networks, particularly the striato-thalamo-cortical pathway, in handling complex rhythms and predicting timing. This lecture underscores the intricate neural basis underlying music perception and its relevance for understanding how musical experiences are shaped by cognitive processes.
