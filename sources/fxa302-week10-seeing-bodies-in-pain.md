## Seeing Bodies in Pain

The chapter 'Seeing Bodies in Pain' by Sander L. Gilman explores the complexities of representing and understanding pain in medical contexts. Gilman argues that the study of how bodies are imagined and represented is crucial for medical humanities, as it influences both the perception and treatment of real bodies. He highlights the conflict between the medical sciences' objective claims about body representations and the subjective nature of pain as understood in the humanities. The chapter discusses historical views on pain, emphasizing that pain's visibility and measurability have been debated since the Enlightenment, with modern scales still failing to capture its full subjective nature. Gilman also critiques the challenges in measuring pain objectively and the role of cultural and individual factors in pain perception and reporting, underscoring the importance of considering these complexities in medical practice and research. This analysis is relevant to creative arts and health by emphasizing the need for interdisciplinary approaches to understanding and representing human experiences of illness and pain.
