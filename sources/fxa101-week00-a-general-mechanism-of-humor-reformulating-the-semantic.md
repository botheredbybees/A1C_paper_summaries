## A General Mechanism of Humor: Reformulating the Semantic Overlap

The paper proposes a cognitive mechanism of humor that is applicable beyond verbal communication and introduces the concept of constraint overlap, which underlies script overlap as defined in prior humor theories. It emphasizes Hofstadter’s theory of analogy-making in understanding humor, suggesting that the essence of humor lies in the evocation of constraints rather than direct encoding of semantic scripts. The author presents a methodology focusing on the cognitive aspects of humor recognition and outlines assumptions about human communication involving analogies and inference processes. The paper discusses how this model can apply to various forms of humorous texts and non-verbal communications, offering an alternative to existing theories while maintaining compatibility with them.
