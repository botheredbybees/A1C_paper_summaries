## Empowering the Voiceless: Disorders of Consciousness, Neuroimaging and Supported Decision-Making

This article explores the potential of neuroimaging technology to communicate with patients suffering from prolonged disorders of consciousness (PDoC) and its implications for obtaining informed consent. The paper discusses current neuroimaging research that reveals covert consciousness in some PDoC patients, enabling them to respond to commands via brain activity patterns. It examines ethical and legal considerations regarding the use of neuroimaging in clinical practice for obtaining informed consent from these patients, who are typically deemed incapable of giving valid consent due to their impaired cognitive abilities and lack of communication. The author also explores the concept of supported decision-making and its relevance in the context of using neurotechnology to enhance decision-making abilities in minimally conscious patients.
