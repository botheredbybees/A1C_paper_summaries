## Introduction to Section II: Sensation, Perception, Attention, and Action

This excerpt from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle introduces the topics of sensation, perception, attention, and action, which are central to understanding brain function. The text explains that sensation involves the detection of environmental changes and their translation into neural signals, while perception involves the identification and recognition of these signals. Attention is described as the prioritization of certain stimuli over others, and action encompasses both isolated motor acts and abstract goals. The author emphasizes the importance of the neural code in translating environmental information into neural signals and highlights the dynamic nature of brain function, which operates in a feedforward and feedback manner rather than a simple feedforward progression. This dynamic system continually processes and represents the world, and new stimuli influence ongoing processes rather than initiating them from a passive state. The text also notes that traditional views of brain function, often presented in textbooks, can oversimplify the complexities of neural processing.
