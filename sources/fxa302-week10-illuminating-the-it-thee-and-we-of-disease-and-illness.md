## Illuminating the It, Thee, and We of Disease and Illness

The chapter discusses Franz Kafka’s 'The Metamorphosis' as a metaphor for the transformative nature of illness on individuals and their social relationships. It explores the psychological and existential impacts of disease on the protagonist, Gregor Samsa, and his family, highlighting the denial, adaptation, and eventual alienation experienced by Gregor. The text delves into how Gregor's condition alters his identity and the dynamics within his family, with his sister Grete initially showing compassion but later becoming more distant and neglectful. The chapter also examines the ethical implications of caregiving and the dehumanization that can occur in the face of prolonged illness, emphasizing the multifaceted experience of disease from multiple perspectives. This discussion is highly relevant to creative arts and health, offering insights into the narrative and existential dimensions of health challenges and the human condition.
