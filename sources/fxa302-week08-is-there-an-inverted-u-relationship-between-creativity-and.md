## Is there an inverted-U relationship between creativity and psychopathology?

Anna Abraham discusses the relationship between creativity and psychopathology, suggesting that while there is a common belief of a link between mental illness and creativity, the empirical evidence is mixed. The author proposes that the relationship between creativity and psychopathology can be understood through an inverted-U function, where moderate levels of certain cognitive dysfunctions can enhance creativity, while severe dysfunctions impair it. This hypothesis is based on the idea that reduced top-down control in information processing can facilitate or hinder creative cognition depending on the severity. Studies on subclinical and clinical populations show that mild to moderate top-down dysfunction may confer advantages in creativity, whereas severe dysfunction impairs it. This inverted-U relationship suggests that a minimal level of cognitive function is necessary for the development of creative ideas into tangible outcomes. The article provides a nuanced view of the creativity-psychopathology link, emphasizing the importance of investigating the specific cognitive mechanisms involved.
