## Collective creativity and wellbeing dispositions: Children’s perceptions of learning through drama

This article explores the impact of drama-based learning on children's creativity and wellbeing, drawing from longitudinal action research conducted by an artist-teacher-researcher in a primary school. The study focuses on the pedagogy of 'Drama Worldbuilding' as a means to foster collective creativity and purposeful action among students. Through interdisciplinary theories involving affect, immersive play, and motivation, the author identifies emerging dispositions and competencies that enhance creative engagement and overall wellbeing. The research highlights the potential for drama to support critical thinking and emotional intelligence in educational settings, providing valuable insights for educators interested in nurturing children's creativity and mental health.
