## The use of commercial computerised cognitive games in older adults: a meta-analysis

This study conducts a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials assessing the impact of commercially available computerized cognitive games on cognitive function in older adults aged over 60 years without cognitive impairment. The analysis, which includes 1,543 participants from sixteen studies, reveals statistically significant improvements in processing speed (SMD = 0.40), working memory (SMD = 0.21), executive function (SMD = 0.21), and verbal memory (SMD = 0.12). However, no significant effects were found for attention or visuospatial abilities. The study concludes that commercially available cognitive games are effective in enhancing cognitive functions in healthy older adults but emphasizes the variability of outcomes across different domains. These findings underscore the potential role of such interventions in supporting mental health and cognitive well-being among aging populations.
