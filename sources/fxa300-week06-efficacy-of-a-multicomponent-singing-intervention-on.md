## Efficacy of a multicomponent singing intervention on communication and psychosocial functioning in chronic aphasia: a randomized controlled crossover trial

This study evaluates the efficacy of a multicomponent singing intervention on communication and psychosocial functioning in chronic aphasia. The research design involves a randomized controlled crossover trial with 54 patients who have acquired brain injury and chronic aphasia along with their family caregivers (n = 43). Participants received either a 4-month singing intervention followed by standard care or vice versa. The intervention included group-based choir singing and melodic intonation therapy sessions, complemented by home training through tablet-assisted activities. Assessments were conducted at baseline, five months post-intervention, and nine months post-baseline to evaluate changes in communication skills, emotional-social functioning, and caregiver well-being.

The findings indicate that the multicomponent singing intervention significantly enhanced everyday communication and responsive speech production from baseline up to five months after the start of the study. These improvements were sustained until the nine-month assessment stage. Additionally, patients experienced better social participation and reduced burden on caregivers. The study concludes with evidence supporting group-based multicomponent singing training as an effective rehabilitation method for chronic aphasia, improving both communicative abilities and psychosocial well-being.
