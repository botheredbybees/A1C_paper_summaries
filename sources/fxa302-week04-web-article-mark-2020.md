## Effects of the Black Death on Europe

The article discusses the profound impact of the Black Death pandemic, occurring from 1347 to 1352, on medieval Europe. The disease fundamentally altered societal structures, economic dynamics, medical knowledge, religious beliefs, and cultural practices. Initially spreading via trade routes like the Silk Road and maritime travel, it devastated populations across cities and countryside, leading to severe depopulation. Economically, feudalism’s rigid hierarchy was disrupted as a shortage of labor led to improved conditions for serfs who could now negotiate better wages and living standards. Socially, the plague challenged traditional hierarchies, prompting uprisings such as the Peasants' Revolt in 1381. Religious practices were also affected as people lost faith in established institutions while medical knowledge was questioned due to the inability of contemporary methods to combat the disease.
