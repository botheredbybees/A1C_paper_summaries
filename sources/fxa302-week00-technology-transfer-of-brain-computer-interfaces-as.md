## Technology Transfer of Brain-Computer Interfaces as Assistive Technology: Barriers and Opportunities

This paper explores the barriers hindering the transfer of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) from research to practical use for individuals with severe physical disabilities. It discusses how BCIs are primarily developed for people with locked-in syndrome or similar conditions and highlights the challenges in their commercial viability due to the small niche market of potential users. The author identifies three main barriers: the narrow focus on reliability over usability, the small potential user base, and the need for greater user inclusion in the design process. Rehabilitation professionals argue that current BCIs lack usability and require more intuitive control strategies. Ethical, legal, social, and cultural considerations are also emphasized for future BCI designs.
