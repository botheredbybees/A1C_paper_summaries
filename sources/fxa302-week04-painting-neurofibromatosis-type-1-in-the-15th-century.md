## Painting neurofibromatosis type 1 in the 15th century

The article 'Painting neurofibromatosis type 1 in the 15th century' discusses a rare medical discovery within an art painting. The authors, Bianucci et al., identify symptoms of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) in a servant depicted in Andrea Mantegna's artwork, 'La Camera degli Sposi,' created between 1465 and 1474. This is significant because it predates the first medical description of NF1 by over 80 years. The painting shows several telltale signs of NF1 such as café-au-lait spots, subcutaneous neurofibromas, and iris Lish nodules. The identification offers a fascinating glimpse into historical medical conditions through art and highlights how visual arts can serve as an invaluable resource for understanding the history of rare diseases.
