## Singing and Wellbeing: The FINAL LECTURE

The lecture delves into the intricate relationship between singing and wellbeing, focusing on the physiological and neurological aspects of singing. Dr. Brendan Lamb discusses how singing engages a complex interplay of air pressure control, vocal fold oscillation, and motor-sensory integration, making each individual's vocal sound unique. The lecture highlights research indicating that professional singers develop enhanced cognitive faculties, including verbal working memory and prefrontal cortex activation, as well as physical health benefits like higher cardiovascular fitness. Additionally, singing is shown to improve vocal strength and quality in individuals with neuromuscular diseases, and there is evidence that it enhances respiratory function and improves quality of life. The lecture concludes with an exploration of how group singing can benefit mental health, such as alleviating symptoms of postnatal depression. These findings underscore the multifaceted benefits of singing, including cognitive, physical, and social well-being.
