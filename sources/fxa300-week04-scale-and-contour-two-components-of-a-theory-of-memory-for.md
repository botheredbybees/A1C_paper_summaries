## Scale and Contour: Two Components of a Theory of Memory for Melodies

Dowling's article develops a theoretical model for how melodies are stored in long-term and short-term memory, focusing on two components: the musical scale and melodic contour. The article presents evidence that scales are overlearned perceptual-motor schemata with lifetime stability and a logarithmic form cross-culturally. Melodic contour, which functions independently of pitch intervals, is demonstrated to be crucial for melody recognition through experiments where participants confuse tonal standard stimuli with same-contour comparisons but not atonal comparison stimuli. This research highlights the interdependence of scale and contour in memory processes relevant to creative arts and health.
