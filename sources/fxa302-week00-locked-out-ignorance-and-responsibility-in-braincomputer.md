## Locked Out: Ignorance and Responsibility in Brain–Computer Interface Communication in Locked-in Syndrome

This paper discusses the ethical implications of brain–computer interfaces (BCIs) for individuals with locked-in syndrome (LIS), a condition where a person is conscious but unable to move or communicate. The authors highlight the social and medical challenges faced by LIS patients, including social stigma and lack of awareness about BCIs as a potential communication tool. They argue that the current ignorance surrounding LIS and BCIs exacerbates the challenges for patients, leading to social exclusion and moral violations of human rights. The paper emphasizes the need for increased awareness and ethical research in BCI technology to improve the quality of life and autonomy for LIS patients.
