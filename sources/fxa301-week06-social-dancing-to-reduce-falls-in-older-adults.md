## Social dancing to reduce falls in older adults

The study examined whether social dancing could prevent falls among seniors aged 78 years on average with no cognitive impairment. The research was conducted as a cluster randomized controlled trial involving 530 participants across 23 self-care retirement villages in Sydney, Australia. Participants were assigned to either receive twice-weekly one-hour social dancing classes (folk or ballroom) for twelve months or maintain their regular activities without intervention. Data on falls during the study period was collected and analyzed using negative binomial regression. No significant difference in fall rates was observed between control and dance groups, with an adjusted incident rate ratio of 1.19 (CI: 0.83–1.71). Post hoc sub-group analysis indicated a higher rate of falls among those with multiple fall histories and participants involved in folk dancing classes. The study concludes that social dancing did not prevent falls or associated risk factors, suggesting the need for modified dance programs incorporating structured exercise elements targeted at both low and high-risk groups.
