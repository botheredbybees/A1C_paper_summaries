## The Neural Basis of Addiction: A Pathology of Motivation and Choice

This review article by Kalivas and Volkow explores the neurobiological basis of addiction, focusing on the role of motivation and choice in the compulsive behavior associated with drug addiction. The authors integrate findings from neuroimaging studies of addicts with cellular studies in animal models to elucidate the cellular and circuitry adaptations that occur in addiction. They argue that while dopamine is essential for the initiation of addiction by reinforcing drug use, chronic drug use leads to adaptations in glutamatergic transmission in the prefrontal cortex and nucleus accumbens. These adaptations decrease the value of natural rewards and the cognitive control over drug-seeking behavior, while simultaneously enhancing the responsiveness to drug-associated stimuli. The authors conclude that the enduring vulnerability to relapse in addicts is a result of these long-lasting changes in brain function, which create a pathological form of neuroplasticity. This review underscores the importance of understanding the neurobiological mechanisms underlying motivation and choice in both addiction and adaptive behaviors, and highlights the potential for developing pharmacotherapies that target these mechanisms.
