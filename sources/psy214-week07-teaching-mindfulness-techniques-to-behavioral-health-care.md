## Teaching Mindfulness Techniques to Behavioral Health Care Staff for Stress Reduction and Self-Care

This quality improvement project examines the effectiveness of a brief mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) program for health care professionals (HCPs). The study, conducted at an outpatient Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation clinic between September and November 2022, involved the development and evaluation of a four-week MBSR program. Participants completed baseline and follow-up data using the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-4), with qualitative and quantitative analysis methods employed to assess outcomes. The results indicate that PSS scores reduced by an average of 28% at week four and 60% at week eight, suggesting a significant decrease in perceived stress levels among participants. The primary theme highlighted by the qualitative data is that staff appreciated the mindfulness techniques for their ease of implementation and effectiveness in managing work-related stress. These findings suggest that brief MBSR programs can be beneficial for HCPs' psychological well-being and quality of care provision, although larger-scale studies are needed to further validate these benefits.
