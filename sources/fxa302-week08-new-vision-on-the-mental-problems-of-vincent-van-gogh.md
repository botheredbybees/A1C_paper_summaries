## New vision on the mental problems of Vincent van Gogh; results from a bottom-up approach using (semi-)structured diagnostic interviews

This paper presents a retrospective analysis of Vincent van Gogh's mental health history using a bottom-up approach based on (semi-)structured diagnostic interviews and extensive correspondence. The study aims to refine the understanding of the complex mental health issues experienced by van Gogh, challenging previous assumptions by excluding unlikely diagnoses and identifying more probable ones. The research concludes that van Gogh likely suffered from a combination of a bipolar mood disorder, traits of borderline personality disorder, alcohol use disorder, and possible focal epilepsy. His mental health deteriorated progressively due to lifestyle factors, such as alcohol and poor nutrition, leading to crises like the ear cutting incident and subsequent delirium and severe depressive episodes. This detailed historical and diagnostic approach provides a nuanced understanding of van Gogh's complex and multifaceted mental health struggles, contributing to the ongoing discourse on diagnosing historical figures.
