## Death, loss and community—Perspectives from children, their parents and older adults on intergenerational community-based arts initiatives in Sweden

The article examines the community-based intergenerational arts initiative Studio DöBra, which aimed to engage children and older adults in discussions about dying, death, and loss through shared arts activities. The study was conducted in Sweden where end-of-life care is increasingly professionalized with low levels of community engagement. Two iterations of Studio DöBra were held in different cities, involving eight children aged nine years and eight older adults over 80 years old in five workshops each. Participants were interviewed post-workshops to understand their motivations for participation, experiences during the sessions, and personal impacts from their involvement. The research found that participants acted independently with agency in bridging generational gaps and creating spaces for dialogue about end-of-life issues. They also reported a sense of community through new intergenerational connections but faced challenges maintaining these relationships due to reliance on external support structures.
