## Week 4 Lecture: Storytelling with Image and Text

This week’s lecture delves into the interplay between images and words in visual storytelling, emphasizing how these elements can work together to create meaning. The lecturer highlights that most people are drawn first to images before engaging with text, suggesting a strategic use of both media to enhance understanding and engagement. Key examples illustrate harmonious interaction where text complements or enhances an image’s message without redundancy. Conversely, the lecture explores paradoxical pairings where contradictions between visual and textual elements can lead to humor or deeper contemplation. The importance of words in adding context, clarifying complex emotions, and illustrating beyond what is visually apparent is underscored. Captions are discussed as tools for enriching the narrative quality of photographs by answering implicit questions about subjects and settings, thus deepening viewer engagement. This interaction between media is crucial not only in traditional photography but also in contemporary forms like comics, where both elements must be used to their full potential. The lecture concludes with an activity inviting students to capture a scene through a window and write a caption that reveals unseen aspects of the experience.
