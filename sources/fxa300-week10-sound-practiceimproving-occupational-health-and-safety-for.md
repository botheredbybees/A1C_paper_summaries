## Sound Practice—improving occupational health and safety for professional orchestral musicians in Australia

The Sound Practice Project, a 5-year study in Australia, aimed to improve occupational health and safety for professional orchestral musicians. The project involved a baseline evaluation, development, and implementation of musician-specific work health and safety initiatives. Key findings from a cross-sectional survey of 377 musicians revealed high rates of performance-related musculoskeletal disorders (PRMDs) and psychological issues, including music performance anxiety and social phobia. Approximately 84% of musicians reported past PRMD episodes, with 50% suffering from current PRMDs. Additionally, 43% of respondents believed they had hearing loss, and 64% used earplugs intermittently. The study highlighted the need for targeted interventions addressing physical, psychological, and auditory health components, emphasizing the interactive relationship between PRMDs and psychological well-being. The findings underscore the importance of holistic health management for professional musicians.
