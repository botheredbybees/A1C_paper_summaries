## Creativity: A Conscious Imaginative Approach

In her paper, Anoushka Beazley examines psychoanalytic definitions of creativity and introduces a creatively relational approach to therapy, particularly when dealing with cultural issues. She begins by reflecting on her personal history, including experiences of racial discrimination in school that influenced her understanding of creativity as a therapeutic process. Her background includes Sri Lankan heritage, being born British, and working in the arts as an actress, writer, and psychotherapist.

Beazley discusses how clients presenting with cultural issues prompted her to explore her own relationship with creativity and its impact on therapy. She draws upon works by Winnicott and Klein to elucidate the psychological dimensions of creativity, distinguishing it from mere artistic production. The paper highlights the interplay between personal experiences (like her school days) and political contexts such as colonization.

A significant part of the article is dedicated to case studies that illustrate how historical trauma influences present-day therapeutic relationships. Beazley uses a socio-genetic perspective to explore how racial dynamics affect client interactions and therapy outcomes, emphasizing the importance of acknowledging and addressing unconscious biases in both therapists and clients. She concludes by advocating for new creative measures in therapy to address societal fissures.
