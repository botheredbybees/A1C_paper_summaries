## Mental Illness and Creativity

Week 8 of the FXA302 Perspectives of the Arts on Health and Wellbeing course explores the historical and contemporary relationship between mental illness and creativity. It covers the long-held belief of a correlation between creative genius and mental illness, drawing on classical Graeco-Roman theories and the Romantic era's portrayal of artists. The lesson discusses how mental illness is depicted in Shakespeare's works and analyzes the romanticized narratives of artistic suffering in biographies. It also critiques the historical and cultural biases that have influenced perceptions of this connection. Theories by Dean Simonton and others suggest that while some traits associated with creativity may overlap with those of mental illness, severe mental illness typically hinders creativity. The course examines specific case studies of artists like Robert Schumann, Vincent van Gogh, Edvard Munch, and Sylvia Plath to illustrate the complex interplay between mental health and creative output.
