## Therapeutic Singing as an Early Intervention for Swallowing in Persons with Parkinson’s Disease

The study investigates the effects of therapeutic singing on swallowing in individuals with Parkinson’s disease (PD) who have not yet developed significant dysphagia symptoms. The research aimed to determine if a group singing intervention could improve swallowing function, measured through electromyography (EMG), and quality of life related to swallowing. Participants, who were on stable antiparkinsonian medication, were divided into two groups: a high-dosage group attending two sessions per week and a low-dosage group attending one session per week. The findings revealed significant improvements in EMG outcomes and Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) motor scores, indicating enhanced swallowing function. Although no significant differences were noted in the Swallowing Quality of Life (SWAL-QOL) scores, the study concluded that therapeutic singing may serve as a promising early intervention strategy for addressing oropharyngeal dysphagia and improving the overall clinical symptoms of PD.
