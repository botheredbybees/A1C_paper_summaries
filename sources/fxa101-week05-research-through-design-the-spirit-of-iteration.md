## Research through Design: The Spirit of Iteration

The article 'Research through Design (RtD): The Spirit of Iteration' by Hao Wang and Sebastian explores the concept of RtD as a method for designers to address complex design challenges. It distinguishes between three types of design research: Research into Design, Research for Design, and Research through Design. While the former two involve understanding existing processes or gathering data for informed decision-making, RtD emphasizes using design itself as a method to generate knowledge beyond traditional boundaries. The authors highlight how RtD can mitigate biases like confirmation bias prevalent in other forms of design research by encouraging designers to engage directly with prototypes and gather authentic feedback from stakeholders. They illustrate this through a case study involving the 'Design Futures' project, where iterative prototyping led to more engaging narratives about biodiversity future scenarios. This process allowed for the creation of an imaginary governmental body, the Ministry of Biodiversity, which facilitated deeper public engagement and reflection on environmental issues. The article underscores RtD's value in making design processes participatory, inclusive, and iterative, ultimately fostering a better understanding of implicit values and hidden thoughts within communities.
