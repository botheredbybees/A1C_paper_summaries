## The Why, the What, and the How of the Medical/Health Humanities

The essay 'The Why, the What, and the How of the Medical/Health Humanities' by Therese Jones, Delese Wear, and Lester D. Friedman explores the integration of humanities into medical education. The authors argue that scientific and clinical training alone are insufficient for healthcare practitioners, as they lack the humanistic elements necessary for understanding and empathizing with patients. The introduction of humanities disciplines, such as literature, history, and philosophy, is seen as a means to address the imbalance between technological advancements and human care. Two main approaches to teaching literature and medicine are discussed: one focusing on literary skills and their practical application in medical practice, and the other emphasizing moral reflection and empathy. The authors highlight the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration and the role of humanities in enhancing healthcare professionals' moral and empathetic capacities, thereby improving patient care. This work is highly relevant to creative arts and health, as it underscores the need for a holistic approach to medical education that includes humanities to foster better health outcomes and patient well-being.
