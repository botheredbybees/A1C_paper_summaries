## The Reflection, Objective, Movement and Action (ROMA) Model©: A New Paradigm for Anti-Racist Social Work Practice

Dan Allen and Allison Hulmes introduce the Reflection, Objective, Movement, and Action (ROMA) Model as a paradigm for anti-racist social work practice, emphasizing the need for social workers to be equipped with the knowledge and skills to address racism and promote social intercultural inclusion. The chapter highlights the challenges faced by Romani and Traveller communities, including antigypsyism and aversive racism, and the perpetuation of racist stereotypes. Aversive racism, a subtle form of prejudice, can lead social workers to feel fearful and helpless when interacting with Romani and Traveller communities. The authors advocate for restorative supervision as a means to address these issues, which includes four stages: reflection, objective, movement, and action. These stages aim to foster critical reflection, identify social inequities, and promote healing from centuries of racial inequity. The ROMA model is presented as a framework to support anti-racist practice and build positive, healthy relationships within social work.
