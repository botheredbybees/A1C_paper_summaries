## FXA300 Sem 2 2026 - Week 8 Lecture

Dr Brendan Lamb's lecture for FXA300 Sem 2 2026 introduces the concept of Music Therapy as a specialized allied health profession that leverages the unique properties of music to enhance cognitive and physical function, foster non-verbal expression, and encourage social interaction. The lecture discusses various therapeutic techniques including passive music listening, active music participation, music and counselling, developmental objectives, music for stimulation, biofeedback, and group therapy, each with distinct applications in healthcare settings. Creative Music Therapy, Guided Imagery and Music (GIM), and Neurologic Music Therapy (NMT) are presented as specific approaches to therapeutic intervention, each highlighting the dynamic interplay between the therapist and client in fostering creativity and emotional well-being. The lecture also explores the use of music in pain relief, particularly in chronic pain conditions, and presents several randomized controlled trials that underscore the analgesic effects of music. Additionally, case studies and theoretical frameworks are provided, such as the use of Melodic Intonation Therapy in treating aphasia and the benefits of rhythmic speech cueing in Parkinson's disease. The lecture concludes with a discussion on the therapeutic impact of music in dementia care, emphasizing the role of personalized playlists and the potential for music-based memory training to improve cognitive functions in advanced dementia. This content is highly relevant to the creative arts and health fields, providing insights into how music can be a powerful tool in addressing neurological and psychological challenges.
