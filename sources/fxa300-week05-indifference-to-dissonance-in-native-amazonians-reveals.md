## Indifference to dissonance in native Amazonians reveals cultural variation in music perception

The study explores the perception of consonance and dissonance in music among indigenous Tsimane' people from Amazonian Bolivia, who have minimal exposure to Western musical culture. It contrasts their responses with those from urban Bolivians and US residents. Despite showing comparable discrimination abilities for familiar sounds and acoustic roughness, the Tsimane' rated both consonant and dissonant chords as equally pleasant, unlike participants from more culturally exposed groups who showed significant preferences for consonance. The findings suggest that aesthetic responses to musical harmony are likely shaped by cultural exposure rather than innate biological predispositions.
