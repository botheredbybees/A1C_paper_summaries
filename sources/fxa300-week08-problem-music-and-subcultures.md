## ‘Problem music’ and subcultures

This chapter explores the debate surrounding 'problem music' and its potential influence on youth behavior and societal norms. It begins by examining historical instances of musical censorship and societal concerns about the impact of rock and roll on young people, particularly highlighting cases like Elvis Presley and the Judas Priest suicide case. The authors delve into research on whether an interest in 'problem music' is associated with delinquency, drug use, and other risky behaviors. They also discuss the role of music in youth subcultures, examining how musical taste serves as a badge of identity and the psychological gratifications that music provides to young people. The chapter addresses the theoretical underpinnings of censorship and the implications for young people's access to pop music, arguing that the evidence does not strongly support limiting access. Overall, the chapter is relevant to creative arts and health by exploring the interplay between music and social behavior, highlighting the importance of understanding cultural contexts in assessing the impact of music on mental and behavioral health.
