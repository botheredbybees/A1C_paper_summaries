## Calcedonies

Calcedonies is a play by Jeff Nisker that addresses the challenges faced by individuals with disabilities within a supposedly supportive healthcare system. The play centers around Ruth, a woman with a neurological condition who is largely paralyzed but retains significant cognitive function. Through Ruth's narrative and interactions, the play highlights systemic injustices and personal triumphs, illustrating the dichotomy between the beauty and potential of individuals with disabilities and the harsh realities they encounter. Nisker weaves Ruth’s story with that of Catherine Frazee, a disabilities scholar, whose insights permeate the script. The play employs poetic language to describe Ruth's favorite rocks, calcedonies, as symbols of resilience and uniqueness. It also includes scenes from Ruth’s life in a group home and hospital, where she uses her remaining abilities to navigate social and medical encounters, often challenging the authority of healthcare providers. The play concludes with a memorial service, underscoring the profound impact of Ruth's life and the broader implications for disability rights and social justice. Calcedonies is a poignant reflection on the intersection of disability, healthcare, and social justice, offering insights into the lived experiences of individuals with disabilities and the systemic barriers they face.
