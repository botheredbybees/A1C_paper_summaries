## A Large-Scale, Cross-Sectional Investigation Into the Efficacy of Brain Training

The paper by Hampshire et al., published in Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, investigates the efficacy of brain training through a large-scale cross-sectional study involving 60,222 participants. The research examines whether individuals who engage in brain training demonstrate cognitive advantages compared to those who do not. Initial findings indicate no overall cognitive benefits for brain trainers compared to non-trainers. However, finer analysis reveals that new starters have lower baseline cognition but may improve over time, while long-term trainees show higher working memory and verbal scores than recent starters. The study also highlights the need for longer training periods to observe significant improvements in cognitive performance, challenging the efficacy claims of short-duration brain training programs.
