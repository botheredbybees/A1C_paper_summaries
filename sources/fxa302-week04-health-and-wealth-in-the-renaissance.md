## Health and wealth in the Renaissance

Stephen Mortlock's article on health and wellbeing during the Renaissance period highlights a transformative era marked by significant advancements in science, art, and medicine. The Renaissance began as a rediscovery of classical philosophy, literature, science, and art, which led to an increase in global exploration, commerce, and wealth accumulation, particularly in Italian cities like Florence and Venice. This economic prosperity fostered patronage for artists and scholars such as Leonardo da Vinci, Botticelli, Michelangelo, and scientific figures including Galileo, Copernicus, and Vesalius.

Artistic expression during this period was characterized by realism and naturalism, with a focus on accurate representation through perspective and light. Meanwhile, the medical Renaissance saw breakthroughs in anatomy, surgery, and epidemiology, most notably with Andreas Vesalius's detailed anatomical studies and William Harvey's discovery of blood circulation.

The article also discusses the social implications of this era, including the spread of Protestantism due to advancements like Gutenberg’s printing press. Despite these developments, major health challenges persisted, such as recurring outbreaks of the Black Death and syphilis, underscoring the need for continued medical research and public health measures.
