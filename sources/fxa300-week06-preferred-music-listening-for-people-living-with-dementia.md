## Preferred Music Listening for People Living with Dementia: Two Home-Based Case Studies Discussing Compilation Process, Autobiographical and Biophysical Responses

This article examines the use of preferred music listening for individuals with dementia in a home setting, focusing on two case studies that explore the compilation process and biophysical responses to personalized playlists. The study aims to provide new insights into how music can be beneficial for managing neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS) like agitation, anxiety, depression, and sleep impairment, which are common among people living with dementia. Music therapy sessions were conducted in participants' homes over a four-month period, during which therapists observed and recorded the participants’ heart rate data, video responses, and emotional reactions to different types of music. The findings indicate that personalized playlists based on individual preferences can effectively manage NPS by aligning musical selections with autobiographical memories and personal tastes, such as up-tempo music encouraging physical activity like dancing while more somber pieces elicit subtle but measurable biophysical responses. This research underscores the importance of understanding individualized approaches to using music for therapeutic benefits in managing dementia symptoms beyond clinical settings.
