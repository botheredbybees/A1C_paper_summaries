## Revisiting the dissociation between singing and speaking in expressive aphasia

This study investigates the production of sung and spoken utterances in a non-fluent patient, C.C., who suffered severe expressive aphasia following a right-hemisphere stroke but had preserved language comprehension and memory. Experiment 1 involved repeating familiar song excerpts under various conditions including spoken lyrics and singing with different melodies or syllables, while experiment 2 focused on novel song excerpts under similar conditions. Results showed no significant difference in the number of words produced between speaking and singing, suggesting a dissociation between verbal and musical production mechanisms. This finding challenges previous claims that singing aids word production in aphasic patients and supports the notion that singing may not facilitate language recovery differently from speaking.
