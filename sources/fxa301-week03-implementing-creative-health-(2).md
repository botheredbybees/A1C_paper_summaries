## Implementing Creative Health

The document discusses the growing body of evidence supporting creative health and its potential to address health challenges, social care issues, and inequalities across diverse communities. It highlights increasing international interest in creativity's role in enhancing public health and well-being, particularly noting the World Health Organization’s recognition of this field through their scoping review on arts and health. The report emphasizes the need for intersectoral approaches involving both health and arts ministries to maximize creative health’s impact, citing successful partnerships like those seen in Wales, Australia, USA, Greece, Finland, and Ireland as models to emulate.

The paper delves into economic benefits of creative health initiatives, arguing that such programs are not only cost-effective but also contribute significantly to social determinants of health. It provides examples where arts interventions lead to reduced healthcare utilization (such as fewer GP appointments) and improved well-being outcomes, which can translate into financial savings for national health services while supporting the broader economy through investments in cultural activities.

Furthermore, the document stresses the importance of articulating these economic benefits clearly to funders and commissioners, suggesting that moving towards routine commissioning of creative health services could enhance long-term value. It also mentions various studies demonstrating positive cost-effectiveness ratios, such as an ROI of £2.30 for every £1 invested in arts-on-prescription schemes, indicating substantial savings in healthcare costs through reduced GP visits and emergency department admissions.
