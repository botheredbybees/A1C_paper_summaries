## TONAL STRUCTURES IN PERCEPTION AND MEMORY

Carol L. Krumhansl's article 'Tonal Structures in Perception and Memory' delves into the psychological underpinnings of music perception, focusing on aspects such as consonance, pitch perception, tonal hierarchies, and memory for melodies. The author begins by distinguishing between tonal and musical consonance, noting that while tonal consonance pertains to the pleasantness of isolated tones, musical consonance is context-dependent and influenced by cultural factors. Krumhansl explores various theories on dissonance, including Plomp & Levelt's model which posits a relationship between frequency differences and perceived dissonance. The review also covers cognitive models that integrate music theory into empirical research, emphasizing the importance of complex internal representations in understanding musical practice such as tuning systems, scale structures, harmony, and melody. Additionally, Krumhansl examines the role of linguistic theories applied to music, highlighting the interdisciplinary nature of music psychology. This article is foundational for researchers interested in the intersection between music cognition and perception, contributing significantly to the field's evolving theoretical frameworks.
