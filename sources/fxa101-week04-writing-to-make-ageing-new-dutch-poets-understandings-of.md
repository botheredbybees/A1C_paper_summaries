## Writing to make ageing new: Dutch poets’ understandings of late-life creativity

This article explores how older professional writers in the Netherlands experience and understand creativity as they age, focusing on Dutch poets over the age of 65 who have achieved eminence in their field. The study utilizes interpretative phenomenological analysis to identify key themes that characterize these writers' perspectives on late-life creativity. Three main superordinate themes emerge: securing sustainable writing practices, negotiating the literary field, and writing as an art of living. The findings challenge prevailing notions of creativity as either a continuous growth process or a decline after midlife, instead emphasizing the diverse experiences and adaptations that older poets undergo in their creative endeavors. This nuanced perspective offers valuable insights into how professional writers navigate aging within the context of their artistic careers.
