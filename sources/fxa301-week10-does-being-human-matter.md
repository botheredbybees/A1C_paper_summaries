## Does Being Human Matter?

In the chapter 'Does Being Human Matter?', Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi critiques Peter Smith's interpretation of animal play and its implications for understanding human play. He questions whether an evolutionary perspective rooted in rigid ontological assumptions can truly capture the dynamic nature of human behavior, which constantly creates and redefines reality. Csikszentmihayli argues that human activities such as science, law, religion, and warfare often originate from playful contexts, challenging traditional views on the function and significance of play. He emphasizes the importance of considering intrinsic motivation in play rather than solely its adaptive functions, suggesting that equating play with internal goals is a significant insight missed in Smith's work. The chapter also highlights the need for recognizing both 'normal' and 'revolutionary' aspects of evolution to understand how human behaviors evolve beyond mere survival benefits. Finally, Csikszentmihayli underscores the importance of cultural specificity and idiosyncratic patterns in understanding complex human activities like play, feeding, or sexual behavior.
