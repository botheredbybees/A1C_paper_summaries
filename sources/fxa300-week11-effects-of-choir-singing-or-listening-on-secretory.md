## Effects of Choir Singing or Listening on Secretory Immunoglobulin A, Cortisol, and Emotional State

The study examines the impact of choir singing versus listening to choral music on immune function and emotional state. It involved thirty-one amateur choir members who participated in two conditions during rehearsals a week apart: singing and listening to choral music. Saliva samples were collected to measure secretory immunoglobulin A (S-IgA) and cortisol levels, while emotional states were assessed using the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS). Results indicated that singing led to increased positive affect and S-IgA, while reducing negative affect. Listening to choral music, however, increased negative affect and decreased cortisol levels. The study suggests that choir singing positively influences both emotional well-being and immune competence, highlighting the differential effects of active versus passive musical engagement.
