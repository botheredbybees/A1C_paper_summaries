## Untouchable: ‘disabling’ cinema’s contract on contact in The Diving Bell and the Butterfly

Gregory Brophy's article examines the intersection of disability and gender in Julian Schnabel’s film adaptation of Jean-Dominique Bauby's memoir, 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly.' The paper focuses on Schnabel's innovative use of first-person perspective to evoke the visceral experience of locked-in syndrome and challenges the conventional notions of masculinity and cinematic gaze. Brophy discusses how the film employs the camera as a body double to explore the intimacy and terror of being touched, drawing on phenomenological theories to analyze the sensory and emotional impact on both the patient and the viewer. The author also critiques the conventional camera's untouchable nature and its role in maintaining the distance between the viewer and the subject, proposing a haptic vision that destabilizes these norms.
