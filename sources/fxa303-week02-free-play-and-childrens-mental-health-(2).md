## Free Play and Children's Mental Health

This article discusses the growing crisis in children's mental health within the UK and emphasizes the role of free play in supporting healthy development and mitigating mental health issues. The author highlights that while there is a recognized increase in demand for mental health support among children, existing reports often overlook positive changes in children’s life experiences as potential solutions to these problems.

The article references studies indicating significant reductions in unsupervised outdoor space available for children since the 1970s and increased pressures related to academic performance. It argues that free play has a critical role in fostering secure emotional attachments, resilience against stress, and overall mental health wellbeing.

Research is cited linking the quality of social environments to levels of toxic versus positive stress experienced by children, which in turn affects their ability to engage in playful activities beneficial for development. Additionally, data from animal studies suggest that play deprivation can lead to underdevelopment of certain brain regions crucial for emotional regulation and sociability.

The review concludes by advocating for increased awareness among caregivers, educators, and healthcare professionals regarding the importance of supporting children's opportunities for free play across various settings.
