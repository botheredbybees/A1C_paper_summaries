## Your Brain on Improv

Dr. Charles Limb, a surgeon and researcher, discusses his career studying creativity and the neuroscience behind music. He uses the example of jazz improvisation to explore how the brain generates complex, spontaneous musical compositions. Limb and his colleague, Alan Braun, conducted a study using functional MRI to observe brain activity during improvisation versus memorized performance. They found increased activity in the medial prefrontal cortex and decreased activity in the lateral prefrontal cortex during improvisation, suggesting a dissociation in self-monitoring and self-expression. This research highlights the brain's ability to inhibit self-criticism and encourage creative freedom during improvisation, providing insights into the neural mechanisms of artistic creativity.
