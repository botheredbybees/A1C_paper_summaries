## Meaning: History

This document explores the concept of meaning in the context of stress, self-care, and mindfulness. It highlights the work of Viktor Frankl, who introduced the idea of the 'Will to Meaning' as a counterpoint to Sigmund Freud's 'Will to Pleasure' and Alfred Adler's 'Will to Power.' Frankl's approach emphasizes the individual's freedom and responsibility in finding meaning, a concept that can be transformative in managing stress and fostering mental health. The document also touches on existentialism and the existential vacuum, which refers to a sense of meaninglessness and emptiness that can arise when people pursue transient pleasures without a deeper sense of purpose. It suggests that individuals are responsible for creating their own meaning, which can be achieved through creative and experiential values, and through adopting an attitudinal value that embraces life despite its challenges. The document concludes with an overview of logotherapy, a therapeutic approach developed by Frankl that includes methods such as dereflection and paradoxical intention to help individuals overcome existential crises and find meaning in their lives. This exploration of meaning and existentialism is highly relevant to the creative arts and health, offering insights into how individuals can cultivate resilience and well-being through a deeper understanding of their own existence.
