## Art therapies and dementia care: A systematic review

This systematic review examines the empirical evidence for using art therapies (ATs) with individuals who have dementia of the Alzheimer's type (DAT), focusing on music, visual arts, drama, and dance/movement therapies from 1990 to 2010. The study critiques the existing literature by addressing four key questions: the focus of empirical research, design and evaluation methods used, reported findings, and the potential for ATs in enriching lives with dementia. It highlights significant gaps in methodological rigor within AT studies, including a lack of detailed operational concepts, measurement tools, and comprehensive evaluations. The review also emphasizes that many existing studies prioritize objective clinical outcomes over subjective well-being, limiting their applicability to enhancing quality of life for those with DAT. Recommendations are made for future research to incorporate more nuanced qualitative approaches focusing on enrichment and subjective well-being.
