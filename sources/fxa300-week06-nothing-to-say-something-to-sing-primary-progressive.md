## Nothing to Say, Something to Sing: Primary Progressive Dynamic Aphasia

This paper presents a case study of a patient with primary progressive dynamic aphasia, characterized by severely reduced spontaneous propositional speech despite preserved abilities in other linguistic tasks such as naming and repetition. The condition is associated with degenerative frontal lobe dementia. Through neuropsychological experiments, the authors found that while the generation of new verbal messages was impaired, musical output remained intact. This suggests disruption at an early stage of verbal message creation rather than a later processing stage. The study contributes to understanding the specific cognitive and neural mechanisms underlying propositional speech production.
