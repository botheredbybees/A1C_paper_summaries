## Investigating the Causal Effects of Arts Education

The study, 'Investigating the Causal Effects of Arts Education,' authored by Daniel H. Bowen and Brian Kisida, explores the causal relationship between arts education and educational outcomes through a randomized controlled trial (RCT) involving 42 elementary and middle schools in Houston, Texas. The researchers find that students receiving additional arts education experiences show reductions in disciplinary infractions, improvements in writing achievement, and increased emotional empathy compared to their counterparts who did not receive such enrichment. In particular, elementary school students experienced further benefits including higher levels of engagement with the school environment, greater college aspirations, and enhanced cognitive empathy. The study underscores that arts education can yield significant impacts on both academic performance and social-emotional development, offering policymakers compelling evidence for maintaining or expanding arts programs in schools despite pressures to prioritize tested subjects.
