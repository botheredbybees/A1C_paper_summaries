## Walking with Tohu – exploring the conditions, influences and ritual practices that lead toward art making

This paper explores the preparatory and philosophical processes that occur before an artist engages in making art, drawing on personal experiences of walking along a beach with a dog named Tohu. Elliot Collins discusses how contemporary art practice is influenced by settler-colonial legacies and indigenous cultures in New Zealand, emphasizing the importance of acknowledging indigenous languages and histories. The author reflects on the ethical considerations involved in using indigenous cultural elements within artistic practices and the evolving social landscape that shapes creative expressions. Through this reflective journey, Collins highlights the role of place and time in informing art creation and the significance of embracing decolonial perspectives.
