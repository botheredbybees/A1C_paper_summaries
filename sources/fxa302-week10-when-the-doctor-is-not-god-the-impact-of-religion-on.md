## When the Doctor Is Not God: The Impact of Religion on Medical Decision Making at the End of Life

This chapter explores the complex interplay between religious beliefs and medical decision-making, particularly at the end of life. It highlights how religious and spiritual values can significantly influence how individuals approach their illness and the medical treatments they accept or reject. The chapter presents three case studies that illustrate how deeply held religious beliefs can create conflicts between patients, their families, and healthcare providers. The narratives underscore the importance of understanding and respecting religious values in medical contexts, emphasizing the need for compassionate and informed communication. One case involves an Orthodox Jewish woman whose son's religious beliefs complicate her medical treatment decisions. Another case discusses a Catholic patient's wife interpreting her husband's religious beliefs differently than his stated preferences. The final case presents a situation where a physician is accused of religious bias in treatment recommendations. These stories reveal the critical role of addressing religious beliefs in clinical settings to ensure patient-centered care and ethical treatment decisions.
