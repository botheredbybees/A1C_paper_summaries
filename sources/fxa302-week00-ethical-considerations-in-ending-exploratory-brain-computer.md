## Ethical Considerations in Ending Exploratory Brain-Computer Interface Research Studies in Locked-in Syndrome

This paper explores ethical challenges in ending exploratory brain-computer interface (BCI) research studies for individuals with locked-in syndrome (LIS). The authors argue that while BCI holds promise for restoring communication to those with LIS, the ethical frameworks typically used for clinical trials (equipoise and nonexploitation) are inadequate for guiding decisions about ending exploratory BCI research. The case of a participant with presumed LIS illustrates these challenges, showing how the lack of clear endpoints and the iterative nature of BCI research complicate decisions about study termination. The paper suggests a framework based on clinician-researcher fiduciary obligations as a more suitable approach for guiding ethical decisions in exploratory BCI research.
