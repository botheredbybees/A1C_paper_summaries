## What is Therapeutic Photography?

The chapter introduces therapeutic photography, emphasizing its structured nature and intended outcomes for users rather than accidental benefits. It traces the origins of therapeutic photography back to Hugh Welch Diamond's work in the mid-19th century at the Surrey County Lunatic Asylum, where he used photographs as part of a pseudoscientific approach towards understanding mental illness. Despite the questionable efficacy of Diamond’s methods, his efforts laid the groundwork for further exploration into the therapeutic uses of photography. Following World War II, the use of photography in therapy expanded to include physical and psychiatric illnesses, aiding recovery and self-expression. The chapter also discusses how photography's democratization has made it accessible to a wider audience, enhancing its potential within therapeutic settings due to its ability to facilitate emotional exploration and communication without requiring artistic talent.
