## The Diving Bell and the Butterfly: Implications of Postoperative Weakness and Distressing Awareness

The editorial discusses research on postoperative weakness and distressing awareness following general anesthesia. Thomsen and colleagues’ study found that patients with atypical butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) experience high rates of postoperative weakness and awareness, leading to distress. The editorial emphasizes the importance of neuromuscular monitoring to prevent residual paralysis and distressing awareness, suggesting that avoiding neuromuscular blocking agents or using neuromuscular monitoring when administered can reduce adverse outcomes. The authors advocate for the routine use of neuromuscular monitoring as a standard of care.
