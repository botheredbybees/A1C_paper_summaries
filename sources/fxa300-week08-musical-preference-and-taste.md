## Musical preference and taste

This chapter explores the diversity of musical preferences and the psychological factors that influence them. It discusses how musical taste can be influenced by the music itself, the listening situation, and the listener. The chapter introduces the concept of experimental aesthetics, which aims to produce laws governing responses to all art forms through controlled laboratory experiments. It examines Daniel Berlyne's theory of arousal potential, which posits an 'inverted-U' relationship between preference and stimulus arousal, suggesting that people prefer stimuli with an intermediate level of arousal. The chapter also considers how cognitive factors, such as familiarity and complexity, affect musical preference. This work is highly relevant to understanding the creative arts and health, as it provides insights into how music can evoke emotions, memories, and thoughts, thereby influencing mental and emotional well-being.
