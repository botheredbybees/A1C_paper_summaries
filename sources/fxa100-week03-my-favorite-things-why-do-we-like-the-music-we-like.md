## My Favorite Things: Why Do We Like the Music We Like?

Chapter 8 of 'This Is Your Brain On Music' by Daniel J. Levitin delves into why people develop preferences for certain types of music, examining the influence of prenatal auditory experiences and early childhood exposure to sounds. The chapter begins with an exploration of how fetuses hear and respond to environmental sounds like their mother's heartbeat in utero. Notably, Alexandra Lamont’s study shows that infants exhibit a preference for music they were exposed to while in the womb, suggesting that auditory memories can be formed before birth.

The concept of childhood amnesia is challenged by this research, indicating that prenatal experiences can be encoded in memory and influence preferences even without conscious awareness. The chapter also discusses the controversial 'Mozart Effect', examining claims about music's ability to enhance cognitive skills like spatial reasoning. Levitin critically evaluates these studies, highlighting their methodological shortcomings.

Further, the author explores long-term benefits of musical engagement, including neurological changes such as increased neural density and larger corpus callosum in musicians compared to non-musicians. This supports the idea that music can have lasting impacts on brain development and function, reinforcing its significance beyond mere enjoyment or entertainment.
