## A Fate Worse Than Death? The Well-Being of Patients Diagnosed as Vegetative With Covert Awareness

This paper explores the well-being of patients diagnosed as being in a vegetative state but who are covertly aware. The author argues against the notion that these patients fare so poorly that it is in their best interest to cease life-sustaining treatment. Utilizing neuroimaging techniques, it has been discovered that a minority of patients previously diagnosed as vegetative are actually aware, capable of experiencing pleasure and pain. The author disputes the pessimistic view that these patients are faring poorly, suggesting that further research into their subjective experiences is necessary to understand their well-being better. The paper challenges the notion that these individuals' lives are not worth living, emphasizing the need to consider their cognitive capacities and experiential interests in evaluating their quality of life.
