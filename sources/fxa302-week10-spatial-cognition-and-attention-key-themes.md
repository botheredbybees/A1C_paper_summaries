## Spatial Cognition and Attention: Key Themes

This chapter focuses on the fundamental workings of spatial cognition and attention, utilizing the neurological syndrome of unilateral neglect as a lens to explore these concepts. Unilateral neglect, typically caused by a stroke affecting the right hemisphere, manifests as an unawareness of information from the left side of space. The chapter highlights how neuropsychological studies can generate hypotheses about spatial cognition and attention, particularly through the examination of the neglect syndrome. Key themes include the role of the posterior parietal cortex (PPC) and the hippocampus in spatial processing, the transformation from egocentric to allocentric reference frames, and the mechanisms of spatial attention. The chapter also discusses how neural competition and the spotlight metaphor of attention help explain the selection and processing of visual features. These insights have implications for understanding how attention and spatial cognition operate in both healthy and impaired conditions, offering a fertile ground for further research in cognitive neuroscience.
