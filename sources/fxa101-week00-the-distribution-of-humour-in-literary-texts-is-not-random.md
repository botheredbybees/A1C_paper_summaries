## The Distribution of Humour in Literary Texts is Not Random

This article uses statistical methods to analyze the distribution of humor in literary texts, specifically focusing on Oscar Wilde's 'Lord Arthur Savile’s Crime' and Douglas Adams's 'The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy'. The study employs the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) to identify instances of humor as 'jab lines', which are incongruities that do not reset the interpretive process. Using time series analysis, the research demonstrates that the distribution of these jab lines is non-random and varies between texts. This finding suggests that humor's placement within a literary work carries significance beyond mere randomness, contributing to broader theories about humor in literature.
