## Neural Bases of Memory

The chapter 'Neural Bases of Memory' explores foundational aspects of memory organization and the amnesic syndrome, particularly focusing on Brenda Milner's research with patient H.M. The chapter discusses the dissociable systems and processes that support declarative, nondeclarative, and short-term memory. It also covers the principle of association and D.O. Hebb's influential model of associative learning. Hebbian learning, embodied in long-term potentiation (LTP), is highlighted as a critical mechanism, with the NMDA subtype of glutamate receptor playing a key role as a synaptic coincidence detector. The chapter delves into the complexities of memory consolidation and reconsolidation, presenting them as significant challenges for cognitive neuroscience but also potential therapeutic opportunities. Additionally, it explores various theories on how the hippocampus functions and its cognitive roles, with a focus on challenges to the standard anatomical model and newer lesioning methods. The content is relevant to creative arts and health by providing insights into cognitive processes and their neural underpinnings, which can inform the creation of therapeutic and educational interventions.
