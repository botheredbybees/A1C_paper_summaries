## Music Therapy Research

Jane Edwards discusses the context, methodology, and trends in music therapy research within the framework of the Oxford Handbook of Music Therapy. The chapter emphasizes the importance of research in developing new knowledge, extending existing knowledge, and establishing theoretical frameworks. Initially, music therapy research was influenced by a commitment to quantitative methods and positivist epistemology, particularly in the United States, focusing on randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to measure the effects of music therapy on client behaviors. Over time, the field has incorporated a wider range of qualitative research methods, such as grounded theory and phenomenology. Key themes include the evolution of research methods, the role of evidence-based medicine, arts-based research, and understanding mechanisms of change in music therapy. The chapter also highlights the importance of peer review and the publication of research in specialized journals. This research is crucial for advancing the field of music therapy and informing practice in health and well-being.
