## I Am Gula, Hear Me Roar: On Gender and Medicine

In the essay 'I Am Gula, Hear Me Roar: On Gender and Medicine,' Rafael Campo explores the underrepresentation of women in the medical field, particularly within literature and popular culture. The author uses a riddle to highlight unconscious biases that prevent people from considering women as physicians, especially in critical scenarios. Campo delves into the historical absence of female figures in medical history and literature, noting that while men like Dr. Jekyll and Dr. Watson are celebrated, women like Elizabeth Blackwell, the first female doctor in the United States, and other pioneering women are often overlooked. This absence not only reflects the broader societal issues of gender bias but also impacts the current perception of women in the medical profession. The essay argues for a more inclusive historical and cultural narrative to acknowledge the contributions of women in medicine and to challenge the stereotypes that persist in contemporary media.
