## Upper limb rehabilitation in chronic stroke using neurologic music therapy: Two contrasting case studies to inform on treatment delivery and patient suitability

This article presents two case studies that examine the use of Therapeutic Instrumental Music Performance (TIMP) for upper limb rehabilitation in individuals with chronic stroke. The research explores how TIMP can be adapted for home-based interventions and evaluates its efficacy based on patient engagement, treatment adherence, and outcomes. The case studies highlight the importance of considering the severity of the stroke, the level of impairment, and the suitability of the home environment for successful rehabilitation. Key findings indicate that flexibility in TIMP protocols, such as emphasizing audio-motor synchronization, can enhance treatment adherence and movement quality. The study underscores the need for tailored interventions and the necessity of assessing homes for accessibility and spatial considerations to ensure successful implementation. The research also calls for the development of outcome measures that can better detect compensatory movements and movement quality. This work is highly relevant to the creative arts and health field, particularly in integrating music therapy techniques into stroke rehabilitation to improve motor function and patient quality of life.
