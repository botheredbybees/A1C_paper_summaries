## Some Workmen Can Blame Their Tools: Artistic Change in an Individual with Alzheimer’s Disease

Rhonda L. Soricelli’s commentary explores the artistic journey of William Utermohlen, a London-based artist diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease at the age of 61. The piece highlights how Utermohlen’s shift towards self-portraits reflects his struggle with memory loss and deteriorating cognitive abilities. Despite the progressive nature of his disease, Utermohlen continued to create, albeit with increasing difficulty and abstraction. His work captures the emotional and psychological toll of Alzheimer’s, offering a poignant narrative of loss and resilience. Soricelli emphasizes the significance of Utermohlen’s artistic legacy in illustrating the inner experiences of those suffering from Alzheimer’s, providing a unique perspective on the disease's impact on individual identity and creativity. The commentary draws attention to the resilience and enduring human spirit in the face of cognitive decline, celebrating Utermohlen’s creative expression as a testament to his enduring will.
