## Public Fetuses

This chapter by Bernice L. Hausman explores the feminist debates surrounding the impact of visualization technologies, particularly ultrasound, on obstetric care and the public perception of pregnancy and the fetus. The chapter highlights how these technologies have transformed the traditional, embodied experience of pregnancy into a more medicalized and technologically mediated one, influencing both healthcare practices and social attitudes towards pregnancy and fetal health. Feminist scholars argue that the increasing use of such technologies not only alters women's relationship to their own bodies but also contributes to a shift in the social and political status of pregnant women, often positioning them as subordinates to medical authority. While some feminists view these technologies as reinforcing existing patriarchal ideologies that marginalize women's autonomy, others see them as potentially empowering, offering a sense of control to women who have experienced multiple pregnancy losses or fertility issues. The chapter underscores the complex interplay between technology, ideology, and social context, and raises questions about the nature of women's existential experiences of pregnancy and childbirth in the contemporary medicalized environment.
