## Walking as a Radical and Critical Art of Inquiry: Embodiment, Place and Entanglement

This editorial introduces a special issue exploring walking as a vital form of inquiry in the arts, emphasizing its embodiment and criticality beyond human-centric perspectives. It traces historical and contemporary practices from Dadaism to Land Art, focusing on how walking disrupts traditional educational paradigms by fostering collective attunement and micropolitical mobilization. The authors highlight examples such as Richard Long's environmental artworks, Adrian Piper’s social interventions in New York City, and Francis Alÿs' political performances, illustrating the potential of walking to challenge normative notions and encourage speculative thinking.
