## Artistic expression in famous painters affected by Alzheimer’s disease: Willem de Kooning, William Utermohlen, and Carolus Horn

This letter to the editor explores the artistic expressions of painters affected by Alzheimer's disease (AD), focusing on Willem de Kooning, William Utermohlen, and Carolus Horn. Despite cognitive decline, these artists maintained creative abilities, suggesting that artistry does not solely depend on declarative memory or executive planning. The study analyzes changes in the artists' works, linking formal changes to selective patterns of impairment and preservation in the visual and motor systems affected by AD. The dual-stream framework and predictive coding theories are employed to explain the observed transformations in artistic style. De Kooning's late works exemplify reduced chromatic richness and simplified contours while retaining broad, rhythmic strokes, indicating preserved procedural memory and motor skills. The letter also discusses practical clinical implications, emphasizing the need to assess visuoperceptual function in AD patients and to use art activities that engage sensorimotor strengths. This study highlights the complex interplay between clinical neurology and art history, offering insights into the neurological basis of creativity.
