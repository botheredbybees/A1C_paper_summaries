## Temporal Entrainment of Cognitive Functions: Musical Mnemonics Induce Brain Plasticity and Oscillatory Synchrony in Neural Networks Underlying Memory

This paper explores the effect of music as a mnemonic device on learning and memory, focusing on the neural dynamics underlying these cognitive functions. The authors conducted experiments using Rey’s Auditory Verbal Learning Test (AVLT) to compare verbal learning and memory in spoken and musical conditions. Electroencephalographic (EEG) analysis revealed significant increases in oscillatory synchrony across various frequency bands during learning in both conditions. Notably, the musical condition showed enhanced coherence in alpha and gamma bands, indicating stronger neuronal synchronization. Additionally, in a study involving multiple sclerosis patients, superior learning and memory performance was observed in the musical condition, with increased power in the low-alpha band in bilateral frontal networks. These findings suggest that musical templates enhance memory by providing temporal structure and redundancy, which can chunk information into more manageable units and improve relational processing. The study provides evidence for the neurophysiological advantages of musical learning, potentially accessing compensatory pathways during compromised prefrontal cortex functions and offering a novel approach to cognitive rehabilitation.
