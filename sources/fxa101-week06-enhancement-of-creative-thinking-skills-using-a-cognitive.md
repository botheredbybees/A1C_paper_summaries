## Enhancement of Creative Thinking Skills Using a Cognitive-Based Creativity Training

This study examines the effectiveness of a brief, cognitive-based creativity training aimed at enhancing creative thinking skills in undergraduate students. The researchers employed a single session lasting 1.5 hours to introduce participants to systematic application of creative thinking techniques. Pre- and post-training assessments were conducted using measures that evaluated divergent thinking, convergent thinking, and creative problem-solving abilities. To control for instrumentation threats, two versions of each task were developed and counterbalanced between pre- and post-measurements across participants.

The study found significant improvements in various creative performance measures following the training session, particularly in the generation of novel ideas during divergent thinking tasks. Additionally, the research highlighted an increase in cognitive flexibility as a mechanism for these enhancements. While the training notably improved divergent thinking skills, it also showed marginal gains in convergent thinking and creative problem-solving abilities.

The results have implications for both educational and organizational settings, suggesting that brief creativity trainings employing similar cognitive techniques could be effectively implemented to foster creative thinking competencies among individuals across different domains.
