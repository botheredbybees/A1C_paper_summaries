## The Brain’s Default Network: Anatomy, Function, and Relevance to Disease

This review article synthesizes findings about the brain's default network, a system activated during internal cognitive processes such as autobiographical memory retrieval, future envisioning, and perspective-taking. The authors discuss the anatomical structure of the default network, including evidence from both human and monkey studies, and propose that it comprises multiple interconnected subsystems. The medial temporal lobe subsystem provides information from past experiences, while the medial prefrontal subsystem facilitates the flexible use of this information. The article highlights the importance of the default network in adaptive functions such as planning for the future and navigating social interactions, and its potential relevance to mental disorders like autism, schizophrenia, and Alzheimer's disease. The review concludes by discussing the functional and anatomical implications of the default network for these diseases, emphasizing its role in cognitive disorders and its potential involvement in Alzheimer's pathology.
