## Do We Necessarily Need Longitudinal Data to Infer Causal Relations?

The article challenges the common assumption that causal inferences require longitudinal data and argues against the necessity of temporal priority for causation. The authors assert that establishing causal relationships does not fundamentally depend on whether data is longitudinal or cross-sectional, but rather on the structural nature of the modeling approach used. They emphasize the importance of incorporating background knowledge and testing for model invariance to ensure reliable causal inference.
