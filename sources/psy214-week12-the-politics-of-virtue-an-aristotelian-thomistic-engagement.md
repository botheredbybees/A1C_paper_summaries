## The politics of virtue: An Aristotelian-Thomistic engagement with the VIA classification of character strengths

Warren Kinghorn's paper critiques the Values in Action (VIA) Classification of Character Strengths, a foundational text in positive psychology. The author argues that the VIA Classification, while presented as universally applicable, actually reflects the political and moral commitments of modern liberal democracy. Kinghorn examines the compilation process and methodological limitations of the VIA Classification, highlighting the inductive nature of the initial draft and the subsequent comparison with a variety of virtue classifications. He then discusses the philosophical context, drawing from Aristotelian, Augustinian, and Thomistic perspectives to argue that virtues are inherently tied to specific political communities and their normative commitments. Kinghorn concludes that future positive psychology research should be transparent about its moral commitments and attentive to cultural contexts in virtue formation.
