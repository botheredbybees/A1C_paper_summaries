## Optimal dose and type of exercise to improve motor symptoms in adults with Parkinson's disease: A network meta-analysis

This study evaluates the dose-response relationship between different types and doses of exercise interventions and motor symptoms in Parkinson's Disease (PD) patients. Conducted as a systematic review and network meta-analysis, the study includes 81 randomized controlled trials involving 4596 patients. The findings indicate that the optimal exercise dose for overall motor symptom improvement is 1300 MET-min/week, with dance being the most effective at 850 MET-min/week. Significant improvements were observed with lower doses as well, ranging from 60–100 MET-min/week for various exercises. The Minimum Clinically Important Difference (MCID) was achieved at different dose levels for various exercise types, providing evidence-based recommendations for PD management. These results highlight the importance of considering both statistical significance and clinical relevance in exercise prescriptions for PD patients.
