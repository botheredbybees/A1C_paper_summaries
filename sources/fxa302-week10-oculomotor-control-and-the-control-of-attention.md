## Oculomotor Control and the Control of Attention

The chapter 'Oculomotor Control and the Control of Attention' from the book 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle explores the intricate relationship between eye movements and attention. It highlights how four key regions — the superior colliculus, occipital and parietal cortex, frontal cortex (including the frontal eye field (FEF) and supplementary eye field (SEF)), and brainstem oculomotor nuclei — contribute to the control of eye movements. The chapter explains that the superior colliculus, while receiving direct projections from the retina, also integrates multimodal sensory information from cortical areas, influencing eye movements. FEF neurons project directly to the brainstem to control vector saccades, while SEF neurons are involved in goal-oriented saccades. Both endogenous and exogenous attention are discussed, with the endogenous system engaging a dorsal frontoparietal network and the exogenous system being right hemisphere-biased. Microstimulation studies in monkeys suggest that the FEF can influence attention similar to eye movements, and multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) has shown that the same neural systems control both eye movements and attention. This interplay underscores the deep connection between motor control and cognitive functions, particularly in the context of attention and action.
