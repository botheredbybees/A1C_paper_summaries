## Emergence and Realization of Genius: The Lives and Works of 120 Classical Composers

Dean Keith Simonton's study on the lives and works of 120 classical composers from the Renaissance to the 20th century explores the emergence and realization of creativity in the arts. The study proposes a model based on individual differences in career development, focusing on the preparatory phase and productivity variables. Simonton identifies 'hits' in terms of themes and works based on actual popularity, and correlates these with eminence, lifetime output, maximum annual output, and various age-related milestones. The research suggests that individual differences in creative potential and career onset significantly influence the timing and quantity of creative output, offering insights into the career trajectories of renowned composers. The study raises questions about the early childhood origins of later creativity, highlighting the importance of understanding the developmental pathways that lead to artistic achievement. This work contributes to the broader understanding of creative processes and their impact on cultural legacy, providing a foundation for further inquiries into the factors that shape a creative life.
