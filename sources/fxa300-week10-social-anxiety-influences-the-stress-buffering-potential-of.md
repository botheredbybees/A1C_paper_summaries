## Social Anxiety Influences the Stress-buffering Potential of Social Presence: Evidence From Cardiovascular and Affective Reactivity Under Stress

Researchers at the University of Padua conducted a study on the role of relationship type and individual differences in social anxiety in the stress-buffering provided by social presence during stress. The study involved a dyadic version of the Trier Social Stress Test (TSST) with 40 dyads, where each dyad consisted of a target participant and a supporter. The results indicated that participants in romantic relationships (Partner Group) exhibited lower heart rate during acute stress compared to randomly paired participants (Stranger Group). However, participants in the Partner Group reported more anxiety and stress during the task, suggesting that social anxiety may hinder the perceived benefit of social support. The findings highlight the importance of understanding the interplay between social support, stress reactivity, and interpersonal affective processes, emphasizing the need for further research on individual differences in social regulation of stress. This research is relevant to creative arts and health as it underscores the role of social support in mitigating stress, a critical factor in mental health and overall well-being.
