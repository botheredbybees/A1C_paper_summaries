## Lenses in Antiquity

This article explores the use of rock crystal lenses in antiquity, focusing on recent discoveries and their potential applications. The authors detail two high-quality lenses found in the Idaean Cave in Crete, which prompted an investigation into similar finds across the Mediterranean region. These findings suggest that the use of lenses was widespread throughout ancient civilizations, with some lenses demonstrating sufficient optical quality to function as magnifying glasses or burning instruments. The article discusses evidence for their use in various contexts such as gem carving and seal engraving, and presents examples from archaeological sites including Crete, Anatolia, and Cyprus. Additionally, it speculates on the methods of producing these lenses using simple techniques. Overall, this study highlights the importance of lenses in ancient societies beyond mere decorative purposes.
