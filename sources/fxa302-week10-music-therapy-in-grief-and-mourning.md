## Music Therapy in Grief and Mourning

The chapter 'Music Therapy in Grief and Mourning' by Clare O’Callaghan and Natasha Michael explores the role of music therapy in helping individuals cope with grief and mourning, particularly in the context of loss associated with life-threatening conditions and bereavement. The authors emphasize that while music traditionally plays a significant role in communal rituals and health practices, its therapeutic value in modern societies is increasingly recognized. Music therapy involves the creative use of music to facilitate emotional expression and support in dealing with loss. The chapter outlines various music therapy processes, including familiar music listening, creative explorations, and guided usages of music, which can aid in the therapeutic process of mourning. Music therapy is shown to provide a refuge from the intense emotions of grief, offering opportunities for emotional release and psychological support. The text also highlights the importance of individualized approaches in music therapy sessions, noting that participants may benefit from engaging with both familiar and unfamiliar musical forms. Additionally, the chapter discusses the potential of music therapy to help participants integrate their loss within their altered identity and life context. The central argument is that music therapy can enhance the quality of life for individuals experiencing grief by providing a medium through which to express and process complex emotions.
