## FXA300 Week 6 Lecture: Research Methods in Music, Mind, and Body

In this week’s lecture for the course ‘Music, Mind and Body’ (FXA300), Dr Brendan Lamb discusses various research methods used in music psychology. The lecture begins with a recap of Week 5, highlighting that musical preference is influenced by factors such as personality traits, age, time of day, season, and emotion. It then moves on to introduce the essential reference materials for conducting research, including Creswell and Creswell’s 'Research Design: Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed Methods Approaches' (6th ed., 2022) and Yin’s 'Case Study Research: Design and Methods' (5th ed., 2023).

Dr Lamb elaborates on the two main categories of research methods—quantitative and qualitative—and introduces a third category, mixed-methods. Quantitative research focuses on identifying causality or correlation through numeric data, whereas qualitative research aims to explain phenomena through descriptive methods such as narrative research and phenomenological studies. Mixed-methods combine both approaches for comprehensive analysis.

The lecture also covers the scientific method, emphasizing its importance in music psychology research. It explains the process of formulating hypotheses that are clearly defined, testable, positive, falsifiable, and measurable. The types of variables, dependent vs. independent variables, confounds, bias, operationalizing variables, and measures such as mean, median, and mode are discussed.

Dr Lamb concludes by discussing case studies, correlational studies, longitudinal studies, experimental designs, and quasi-experimental studies. He provides details on designing research and highlights the importance of understanding different methodologies in studying musical phenomena.
