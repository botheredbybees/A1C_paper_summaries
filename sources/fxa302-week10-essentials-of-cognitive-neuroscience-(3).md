## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience

The document discusses the cognitive functions of the motor system, particularly the role of mirror neurons in motor control and cognition. The chapter begins by exploring the idea that high-level cognition may have evolved from increasingly abstracted versions of motor control, suggesting a deep connection between motor systems and higher cognitive functions. It highlights the work of Giacomo Rizzolatti and colleagues who discovered mirror neurons in the premotor cortex, which are neurons that fire both when a monkey performs an action and when it observes another individual performing the same action. The study reported in the document, involving Gallese et al. (1996), identifies neurons in sector F5 of the premotor cortex that respond to observed actions in a manner similar to those performed by the monkey itself. These mirror neurons are believed to underlie learning through imitation and possibly speech perception. The relevance of these findings to the creative arts and health is significant as they provide insights into how the brain processes and integrates sensory and motor information, which could have implications for understanding motor disorders and enhancing therapeutic interventions in neurorehabilitation. Moreover, the concept of embodied cognition, supported by the existence of mirror neurons, challenges traditional views of cognition and highlights the interconnectedness of sensory perception and motor behavior in the brain.
