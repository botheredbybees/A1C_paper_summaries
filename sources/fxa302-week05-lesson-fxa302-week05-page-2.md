## Hearing Loss and Musical Composition: The Cases of Beethoven and Smetana

This lesson explores how hearing loss has affected musical composition through historical cases. It begins by discussing societal attitudes towards deafness from Ancient Greece to modern times, emphasizing stigma and psychological impacts. Notably, composers like Ludwig van Beethoven and Bedřich Smetana continued their creative work despite progressive hearing loss. The concept of 'inner hearing' is introduced as a compensatory skill where musicians can visualize music without auditory input. Case studies on Beethoven's struggle with tinnitus and his prolific output during deafness are analyzed, highlighting his reliance on written communication and the theoretical contributions to understanding his condition. Similarly, Smetana’s sudden onset of deafness and depiction of invasive tinnitus in musical form illustrate the enduring impact on creativity despite physical limitations.
