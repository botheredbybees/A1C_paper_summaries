## Is perceiving bodily action?

This paper challenges the thesis that perceiving is bodily action (PBA), a view prominent in enactivist and embodied cognition literature. The author argues that research on eye movements and paralysis does not support PBA, contrary to the claims of its proponents. The paper discusses the work of theorists like J.J. Gibson and Alva Noë, who argue that perception is an active process involving bodily actions. However, the author contends that eye movement research does not necessarily support the idea that perception requires bodily movement, and paralysis research poses a strong challenge to PBA. The author suggests that while understanding bodily movements is important for understanding perception, it does not necessarily mean that perception itself is a bodily action.
