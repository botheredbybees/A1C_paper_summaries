## Courting Discomfort in an Undergraduate Health Humanities Classroom

In this chapter, Michael Blackie and Erin Gentry Lamb explore the role of discomfort in health humanities education. They argue that discomfort is essential for students to critically engage with complex healthcare issues and to recognize their roles as participants in the healthcare system. The authors describe a classroom scenario where students presented on reproductive technology, specifically pre-implantation genetic diagnosis (PGD), in a way that oversimplified ethical and social complexities. The intervention of a visitor, Claire, who revealed herself as a savior sibling with autism, disrupted the class's assumptions and highlighted the importance of diverse perspectives in health humanities education. This incident underscores the necessity of engaging with uncomfortable topics to foster critical thinking and ethical awareness among students.
