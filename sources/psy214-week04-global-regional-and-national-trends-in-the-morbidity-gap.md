## Global, regional, and national trends in the morbidity gap and contributing diseases, injuries, and risk factors, 1990–2023: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023

This study examines global trends in morbidity gaps from 1990 to 2023, analyzing the compression of morbidity hypothesis, which posits that effective prevention and care will delay disability more than death. The research uses data from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) for 204 countries and territories, revealing a global increase in morbidity gaps, particularly among high socio-demographic index (SDI) regions. The findings suggest that while life expectancy has improved, years lived in poor health have also increased. Chronic conditions such as musculoskeletal disorders, mental disorders, sense organ diseases, unintentional injuries, and non-communicable diseases are primary contributors to these morbidity gaps. Additionally, modifiable risk factors like high fasting plasma glucose levels and obesity significantly contribute to unhealthy life expectancy. The study highlights the need for comprehensive approaches focusing on both mortality reduction and healthy aging strategies to address this growing issue.
