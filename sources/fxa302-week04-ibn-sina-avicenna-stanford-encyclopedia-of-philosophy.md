## Ibn Sina [Avicenna] (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

Ibn Sina, also known as Avicenna, was a prominent philosopher and physician in the Islamic world during the 10th and early 11th centuries. Born around 970 CE, he combined Greek philosophy and scientific thought with Islamic traditions to create a comprehensive system of knowledge that encompassed science, religion, and mysticism. His work had significant influence on intellectual life in both the Islamic world and Europe, where his medical Canon was widely used until the 17th century. Avicenna's philosophical contributions also impacted medieval and Renaissance scholars. Despite the political upheavals of his time, he maintained a prolific career, serving various rulers as a physician and counselor while continuing to develop his scientific and philosophical ideas.
