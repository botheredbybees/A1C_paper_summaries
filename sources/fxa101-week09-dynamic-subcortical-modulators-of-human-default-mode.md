## Dynamic subcortical modulators of human default mode network function

This study investigates the role of subcortical structures in modulating the human default mode network (DMN) activity. The research used ultra-high field functional magnetic resonance imaging (7 T fMRI) to examine the influence of the basal forebrain (BF) and mediodorsal thalamus (MD) on DMN cortical regions during rest and task states. The study found that the BF showed activity suppression when transitioning from rest to external task conditions, suggesting a role in regulating DMN activity between internal and external cognitive states. Conversely, the MD was engaged during internally focused cognition, exhibiting broad excitatory influence on DMN cortical regions. These findings provide evidence for distinct roles of the BF and MD in controlling DMN function, contributing to understanding the mechanisms underlying cognitive state transitions and self-oriented higher cognition. This research is relevant to creative arts and health by offering insights into neural processes that support flexible cognitive states, which are essential for creativity and mental health.
