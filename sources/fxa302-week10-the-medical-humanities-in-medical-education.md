## The Medical Humanities in Medical Education

The chapter discusses the role of the medical humanities in addressing structural issues within medical education and practice, focusing on the concept of democracy and the importance of fostering a culture that values diverse viewpoints and encourages creativity. It draws a parallel between the role of artists in society, as noted by Sir Nicholas Serota, and the need for a similar democratizing force within the medical field. The author argues that traditional medical education often fails to address the cultural and structural issues that can lead to poor communication, high error rates, and low empathy among healthcare providers. The chapter suggests that incorporating the medical humanities—such as literature and arts—into medical education could foster the necessary cultural shift, promoting empathy, understanding of complex professional relationships, and better patient care. It concludes by highlighting the potential of narrative fiction in enhancing social abilities and empathy, underscoring the broader relevance of the humanities in improving healthcare delivery and patient safety.
