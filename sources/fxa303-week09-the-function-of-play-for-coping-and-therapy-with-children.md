## The Function of Play for Coping and Therapy with Children Exposed to Disasters and Political Violence

The article by Cohen and Gadassi (2018) explores the role of play in children's coping mechanisms following collective traumatic events such as political violence or natural disasters. The study identifies specific patterns of individual, dyadic, and social play that are associated with measures of adaptation post-trauma. Play therapy is highlighted as a critical component for both preventive and therapeutic interventions, especially when integrated with cognitive-behavioral techniques or involving parents. However, the authors stress the need for more research to clarify the effectiveness and validity of these integrative models in assessing children's resilience and risk factors following traumatic events. The multifaceted nature of post-traumatic play suggests a necessity for tailored therapy approaches that vary based on individual needs, therapist involvement levels, and specific techniques used.
