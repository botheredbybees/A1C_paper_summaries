## A historical overview of music therapy for adults in mental health settings

This chapter provides a historical overview of music therapy for adults in mental health settings. It discusses the shift in perception regarding psychological interventions as essential components of comprehensive mental health care. Music therapy is described as a clinical use of music interventions to achieve individualized goals within a therapeutic relationship. The chapter outlines the professional training and certification requirements for music therapists, emphasizing their role in various clinical settings, including mental health. It highlights that music therapy is often misconceived as merely listening to music or being exclusive to musicians. Instead, it is a versatile therapeutic approach that utilizes active music-making to address a wide range of clinical objectives. The chapter also mentions the historical context of music therapy's origins in Veterans Affairs hospitals and its subsequent expansion to various settings. It underscores the potential of music therapy to enhance psychological well-being and support therapeutic change through self-expression, self-awareness, and insight. The relevance of music therapy in mental health care is highlighted, addressing the need for evidence-based practice and the importance of ongoing research to strengthen the profession's scientific literature base.
