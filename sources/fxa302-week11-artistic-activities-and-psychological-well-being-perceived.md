## Artistic activities and psychological well-being perceived by patients with spinal cord injury

This study investigates the impact of artistic activities on the psychological well-being of patients with spinal cord injuries (SCI). A total of 19 hospital patients with SCI participated in arts-based activities like painting, woodworking, and working with clay during two phases: the reactivation phase, when their physical and psychological conditions began to improve, and the pre-discharge phase. The Psychological General Well-Being Index (PGWBI) was used to measure subjective well-being. The results indicated a statistically significant improvement in the PGWBI global score from a moderate distress level to the absence of distress. Four out of six PGWBI domains (general health, vitality, depressed mood, and positive well-being) showed significant changes. The research highlights the potential of artistic activities in enhancing the psychological well-being of patients with SCI, complementing traditional medical treatments.
