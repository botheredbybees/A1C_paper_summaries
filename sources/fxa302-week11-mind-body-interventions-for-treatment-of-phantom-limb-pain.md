## Mind-Body Interventions for Treatment of Phantom Limb Pain in Persons with Amputation

The article by Moura et al. (2012) provides a literature review on the use of mind-body interventions for treating phantom limb pain (PLP) in individuals who have undergone amputation. The review highlights that PLP is a significant chronic pain issue affecting many amputees and that conventional treatments often fall short, leading to a search for alternative therapies. Mind-body interventions, such as hypnosis, imagery, and biofeedback, show promising results in alleviating PLP. However, the existing literature is limited, with considerable variability in the application of these techniques and the lack of rigorous studies on methods like meditation, yoga, and tai chi/qigong. The authors recommend more research to explore the efficacy and mechanisms of action for these interventions, particularly in terms of cortical reorganization, autonomic nervous system regulation, stress management, and quality of life. The findings suggest that a mind-body approach to PLP management is promising and may offer both temporary and long-term relief, either alone or in combination with conventional therapies.
