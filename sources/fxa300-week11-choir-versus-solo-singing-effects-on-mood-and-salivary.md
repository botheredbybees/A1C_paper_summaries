## Choir versus Solo Singing: Effects on Mood, and Salivary Oxytocin and Cortisol Concentrations

This study investigates the effects of choir singing and solo singing on mood and salivary oxytocin (OXT) and cortisol concentrations in healthy individuals. The research aimed to determine whether the social interaction involved in choir singing influences OXT secretion compared to the non-social activity of solo singing. Participants underwent both activities in a randomized order, with mood and physiological responses measured before, during, and after each session. The findings revealed that both choir and solo singing improved mood and reduced worry and sadness, alongside a decrease in salivary cortisol concentrations. However, contrary to expectations, salivary oxytocin levels did not increase during choir singing but remained stable or decreased, suggesting that the social experience of choir singing does not necessarily induce peripheral oxytocin secretion. The study highlights the importance of considering stress and arousal levels when interpreting oxytocin concentrations and emphasizes the need for controlled experimental conditions in future studies.
