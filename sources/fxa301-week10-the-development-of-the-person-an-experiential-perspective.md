## The Development of the Person: An Experiential Perspective on the Ontogenesis of Psychological Complexity

The chapter 'The Development of the Person: An Experiential Perspective on the Ontogenesis of Psychological Complexity' by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi and Kevin Rathunde explores the concept of personhood from a sociocultural perspective. It argues that defining a person is not solely based on biological attributes but is significantly influenced by societal norms and cultural constructs. The authors illustrate how different cultures, such as traditional Hindu societies or indigenous Amazon tribes, have varying conceptions of what constitutes a 'person', reflecting the social and historical contexts within which these definitions are formed.

The chapter highlights the adaptive nature of these personhood concepts, suggesting that certain common elements across diverse cultural models contribute to human survival and societal stability. It also discusses how these cultural images shape laws, institutions, and behaviors, particularly in domains like child-rearing and education. For instance, a culture viewing individuals as competitive may foster educational systems emphasizing competition, while cooperative conceptions might encourage mutual support.

Moreover, the text contrasts the traditional view of personality, often seen as a mask or facade, with the concept of personhood which encompasses shared human qualities essential for social interaction and cultural continuity. The chapter concludes by underscoring that understanding these sociocultural constructs is crucial for comprehending individual development within specific societal contexts.
