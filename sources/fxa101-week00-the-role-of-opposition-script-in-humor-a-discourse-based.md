## The Role of Opposition Script in Humor: A Discourse-Based Argument for Cognitive Complexity

This paper explores the concept of humor through Victor Raskin's Script-Based Semantic Theory of Humor (SSTH) and Salvatore Attardo’s General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH). The study argues that humor arises from script opposition, which involves the clash between two incompatible scripts or mental representations. It analyzes a sample of humorous texts across various contexts to demonstrate how discourse framing enhances the effectiveness of script opposition. Key findings suggest that humor is not only semantically complex but also deeply rooted in social and cultural interactions, serving multiple social functions such as managing relationships and critiquing institutions. The research emphasizes an integrated perspective that combines semantic incongruity with discursive framing and shared cultural knowledge to understand humor's cognitive complexity.
