## Teaching Autism through Naturalized Narrative Ethics

Julie M. Aultman's chapter explores the integration of naturalized narrative ethics in medical education to bridge the gap between medical ethics and medical humanities. The chapter outlines a pedagogical approach called 'Narratives of Autism,' which involves first-year medical and pharmacy students listening to stories from parents of children with autism. Aultman argues that traditional bioethics approaches often fall short in addressing the ethical complexities faced by families of individuals with autism. By engaging with chaotic narratives, such as those told by 'Chloe,' students gain a deeper understanding of the lived experiences of individuals and families affected by autism, enhancing their moral imagination, self-reflection, and empathy. However, Aultman also highlights the limitations of this approach, acknowledging that narrative ethics alone may not fully address the ethical challenges in healthcare settings. This work is relevant to creative arts and health by emphasizing the importance of storytelling and narrative in fostering ethical sensitivity and understanding in healthcare professionals.
