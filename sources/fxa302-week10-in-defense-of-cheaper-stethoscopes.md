## In Defense of Cheaper Stethoscopes

Jay Baruch discusses the significance and emotional attachment doctors have to their stethoscopes, highlighting the practicality and sentimental value of a cheaper, less advanced model over expensive versions. He recounts an incident with a colleague who uses his stethoscope due to its comfort and the 'spirit' it embodies, suggesting it represents wisdom and experience. Baruch reflects on the symbolic importance of the stethoscope in medical education and practice, emphasizing the role it plays in fostering empathy and humility. He argues that becoming a better physician involves continuous learning and self-reflection, often from unexpected sources such as non-medical staff and patients. The essay underscores the importance of maintaining a humble and open attitude towards medical practice and lifelong learning, which is crucial for effective healthcare delivery.
