## Arts for ageing well: a propensity score matching analysis of the effects of arts engagements on holistic well-being among older Asian adults above 50 years of age

This study by Ho et al., published in BMJ Open, examines the relationship between arts engagement and holistic well-being among older Asian adults aged 50 and above in Singapore. The research employs a cross-sectional stratified household survey methodology to assess both active and passive engagements in various art forms such as music, dance, theatre, literature, visual arts, heritage activities, film, and craft events. Participants reported their frequency and duration of participation, along with validated psychometric assessments on quality of life, physical health, psychological well-being, emotional resilience, social support, spiritual well-being, and meaning in life.

Key findings reveal that passive engagement in the arts was associated with better holistic wellness and social support; individuals who passively engaged in arts-related activities reported higher quality of life, perceived health, and a sense of belonging compared to those who did not. Additionally, active participation in arts produced significant positive effects on physical health, self-rated health, spiritual well-being, meaning in life, and a sense of peace. These results suggest that arts engagement can serve as a valuable intervention for promoting holistic well-being among aging populations, aligning with the growing emphasis on creative practices to foster healthy and active aging.
