## Oppositions, Overlaps, and Ontologies: The General Theory of Verbal Humor Revisited

This paper critically examines the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) and its predecessor, the Semantic Script Theory of Humor (SSTH). The author argues that SSTH's concepts of script opposition and overlap are not well-defined or sufficient for identifying joke-carrying texts. He critiques the GTVH’s reliance on these poorly defined concepts and suggests that the theory fails to account adequately for humor generation. Additionally, the paper questions the adequacy of the Ontological Semantics Theory of Humor (OSTH) in providing a computational basis for recognizing jokes versus non-jokes. The author contends that SSTH/GTVH does not include the critical notion of incongruity necessary for humorous interpretation and proposes an alternative perspective based on appropriate incongruities.
