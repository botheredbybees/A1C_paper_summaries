## Neural Pathways Underlying the Production of Pitch and Rhythm in Aphasia

This study explores the neural pathways involved in pitch, melodic contour, and rhythm production during singing for patients with poststroke aphasia (PSA). The research indicates that PSA patients exhibit lower accuracy in these aspects compared to healthy controls. Using whole-brain white matter correlational tractography, the authors identified a left-hemisphere dominant network associated with singing production, which includes both auditory-motor integration pathways and additional associative and projection pathways extending into the right hemisphere. These findings highlight the dual stream model of neural organization involved in vocal communication and suggest that singing-based rehabilitation methods can leverage music's structural elements to aid language recovery.
