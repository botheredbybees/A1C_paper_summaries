## Late-life Cognitive Activity and Dementia: A Systematic Review and Bias Analysis

This systematic review and bias analysis evaluates the relationship between late-life cognitive activity and reduced risk of Alzheimer's disease (AD) and all-cause dementia. The authors conducted a comprehensive search of epidemiological studies through June 2014 to identify relevant research. They found that engaging in cognitive activities in later life is often proposed as a preventive measure against AD, but the evidence supporting this causal relationship remains unclear. The review included 12 studies with over 13,939 participants and analyzed their findings methodologically for potential biases such as confounding variables and reverse causation. Results indicated that most studies showed an inverse association between cognitive activity and incidence of dementia, though differences in how cognitive activity was operationalized prevented a meta-analysis of effect estimates. The bias analysis suggested that the observed associations are likely robust to unmeasured confounding and partially explained by reverse causation. However, more research is necessary to confirm this relationship and determine the optimal type, duration, intensity, and timing of cognitive activities for dementia prevention.
