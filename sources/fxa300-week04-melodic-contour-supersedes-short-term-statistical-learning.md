## Melodic contour supersedes short-term statistical learning in expressive accentuation

This research article investigates the role of melodic contour and statistical learning in expressive accentuation within music, specifically focusing on how listeners perceive dynamic accents (transient increases in volume). The study proposes a novel paradigm based on an artificial Markov-chain melodic grammar to examine whether structural saliency driven by Gestalt principles or short-term statistical properties influences aesthetic preferences. Results indicate that while participants exhibit knowledge of the statistical properties of melodies, they do not show a preference for accents aligned with high information content (IC) notes. Instead, accentuation aligns more closely with melodic contours as dictated by Gestalt principles. This finding suggests that intrinsic structural features such as melodic shape play a significant role in expressive musical interpretation, whereas acquired statistical regularities have less impact on listener preferences. The study contributes to understanding the interplay between domain-general and domain-specific processing during music listening.
