## The effects of mother–infant singing on emotional closeness, affect, anxiety, and stress hormones

The article by Fancourt and Perkins explores the impact of mother-infant singing on emotional bonding, affect, and stress in both mothers and infants. It posits that singing, an evolutionary adaptation possibly designed to support mother-infant bonding, leads to enhanced emotional closeness, positive affect, and reduced anxiety and stress hormones. The study, conducted as a within-subjects comparison of mother-infant singing with other social interactions, found singing to be particularly effective in increasing maternal perceptions of emotional closeness. Additionally, it demonstrated reductions in psychological and biological markers of anxiety. These findings underscore the importance of singing in fostering positive mental health and well-being among mothers and infants, contributing to the broader understanding of how music can play a crucial role in social and emotional development within the creative arts and health domains.
