## Occupational Medicine

Jack Coulehan’s personal narrative in 'Occupational Medicine' recounts his experiences as a medical advisor to co-workers at the Wheeling Steel plant during his college summers. Despite having no formal medical training, Coulehan assumed the role of 'Doc,' offering advice on medical issues to his fellow workers. The narrative highlights the stark contrasts between his academic life and the harsh realities of the steel plant, where he observed labor practices and the health challenges faced by workers. Coulehan’s interactions with Dwayne, a troubled worker with liver issues, underscore the human side of occupational health and the importance of addressing workers' health concerns. The text emphasizes the practical application of medical knowledge in real-life settings and the ethical considerations of providing medical advice without formal training.
