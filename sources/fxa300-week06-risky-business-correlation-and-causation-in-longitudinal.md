## Risky Business: Correlation and Causation in Longitudinal Studies of Skill Development

This article explores the limitations of using regression-adjusted correlations to test developmental theories, specifically focusing on skill-building theories in early childhood education. The authors argue that such correlations are insufficiently 'risky' tests because they can be consistent with multiple plausible alternative theories. Using mathematics as a case study, the research reveals that experimental manipulations of early math skills result in smaller impacts on later achievement than nonexperimental studies suggest. Furthermore, falsification tests indicate puzzling cross-domain associations between early math and later literacy achievement. The authors propose an alternative model incorporating stable factors and skill-building processes to explain observed patterns, challenging the conventional understanding of developmental theories and their implications for policy.
