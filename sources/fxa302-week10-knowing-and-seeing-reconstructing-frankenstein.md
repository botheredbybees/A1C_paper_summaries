## Knowing and Seeing: Reconstructing Frankenstein

Paul Root Wolpe's chapter explores Mary Shelley's 'Frankenstein' and its enduring impact on cultural perceptions of science and technology. The chapter discusses how the novel has been distorted over time to reflect contemporary concerns, often losing the original themes Shelley intended. Wolpe emphasizes the importance of the novel's narrative structure, which includes layers of storytelling that reflect the characters' perspectives and the ethical challenges posed by scientific advancements. Key themes include the pursuit of knowledge without moral restraint and the distinction between appearance and essence, highlighting Shelley's prescient insights into the ethical dilemmas of modern biotechnology. Wolpe argues that Shelley's work remains relevant for understanding the responsibilities and risks associated with scientific research in the 21st century.
