## From singing to speaking: facilitating recovery from nonfluent aphasia

The article explores the potential of melodic intonation therapy (MIT) in aiding patients with nonfluent aphasia, a condition often resulting from left frontal lobe damage following a stroke. The authors discuss the historical observation that aphasic patients often sing better than they speak and present MIT as a therapeutic approach. MIT involves the rhythmic intonation of words and phrases using a melodic contour, along with left-hand tapping, which engages right-hemisphere brain regions. This therapy is particularly beneficial for patients with extensive left-hemisphere lesions where natural recovery is limited. The study highlights that MIT can activate fronto-temporal regions in the right hemisphere, offering a pathway for recovery when left-hemisphere functions are severely impaired. The efficacy of MIT is supported by neuroimaging evidence showing changes in brain connectivity and activity patterns. The findings suggest that MIT may facilitate the recruitment of homologous right-hemispheric language and speech-motor regions, contributing to improved language function in nonfluent aphasia patients. The relevance to creative arts and health underscores the potential of integrating musical interventions in neurological rehabilitation, demonstrating how creative arts can support therapeutic outcomes.
