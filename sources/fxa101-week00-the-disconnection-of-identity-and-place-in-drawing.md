## The Disconnection of Identity and Place in Drawing: Superficial Exploration of Cultural Landscapes

This paper discusses the disconnection between personal identity and sense of place in cultural landscapes when artists use superficial drawing practices. It argues that such approaches often fail to capture deeper meanings embedded within these places, focusing instead on formal visual qualities like form and color. Through a series of plein-air drawings at three distinct sites—an agricultural valley, an urban river walk, and a heritage village—the research examines how disconnected representations emerge when artists lack context or reflective practices. The findings suggest that without deep engagement with the cultural and historical dimensions of these landscapes, drawings may become superficial, detached from genuine understanding and connection to place.
