## Conceptualizing Music Therapy: Five Areas that Frame the Field

Jane Edwards presents a comprehensive overview of music therapy in the context of the Oxford Handbook of Music Therapy. The chapter begins by addressing the challenge of succinctly defining music therapy to new practitioners and students. Edwards highlights the importance of engaging with others to explain the essence of music therapy, which involves the use of music in therapeutic processes. The text introduces five key constructs that frame the field of music therapy: contexts and populations across the lifespan, models and approaches, methods, research, and training and professional issues. Edwards argues that music therapy is a relational therapy requiring qualified practitioners who undergo appropriate training and continuous professional development. She emphasizes the variability of music therapy services based on context and client needs, making it difficult to provide a one-size-fits-all definition. The chapter also includes definitions of music therapy from various sources, including the World Federation for Music Therapy (WFMT), which underscores the importance of cultural sensitivity and professional standards in practice. The relevance of music therapy to creative arts and health lies in its ability to address diverse needs across different settings, highlighting the therapeutic benefits of music for individuals and communities.
