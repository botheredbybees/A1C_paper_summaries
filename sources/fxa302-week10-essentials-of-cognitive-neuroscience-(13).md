## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience

The text discusses various aspects of cognitive neuroscience, focusing on memory retrieval and the impact of brain regions, particularly the hippocampus, on memory functions. It examines the caudal-to-rostral gradient of dorsal-stream input into the hippocampus and the differences observed between taxi drivers and bus drivers in terms of gray matter volume and memory performance. The text also explores Tulving’s distinction between autonoetic, noetic, and anoetic states of awareness regarding memory retrieval. It highlights the case of H. M., demonstrating spared motor learning despite amnesia, and discusses the debate around encoding versus retrieval-based accounts of anterograde amnesia. The relevance to creative arts and health lies in understanding how brain function influences cognitive processes, which can inform artistic and therapeutic approaches.
