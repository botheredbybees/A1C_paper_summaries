## FXA302 Week 8 Welcome

This document is a welcome message for the eighth week of the FXA302 course, which explores the historical influence of the theory of humours on medical and philosophical thought, particularly during the Graeco-Roman era. The theory, which persisted in various forms until the 18th century, is discussed in relation to its symbolic presence in the creative arts. The week's content delves into how the concept of melancholia, or black humour, was intertwined with introspection and depression in the Romantic era, and how this notion linked creative genius with mental suffering. Four case studies of prominent artists—composer Robert Schumann, poet and novelist Sylvia Plath, and painters Vincent van Gogh and Edvard Munch—are used to analyze the impact of mental illness on creative work, including changes in subject matter and artistic style. The document also highlights the role of creativity as a means of emotional regulation. This content is intended to provoke thought and deepen understanding of the intersection between mental health and creative arts.
