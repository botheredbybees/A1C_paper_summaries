## Imagination and Creativity in Organizations

This article explores the role of imagination in organizational creativity, shifting focus from individual cognition to a relational and aesthetic approach. It introduces a theoretical model based on English Romanticism, which redefines creativity as an interplay between primary and secondary modes of imagination, emphasizing the shared construction of perceptual images and possible futures among organizational members. The primary imagination involves blending sensory experiences and memories, while the secondary imagination involves envisioning non-present scenarios. The paper argues that this perspective addresses the limitations of existing theories by maintaining a generative role of mind without ontological reductionism. It also discusses implications for entrepreneurial action and creative processes within organizations, highlighting the importance of imagination in overcoming perceptual differences and fostering collaborative creativity.
