## Positive Psychology: Overview

The document provides an overview of positive psychology, a subdiscipline of psychology that focuses on the science of optimal functioning or “flourishing.” It contrasts positive psychology with clinical psychology, emphasizing its focus on the positive attributes and activities that foster well-being rather than solely on mental illness. The history of positive psychology is traced back to the late 1990s under the leadership of Martin Seligman, often referred to as the “father of positive psychology.” The text discusses the distinction between positive psychology and positive thinking, offering different conceptualizations of positive thinking, such as optimism, cognitive restructuring, and coping mechanisms like benefit-finding and meaning-making. It also addresses the risks associated with the practice of positive thinking, including the potential for avoidance of negative realities and the risk of personal responsibility for failure. The document concludes with an example illustrating how contrasting approaches, including optimism and coping strategies, can be applied in real-life situations to foster resilience and well-being.
