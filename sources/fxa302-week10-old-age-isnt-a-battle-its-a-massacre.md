## Old Age Isn’t a Battle, It’s a Massacre

This essay explores Philip Roth's portrayal of aging in his novel 'Everyman' and contrasts it with traditional Western views on aging. The protagonist of 'Everyman' embodies the struggles of aging in contemporary society, including deteriorating physical health and diminishing sexual prowess. Roth's narrative is situated within a broader context of his other works, which also feature older characters grappling with similar issues. The essay examines how Roth's characters reject conventional morality and religious beliefs, focusing instead on the raw realities of aging, death, and sexuality. This perspective challenges societal expectations of aging gracefully and highlights the limitations of medical interventions in addressing the existential challenges of old age. The text also draws parallels with ancient philosophical views on aging, such as those of Plato and Cicero, which emphasize the importance of virtue and wisdom over physical well-being. By juxtaposing these classical ideals with Roth's contemporary critique, the essay underscores the complexities and moral ambiguities of aging in modern society.
