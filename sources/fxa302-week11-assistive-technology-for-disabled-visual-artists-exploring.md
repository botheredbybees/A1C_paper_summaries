## Assistive technology for disabled visual artists: exploring the impact of digital technologies on artistic practice

This article explores the current practice of physically impaired visual artists and their experiences with digital technologies as assistive tools. Chris Creed conducted an online survey and follow-up interviews with professional disabled artists, revealing that they primarily use mainstream digital technologies like Adobe Photoshop and Illustrator, rather than traditional assistive tools such as mouth sticks and custom grips. The study highlights a lack of awareness and utilization of novel digital technologies such as eye gaze tracking, mid-air gesturing, and voice recognition. The research underscores the importance of collaborative efforts between disabled artists, technology specialists, and disability arts organizations to develop new assistive tools that better support creative and administrative aspects of artistic practice.
