## Music in mind, a randomized controlled trial of music therapy for young people with behavioural and emotional problems: study protocol

This study presents a protocol for a randomized controlled trial examining the efficacy of improvisational music therapy in improving communication and interaction skills among young people aged 8–16 years with behavioral and emotional problems. The research aims to address existing uncertainties regarding the effectiveness of music therapy, which lacks robust empirical evidence despite its widespread use. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either standard care or standard care plus twelve weekly sessions of music therapy over a period of fifteen months in six centers across Northern Ireland's Child and Adolescent Mental Health Services. Outcomes include measures for communication skills, self-esteem, social functioning, depression, and family functioning, with follow-up assessments at one and thirteen weeks post-treatment. The trial will provide critical evidence to support or challenge the clinical use of music therapy for young people dealing with social, emotional, and behavioral issues.
