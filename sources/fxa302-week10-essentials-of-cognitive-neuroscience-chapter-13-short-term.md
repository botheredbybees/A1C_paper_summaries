## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience - Chapter 13: Short-Term and Working Memory

Chapter 13 of 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Postle delves into the distinction between short-term memory (STM) and working memory, exploring their roles and neural substrates. STM is described as the temporary storage of information without the need for medial temporal lobe (MTL) involvement, whereas working memory involves the manipulation of this stored information for tasks such as mental arithmetic. Early lesion studies suggested that the prefrontal cortex (PFC) is crucial for working memory tasks but not for STM. Subsequent electrophysiological studies revealed elevated neural activity in the PFC during the delay periods of working memory tasks, which was initially misinterpreted as a neural correlate of STM. The chapter discusses how the integration of cognitive psychology theories with neurophysiological findings has led to a better understanding of the PFC's role in working memory. Modern multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) techniques have provided more specific and sensitive insights, showing that representations of information held in STM are supported in regions responsible for perception, including V1 for visual STM. The chapter also emphasizes the dynamic trajectory of PFC activity during working memory tasks, suggesting a complex, high-dimensional state space. This chapter highlights the evolving understanding of STM and working memory, and their relationship with long-term memory (LTM) and cognitive control.
