## Does Cultural Capital Structure American Consumption?

Douglas B. Holt's article examines whether Pierre Bourdieu’s theory on cultural capital and consumption patterns applies to contemporary American society. The study critiques the traditional Warnerian approach, which focuses on social class through lifestyle analysis but lacks a comprehensive theoretical framework for understanding how consumption practices reproduce class distinctions. Holt proposes reformulating Bourdieu’s theory to focus on mass culture rather than high culture and emphasizes consumption practices over objects. Through ethnographic interviews in an eastern American county, Holt identifies six dimensions of taste that distinguish individuals with different levels of cultural capital: formal versus material aesthetics, referential versus critical interpretations, idealism versus materialism, cosmopolitan versus local tastes, individualist versus communal consumer subjectivity, and autotelic versus self-actualizing leisure. These findings suggest that consumption remains a significant site for the reproduction of social class boundaries in modern society.
