## Deciphering Sounds Through Patterns of Vibration on the Skin

The paper explores the potential of using spatiotemporal patterns of vibration on the skin as a sensory substitution method for auditory information in individuals with hearing loss or deafness. Researchers developed a wristband containing four vibratory motors that convert sound frequencies into distinct spatial patterns. Participants, ranging from ages 23 to 84 and mostly experiencing severe or profound hearing loss, were tested over one month on their ability to distinguish between similar vibration patterns and identify sounds through these vibrations alone. The study found significant improvement in performance with time, indicating plastic changes that enabled participants to interpret auditory information via skin sensations.
