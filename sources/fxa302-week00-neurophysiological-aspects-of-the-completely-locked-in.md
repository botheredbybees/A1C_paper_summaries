## Neurophysiological aspects of the completely locked-in syndrome in patients with advanced amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

This study explores the neurophysiological characteristics of patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) in a completely locked-in state (CLIS), where they are fully paralyzed and unable to communicate. The researchers used various neurophysiological metrics, including resting-state EEG, auditory evoked potentials (AEPs), and somatosensory evoked potentials (SEPs), to assess brain function. Results showed altered neurophysiological metrics with no common pattern among patients. EEG slowing and attenuated alpha waves were observed in all patients, while AEPs and SEPs were significantly altered. The study underscores the difficulty in assessing cognitive and perceptual states in CLIS patients due to the heterogeneity of results. It suggests that no single neurophysiological index can be used to evaluate the psychophysiological state in CLIS patients. The findings contribute to understanding the late stages of ALS when patients are completely paralyzed.
