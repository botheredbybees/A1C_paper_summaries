## Multi-faceted functions of secretory IgA at mucosal surfaces

This review article by Blaise Corthésy delves into the multifaceted functions of secretory IgA (SIgA) at mucosal surfaces. SIgA is crucial for the protection and regulation of intestinal, respiratory, and urogenital mucosal epithelia, preventing the invasion of microorganisms and allergens. The primary role of SIgA, known as immune exclusion, limits the access of various antigens to these barriers. The article highlights the complexity of SIgA's molecular structure and its involvement in various protective mechanisms, such as intercepting viruses, neutralizing pathogens, and sampling antigen-SIgA complexes by M cells. Additionally, SIgA contributes to the shaping of the mucosal immune system, promoting a non-inflammatory, tolerogenic pattern. This review underscores the essential role of SIgA in maintaining mucosal homeostasis and its broader implications for immune surveillance and systemic immunity.
