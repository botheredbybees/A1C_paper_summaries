## Plato’s Puppets of the Gods: Representing the Magical, the Mystical and the Metaphysical

Ken Moore explores an extended metaphor in Plato’s Laws where human beings are likened to 'puppets of the gods,' a concept that delves into ancient Greek and Near Eastern traditions concerning puppetry, particularly its role in religious and mystical rituals. The metaphor emphasizes the notion of self-mastery through controlling one's desires with reason, represented by the 'golden cord' guiding individuals along the correct path. Moore also discusses how Plato’s use of this metaphor relates to his broader philosophical ideas on metaphysics and the Theory of Forms. He highlights that while ancient Greek puppets were used in various rituals, they often symbolized divine entities or mythical characters, thus linking them to religious practices. This study elucidates the multifaceted nature of Plato's analogy, connecting it with cultural traditions, psychological theories, and philosophical constructs.
