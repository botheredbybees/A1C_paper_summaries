## Does regular engagement with arts and creative activities improve adolescent mental health and wellbeing? A systematic review and assessment of causality

This systematic review investigates the relationship between regular engagement with arts and creative activities and adolescent mental health and wellbeing, focusing on causality. The study includes quantitative research published from 2014 to 2024 assessing the link between consistent participation in such activities and improved mental health outcomes for adolescents aged 10-19 years. Of 7769 screened records, 28 were selected for inclusion, with most studies suggesting a positive association between arts engagement and mental health improvement. The review uses Bradford Hill viewpoints to evaluate causal support but finds limited evidence from experimental designs or dose-response relationships. The discussion highlights the need for further research using randomized experiments or applying causal inference methods to observational data to better establish causality in this relationship, which is increasingly relevant given declines in public funding for arts programs.
