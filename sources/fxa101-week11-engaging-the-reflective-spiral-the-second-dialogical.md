## Engaging the Reflective Spiral: The Second Dialogical Movement

Christopher Johns discusses the process of engaging in reflection through the reflective spiral, emphasizing the importance of moving beyond initial experiences to gain deeper insights. He critiques models of reflection that present reflection as a linear process, instead advocating for a more dynamic and heuristic approach. Johns introduces the Model for Structured Reflection (MSR) designed to guide practitioners through a reflective process that incorporates various cues related to feelings, ethics, knowledge, and past experiences. The MSR is structured across five phases: preparatory, descriptive, reflective, anticipatory, and insight. This model encourages practitioners to reflect on the impact of their actions on others and themselves, and to explore new ways of responding to situations. The text emphasizes the importance of suspending interpretations and engaging in dialogue with the reflective cues to deepen understanding and foster personal growth. This approach is particularly relevant to creative arts and health professionals as it encourages self-reflection and continuous improvement in practice.
