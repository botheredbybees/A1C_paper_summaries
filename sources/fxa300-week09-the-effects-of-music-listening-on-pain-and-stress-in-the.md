## The effects of music listening on pain and stress in the daily life of patients with fibromyalgia syndrome

This study explores the effects of music listening on pain and stress in women with fibromyalgia syndrome (FMS). The research utilized an ecological momentary assessment design over 14 days to gather data on pain intensity, perceived control over pain, stress levels, and music listening behavior from 30 participants. Saliva samples were also collected to measure cortisol and alpha-amylase levels, indicators of stress-responsive systems. The analysis revealed that music listening significantly increased perceived control over pain, especially when the music was positive and listened to for relaxation or activation purposes. However, no significant effect was found on pain intensity. The study highlights the potential role of music therapy in symptom control for chronic pain patients and suggests that more intensive engagement with music might be necessary to understand the underlying biological mechanisms of its health benefits.
