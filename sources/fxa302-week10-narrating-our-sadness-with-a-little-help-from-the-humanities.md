## Narrating Our Sadness, with a Little Help from the Humanities

The chapter 'Narrating Our Sadness, with a Little Help from the Humanities' by Brad Lewis explores the various models and narratives used to understand depression. It begins by noting the global prevalence and impact of depression, highlighting its complexity and multifaceted nature. Lewis discusses the biomedical model, which views depression as a brain disorder, but also mentions other models such as psychoanalytic, cognitive-behavioral, existential-humanist, and biopsychosocial approaches. The chapter emphasizes the importance of narrative theory in understanding these models, focusing on key narrative elements like metaphor, plot, character, and point of view. Metaphor, according to Lewis, shapes our perceptions and understanding of depression, while plot and character help structure our experiences and identities. Point of view is highlighted as crucial in shaping how individuals and therapists approach and understand depression. The chapter concludes by advocating for a values-based practice in clinical decision-making, emphasizing that decisions should consider both empirical data and personal values. This approach aligns with the narrative theory's insight that there are multiple valid ways to narrate and address depression, thereby providing a holistic and flexible framework for dealing with mental health challenges.
