## The Influence of Solo Performance Opportunities on Self-Reported Levels of Musical Performance Anxiety Among Undergraduate College Music Majors

This thesis investigates the relationship between the frequency of solo performance opportunities and self-reported levels of musical performance anxiety (MPA) among undergraduate music majors at a research-intensive university in the southern United States. The study utilized a survey with eight questions to gather demographic data and measure changes in MPA over time. Data analysis revealed a slight decline in MPA levels as students progressed through their undergraduate programs. A significant correlation was found between MPA reduction and the number of solo performances in specific contexts such as jury and barrier examinations, studio classes, master classes, and ensemble concerts. The research underscores the importance of repeated solo performance experiences in managing and reducing MPA, thereby enhancing performance quality and overall musical development. This study contributes to the understanding of how structured performance opportunities can positively influence the psychological well-being and professional growth of aspiring musicians.
