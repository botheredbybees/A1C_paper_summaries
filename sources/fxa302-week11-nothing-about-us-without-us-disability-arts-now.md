## Nothing About Us Without Us: Disability Arts Now

The article by Emily Watlington discusses the role of art in the disability justice movement in the United States. It highlights how disabled artists have used various forms of art—such as memoirs, paintings, installations, videos, and performances—to challenge ableist beliefs and advocate for greater inclusion and accessibility. The article mentions specific artworks by artists like Christine Sun Kim and Park McArthur, who use their work to bring attention to the daily challenges and joys of living with disabilities. The piece underscores the importance of diverse, first-person narratives in shaping a more inclusive and understanding society. It also emphasizes the necessity of flexible, adaptable approaches to accessibility, which are central to the disability justice movement. The article concludes by noting the growing recognition of disability arts in mainstream culture, with recent examples like the success of CODA at the Academy Awards, illustrating the cultural shift brought about by these artistic efforts.
