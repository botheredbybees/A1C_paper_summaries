## Choral singing therapy following stroke or Parkinson’s disease: an exploration of participants’ experiences

This study explores the experiences and motivations of participants with stroke or Parkinson’s disease (PD) who engage in choral singing therapy (CST). The research highlights that people with these conditions often face challenges related to communication, social participation, and mood, which can significantly impact their quality of life. Participants in the study reported that CST provided an enjoyable social activity that improved their mood, voice, and language abilities. Additionally, the therapy was perceived to help participants manage the social isolation and low mood associated with their conditions. The findings suggest that choral singing therapy can be a low-cost, accessible approach to enhancing the well-being of individuals with stroke or PD, offering both therapeutic and social benefits. The study underscores the importance of community-based interventions that address the multifaceted needs of individuals with chronic neurological conditions.
