## Effects of dance on gait and dual-task gait in Parkinson’s disease

This study investigates the impact of a dance-based intervention, inspired by the Dance for Parkinson's (DfPD) program, on the gait of individuals with Parkinson’s disease (PD). The research aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention on gait under various dual-task conditions and terrains. Participants were divided into a dance group (DG) and a control group (CG). The DG underwent a 12-week dance intervention, while the CG continued with their usual treatment. The study found that the DG showed significant improvements in gait parameters such as velocity, cadence, and step length, both in single-task and dual-task conditions, especially when walking on even surfaces. The intervention also showed beneficial effects on uneven surfaces, particularly during dual-task conditions, though the benefits were less pronounced. The research concludes that the DfPD-based classes significantly improved gait parameters under various conditions, suggesting that such interventions can enhance the independence and quality of life for individuals with PD. This study supports the need for larger-scale randomized controlled trials to further validate these findings.
