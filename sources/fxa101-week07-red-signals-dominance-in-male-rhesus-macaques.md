## Red Signals Dominance in Male Rhesus Macaques

The study by Khan et al. (2011) explores the hypothesis that red coloration signals dominance in rhesus macaques, similar to findings in humans. The researchers conducted an experiment in which male rhesus macaques were given the opportunity to steal food from human experimenters wearing different colored uniforms (red, blue, and green). The macaques consistently avoided the experimenter wearing red, regardless of the sex of the experimenter. This finding provides the first experimental evidence that red may be a signal of dominance in nonhuman primates, supporting the notion that humans are influenced by evolved psychological predispositions to react submissively to opponents wearing red uniforms in combat sports. The results suggest that the aversion to red in competitive situations has deep evolutionary roots and is not entirely culturally determined, implying that athletic associations should consider recommendations to minimize the effects of color psychology on the outcome of combat sports.
