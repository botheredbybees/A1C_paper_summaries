## The locked-in syndrome : what is it like to be conscious but paralyzed and voiceless?

This article explores the locked-in syndrome (LIS), a condition where patients are conscious but unable to communicate through speech or body movements due to selective motor de-efferentation. The most common cause of LIS is ventral pontine lesions leading to acute cases often mistaken for coma. Diagnosis can be delayed by up to 4-6 years, significantly impacting patient care. The study highlights the importance of recognizing LIS early to provide appropriate medical care, communication aids, and ethical support for quality of life and autonomy decisions. Chronic LIS patients report meaningful quality of life and low demand for euthanasia, emphasizing the need for a renewed ethical and medicolegal framework.
