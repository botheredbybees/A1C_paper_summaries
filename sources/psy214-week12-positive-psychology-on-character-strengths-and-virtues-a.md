## Positive Psychology on Character Strengths and Virtues: A Disquieting Suggestion

The article critiques the Values in Action (VIA) classification of character strengths and virtues proposed by Peterson and Seligman, arguing that it fails to align with the classical virtue ethical perspective. Banicki examines two fundamental features of virtue ethics: the unity of virtue and the constitutive relationship between virtue and happiness. He contends that the VIA framework contradicts these principles by treating individual virtues and strengths as independent variables and endorsing the fact/value distinction. The paper concludes by discussing potential responses to the critique from the VIA proponents.
