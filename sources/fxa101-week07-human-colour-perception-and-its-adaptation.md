## Human colour perception and its adaptation

Michael A. Webster's review article delves into the mechanisms of human colour perception and the effects of adaptation on these mechanisms. The visual system initially processes colour information through the activity of cone photoreceptors, each with distinct spectral sensitivities. Subsequent processing occurs in post-receptoral channels that respond to specific combinations of cone signals. The article highlights two types of adaptation: retinal light adaptation, which adjusts to changes in the average scene illumination, and cortical contrast adaptation, which modifies sensitivity to the distribution of colours within scenes. These adaptations significantly influence how we perceive colour, making our visual experiences dynamic and context-dependent. Understanding these processes is crucial for fields within creative arts and health that rely on accurate colour perception and its physiological underpinnings.
