## Attitudes towards Personhood in the Locked-in Syndrome: from Third- to First- Person Perspective and to Interpersonal Significance

This paper explores the concept of personhood in patients with locked-in syndrome (LIS), focusing on the discrepancy between third-person perspectives and first-person experiences. The study highlights that while LIS patients retain their sense of personal identity despite severe physical impairments, others often underestimate their quality of life and overestimate the challenges they face. The research underscores the importance of interpersonal recognition in establishing personhood for LIS patients, emphasizing the role of effective communication and supportive environments in reinforcing their status as persons. The paper also discusses the ethical and medical implications of treating LIS patients, including attitudes towards pain perception, end-of-life care, and autonomy.
