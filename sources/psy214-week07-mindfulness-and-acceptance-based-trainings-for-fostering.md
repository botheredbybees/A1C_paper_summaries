## Mindfulness and acceptance-based trainings for fostering self-care and reducing stress in mental health professionals: A systematic review

This systematic review evaluates the effectiveness of mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT), mindful self-compassion (MSC), and acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) in enhancing self-care and reducing stress among mental health professionals. The study synthesizes findings from twenty-four quantitative articles sourced from PsycInfo and PubMed, focusing on methodologies such as MBSR, MBCT, MSC, and ACT. Results indicate that all training programs improve mindfulness levels, with some also boosting self-compassion. Psychological flexibility tends to increase in ACT studies over time. Furthermore, MBSR, MSC, and ACT tend to reduce stress or burnout. However, evidence for enhancing psychological well-being is less conclusive. The review underscores the need for further research on training adaptations and their efficacy.
