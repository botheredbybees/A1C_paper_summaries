## Effectiveness of music interventions on dental anxiety in paediatric and adult patients: a systematic review

This systematic review investigates the effectiveness of music interventions in reducing dental anxiety among both pediatric and adult patients. The study identifies dental anxiety as a significant problem that impedes quality dental care, with patients often avoiding necessary procedures due to fear and anxiety. Music interventions, classified as either passive (music listening) or active (music therapy), were examined for their potential to alleviate dental anxiety. The review included seven studies, involving a total of 598 patients, comprising 513 adults and 85 children. Results showed that for adults, there was evidence to suggest that music listening could significantly reduce anxiety levels, especially for females. However, for children, the evidence was inconclusive, with one study indicating no reduction in anxiety and another showing a minor reduction. The review concludes that pre-recorded music should be offered to adult patients during dental procedures to potentially reduce anxiety. Further research is recommended to establish more definitive evidence for the effectiveness of music interventions, particularly in pediatric populations, to better inform clinical practice and guidelines.
