## Francisco José de Goya y Lucientes (1746–1828), the suffering artist

The article explores the life and work of Francisco José de Goya y Lucientes, a pivotal figure in European art, focusing on his illness and its impact on his artwork. Goya experienced a mysterious illness in 1792-93 that left him profoundly deaf and subsequently altered his artistic style. The authors propose various medical hypotheses to explain Goya's illness, including Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada disease, Cogan's syndrome, and syphilis, among others. Goya's transition from a celebrated court painter to a more introspective artist reflects not only his physical ailments but also the tumultuous historical period in Spain. This interdisciplinary approach, examining the interplay between health, personality, and art, offers a unique perspective on understanding the creative process and the impact of health on artistic output.
