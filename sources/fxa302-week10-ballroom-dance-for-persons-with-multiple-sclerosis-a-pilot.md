## Ballroom dance for persons with multiple sclerosis: a pilot feasibility study

The study evaluates the feasibility and exercise intensity of recreational ballroom dance for individuals with multiple sclerosis (MS). Seven participants with MS engaged in two-hour dance sessions weekly for six weeks, including dance types such as rumba, foxtrot, and waltz. The study also included a control group that did not participate in the dance program. Heart rate and ratings of perceived exertion (RPE) were monitored during dance sessions. The results indicated that ballroom dance provided a light to moderate exercise intensity for individuals with MS. Post-dance program assessments showed improvements in quality of life, cognitive function (PASAT), and the MS Functional Composite Score. The control group showed no changes. This pilot study suggests that ballroom dance is a feasible and enjoyable physical activity that can provide exercise benefits and improve quality of life and cognitive function for individuals with MS.
