## Music and Disability

Joseph N. Straus explores the intersection of music and disability through the lens of disability studies rather than the medical model. The chapter challenges the traditional medicalized view of disability by presenting music-making practices of individuals with autism, deafness, and mobility impairments as socially and culturally rich practices rather than deficits to be corrected. Straus discusses three types of disability-related music-making: autistic music-making, which emphasizes local coherence, private associations, and imitation; deaf music-making, which involves visual, tactile, and kinesthetic engagement; and mobility-inflected music-making, which shapes the perception of music as a continuous flow. The central argument is that disability should be understood as an aspect of human diversity, enabling unique forms of musical expression and appreciation, rather than a pathology to be cured.
