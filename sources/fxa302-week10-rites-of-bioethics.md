## Rites of Bioethics

Tod Chambers' chapter, 'Rites of Bioethics,' explores the role of ritual in modern medical ethics, particularly focusing on the informed consent process. Chambers argues that rituals, such as informed consent, have transformed from moral declarations to legalistic procedures, reflecting a shift from paternalism to patient autonomy in medical practice. The chapter discusses how the ritual of informed consent has lost its performative power to alter identity and social relations, with patients and healthcare professionals often viewing it as a legal formality rather than a meaningful ethical engagement. Chambers also references traditional Confucian philosophy to highlight the importance of ritual in shaping social ethics, suggesting that informed consent could potentially embody a new social ethic in the patient-physician relationship if understood as a ritual performance rather than a legal requirement.
