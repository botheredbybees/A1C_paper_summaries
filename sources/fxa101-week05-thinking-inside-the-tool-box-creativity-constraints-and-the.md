## Thinking Inside the Tool Box: Creativity, Constraints, and the Colossal Portraits of Chuck Close

Patricia D. Stokes explores the relationship between creativity and expertise through a problem-solving model that challenges common notions about the creative process. The article argues against the cliché that creativity requires thinking outside-the-box, suggesting instead that experts can only solve problems within the constraints of their existing knowledge (‘thinking inside the tool box’). A second cliché, which posits that creativity thrives without constraints, is also addressed; Stokes notes that while freedom from constraints might seem beneficial, it often leads experts to repeat past successes. To mitigate this issue, she proposes a model based on paired constraints—a concept where each constraint both limits and directs the problem-solving process. This model is illustrated through an analysis of how renowned painter Chuck Close uses specific constraints in his artistic practice to overcome creative barriers. The article’s central argument is that creativity can be enhanced by carefully selecting and managing constraints, rather than attempting to eliminate them entirely.
