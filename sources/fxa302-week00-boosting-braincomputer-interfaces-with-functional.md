## Boosting Brain–Computer Interfaces with Functional Electrical Stimulation: Potential Applications in People with Locked-In Syndrome

This review discusses the potential of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) combined with functional electrical stimulation (FES) for enhancing communication in people with locked-in syndrome (LIS), a condition characterized by severe whole-body paralysis but preserved consciousness. Current assistive communication technologies are limited for individuals with total LIS due to the lack of reliable residual movements. Non-implantable BCI systems, such as scalp EEG and fNIRS, have shown promise but vary in effectiveness across individuals. The review proposes that combining BCI with FES could enrich communication capabilities by restoring body and facial movements, allowing for the expression of non-verbal cues and potentially vocalization. Existing research on BCI-FES in stroke and spinal cord injury patients supports this approach, suggesting its potential to improve quality of life for LIS patients. Challenges include the need for further research on the feasibility and practicality of BCI-FES systems in LIS.
