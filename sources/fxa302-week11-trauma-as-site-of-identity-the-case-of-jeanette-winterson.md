## Trauma as Site of Identity: The Case of Jeanette Winterson and Frida Kahlo

Reina van der Wiel's article explores the concept of trauma as a foundational aspect of identity through the lens of Jeanette Winterson's work and Frida Kahlo's life. Trauma, often associated with a crisis of identity, paradoxically becomes the basis for self-identity in certain cases. Van der Wiel draws on Dominick LaCapra's notion of 'founding traumas' to argue that while such traumas may provide a basis for identity, they also pose questions about identity that are often left unaddressed. Winterson's use of first-person narration and autobiographical elements in her fiction, particularly in novels like 'Oranges Are Not the Only Fruit' and 'Weight,' exemplify this concept. The author contends that Winterson's recurrent engagement with her traumatic past, rather than being purely symptomatic of personal trauma, reflects a broader cultural trend of using trauma to construct a 'true' self. This trend, often called 'traumaculture,' emphasizes the continual reiteration of trauma to sustain a specific identity, rather than the resolution or working through of traumatic experiences. Van der Wiel's analysis highlights the complex relationship between trauma, self-narration, and identity formation in contemporary literature and art.
