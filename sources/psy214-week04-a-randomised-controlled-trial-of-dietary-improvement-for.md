## A randomised controlled trial of dietary improvement for adults with major depression (the ‘SMILES’ trial)

This research article presents a randomized controlled trial (RCT) called the SMILES trial, which aimed to investigate whether dietary improvement can be an effective treatment for major depressive episodes. The study involved a diet intervention group receiving seven individual nutritional consulting sessions over twelve weeks and a social support control group. Participants were assessed using the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS). Results indicated that the dietary support group showed significantly greater improvements in depression symptomatology compared to the control group, with remission rates also higher among those who received dietary intervention. The study concluded that dietary improvement may provide an efficacious and accessible treatment strategy for managing major depressive disorder, potentially extending benefits to common co-morbidities. This research highlights the importance of diet quality in mental health management and suggests a need for further exploration into nutritional interventions within therapeutic contexts.
