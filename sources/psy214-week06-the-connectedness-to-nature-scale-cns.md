## The Connectedness to Nature Scale (CNS)

The Connectedness to Nature Scale (CNS) is a measurement tool designed to assess the degree to which people feel a sense of connection and belonging within the natural world. Developed by Mayer and Frantz in 2004, this scale evaluates an individual's emotional and psychological relationship with nature as a personality trait. Key findings from research indicate that individuals who score higher on the CNS tend to exhibit more environmentally responsible behaviors and report higher levels of subjective well-being. The scale consists of a series of questions where respondents rate their agreement or disagreement on various statements related to feelings of unity, kinship, and understanding within nature. This tool is valuable in psychological studies focusing on environmental attitudes and human-nature relationships, particularly in contexts relating to stress management, self-care, and mindfulness practices.
