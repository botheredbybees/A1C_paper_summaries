## Earliest evidence of malignant breast cancer in Renaissance paintings

This article discusses the earliest pictorial representations of breast cancer in Renaissance paintings, specifically highlighting two works: 'The Night' by Michele di Rodolfo del Ghirlandaio and 'The Allegory of Fortitude' by Maso di San Friano. The authors propose that these paintings depict signs consistent with breast cancer, such as nipple retraction, bulges, and skin changes indicative of the disease. They contrast this evidence with previous assessments based on Michelangelo's statue, arguing that the painted details provide a clearer diagnosis due to color representation and additional anatomical features. The study also delves into historical medical practices surrounding breast cancer treatment during antiquity and the Renaissance, noting significant advancements in surgical techniques despite practical limitations such as lack of anesthesia and aseptic conditions. This work underscores the evolving understanding of breast cancer through visual art and its implications for early modern medicine.
