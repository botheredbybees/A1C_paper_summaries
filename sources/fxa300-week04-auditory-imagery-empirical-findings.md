## Auditory Imagery: Empirical Findings

Timothy L. Hubbard's paper 'Auditory Imagery: Empirical Findings' reviews the existing literature on auditory imagery, covering a wide array of topics including auditory features (pitch, timbre, loudness), complex auditory stimuli such as music and verbal content, and its relationship with perception and memory processes like detection, encoding, and recall. The review highlights how auditory imagery can significantly impact these areas, enhancing discrimination while potentially interfering with perceptual tasks. It also explores individual differences influencing auditory imagery, including musical ability and experience, psychopathology, and synesthesia. Hubbard concludes that auditory imagery preserves structural properties of stimuli, involves brain regions similar to those used in perception, includes both depictive and descriptive components, and functions as a mnemonic tool distinct from rehearsal. This research underscores the critical role of auditory imagery in understanding cognitive processes related to music, language, and memory.
