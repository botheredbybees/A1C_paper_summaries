## Psychological well-being in professional orchestral musicians in Australia: A descriptive population study

The study by Kenny, Driscoll, and Ackermann (2014) investigates the psychological well-being of professional orchestral musicians in Australia, focusing on anxiety, depression, and health behaviors. The research surveyed 377 musicians from eight full-time professional symphonic and pit orchestras in Australia, revealing significant gender and age differences in anxiety and depression levels. Female musicians exhibited higher trait anxiety, music performance anxiety, social anxiety, and depression than their male counterparts. Younger musicians under 30 years old reported higher levels of anxiety compared to older musicians, with younger female musicians experiencing the most severe music performance anxiety. The study identified a substantial percentage of musicians potentially meeting criteria for social phobia and post-traumatic stress disorder. Additionally, 32% of participants screened positive for depression, which was correlated with higher anxiety scores. The research highlights the need for targeted occupational health and safety policies and programs to address the mental health and well-being challenges faced by professional musicians.
