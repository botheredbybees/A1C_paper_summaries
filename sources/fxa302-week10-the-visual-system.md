## The Visual System

This chapter from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle explores the visual system's role in object recognition and localization. The visual system is organized into two broad classes of information processing: 'what' and 'where'. The 'what' pathway, localized in the temporal cortex, is responsible for visual object recognition, while the 'where' pathway, located in the parietal cortex, handles spatial localization. Postle outlines the hierarchical processing steps in the ventral visual processing stream, from primary visual cortex (V1) to the inferotemporal (IT) cortex, highlighting the distributed nature of object representation. A key finding is the unique processing of faces in the temporal cortex, suggesting an interface between perception and memory. The chapter also discusses the role of feedback in resolving visual illusions, emphasizing the disambiguation of conflicting bottom-up signals. This study is crucial for understanding the neural basis of visual cognition, which has significant implications for creative arts and health, particularly in how visual art can influence perception and memory.
