## Music and Rehabilitation: Neurological Approaches

The chapter 'Music and Rehabilitation: Neurological Approaches' by A. Blythe LaGasse and Michael H. Thaut explores the integration of music therapy with neurological research to enhance rehabilitation outcomes. Historically, music therapy was grounded in social science models focusing on emotional well-being and social interaction. However, recent advancements in neuroscience have led to a paradigm shift, emphasizing the therapeutic potential of music based on its impact on brain function. The chapter discusses how engaging with musical stimuli activates various regions of the brain, including motor areas, which can be harnessed for rehabilitation purposes. It highlights empirical evidence showing that rhythmic entrainment and motor synchronization facilitated by music can aid in recovery from neurological conditions such as Parkinson’s disease, stroke, and multiple sclerosis. Furthermore, it outlines the development of Neurologic Music Therapy (NMT) as a clinical model grounded in scientific research, illustrating how the understanding of music's role has evolved to encompass both its artistic value and its therapeutic applications in promoting health and well-being.
