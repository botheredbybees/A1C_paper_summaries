## A Systematic Review of Arts-Based Interventions Delivered to Children and Young People in Nature or Outdoor Spaces: Impact on Nature Connectedness, Health and Wellbeing

This systematic review synthesizes literature on arts-based interventions delivered to children and young people in nature or outdoor spaces, focusing on their impact on health and wellbeing. The study identifies a significant decrease in time spent by youth in natural environments over the past three decades, exacerbated further by the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite substantial evidence of positive health impacts from natural settings for adults, less research exists specifically examining this relationship for children and young people. The review aims to bridge the gap by exploring arts-based interventions that incorporate both nature exposure and creative activities designed for youth.

The methodology involves a systematic search across eight major databases and hand-searching of relevant journals and books. Studies focusing solely on nature or arts but not both are excluded, leading to only 11 studies being included in this review out of the initial 9,314 records identified. The interventions span various types of artistic media (visual art, music, drama, dance) and natural settings (forests, woodlands, beaches). The overall findings suggest that arts-in-nature can engage all children inclusively, increasing their awareness of environmental issues and promoting pro-environmental behaviors while potentially reducing eco-anxiety. However, the quality of quantitative studies included is noted as low or unclear, indicating a need for further research to strengthen evidence in this area.
