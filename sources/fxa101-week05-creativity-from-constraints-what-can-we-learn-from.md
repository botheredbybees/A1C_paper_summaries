## Creativity from Constraints: What can we learn from Motherwell? From Modrian? From Klee?

Patricia D. Stokes presents a model of creativity and problem-solving that centers on the concept of paired constraints, one precluding existing reliable solutions and another promoting novel ones. Through analysis of artists like Motherwell, Mondrian, and Klee, she illustrates how these constraints can drive innovation in art, particularly in shifting from realistic to abstract representations. The article emphasizes the importance of redefining goals and selecting task elements that facilitate creative expression, illustrating this through examples such as Motherwell's 'Elegy to the Spanish Republic' series. By examining Mondrian’s stylistic evolution towards abstraction, Stokes demonstrates how constraints can foster a continuous search for novel solutions within an artistic domain. The relevance to the field of creative arts and health lies in understanding the mechanisms that enable individuals to break free from conventional patterns and generate innovative ideas, applicable to various domains including healthcare innovation.
