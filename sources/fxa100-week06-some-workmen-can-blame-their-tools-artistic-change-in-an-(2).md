## Some workmen can blame their tools: artistic change in an individual with Alzheimer’s disease

This paper examines the impact of Alzheimer's disease on the artistic abilities of William Utermohlen, a renowned artist diagnosed with probable Alzheimer’s at age 61. The study presents a detailed neuropsychological assessment and case report that tracks changes in his cognitive function and drawing skills over time. Early signs of impairment included difficulties with memory, abstract reasoning, visuoperceptual and visuospatial abilities. These cognitive deficits translated into visual art where Utermohlen's self-portraits progressively displayed inaccuracies in spatial organization and facial feature representation. This case study highlights the correlation between neurological deterioration due to Alzheimer’s disease and a decline in an artist’s ability to execute precise and coherent visual representations, contributing valuable insights into the interplay of cognitive health and artistic expression.
