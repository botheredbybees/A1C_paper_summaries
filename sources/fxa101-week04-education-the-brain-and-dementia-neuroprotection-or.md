## Education, the brain and dementia: neuroprotection or compensation?

This paper explores the potential protective role of education in reducing dementia risk while examining brain pathology at death in elderly populations from three large, longitudinal studies (EClipSE). The study found that higher levels of education were associated with a lower incidence of clinical dementia and greater brain weight but did not influence neurodegenerative or vascular pathologies. Despite similar levels of neuropathological burden, individuals with more education had a reduced risk of developing dementia. This suggests that education may promote cognitive reserve rather than directly protect against the development of neurological diseases. The findings highlight the importance of understanding mechanisms behind functional protection in the presence of pathology for society and public health strategies.
