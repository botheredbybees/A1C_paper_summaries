## Investigating Musical Performance Anxiety among Music College Singing Students: A quantitative analysis

The study by Kokotsaki and Davidson (2003) investigates musical performance anxiety among singing students at the Guildhall School of Music. The research re-examines the issue of performance anxiety, particularly among second- and third-year vocal studies students during mid-year examinations. The study reveals that female students exhibit higher levels of anxiety than the general population and that there is a proportional relationship between trait and state anxiety. The findings confirm that more advanced musicians with higher levels of pre-performance anxiety tend to perform better under anxious conditions. Additionally, the study explores the influence of gender and experience on anxiety levels, concluding that experienced performers are better equipped to manage the negative effects of anxiety, thus potentially enhancing their performance quality.
