## The Singing Neanderthals: the Origins of Music, Language, Mind and Body

Steven Mithen's work, 'The Singing Neanderthals: the Origins of Music, Language, Mind and Body,' explores why humans are musical beings and how this capacity relates to our evolutionary history. The book argues that musicality is a fundamental part of being human and predates language in terms of evolutionary appearance. Mithen proposes a hypothesis suggesting that early human ancestors used a holistic protolanguage characterized by musical emotive expression, which he calls 'Hmmmmm.' This system integrated pitch, rhythm, and melody to communicate information and express emotions. The book synthesizes evidence from neuroscience, psychology, linguistics, musicology, and palaeoanthropology to argue that Neanderthals had a complex vocal communication system similar to but distinct from modern human language. Mithen's work contributes significantly to understanding the origins of both music and language in humans, offering insights into prehistoric social life and cognitive development.
