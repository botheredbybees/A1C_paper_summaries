## Continuum Model of Music and Therapy Within Music Therapy

The chapter explores the complexities of defining and practicing music therapy within mental health settings. It highlights that music therapy is a broad profession with diverse interventions and clinical objectives, making it challenging to succinctly describe. The text emphasizes that music therapists use music as a primary medium to address the unique needs of service users, often integrating verbal dialogue and other therapeutic techniques. The continuum model proposes varying degrees of music and therapy integration, from music-centered psychotherapy to verbal psychotherapy with music, illustrating how the balance between music and dialogue shifts depending on the client's needs and stage of treatment. Key questions arise about the optimal ratio of music to therapy and whether such a balance exists. Scholars and practitioners must navigate these nuances during their training and practice, adapting their approaches based on the client's clinical goals and personal preferences. This model is particularly relevant for those working with individuals who have mental health conditions and substance use disorders, as it underscores the importance of flexible and adaptable therapeutic strategies.
