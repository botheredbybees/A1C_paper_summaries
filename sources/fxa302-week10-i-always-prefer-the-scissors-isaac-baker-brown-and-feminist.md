## “I Always Prefer the Scissors”: Isaac Baker Brown and Feminist Histories of Medicine

This chapter examines the work of Victorian gynecologist Isaac Baker Brown, known for his controversial practice of clitoridectomy to treat women's 'nervous diseases.' It situates Brown's actions within the historical context of medical ethics, gender roles, and the burgeoning feminist movement. Brown's influential text, 'On the Curability of Certain Forms of Insanity, Epilepsy, Catalepsy, and Hysteria in Females,' detailed his surgical methods, which were met with significant backlash from his peers. The chapter explores the broader implications of Brown's work on medical ethics, the professionalization of gynecology, and the societal perceptions of women's bodies and sexuality in Victorian Britain. It argues that understanding Brown's context is crucial for examining the historical development of feminist theories on gender and sexuality.
