## The Relationship Between Character Strengths and Academic Achievement

This dissertation explores the relationship between character strengths and academic achievement, particularly focusing on the predictive power of character strengths on grade point average (GPA) compared to established predictors such as grit, self-control, and conscientiousness. The study is based on previous research by Boerma and Neill (2020) which identified these predictors of college students' self-reported GPA and academic satisfaction. The current work hypothesizes that character strengths like perseverance, judgment, self-regulation, and love of learning have incremental validity in predicting GPA, while strengths like hope, self-regulation, perseverance, prudence, and fairness have incremental validity in predicting academic satisfaction beyond life satisfaction. The study aims to investigate whether character strengths provide additional predictive value over these established predictors. Key findings suggest that certain character strengths, such as humility, curiosity, and prudence, independently predict academic outcomes, indicating their potential significance in academic performance. The research underscores the importance of character strengths in understanding and enhancing academic achievement, offering insights for educators and practitioners in developing interventions that foster these strengths.
