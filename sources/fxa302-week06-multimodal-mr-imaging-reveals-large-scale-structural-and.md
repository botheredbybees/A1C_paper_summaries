## Multimodal MR-imaging reveals large-scale structural and functional connectivity changes in profound early blindness

This study examines the neuroplastic changes in individuals who have experienced profound blindness from birth or early life stages, using multimodal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) techniques to analyze both structural and functional brain connectivity. The researchers found reductions in cortical volume and thickness within visual processing areas of the occipital and temporal cortices but observed increased cortical volumes in parietal regions. White matter analysis indicated patterns of increased and decreased connectivity, predominantly left-lateralized for language-related areas. Resting state functional MRI also revealed both increased and decreased functional connectivity differences between blind individuals and sighted controls. The findings highlight the complex nature of brain reorganization in the context of sensory deprivation, providing evidence of compensatory neural changes that support enhanced non-visual cognitive functions.
