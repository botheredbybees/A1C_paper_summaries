## What kind of Research is Research Through Design

Richard Herriott's paper critically examines the nature and distinction of 'research through design' (RtD) as a method in contrast to traditional scientific research methods. The central argument revolves around questioning whether RtD fundamentally differs from established methodologies and if such differentiation is beneficial or detrimental to its practice and recognition.

Herriott introduces Frayling's tripartite classification of art/design research—research into, through, and for—to frame the discussion on RtD. He then delves into the concept of a 'designerly way of knowing,' suggesting that this unique approach might justify the use of RtD over conventional methods.

The paper presents models of standard research in design alongside examples of RtD processes to compare their similarities and differences, challenging the assumption that they are fundamentally distinct. It also critiques the theory-building aspect of RtD as noted by Zimmerman et al., acknowledging its growing prevalence despite perceived weaknesses.

Herriott's analysis is relevant to creative arts and health fields as it addresses the validity and utility of practice-based research methods in generating knowledge applicable to these domains, emphasizing the potential for bridging theory and practical application.
