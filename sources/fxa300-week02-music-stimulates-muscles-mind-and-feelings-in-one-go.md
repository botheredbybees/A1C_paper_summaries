## Music stimulates muscles, mind, and feelings in one go

This opinion piece discusses the application of rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS) in gait training for Parkinson's disease (PD) patients. Rhythmic auditory stimulation involves using music with embedded metronome pulsations to enhance bodily functions such as movement and cognition, which is particularly effective for PD-related mobility issues. The article outlines several studies demonstrating the efficacy of RAS, including a 3-week home-based training protocol that showed significant improvements in velocity and step cadence compared to self-pacing training or no intervention. However, it also highlights the lack of sufficient evidence regarding the use of functionally optimized music for gait training in PD patients. The author suggests adjusting the tempo based on individual patient needs, especially for those with a tendency towards gait festination, recommending slower tempos that align closely with habitual walking cadence to enhance stride length and timing regulation. The piece emphasizes the broader therapeutic benefits of rhythmic stimulation beyond motor functions, including cognitive and emotional aspects, highlighting the importance of music's role in enhancing human well-being.
