## Frida Kahlo’s Bodily Geographies

The article examines Frida Kahlo’s work in the context of her personal and cultural experiences, particularly her approach to her body and its representation in her art. Kahlo’s fragile health and reproductive challenges are explored alongside her critical stance on traditional gender and maternal roles in Mexican society. Her artwork challenges the objectification of women and the restrictive maternal narratives through her exploration of bodily geographies, including representations of miscarriage, abortion, breastfeeding, and conception. Kahlo’s work is analyzed as a feminist critique of the male-dominated discourse on the female body, and her contributions are situated within the broader contexts of Mexican culture, Surrealism, and the history of feminism. The paper also discusses how Kahlo’s art penetrates the privileged domain of the female body, using medical imagery to disrupt socially constructed gender boundaries. This examination highlights Kahlo’s significance as a pioneering female artist and her enduring impact on feminist art and cultural studies.
