## Reflections in Art

This paper explores the visual perception of reflections in art, focusing on how artists depict reflective surfaces in paintings despite the lack of dynamic movement that would be expected in real-life mirrors. The authors analyze the rules of reflection from both curved and flat surfaces, discussing how deviations from physical optics can still convey a convincing reflective appearance. They highlight examples from natural scenes and art history, such as the use of highlights in paintings and the mimicry of reflective eyespots in animals. The study underscores the importance of symmetry and brightness in triggering the perception of a reflective surface, even when the depicted reflection does not move with changes in the viewer's perspective. The article also considers the implications of mirror recognition in higher-order cognition and suggests that long experience with reflective water surfaces may have contributed to our ability to interpret flat surface reflections.
