## Singing for adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

The Cochrane review by McNamara et al. (2017) examines the impact of singing as a therapeutic intervention for adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The review synthesizes evidence from randomized controlled trials to assess the effects of structured singing training on health-related quality of life and dyspnea in individuals with COPD. Singing is characterized as a complex physical activity that engages respiratory muscles and requires controlled airflow and large lung volumes. Given the progressive and chronic nature of COPD, which involves airflow obstruction, singing presents a potential non-pharmacological approach to enhancing health outcomes in affected individuals. The review identifies randomized controlled trials that involve structured and supervised singing training sessions over a minimum duration of four weeks. The central argument of the review is that singing can improve quality of life and alleviate dyspnea symptoms in COPD patients. Key findings indicate that singing may positively influence respiratory muscle strength, pulmonary function, and overall physical activity levels. The review emphasizes the relevance of singing as an intervention for COPD, potentially offering a creative and engaging approach to managing the condition. This is particularly significant in the context of creative arts and health, as it showcases the potential of musical activities to contribute to therapeutic outcomes.
