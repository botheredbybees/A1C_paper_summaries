## Concluding Remarks

This chapter by Adrian North and David Hargreaves concludes their book on the social and applied psychology of music, emphasizing the shift from the 1980s paradigm, which focused heavily on experimental cognitive psychology and classical music as high art, to a new paradigm that considers the social and everyday context of music. The authors argue that the previous paradigm failed to highlight the social, emotional, and financial value of music in people's daily lives, making it less effective in defending music's importance in society. They suggest that the new paradigm, emerging alongside the digital revolution and the dominance of pop music, addresses more relevant questions about music's role in contemporary culture, such as its influence on social behavior, health, and education. The chapter also highlights the historical relevance of these issues, noting that ancient philosophers like Plato and Aristotle addressed similar concerns, making the argument that the social and applied psychology of music is well-positioned to advocate for music's value in the 21st century.
