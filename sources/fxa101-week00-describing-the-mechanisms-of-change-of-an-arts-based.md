## Describing the Mechanisms of Change of an Arts-Based Intervention for Pregnant and Parenting Adolescents Experiencing Violence and Poor Mental Health in South Africa

This paper describes an arts-based intervention called Sisonke Ntwana for pregnant and parenting adolescents in South Africa experiencing violence and poor mental health. The study recruited 28 participants aged under 19, who had experienced intimate partner violence or non-partner rape. A six-session art therapy intervention was conducted using collage, body mapping, projective drawing, sandtray techniques, psychoeducation, mindfulness practice, and safe parenting skills. Feasibility and mechanisms of change were assessed through focus groups and interviews with participants (n=7) and endline interviews with the remaining participants (n=18), interventionists, and lived experience researchers. Results showed enhanced socio-emotional skills, increased self-expression, critical awareness, improved understanding of trauma impacts across generations, and some privacy concerns in group settings.
