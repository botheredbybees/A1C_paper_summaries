## The Effect of Implied Harmony, Contour and Musical Expertise on Judgments of Similarity of Familiar Melodies

The study by Schubert and Stevens (2006) explores how musical expertise and structural elements like contour and implied harmony influence judgments of similarity between familiar melodies. Participants, including novices and expert musicians, rated the similarity of nursery rhyme tunes with altered harmonic and pitch structures. The results indicate that both groups use melodic contour to assess similarity but that experts also consider implied harmony, suggesting a deeper understanding of musical structures. This research highlights the importance of structural depth in melodic perception and memory, with implications for music cognition theories and automated music retrieval systems.
