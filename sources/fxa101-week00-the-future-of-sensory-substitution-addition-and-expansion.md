## The future of sensory substitution, addition, and expansion via haptic devices

This article discusses the advancements and future potential of haptic devices in sensory substitution, expansion, and addition for individuals with sensory impairments or to enhance human sensory perception beyond natural capabilities. It explores how non-invasive haptic technology converts information from one sense into tactile sensations on the skin, facilitating alternative pathways for communication between the environment and the brain. Examples include vision-to-touch systems like Bach-y-Rita's dental chair device and the BrainPort tongue display unit, as well as sound-to-touch devices aiding deaf individuals to perceive auditory stimuli through vibrations on the body. The piece also delves into sensory expansion and addition scenarios where humans can detect forms of energy not traditionally perceivable, such as electromagnetic radiation or magnetic fields.
