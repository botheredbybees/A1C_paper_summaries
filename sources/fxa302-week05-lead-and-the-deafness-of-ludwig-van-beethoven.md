## Lead and the Deafness of Ludwig Van Beethoven

This study reexamines the cause of Ludwig van Beethoven's hearing loss, focusing on lead poisoning as a probable factor. The authors critically evaluate various hypotheses such as otosclerosis and autoimmune disease but find significant evidence against these causes. They note that otosclerosis lacks supporting anatomical findings from Beethoven’s autopsy, while autoimmune disease typically presents with rapid progression over weeks or months rather than years. Additionally, there is no evidence of mercury in his hair and bones, ruling out syphilis as a cause. Notably, high levels of lead were found deep within Beethoven's bone samples, indicating prolonged exposure to the metal. This chronic exposure aligns with Beethoven’s slow progressive hearing loss and sensory symptoms, rather than motor neuropathy from acute poisoning. The authors conclude that repeated consumption of contaminated wine, likely containing lead, is a plausible explanation for his deafness.
