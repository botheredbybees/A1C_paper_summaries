## Emerging Maker: Alice Walton

In this article, ceramic artist Alice Walton discusses her creative process and inspirations for her sculptural and abstract forms. Walton focuses on creating complex and intense surface textures in her work, aiming to provoke intrigue and draw viewers in to investigate her pieces closely. She explores the idea of transforming everyday, mundane objects into something extraordinary through her artistic process. Walton’s work often begins with her observations of objects and materials in her daily life, which she then combines with her memories and imagination to create abstract landscapes. She works with colored clay, using a variety of handbuilding techniques such as press molding, slabbing, and coiling, to create intricate and textured sculptures. Walton emphasizes the meditative process of working with clay, which allows her to make immediate marks and manipulate the material as she sees fit. Her inspiration includes the bold use of color in the architecture of Rajasthan, India, and the work of Danish ceramic artist Anders Ruhwald and American artist Agnes Martin. Walton values the support she received during her studies, particularly from the Eduardo Paolozzi Travel Grant and the Queen Elizabeth Scholarship Trust, which helped her gain confidence in her work and career. She aims to continue developing her unique artistic voice, exploring new color palettes and decoration techniques, and considering how her work might change with different surroundings.
