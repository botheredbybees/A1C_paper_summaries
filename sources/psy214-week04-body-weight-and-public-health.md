## Body weight and public health

The document 'Body Weight and Public Health' explores the multifaceted impacts of body weight on various aspects of public health, focusing particularly on how excessive or inadequate body weight can lead to a range of chronic conditions. According to the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), common diseases associated with body weight include cardiovascular disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, certain cancers, sleep apnoea, asthma, infertility, pregnancy complications, arthritis, and cognitive decline. Additionally, it highlights that poor body weight can contribute to mental health concerns such as oxidative stress and inflammation.

The document also delves into the methods for assessing body weight, primarily using the Body Mass Index (BMI) which involves calculating a person's weight in kilograms divided by their height in meters squared. A healthy BMI range is generally defined between 18.5 to 25. The report emphasizes that while BMI is easy and inexpensive to measure and correlates reliably with chronic health risks for most individuals, it may not be accurate for specific groups such as athletes or elderly people due to factors like body composition and age differences.

Overall, the document underscores the importance of understanding and addressing public health concerns related to body weight in order to improve overall community wellness.
