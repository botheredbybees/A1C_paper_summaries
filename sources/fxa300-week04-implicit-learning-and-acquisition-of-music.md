## Implicit Learning and Acquisition of Music

This article explores the role of implicit learning in music acquisition and perception, highlighting its importance across various musical contexts and skill levels. The authors argue that nonmusicians and musicians alike can acquire complex melodic, harmonic, and structural features implicitly from mere exposure to music. Despite growing evidence supporting this process, questions remain regarding cross-cultural validity and the complexity of structures learned implicitly. The review discusses how implicit learning underpins long-term acquisition of musical knowledge, influencing parsing processes, performance, production, and interaction between musicians. It underscores that while many aspects of musical competence are shaped by tacit knowledge acquired through exposure, further research is needed to fully understand the extent of implicit learning in music cognition.
