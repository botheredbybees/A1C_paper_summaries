## Promoting Reflection in Learning: a Model

The chapter 'Promoting Reflection in Learning: a Model' by Boud, Keogh, and Walker focuses on the role of reflection in learning experiences. The authors propose a model that includes the initial experience, reflective processes, and outcomes of reflection. They define reflection as a form of response to experience, which involves intellectual and affective activities that lead to new understandings and appreciations. The model acknowledges that reflection can be prompted by various factors, including inner discomfort, dissatisfaction with one's situation, or positive experiences. The authors emphasize the importance of conscious reflection for effective learning and highlight the need for teachers and learners to understand and facilitate reflective processes. This model contributes to the understanding of experiential learning and its relevance to creative arts and health by promoting self-awareness and personal growth through reflection.
