## Conveying the Experience of Alzheimer’s Disease through Art: The Later Paintings of William Utermohlen

The chapter explores the later paintings of William Utermohlen, a British artist diagnosed with Alzheimer's Disease, as a narrative of his subjective experience of the illness. Utermohlen's works from 1990 to 2000 serve as a clinical journal of the progression of his cognitive disorders, including memory and concentration issues, disorganization of temporal and spatial mental representation, and difficulty in recognizing objects and functions. These symptoms are depicted through changes in his artistic technique, reflecting a gradual dimming of clear thought and judgement. The paintings also convey Utermohlen's emotional experience of isolation, loss of self-control, and the struggle to maintain a sense of identity. The artist uses his art as an attempt at self-healing and a way to preserve his world, depicting his mental and sensorial condition through visual means rather than verbal communication. The chapter highlights the unique value of Utermohlen's art in providing a subjective and clinical perspective on Alzheimer's Disease, offering insights into the artist's awareness of his condition and the creative process as a form of resistance against psychic disorganization.
