## Using Space and Time to Encode Vibrotactile Information: Toward an Estimate of the Skin’s Achievable Throughput

This study investigates the capacity of human skin to transmit high-throughput information using vibrotactile encoding methods, focusing on spatial and temporal coding techniques. The research explores how patterns can be encoded in both space and time, or through intensity modulation, to maximize information throughput. In an experiment conducted on the lower back, participants identified vibrotactile patterns presented by arrays of vibratory motors. Results indicate that spatiotemporal sweeps yield better performance than spatially encoded patterns alone, and determine the minimum distance (6 cm) necessary for resolving two distinct tactile patterns. The study concludes that to achieve high-throughput sensory substitution, it is crucial to utilize multiple dimensions of information encoding, overcoming limitations imposed by skin's resolution.
