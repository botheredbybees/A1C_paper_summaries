## Changing the Conceptualization of Stress in Social Anxiety Disorder: Affective and Physiological Consequences

This research explores the affective and physiological reactions to social evaluation in individuals with social anxiety disorder (SAD) and the efficacy of an arousal reappraisal intervention. The study reveals a disconnect between subjective ratings of anxiety and negative affect and physiological responses, indicating that socially anxious individuals do not necessarily exhibit different physiological reactions compared to nonanxious controls during stressful public speaking tasks. However, when socially anxious and nonanxious participants were instructed to reframe stress arousal positively, both groups showed reduced attentional bias and improved physiological functioning, such as decreased vasoconstriction and increased cardiac efficiency. This suggests that reappraising stress arousal can lead to more adaptive physiological responses, potentially improving outcomes for individuals with SAD. The findings highlight the importance of cognitive appraisals in the context of stress and suggest that reappraisal interventions may be beneficial in managing SAD, emphasizing the need for further research to explore the broader implications of stress reappraisal in psychological disorders.
