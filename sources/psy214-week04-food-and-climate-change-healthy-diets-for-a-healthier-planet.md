## Food and Climate Change: Healthy diets for a healthier planet

The document discusses the significant impact of food production, distribution, and consumption on climate change. It highlights that approximately one-third of human-caused greenhouse gas emissions are related to food systems, with agriculture and land use contributing most significantly through methane from livestock digestion, nitrous oxide from fertilizers, and carbon dioxide from deforestation for agricultural expansion. Animal-based foods, particularly red meat, dairy products, and farmed shrimp, have the highest greenhouse gas emissions due to their resource-intensive production processes and environmental impacts such as forest clearance and mangrove destruction.

In contrast, plant-based diets are associated with lower environmental footprints because they require less energy, land, water, and generate fewer greenhouse gases. The report emphasizes that shifting towards more sustainable food systems through reduced consumption of animal products, increased use of alternative proteins like insect-based foods or cell-cultured meat, improved livestock feeding practices, better agricultural management, and significant reduction in food waste could greatly mitigate climate impacts. It encourages individuals to adopt healthier eating habits, reduce food waste, compost organic materials, and support sustainable agriculture through conscious consumer choices.
