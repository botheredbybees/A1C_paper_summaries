## Psychodynamic Psychology – Introduction to Psychology – 1st Canadian Edition

The chapter on psychodynamic psychology in the Introduction to Psychology – 1st Canadian Edition explores the foundational theories and concepts developed by Sigmund Freud and later expanded upon by Carl Jung. Freud's theory posits that psychological forces underlie human behavior, with a significant portion of mental life being unconscious and influenced by past experiences, especially from early childhood. Consciousness is divided into three levels: conscious (aware of current thoughts and surroundings), preconscious (memories easily recalled), and unconscious (repressed or unacknowledged thoughts). Freud's model also includes the id, ego, and superego as components of personality. Jung introduced concepts like archetypes, collective unconscious, and individuation, emphasizing integration over conflict resolution. Archetypes are universal symbols present in the collective unconscious, such as the anima and animus representing feminine and masculine aspects of personality. The process of individuation involves integrating conscious and unconscious elements to achieve wholeness and personal fulfillment. These theories remain influential in understanding human behavior, particularly in therapeutic contexts like psychoanalysis, where unconscious conflicts are explored through talk therapy.
