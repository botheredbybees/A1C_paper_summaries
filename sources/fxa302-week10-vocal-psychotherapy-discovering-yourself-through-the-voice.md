## VOCAL PSYCHOTHERAPY: Discovering Yourself through the Voice

Diane Austin's chapter on vocal psychotherapy emphasizes the therapeutic potential of the human voice in music therapy. The chapter begins by highlighting the intimate connection between voice, body, and emotions, explaining how singing can facilitate emotional expression and integration. It delves into the physiological and psychological benefits of deep breathing and vocalization, particularly in addressing trauma and neglect. Austin discusses the critical role of the voice in early parent-child bonding, underscoring the importance of vocal interactions for emotional and psychological development. The chapter also explores how the choice of songs in therapy sessions can reveal clients' emotional states and unresolved issues. Austin provides examples illustrating how songs can act as transitional objects, offering a sense of security and continuity. Through the exploration of voice, breath, and song, the chapter underscores the voice's role in healing and self-discovery within the therapeutic context.
