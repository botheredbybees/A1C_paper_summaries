## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience - Chapter 8: WHERE DOES SENSATION END? WHERE DOES PERCEPTION BEGIN?

This excerpt from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' delves into the complexities of distinguishing between sensation and perception in the visual system. The text discusses how visual information is processed from the retina through various stages to the primary visual cortex (V1) and beyond. It highlights that while damage to lower stages of this pathway results in blindness, higher-level processing plays a crucial role in perception by influencing lower-level sensory processing through feedback loops. The concept of predictive coding is introduced as a fundamental principle governing brain function, suggesting that the brain constantly updates a model of future events based on prior experiences and current sensory inputs. This principle is illustrated through examples that demonstrate how higher cortical areas can control the delivery of sensory information by modulating thalamic activity. The excerpt also touches on the integration of predictive coding with broader theories in theoretical neuroscience, such as dynamical systems and Bayesian statistics. The text underscores the interdisciplinary nature of cognitive neuroscience, bridging specific sensory processing with overarching theoretical frameworks, thereby emphasizing the interconnectedness of brain functions and the continuous updating of predictions to optimize perception and behavior.
