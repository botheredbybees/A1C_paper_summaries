## Community art therapy (CAT): learning from art therapy graduate students’ perceptions

The article explores the transition of art therapy students from clinical to community models through their experiences in Community Art Therapy (CAT) projects during internships. The study, based on qualitative analysis, highlights five main themes: perceptions of CAT compared to clinical settings, specific roles of community art therapists, the role of art, a sense of belonging, and the power of group dynamics. It emphasizes that community art therapy fosters co-creation of knowledge through metaphorical expressions of identity and belonging. Students reported experiencing a continuous dialectic between intervention and personal involvement. The article concludes that CAT should be taught as a distinct topic in art therapy programs to better prepare future practitioners.
