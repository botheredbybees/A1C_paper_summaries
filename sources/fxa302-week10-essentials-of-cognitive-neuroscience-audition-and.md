## Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience - Audition and Somatosensation

This chapter from 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle delves into the key themes of auditory perception and somatosensation. The auditory perception section details the intricate mechanical processes in the inner ear, including spectral decomposition by the basilar membrane and the neural transduction of sound waves into electrical signals. It also covers the auditory brainstem's role in generating auditory evoked potentials (AEPs) and how event-related potentials (ERPs) derived from EEG can measure neural events. The chapter discusses the organization of the primary auditory cortex, which contains a tonotopic map. The somatosensation section explores the diverse receptors responsible for detecting mechanical, thermal, and painful stimuli on the skin. It highlights the somatotopic maps within the somatosensory cortex (S1) and the insights gained from optogenetic manipulation and electrophysiology studies. The chapter also covers somatosensory plasticity, particularly in the context of learning and neurological disorders such as phantom limb and phantom pain. Postle acknowledges the underrepresentation of non-visual sensory systems in cognitive neuroscience texts but provides a comprehensive overview of these critical systems.
