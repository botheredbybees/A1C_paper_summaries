## The Genesis of a Tune in the Mind: An Interview Study About Novel Involuntary Musical Imagery Repetition

This study explores the phenomenon of novel involuntary musical imagery repetition (IMIR), focusing on how it relates to creativity among composers. The research involves semistructured interviews with six composers who discuss their experiences with novel IMIR, which are defined as spontaneously recurring but unfamiliar musical ideas. Through grounded theory analysis, two models emerged: one detailing the internal and external features of novel IMIR, including its relationship to experience and emotional valence; and another outlining methods for translating internal imagery into composed music. The study reveals that such imagery is more likely when individuals are in specific mental or physical states and suggests a link between spontaneous cognition and creativity, particularly in terms of insight moments. This research contributes to the understanding of musical creativity by highlighting the role of involuntary musical imagery in compositional processes.
