## An ALE Meta-Analytic Review of Top-Down and Bottom-Up Processing of Music in the Brain

This paper presents a comprehensive meta-analysis of neuroimaging studies focusing on the processing of music in the human brain, using activation likelihood estimation (ALE) methodology to integrate data from various studies. The research underscores that music is an excellent model for understanding how the brain integrates top-down and bottom-up information during complex multi-modal activities such as perception, production, and imagery. Findings reveal distinct neural mechanisms engaged in these processes, with auditory cortices and sensorimotor cortices playing key roles in both perception and production of music, whereas parietal regions are more involved in music imagery. This study highlights the brain's adaptive capabilities in processing similar information through different structures depending on whether it is sourced from environmental interaction (bottom-up) or internally generated content (top-down). The insights gained are relevant for creative arts and health studies as they provide a deeper understanding of neural processes underlying multi-modal human activities.
