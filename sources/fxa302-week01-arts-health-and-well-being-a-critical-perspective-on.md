## Arts, Health and Well-Being: A Critical Perspective on Research, Policy and Practice

Norma Daykin’s book 'Arts, Health and Well-Being: A Critical Perspective on Research, Policy and Practice' critically examines the intersection between arts and health domains, highlighting their potential to address societal challenges like demographic changes and social inequalities. The text emphasizes that combining arts with healthcare is not merely a scientific endeavor but one shaped by political governance, regulation, and social action. Daykin argues for understanding the production and reception of artwork as socially embedded processes influenced by hierarchies such as class, gender, ethnicity, caste, and sexual identity. She explores how artistic knowledge and practice are also socially and politically contextualized.

The book draws on social science models to frame arts and health initiatives as a social movement, addressing boundary work and the role of boundary objects in this interdisciplinary field. It highlights the limitations of scientific medicine, which has led to an interest in more holistic approaches to healthcare. Daykin argues that less than 10% of what affects our well-being comes from access to medical care, necessitating shifts towards preventive health measures.

Key challenges include bridging separate histories and organizational practices between arts and health sectors while fostering a shared vision for their integration. The book is valuable for students and practitioners in sociology, psychology, social work, nursing, psychiatry, creative and performing arts, public health, policymakers, and others involved in these fields.
