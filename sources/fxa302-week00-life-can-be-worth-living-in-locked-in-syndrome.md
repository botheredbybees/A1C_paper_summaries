## Life can be worth living in locked-in syndrome

This paper explores the quality of life (QoL) and psychosocial adjustment in patients with locked-in syndrome (LIS), a condition characterized by awareness and consciousness but almost complete physical immobility and loss of verbal communication. The study challenges the assumption that severe motor disability invariably leads to poor QoL and depression, presenting evidence that despite extreme motor impairment, many LIS patients maintain a good QoL. The research highlights the importance of social support, problem-oriented coping strategies, and emotional coping mechanisms in psychological adjustment. It also emphasizes the role of alternative communication devices in enhancing QoL and social participation, thereby refuting the notion that LIS patients invariably wish for euthanasia or end-of-life decisions.
