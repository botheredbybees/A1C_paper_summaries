## Mental Illness, Music and Literature: Perspectives on Health and Wellbeing

This lesson explores the interplay between mental health issues and artistic expression through the case studies of Robert Schumann and Sylvia Plath. Schumann, a 19th-century composer, faced physical and mental health challenges, including focal dystonia and mental decline. His career shift from pianist to composer due to his condition is discussed, along with his final work, the _Geistervariationen_, composed under hallucinatory conditions. Schumann's legacy, including the influence of his wife Clara and friend Johannes Brahms, is examined. Plath's case study delves into her early success, depression, and confessional writing style, culminating in the novel _The Bell Jar_ and the poetry collection _Ariel_. The lesson highlights how both artists' works reflect their mental health struggles and contributes to discussions in the medical humanities.
