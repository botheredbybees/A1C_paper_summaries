## Breast Cancer in a Renaissance Book of the Dead

This article explores the historical context of breast cancer in Renaissance society through an analysis of convent necrologies, which are records detailing the lives and deaths of nuns. Emma Nicholls highlights a specific case from 1612, documenting the illness and treatment of Sister Humiltà at Santa Maria del Portico convent in Florence, Italy. The detailed account reveals the extent to which this nun suffered from what was likely a breast tumor or ulceration for over nine years. This case study not only sheds light on medical practices but also illustrates the societal role of nuns as healthcare providers during the Renaissance period. Additionally, the article discusses retrospective diagnoses of breast cancer in artworks by Renaissance artists, highlighting the collaborative efforts between art historians and medical specialists to interpret historical evidence accurately. Nicholls emphasizes the importance of cross-disciplinary research for a deeper understanding of historical health issues.
