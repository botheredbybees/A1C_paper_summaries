## Francisco José de Goya y Lucientes, 1746–1828

Francisco José de Goya y Lucientes (1746-1828) was a pioneering Spanish artist renowned for his unflinching portrayal of human nature and social critique. Goya's work, including his etchings and paintings, delves into the extremes of human behavior, depicting violence, political upheaval, and the horrors of war. His etching 'The Dream/Sleep of Reason Brings Forth Monsters' (1797) exemplifies his fascination with the human psyche and the irrational aspects of life. Goya's depictions of sensuality in his 'Clothed Maja' and 'Naked Maja' series challenged the norms of the time, leading to accusations of obscenity by the Inquisition. Despite his radicalism, Goya was a court painter and an unflinching portraitist of the Spanish aristocracy. After contracting a serious illness in 1792, which left him deaf, his work became increasingly grim and focused on the dark realities of life and human suffering. Goya's exploration of the human condition, from the extremes of passion to the nightmares of unreason, is relevant to creative arts and health, offering insights into the psychological and social impacts of trauma and the transformative power of artistic expression.
