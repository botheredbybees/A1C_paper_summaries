## Rhythm in disguise: why singing may not hold the key to recovery from aphasia

This study investigates the role of melody and rhythm in speech recovery for non-fluent aphasia patients, questioning whether singing is crucial for rehabilitation. The research involved testing 17 non-fluent aphasics by altering lyrics and controlling various factors such as vocal frequency variability and pitch accuracy. Contrary to previous beliefs, the findings suggest that singing may not be decisive for improving speech production in these patients. Instead, rhythm appears to play a critical role, particularly for those with basal ganglia lesions, which accounted for over 50% of the variance related to rhythmicity. The study also indicates that the use of familiar lyrics and motor automaticity are more influential than melody in aiding speech recovery among non-fluent aphasia patients.
