## Face Pareidolia Recruits Mechanisms for Detecting Human Social Attention

This paper explores the phenomenon of face pareidolia, where individuals perceive faces in non-human objects. The authors hypothesize that this perception involves activation of visual mechanisms specialized for detecting social cues from human faces rather than a mere cognitive association. They focus on sensory adaptation to gaze direction and find that exposure to face-like objects with specific directional attributes (e.g., leftward gaze) causes subsequent perceived changes in the gaze direction of actual human faces, indicating shared neural mechanisms between pareidolia and genuine face processing. The study employs both behavioral and neuroimaging techniques, suggesting a deep interplay between visual perception and social cognition.
