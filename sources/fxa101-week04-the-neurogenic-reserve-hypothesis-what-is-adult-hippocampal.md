## The neurogenic reserve hypothesis: What is adult hippocampal neurogenesis good for?

In this article, Gerd Kempermann proposes the neurogenic reserve hypothesis to explain the functional benefits of adult hippocampal neurogenesis beyond immediate computational advantages. He suggests that activity-dependent neurogenesis provides a reserve capacity for sustained cellular plasticity throughout life, enabling flexibility and adaptability in learning and memory processes. The author highlights the role of new neurons in mitigating catastrophic interference, encoding temporal and contextual information, and supporting emotional behavior. Specifically, adult hippocampal neurogenesis is thought to optimize structural connectivity by counteracting network bottlenecks and enhancing sparse coding for temporal resolution. Additionally, Kempermann discusses the potential influence of genetic variability on these processes. Overall, this hypothesis underscores the importance of maintaining neurogenic reserves in the face of neurodegenerative diseases and aging.
