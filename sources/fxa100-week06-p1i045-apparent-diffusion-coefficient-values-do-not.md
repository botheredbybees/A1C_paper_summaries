## P.1.i.045 Apparent diffusion coefficient values do not correlate with the duration of the disease in patients with multiple sclerosis treated with interferon beta

This study investigates whether there is a correlation between apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values within normal-appearing white matter (NAWM) and normal-appearing grey matter (NAGM), and the duration of disease in patients with multiple sclerosis (MS) treated with interferon beta (IFN-Beta). The research aims to assess whether ADC values can serve as a biomarker for monitoring disease progression or treatment effectiveness over time. Despite MS being characterized by demyelination and axonal degeneration, which are detectable through advanced imaging techniques like diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), the study found no significant correlation between ADC values and disease duration in patients undergoing IFN-Beta therapy. The findings suggest that while ADC changes may reflect tissue pathology, they do not reliably indicate long-term disease progression or treatment efficacy.
