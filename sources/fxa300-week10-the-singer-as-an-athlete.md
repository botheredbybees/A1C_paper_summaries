## The Singer as an Athlete

The chapter 'The Singer as an Athlete' in 'The Owner's Manual to the Voice: A Guide for Singers and Other Professional Voice Users' by Rachael Gates and others explores the physiological demands of singing on the human body. It emphasizes that singing is an anaerobic exercise, which involves intense muscular activity but does not rely on sustained oxygen intake like aerobic exercises. The text highlights that singing places significant demands on the muscles in the head, neck, and torso, leading to the development of fine muscle control and efficient breath management. It discusses various breathing techniques, such as combination breathing for classical singing and costal breathing for other styles, and explains how these techniques support different vocal styles. The chapter also touches on the concept of vital capacity and how it can be expanded through controlled breathing exercises, enhancing the singer's endurance and vocal projection. The relevance of this information to creative arts and health lies in understanding the physical demands of singing and how proper technique can enhance vocal health and performance.
