## The Effects of Music on the Brain – video transcript

This video transcript explores how Dr. Oliver Sacks's brain reacts to different pieces of classical music, specifically Bach and Beethoven compositions. The research conducted at Columbia University includes a device that measures emotional responses while neuroimaging captures the brain activity during listening sessions. Dr. Sacks expresses strong feelings towards Bach’s music, particularly noting the harmonic modulations and soprano parts. Interestingly, the brain scan reveals significant activation in areas crucial for appreciating musical complexity, especially the amygdala when hearing Bach compared to Beethoven. Despite Dr. Sacks's uncertainty in differentiating between the two composers during the test, his brain activity clearly distinguished Bach from Beethoven, highlighting the brain’s ability to recognize and process nuanced differences in music even unconsciously.
