## Exposure Effects on Music Preference and Recognition

The study explores the impact of repeated exposure to melodies on subsequent preference and recognition, focusing on how familiarity influences liking and identification. In three experiments, participants were exposed to familiar and unfamiliar melodies, with results indicating that repetition increases liking for unfamiliar melodies and aids in recognizing familiar ones. The effects of repetition on recognition were found to persist longer than on preference. The research also highlights that changes in melody timbre and study tasks have a significant influence on recognition but less impact on liking. The findings suggest that the mere exposure effect on liking and recognition may be linked to implicit and explicit forms of memory, respectively, challenging some traditional views on the psychological mechanisms behind these phenomena. The study contributes to understanding how repeated exposure to music affects preferences and memory, with implications for the creative arts and health by shedding light on how music appreciation and memory can be influenced.
