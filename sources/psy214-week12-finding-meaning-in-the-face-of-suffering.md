## Finding Meaning in the Face of Suffering

The article 'Finding Meaning in the Face of Suffering' discusses Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy (MCP), a structured intervention designed to help patients with advanced cancer find meaning and cope with existential distress. The therapy is grounded in the work of Viktor Frankl and Irvin Yalom, emphasizing concepts such as the possibility of finding meaning in life, the will to find meaning, freedom of will, and various sources of meaning. MCP aims to enhance patients' sense of meaning and purpose, thereby improving their quality of life and reducing psychological distress. The therapy includes individual and group formats, each comprising sessions focused on exploring existential concepts and personal sources of meaning. Research has shown MCP to be effective in enhancing spiritual well-being, reducing anxiety and hopelessness, and decreasing the desire for hastened death in patients with advanced cancer. The therapy is recommended for patients with moderate to severe distress and should be conducted in the context of a supportive therapeutic relationship. MCP highlights the transformative potential of suffering and encourages patients to develop a coherent narrative around their life's meaning, fostering a sense of legacy and personal growth.
