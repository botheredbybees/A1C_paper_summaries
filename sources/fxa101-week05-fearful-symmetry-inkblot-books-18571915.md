## Fearful Symmetry: Inkblot Books (1857–1915)

The article 'Fearful Symmetry' discusses the historical emergence of inkblot books from the mid-nineteenth to early-twentieth centuries, exploring their artistic and psychological dimensions. Justinus Kerner, a German doctor-poet, pioneered these works with his book ‘Kleksographien’ in 1857, seeing blot patterns as revelations of an invisible world rather than reflections of personal psyche. In contrast, Ruth McEnery Stuart and Albert Bigelow Paine's 'Gobolinks' adopted a more lighthearted tone, encouraging children to interpret inkblots creatively. The article also mentions how these books relate to broader themes in the era’s culture, such as new media technologies intersecting with occult practices, exemplified by Cecil Henland's 'The Ghosts of My Friends'. Ultimately, these inkblot books highlight the human tendency towards pareidolia and its role in both art creation and religious experience.
