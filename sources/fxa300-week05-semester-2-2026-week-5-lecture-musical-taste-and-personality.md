## Semester 2 2026 Week 5 Lecture: Musical Taste and Personality

This lecture from the University of Tasmania's FXA300 Music, Mind and Body course explores the relationship between musical taste and personality. It begins with a recap of how music is processed in the brain through bottom-up and top-down mechanisms, involving auditory cortices and other neurological regions. The lecture then delves into how personality traits influence musical preferences based on the Big Five Personality Traits model (OCEAN). Research by Rentfrow et al. (2011) identifies that emotional and aural qualities in music are key determinants of preference across genres, while Greenberg et al. (2022) examines correlations between personality traits and musical preferences across 53 countries. The lecture also covers how time-of-day and seasonal factors affect the intensity of preferred music, as shown by Park et al. (2019), and highlights the significant role age plays in shaping one's musical taste through developmental milestones like the 'reminiscence bump'. This discussion underscores the intricate interplay between neurological processing, personality traits, cultural contexts, and temporal factors in determining individual preferences for music.
