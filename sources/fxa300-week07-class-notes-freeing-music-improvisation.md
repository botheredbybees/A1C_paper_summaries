## Class Notes: Freeing Music improvisation

In this class session led by Alison Young, the concept of musical improvisation is explored through live performances by Clea, Margaret, and Tami, who are playing recorder, violin, and harpsichord, respectively. The session begins with a comparison between a composed piece by Telemann and an improvised rendition of the same piece, highlighting the added color and expression that improvisation brings. Clea demonstrates the use of runs to add complexity between intervals, while Margaret showcases violin-specific techniques like noodles and trills. Tami, playing the harpsichord, illustrates the role of the bass line and how harmonies and runs can be improvised based on the bass line. The group performs Telemann's Trio Sonata for Recorder, Violin and Continuo in A minor, demonstrating how improvisation can enhance the expressiveness and dynamism of a composition. This session underscores the historical significance of improvisation in classical music and its impact on the creative arts and health by fostering musical expression and collaborative performance.
