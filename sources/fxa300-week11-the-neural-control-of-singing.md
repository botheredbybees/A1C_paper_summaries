## The neural control of singing

Jean Mary Zarate’s review article examines the neural control of singing, highlighting the unique opportunity it offers for studying music performance due to the integration of sensory-motor processes within the body. The article discusses sensory-motor control observed from the vocal tract and outlines the complex neural networks governing vocal motor control and sensory feedback processing. Zarate explores how sensory feedback is integrated with vocal motor control through somatosensory and auditory mechanisms, presenting recent fMRI research on these processes. Additionally, the article delves into neuroplasticity resulting from various lengths and types of training, the phenomenon of vocal amusia, and singing performance in individuals with congenital amusia. It also considers the auditory-motor control network for singing in relation to dual-stream models of auditory processing in music and speech, suggesting refinements to both theoretical models and the singing network itself. The work underscores the importance of singing as a means to understand the intricate relationship between brain and behavior in music performance.
