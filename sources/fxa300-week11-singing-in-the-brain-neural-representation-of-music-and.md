## Singing in the brain: Neural representation of music and voice as revealed by fMRI

This research article explores the neural representation of music and voice in the brain through functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) scans. The study aimed to determine if specific regions in the anterior temporal lobe respond more strongly to music than to other auditory stimuli, including spoken voice. By testing 24 healthy young adults with high-resolution fMRI, the researchers observed that music, whether vocal or instrumental, preferentially engages regions in the superior temporal gyrus (STG), particularly the anterior planum polare, bilaterally. In contrast, human voice, whether spoken or sung, activated more strongly a large area along the superior temporal sulcus. The findings were consistent with the use of a “silent” sparse acquisition sequence, minimizing the influence of scanner noise. The study concludes that activity in music-preferred regions cannot be accounted for by basic acoustic parameters, suggesting these areas integrate a combination of acoustic attributes in a non-linear fashion to result in the perceived musicality of the stimuli.
