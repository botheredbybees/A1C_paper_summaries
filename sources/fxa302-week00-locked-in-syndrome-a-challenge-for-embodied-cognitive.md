## Locked-in syndrome: a challenge for embodied cognitive science

This paper explores the implications of Locked-in Syndrome (LIS) for embodied approaches in cognitive science. LIS is characterized by severe global paralysis with preserved cognitive function. The authors argue that LIS challenges traditional embodied cognitive science by questioning the necessity of bodily movement for cognition. They evaluate three perspectives—sensorimotor approach, extended cognition, and enactivism—finding that enactivism best addresses the LIS challenge by emphasizing the body's role in autonomous agency, not just movement. Enactivism conceptualizes cognition as sense-making within embodied autonomy, offering a robust framework for understanding cognition in LIS.
