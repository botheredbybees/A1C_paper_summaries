## Self-selected ‘‘favourite’’ stimulative and sedative music listening – how does familiar and preferred music listening affect the body?

This study examines the psychophysiological effects of self-selected, favorite music on both aroused and relaxed emotions in healthy individuals. Thirty-eight participants were asked to select their preferred stimulative (uplifting) and sedative (relaxing) music pieces that are familiar and favored by them. The researchers measured physiological responses such as heart rate, respiratory rate, and expiratory carbon dioxide content alongside emotional states through visual analogue scales. Results indicated that stimulative music increased arousal with higher heart rates and faster breathing but did not affect relaxed emotions. Conversely, sedative music induced both aroused and relaxed feelings while slightly increasing heart rate. The study highlights the differential impact of self-selected favorite music on physiological responses and emotions, emphasizing its potential as an emotion regulator in everyday life.
