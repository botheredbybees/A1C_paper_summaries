## Sex- and Age-Related Differences in the Musical Behaviour, Interests and Attitudes Towards Music of 232 Secondary School Students

This study explores sex- and age-related differences in musical behavior, interests, and attitudes towards music among secondary school students in the United Kingdom. The research aims to develop a reliable instrument for assessing these attitudes within the context of secondary school music education. A cross-sectional survey involving 232 students from various academic and musical backgrounds was conducted at a mixed comprehensive school. Results indicate that girls tend to have more favorable attitudes towards music compared to boys, with this favorability decreasing less sharply with age in girls than in boys. The study also highlights the disparity between these positive attitudes and the lower participation of girls in professional music careers. It suggests that social stereotypes and cultural expectations play significant roles in shaping students' engagement with music both within and outside educational settings.
