## Show and Tell: Photography and Storytelling to Better Understand the End-of-Life Experience of Families in the Intensive Care Unit

This article examines the feasibility of using photography and storytelling to understand end-of-life experiences in intensive care units (ICUs) for families of patients. The study highlights that ICU mortality rates are relatively high, despite recent trends showing a decline. Families often experience distressing situations during end-of-life stages due to insufficient communication and lack of symptom management. The research aims to explore whether family-directed photography and storytelling can provide meaningful documentation of these experiences and serve as keepsakes for memory making. Through this method, the study seeks to investigate the impact of ICU environments on post-mortem grief and gain a deeper understanding of families' lived experiences during end-of-life stages in ICUs. The theoretical framework used is Meleis's Transitions Theory, which emphasizes the multidimensional nature of transitions, including changes in life, health, relationships, and environment. The research underscores the need for improved communication, care quality, and family-focused interventions to enhance outcomes for bereaved families.
