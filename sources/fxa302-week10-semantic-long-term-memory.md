## SEMANTIC LONG-TERM MEMORY

The chapter on semantic long-term memory in 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience' by Bradley R. Postle explores the organization and retrieval of semantic knowledge, which encompasses facts and meanings that are not tied to personal experiences. Postle discusses the findings from studies on patients like H. M., who despite severe impairments in episodic memory, retained normal semantic memory. The chapter delves into the theoretical frameworks explaining the structure of semantic memory, including taxonomic organization, functional modality, and lexical representation. It also addresses the challenges of attributing neuroimaging signals to knowledge versus language or visual perception processes. The role of the medial temporal lobe (MTL) and anterior temporal lobe in semantic memory is highlighted, along with debates in the field regarding the core deficits in frontotemporal dementia. Postle underscores the importance of interdisciplinary approaches, including cognitive neuroscience, linguistics, and philosophy, in understanding how knowledge is represented in the brain. This work is particularly relevant to creative arts and health as it provides insights into how cultural and artistic knowledge is preserved and accessed, despite neurological impairments.
