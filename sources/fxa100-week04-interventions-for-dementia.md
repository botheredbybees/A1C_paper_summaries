## Interventions for Dementia

This document outlines pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions used in dementia care. Pharmacological treatments include drugs like Acetylcholine Inhibitors (e.g., Aricept, Exelon) and Memantine, which address symptoms of cognitive decline but do not cure the disease or halt progression. Non-pharmacological methods include Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST), Reminiscence Therapy, Validation Therapy, Reality Orientation, Physical Exercise, Multisensory Stimulation (Snoezelen rooms), and Aromatherapy. CST aims to engage individuals with dementia in themed activities that enhance cognitive function and social interaction. Reminiscence Therapy taps into long-term memory to improve quality of life and emotional well-being. Validation therapy involves acknowledging the emotions of those with dementia, regardless of their accuracy, to promote happiness. Reality orientation helps reinforce self-identity and environmental awareness. Physical exercise reduces depressive symptoms and behavioral disturbances. Multisensory stimulation and aromatherapy provide sensory experiences that can calm individuals and enhance communication between caregivers and patients.
