## The effects of music exposure and own genre preference on conscious and unconscious cognitive processes: A pilot ERP study

This study explores the impact of music exposure and genre preference on cognitive processes, focusing on both conscious and unconscious brain activity. Participants included classical and rock musicians who performed a visual oddball task while listening to either classical or rock music. Event-related potentials (ERPs) were recorded to analyze the effects of music on brain activity. The study found that classical music exposure enhanced pre-attentive processing, as indicated by the N2 component, and reduced the P3 amplitude for musicians familiar with their own genre. These results suggest that music, particularly preferred genres, can influence cognitive performance at both conscious and unconscious levels, indicating that the Mozart Effect may be more broadly applicable across different music genres and dependent on personal preference.
