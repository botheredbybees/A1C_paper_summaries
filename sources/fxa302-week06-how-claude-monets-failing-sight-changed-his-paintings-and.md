## How Claude Monet’s Failing Sight Changed His Paintings and the World of Art

This transcript explores how Claude Monet's declining eyesight due to cataracts influenced his later works and potentially transformed Impressionist painting. Initially, Monet painted realistic scenes with nuanced coloration. As his vision deteriorated in the early 1900s, colors in his paintings became increasingly blurred and shifted towards a brownish-yellow hue. Despite his visual impairment, Monet continued to experiment with light and color by compensating with blue tones to neutralize the yellow cast from his cataracts. In 1923, he underwent surgery on one eye, which allowed him to see colors differently but also introduced challenges in distinguishing pigments. To overcome these limitations, Monet focused on large-scale works like the water lilies series, where detail was less critical and color shifts could be more pronounced. After surgery, he retouched some paintings while discarding others that did not meet his high standards for clarity and aesthetic quality. This period of struggle demonstrates Monet’s resilience in adapting to physical limitations, offering inspiration for artists facing similar challenges.
