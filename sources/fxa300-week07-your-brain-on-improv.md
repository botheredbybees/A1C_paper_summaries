## Your brain on improv

Dr. Charles Limb, a surgeon and neuroscientist with a deep fascination for sound, explores the neurobiological underpinnings of creativity, particularly in the context of jazz improvisation. Limb posits that creativity is a product of the brain and sets out to scientifically investigate artistic creativity. He employs functional MRI (fMRI) to study complex processes like jazz improvisation. In one study, he observes jazz musicians playing a memorized scale versus improvising on the same scale, noting significant activity in the medial prefrontal cortex and deactivation in the lateral prefrontal cortex during improvisation. These findings suggest a dissociation in the frontal lobe, with one area involved in self-monitoring deactivating while another area associated with autobiographical expression activates, allowing for uninhibited creativity. Additionally, Limb explores the neurological activity during interactive musical performances, highlighting the challenge and acclimation process for musicians in the MRI setting. This research contributes to understanding the neural mechanisms behind spontaneous creativity and collaborative musical interactions.
