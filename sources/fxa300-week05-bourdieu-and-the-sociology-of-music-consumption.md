## Bourdieu and the Sociology of Music Consumption

Nick Prior’s paper critically assesses the influence of Pierre Bourdieu on the sociology of music consumption, particularly focusing on how musical preferences reflect and reproduce social class inequalities. Initially highlighting Bourdieu's seminal work in 'Distinction,' which asserts that tastes in high culture such as classical music are markers of one's social status and cultural capital, Prior examines the broader impact of this framework on understanding music taste dynamics. The paper argues that while Bourdieu’s ideas have been instrumental in shaping critical sociology focused on power and inequality within musical preferences, recent developments challenge the static nature of these classifications due to changing consumption patterns driven by digital technologies. Key questions explored include whether Bourdieu's claims still hold true today given social changes and whether his concepts are robust enough to capture the fluidity and specificity of contemporary music interactions. Prior’s work is significant for creative arts and health studies as it underscores the dynamic relationship between cultural practices and societal structures, emphasizing the evolving nature of how we engage with music in a rapidly changing sociocultural landscape.
