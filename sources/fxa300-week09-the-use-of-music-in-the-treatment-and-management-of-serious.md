## The Use of Music in the Treatment and Management of Serious Mental Illness: A Global Scoping Review of the Literature

This paper presents a scoping review that explores the use of music in the treatment and management of serious mental illnesses (SMIs), including schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, major depressive disorder, and post-traumatic stress disorder. The study aims to identify research trends, gaps, and opportunities in the field. It includes a systematic search of eight databases, resulting in 349 studies for data extraction. The findings indicate that listening to recorded music is the most common musical activity and that randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are the predominant study design. Despite promising results reported by over two-thirds of the studies, evidence synthesis is hindered by insufficient reporting, varied outcomes, and complex interventions. The review highlights the need for standardized reporting, increased replicability, and improved understanding of mechanisms and causal pathways to enhance research and practice in the field of music and mental health.
