## Music listening as a means of stress reduction in daily life

This study explores the role of music listening in reducing stress in daily life. It investigates the effect of music listening on both subjective stress levels and physiological markers of stress, such as cortisol and alpha-amylase levels. Fifty-five healthy university students were monitored using ambulatory assessment during both regular term weeks and examination weeks. Participants rated their music-listening behavior and perceived stress levels four times a day, while a subset provided saliva samples for cortisol and alpha-amylase analysis. The results indicate that music listening can reduce subjective stress levels, especially when done for relaxation purposes. Additionally, listening to relaxing music was associated with lower cortisol concentrations and decreased alpha-amylase activity, suggesting a potential physiological mechanism underlying the stress-reducing effect of music. These findings contribute to the understanding of how music can serve as a stress-reduction tool in everyday contexts.
