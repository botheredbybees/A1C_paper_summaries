## Empathy, the song and the singer: a legacy of Robert Schumann

John Cox’s article explores the central role of empathy in musical appreciation, particularly in the relationship between a singer and their audience. Cox discusses the neural pathways involved in musical perception, emphasizing that music activates interconnected circuits in the brain including auditory pathways, the amygdala, insula, and the limbic system. He references the work of musicians and philosophers to argue that empathy is crucial for understanding musical expression and the emotional connection it fosters between performers and listeners. The article also delves into the impact of mental disorders on artistic creativity, using the example of Robert Schumann’s life and work. Cox suggests that engaging with great music, such as Schumann’s Dichterliebe, can enhance empathy and therapeutic effectiveness in medical practice. The article underscores the relevance of studying music and the arts for developing a deeper understanding of mental health and creativity.
