## The Air of History (Part II): Medicine in the Middle Ages

The article 'Medicine in the Middle Ages' by Rachel Hajar delves into how medical knowledge and practices were shaped during this era, emphasizing the significant influence of superstition and religious beliefs over scientific inquiry. The Roman Catholic Church's dominance led to a stagnation in medical progress due to its strict control over what constituted acceptable medical theories and treatments. Illnesses were often seen as divine punishment or signs of sin rather than conditions needing scientific investigation. Medical practices during this period, such as bloodletting and the use of herbal remedies, were deeply intertwined with spiritual beliefs. The reliance on ancient Greek texts, particularly those by Galen, further entrenched these non-scientific approaches despite their limitations. The article highlights how the study of medicinal plants became an integral part of monastic life, contributing to both folk medicine and professional practices. Overall, Hajar argues that the medical landscape in medieval times was marked by a blend of traditional wisdom from antiquity and spiritual influences, leading to a significant delay in the development of scientific medicine.
