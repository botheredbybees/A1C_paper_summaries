## Irrelevant sound interference on phonological and tonal working memory in musicians and nonmusicians

This study investigates the impact of irrelevant sounds on phonological and tonal working memory in professional musicians and nonmusicians. The researchers aimed to determine whether similar or distinct cognitive processes govern the temporary retention of verbal and musical information, such as words and tones. Participants performed tasks that required recognition and recall under varying conditions: silence, irrelevant speech, and irrelevant tones. Results indicated better performance by musicians on tonal memory tasks but not phonological ones. Irrelevant tones significantly disrupted tone recognition in both groups but did not affect pseudoword recognition. The study supports the hypothesis that auditory interference affects different types of working memory processes uniquely.
