## Values in Action Scale and the Big 5: An Empirical Indication of Structure

This study investigates the validity of Peterson and Seligman's classification system of 24 character strengths and six virtues using self-report measures from 123 undergraduate students. The research examines the relationship between these character strengths and the Five Factor Model of personality, as well as a measure of social desirability. The findings suggest that the 24 character strengths do not produce a factor structure consistent with the six higher-order virtues proposed by Peterson and Seligman. Instead, they are better represented by a one and a four-factor solution. Additionally, significant relationships were found between the character strengths and the Five Factor Model. The results challenge the hierarchical classification of character strengths and virtues, highlighting the complexity of linking individual traits to broader psychological constructs.
