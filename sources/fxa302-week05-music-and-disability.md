## Music and Disability

Joseph N. Straus's chapter 'Music and Disability' challenges the traditional medicalized approach to understanding disabilities in music by advocating for a perspective rooted in disability studies. The author contrasts the dominant medical model, which views disability as a pathology requiring cure or normalization, with a social and cultural model that recognizes disability as an aspect of human diversity deserving acceptance and appreciation. Straus explores three forms of musical engagement influenced by specific types of disabilities: autistic music making, deaf music making, and mobility-inflected music making. Each mode is discussed in terms of how it shapes the creation, performance, and comprehension of music without seeking to ‘cure’ or remediate perceived deficits. The chapter emphasizes the potential for disability to enrich musical practice through unique cognitive styles and perspectives.
