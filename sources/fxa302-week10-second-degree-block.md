## Second Degree Block

Amy Haddad's poem 'Second Degree Block' reflects on her experience as a novice nurse caring for a patient with a heart condition. The poem delves into the technical aspects of monitoring heart rhythms through EKGs and the emotional distance between the patient and the caregiver. Haddad uses the EKG strips as a metaphor for the physical and emotional space between her and the dying patient. The poem explores the contrast between the clinical detachment required in medical settings and the more profound human connection that is crucial during end-of-life care. Haddad's recollections highlight the tension between technological monitoring and the need for personal, empathetic presence. This work is particularly relevant to the intersection of creative arts and healthcare, illustrating how poetry can convey the complexities of medical practice and human emotion.
