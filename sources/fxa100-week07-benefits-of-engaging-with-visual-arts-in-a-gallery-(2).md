## Benefits of Engaging with Visual Arts in a Gallery Environment for People Living with Dementia in Australia

Shan Crosbie’s essay discusses the benefits of engaging with visual arts in a gallery environment for people living with dementia in Australia. The essay draws on Crosbie’s six-week internship at the Learning and Access department of the National Gallery of Australia (NGA) and its 'Art and Dementia' program. Crosbie highlights the growing prevalence of dementia in Australia, citing it as the second largest cause of death and leading cause of disability in older Australians. She emphasizes the importance of strategies that facilitate engagement with visual arts for dementia patients, referencing a 2009 NGA study titled 'You do it for the moment' by Macpherson et al. This study found that gallery interventions not only engage dementia patients in higher intellectual activities but also reduce their sense of excess disability. The essay underscores the social, cognitive, and emotional benefits of such programs, noting that they enhance patients' residual strengths and provide a temporary escape from the condition’s limitations. Furthermore, Crosbie discusses the positive impact of these programs on carers, who also benefit from the activities without the added pressure of being present during sessions.
