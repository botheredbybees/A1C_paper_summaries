## Music therapy interventions in Parkinson’s disease: the state-of-the-art

Alfredo Raglio's paper provides a comprehensive overview of music therapy interventions for Parkinson's disease (PD), focusing on both motor and non-motor symptoms. PD, a neurological disorder involving dopamine depletion, causes motor symptoms such as bradykinesia, tremor, and rigidity, as well as non-motor symptoms like anxiety and depression. Music therapy is shown to modulate emotions, behaviors, movements, and cognitive functions through the therapeutic effects of rhythm and melody. Rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS) techniques enhance motor functions and improve gait, limb coordination, and balance by compensating for the impaired timing system in PD patients. Additionally, relational music therapy approaches, which focus on building empathetic relationships through improvisation and musical interaction, address psychological aspects of PD. The author emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between rehabilitative and relational music therapy approaches to better understand their unique benefits and applications. However, the literature lacks strong scientific studies supporting the effectiveness of these interventions, highlighting a need for further research.
