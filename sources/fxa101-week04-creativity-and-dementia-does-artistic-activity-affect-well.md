## Creativity and dementia: Does artistic activity affect well-being beyond the art class?

The study investigates the impact of the Alzheimer's Association's Memories in the Making (MIM) art activity program on individuals with dementia. The research aimed to evaluate whether benefits observed during MIM sessions extend beyond these sessions and examined the psychometric qualities of the Greater Cincinnati Chapter Well-Being Observation Tool used for assessment. Seventy-six participants with middle- to late-stage dementia were evaluated at different stages of a 12-week MIM program, involving both interns observing behavior within sessions and care facility staff rating functioning outside these sessions. Interns reported significant improvements in five well-being domains from the start to the end of the program, while staff noted no changes in resident well-being over the same period. The study also highlighted weaknesses in inter-rater reliability of the assessment tool and found that it measures two factors—interpreted as 'Well-Being' and 'Ill-Being'—rather than the seven domains originally claimed. Despite quantitative ambiguity regarding MIM's effectiveness, anecdotal evidence suggested some benefits for participants during sessions.
