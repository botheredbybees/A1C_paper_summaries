## Where Does Brain Neural Activation in Aesthetic Responses to Visual Art Occur?

This study explores the neural correlates of visual aesthetic experience (VAE) through a meta-analysis of neuroimaging data from 47 fMRI experiments involving artworks. The research identifies both general and content-specific brain activations, suggesting that VAE engages widely distributed networks across both hemispheres, including areas specialized for processing specific categories of visual stimuli such as faces, places, and bodies. Key findings reveal maximal activation in the ventral visual stream (VVS) and additional brain regions like the anterior cingulate cortex and orbitofrontal cortex, indicating that aesthetic responses are intertwined with sensory perception and cognition. This study provides insights into how visual art triggers neural processes associated with beauty and appreciation, relevant to understanding creative arts' impacts on health and well-being.
