## The impact of rhythm complexity on brain activation during simple singing: An event-related fMRI study

This study investigates the impact of rhythm complexity on brain activation during simple singing in non-musicians. Using an event-related fMRI experiment, the researchers tested participants with rhythm sequences that had to be repeated as monotonous sung vowel changes, varying in regularity and complexity. The study found common activations in bilateral supplementary motor area, premotor cortex, left cingulate gyrus, and right basal ganglia for all conditions. Increased complexity, particularly irregular groupings, resulted in additional activation of the left pars orbitalis, insula, and bilateral cingulate gyrus, indicating that rhythm complexity influences lateralization and activation of specific brain areas during singing. These findings suggest that singing, especially when rhythm is emphasized, may be beneficial for language rehabilitation in patients with motor speech disorders and aphasia.
