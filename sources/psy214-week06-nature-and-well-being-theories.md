## Nature and Well-being: Theories

This document outlines several theories related to the relationship between nature and well-being, specifically focusing on Attention Restoration Theory (ART), Stress Reduction Theory, The Biophilia Hypothesis, and Nature Connectedness. ART posits that exposure to natural environments can restore attention by providing a break from directed attention and offering fascination that captures but does not overly tax one's mental capacity. This theory is supported by both subjective reports and objective measurements, though limitations exist in terms of definitions, concepts, sample sizes, and individual differences.
Stress Reduction Theory emphasizes the physiological and affective benefits of nature exposure, suggesting a potential evolutionary basis for these effects. The Biophilia Hypothesis proposes that humans have an innate tendency to seek connections with nature due to their biological origins. Finally, Nature Connectedness explores how a sense of connection to nature relates positively to both hedonic (pleasure-based) and eudaimonic (meaning-based) well-being.
