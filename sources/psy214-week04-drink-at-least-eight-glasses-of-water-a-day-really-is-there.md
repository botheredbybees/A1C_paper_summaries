## Drink at least eight glasses of water a day. Really? Is there scientific evidence for “8 × 8”?

Heinz Valtin's paper critically examines the widespread belief that one should drink at least eight glasses of water daily, commonly referred to as the '8 × 8' rule. This recommendation is pervasive in popular media and healthcare advice despite a lack of solid scientific evidence supporting it. The study reviews literature on fluid intake, focusing particularly on the origins of this recommendation and whether there exists any substantial evidence to back it up. Valtin's review reveals that the 8 × 8 guideline likely does not have its roots in rigorous scientific studies but rather may stem from less formal nutritional advice or popular opinion. Furthermore, existing research shows no definitive need for such high fluid intake among healthy adults living in temperate climates and engaging in sedentary activities. However, Valtin emphasizes that higher water intakes are beneficial under specific conditions like vigorous exercise or certain health issues. The paper concludes by questioning the widespread adoption of this rule without robust empirical support and encourages further scrutiny on the topic.
