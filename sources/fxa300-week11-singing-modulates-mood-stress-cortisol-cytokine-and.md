## Singing modulates mood, stress, cortisol, cytokine and neuropeptide activity in cancer patients and carers

This study explores the impact of singing on mood, stress, and immune responses in individuals affected by cancer, including carers and patients. The research involved 193 participants across three groups: current carers, bereaved carers, and cancer patients. Participants engaged in a 70-minute choir rehearsal, and before and after the session, they completed mood and stress scales and provided saliva samples to measure cortisol, beta-endorphin, oxytocin, and cytokine levels. The findings revealed significant reductions in negative affect and increases in positive affect, alongside increases in cytokines such as GM-CSF, IL17, IL2, IL4, and sIL-2rα. Additionally, there were reductions in cortisol, beta-endorphin, and oxytocin levels. This preliminary study suggests that singing can improve mood and modulate components of the immune system, although further research is needed to determine specific effects for different patient groups and the potential for long-term benefits.
