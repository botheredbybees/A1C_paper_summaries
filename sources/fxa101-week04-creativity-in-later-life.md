## Creativity in Later Life

The article 'Creativity in Later Life' by K.A. Price and A.M. Tinker explores the role of creativity as a potential coping mechanism for aging individuals and populations. The authors emphasize that the growing elderly population presents significant challenges, necessitating strategies to help older people adapt to societal changes unprepared for their needs. Creative activities are highlighted as beneficial in promoting social interaction, cognitive stimulation, self-worth, and can be therapeutic tools for managing health issues such as depression and dementia. However, barriers to participation exist and vary geographically, often relying on voluntary organizations rather than formalized public services. The literature suggests that creative activity could significantly contribute to improved health and well-being but calls for further research to identify key contributing factors and improve access to these beneficial activities.
