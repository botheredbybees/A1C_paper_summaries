## The impact of stress on motor performance in skilled musicians suffering from focal dystonia: Physiological and psychological characteristics

This study investigates the impact of stress on motor performance in skilled musicians suffering from focal dystonia (FDM). The research evaluates motor performance under acute stress using the Trier Social Stress Test (TSST), measuring EMG activity, ECG, and cortisol levels. While motor impairments did not change significantly under stress, an increase in muscular co-contractions was observed, reflecting a physiological response. Psychological traits linked to higher anxiety, stress, and perfectionism were found in 40% of FDM patients, who developed dystonia an average of ten years earlier than others. The findings suggest that acute stress does not directly affect fine motor control but may have long-term impacts on motor function through psychological factors.
