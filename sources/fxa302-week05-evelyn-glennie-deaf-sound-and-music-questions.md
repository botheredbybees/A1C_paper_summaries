## Evelyn Glennie | Deaf, Sound and Music Questions

Evelyn Glennie discusses her experiences with profound deafness and how it has influenced her career as a musician. She began losing her hearing at age eight due to nerve deterioration, which ultimately led to the use of hearing aids and attendance at a specialized school for the deaf. However, Glennie chose to remain in mainstream education. Through this journey, she discovered that by removing her hearing aids, she could hear better through her body’s vibrations. She emphasizes that her understanding of music is not just auditory but also somatic, feeling vibrations through her feet and body when performing percussion instruments. Her unique approach allows her to distinguish different pitches based on where they are felt in the body. Glennie's method can be experienced by anyone who touches a vibrating surface while listening to sound. She finds it rewarding to teach others how to feel music through their entire body, rather than just ears. This ability has enriched her emotional and physical connection with various instruments, each evoking distinct sensations and feelings. Her hearing impairment does not hinder vocal communication but requires occasional struggle with word formation for words rarely used.
