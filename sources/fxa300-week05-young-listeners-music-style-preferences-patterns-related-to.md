## Young Listeners’ Music Style Preferences: Patterns Related to Cultural Identification and Language Use

This study explores the music style preferences of young listeners in grades 4, 5, and 6, focusing on their cultural identification and language use. The research involved participants rating their preference for instrumental and vocal selections from various styles, including popular music with versions performed in English, Spanish, or an Asian language. Key findings include significant but small correlations between cultural identification and preferences for songs representing those cultures, as well as a relationship between the number of languages spoken and musical style preference. The study reveals that young listeners prefer music reflective of their cultural background, and bilingual students showed higher affinity towards versions performed in their native language compared to monolingual counterparts. These findings contribute to understanding how cultural identity influences musical preferences among youth.
