## A 6-month randomised controlled trial investigating effects of Mediterranean-style diet and fish oil supplementation on dietary behaviour change, mental and cardiometabolic health and health-related quality of life in adults with depression

This study protocol describes a 6-month randomized controlled trial (RCT) examining the impact of Mediterranean-style diet and fish oil supplementation on dietary behavior, mental health, cardiometabolic health, and quality of life in adults with depression. The primary aim is to determine if healthier diets can be adopted by individuals experiencing depressive symptoms and whether such changes can improve their physical and psychological well-being. Participants were randomly assigned into two groups: one received nutrition education, food hampers, cooking workshops based on Mediterranean dietary principles, and fish oil supplementation; the other group attended social gatherings as a control condition. Mental health outcomes were measured using standardized scales like DASS (Depression Anxiety Stress Scale) and PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule), along with anthropometric measurements, blood samples, and quality-of-life assessments at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. The study contributes to the growing body of evidence suggesting that dietary patterns can influence mental health outcomes, particularly in relation to depression and cardiometabolic conditions.
