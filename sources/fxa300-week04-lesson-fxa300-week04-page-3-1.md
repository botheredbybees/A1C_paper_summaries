## Music and Regions of the Brain

This lesson explores how different aspects of music are processed in specific regions of the brain. It covers four key features: pitch, intervals and contour, tonality, and rhythm. For pitch, it discusses the primary auditory cortex for individual frequencies and the secondary auditory cortex for processing pitch height (posterior) and chroma (anterior). Tonality involves the corticolimbic system, including the dorsal cingulate cortex, prefrontal cortex, thalamus, amygdala, retrosplenial cortex, brain stem, cerebellum, and left parahippocampal gyrus. Intervals and contour are processed in networks involving the secondary auditory cortex, superior temporal gyrus, planum polare, left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and right inferior frontal cortex. Rhythm is discussed through neuronal, perceptual, autonomic, motor, and social aspects, with the cerebellum and striato-thamalo-cortical pathway being crucial for timekeeping and predicting timing.
