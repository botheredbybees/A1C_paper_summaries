## The Ageing Brain

This document provides an overview of the ageing brain's structure and changes over time. It discusses the basic anatomy of the brain, including neurons and their functions, as well as grey and white matter distinctions. The review highlights methods for measuring brain volume changes using post-mortem analysis and live imaging techniques like MRI. A cross-sectional study involving 73 individuals aged 20 to 88 years showed decreases in nearly all brain areas alongside an increase in ventricle volumes with age.

Specifically, the document examines structural and functional changes in parietal and frontal lobes through a longitudinal study by Kramer (UCSF) spanning 4-6 years. This research identifies two distinct groups within the population data derived from longitudinal analysis, suggesting variability in how individuals experience brain ageing.

Additionally, there is evidence of selective grey matter reduction particularly in the cerebrum and frontal lobes with advancing age, while reports on white matter decline are less consistent. The document concludes that age-related volume loss often results in ventricle enlargement to compensate for these changes, emphasizing that such structural and functional alterations can be indicative of preclinical disease states even among healthy older adults.
