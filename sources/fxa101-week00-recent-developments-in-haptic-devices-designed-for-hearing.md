## Recent Developments in Haptic Devices Designed for Hearing-Impaired People: A Literature Review

This paper reviews recent advancements in haptic devices designed to assist hearing-impaired individuals by converting auditory signals into tactile stimuli. The authors categorize and analyze studies based on PRISMA guidelines, focusing on the design, function, and benefits of various haptic devices that provide sensory augmentation or substitution for users with hearing loss. They highlight areas such as wearable technology, vibrotactile feedback systems, and direct contact versus indirect (e.g., ultrasound) methods. The review emphasizes potential applications in sports like water polo, where athletes can receive tactile alerts for critical auditory cues like referees' whistles. Additionally, it discusses broader implications of haptic devices in enhancing accessibility and inclusion in society through technological innovation.
