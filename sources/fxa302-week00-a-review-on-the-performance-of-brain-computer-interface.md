## A Review on the Performance of Brain-Computer Interface Systems for LIS and CLIS Patients

This paper reviews the performance of brain-computer interface (BCI) systems for patients with locked-in syndrome (LIS) and complete locked-in syndrome (CLIS). The study aims to contribute to the growing research area by exploring BCI performance on actual LIS and CLIS patients, providing an overview of different modalities and paradigms used in various stages of the locked-in syndrome, and discussing the contributions and limitations of BCIs for these patients. It highlights the impact of BCIs on the quality of life of LIS patients and emphasizes the need for practical insights to address the challenges faced by these patients when using BCIs. The review includes detailed discussions on the clinical features of LIS and CLIS, the diagnostic criteria, and the importance of precise classification to avoid misdiagnosis. It also explores the differences in brain signals between LIS patients and healthy individuals, suggesting that spectral power changes in the beta range may indicate distinct characteristics in LIS patients. The paper concludes by discussing the life expectancy and quality of life of LIS patients, emphasizing the importance of BCIs in improving their communication and quality of life.
