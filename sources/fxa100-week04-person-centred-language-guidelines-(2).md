## Person-centred Language Guidelines

The document 'Person-Centred Language Guidelines' by the Alzheimer Society aims to promote respectful and person-centric communication regarding individuals with dementia. It outlines that language historically used to describe dementia has been predominantly negative, contributing to stigma and stereotypes surrounding the condition. The guidelines introduce principles such as recognition of individuality, dignity and respect, acceptance and understanding, support for relationships, trust in care provisions, and upholding human rights for people living with dementia. They suggest replacing derogatory terms like 'screamer,' 'hoarder,' or 'burden' with more constructive descriptions that focus on the behavior rather than labeling the person, such as specifying behaviors like pacing when upset or wandering at night. The guidelines advocate for a shift towards language that emphasizes care and respect, which can help reduce fear and stigma around dementia.
