## A constructive-critical response to Creative Health: The Arts for Health and Wellbeing (July 2017) by the All–Party Parliamentary Group on Arts, Health and Wellbeing

Kate Phillips critiques the report 'Creative Health: The Arts for Health and Wellbeing' by the All-Party Parliamentary Group on Arts, Health, and Wellbeing published in July 2017. This article highlights the need for rigorous and balanced research within arts and health practices, particularly advocating for formal systematic reviews to strengthen evidence-based approaches. Phillips points out that while previous literature like Staricoff's (2004) review has been influential in promoting arts activities in healthcare settings, it lacks methodological transparency, such as a comprehensive search strategy or clear quality assessment criteria. The report under scrutiny is also critiqued for its broad and somewhat uncritical presentation of evidence supporting the use of arts in health promotion and wellbeing improvement. Phillips raises questions about whether the intrinsic value of art can be upheld while still serving as an effective public health intervention, suggesting a potential tension between instrumentalism and intrinsic value. The article underscores the importance of philosophical clarity regarding ideological commitments within the field of arts therapy and broader arts and health practices.
