## Imagination and Creativity in Science: an ‘Embodied’ Perspective

Valentina Savojardo's paper explores the philosophical conundrum of imaginative use in science, where imagination sometimes distances us from reality and at other times helps us understand it better. Savojardo discusses how imaginative activities in science are constrained by embodied simulation, a neuroscientific concept, suggesting that the epistemic value of imagination lies in its embodiment. The paper argues that scientific imagination, when constrained by the body's background knowledge and experiences, facilitates creative thinking and methodical processes essential for scientific inquiry. This connection between imagination and method highlights the importance of understanding imagination as an embodied mental activity to grasp its role in enhancing scientific knowledge.
