## Culture, Democracy and the Right to Make Art: The British Community Arts Movement

The book 'Culture, Democracy and the Right to Make Art: The British Community Arts Movement' is a compilation edited by Alison Jeffers and Gerri Moriarty, which explores the development of the British Community Arts Movement from its inception in 1968 through to the present day. It highlights the experimental nature of this movement, emphasizing practices that aimed at inclusivity, cultural democracy, and the right for everyone to make art regardless of social or economic barriers. The book delves into specific regional experiences across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland, offering a comprehensive picture of how community arts projects have evolved over decades. Key themes include the influence of these initiatives on contemporary participatory and community arts practices, their role in cultural policy formation, and their impact on social change and civic engagement. The relevance to creative arts and health lies in its discussion of how artistic expression can serve as a tool for addressing societal issues and promoting well-being among diverse communities.
