## Creative Careers: The Life Cycles of Nobel Laureates in Economics

This article explores the life cycles of creativity among Nobel laureates in economics, identifying two polar types of innovators: conceptual and experimental. Conceptual innovators work deductively and achieve their most significant contributions early in their careers, often challenging established tenets of the discipline. Experimental innovators, on the other hand, accumulate knowledge through experience, leading to more substantial contributions later in life. The authors argue that while the conventional wisdom suggests creativity peaks in the mid-30s or early 40s, there is considerable variation within a single discipline, with Nobel laureates showing earlier peak productivity for conceptual innovators compared to experimental ones. This research underscores the importance of understanding individual differences in creative life cycles rather than relying on generalizations about disciplines as a whole.
