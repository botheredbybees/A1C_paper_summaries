## Historical and cultural aspects of man's relationship with addictive drugs

Marc-Antoine Crocq's paper delves into the historical and cultural dimensions of man's relationship with addictive drugs, focusing on two main aspects: the cultural history of drug use and the historical roots of the scientific understanding of addiction. The paper outlines how various psychoactive substances have been utilized across different epochs and cultures, often in religious ceremonies, for medicinal purposes, or as staple commodities in society. It highlights the role of cultural acculturation and the varying patterns of drug use, such as the prevalence of alcohol in various societies and the introduction of substances like tobacco and coca from the New World to the Old World. Crocq also discusses the evolution of views on addiction, including the recognition of pathological use and the inclusion of addiction within medical science. The paper underscores the complexity of addiction, touching upon debates regarding whether addiction is a disease or a moral failing, and the ongoing discussions about the causes and appropriate interventions for addiction.
