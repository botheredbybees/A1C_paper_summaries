## What is Therapeutic Photography?

The chapter introduces therapeutic photography as an activity intended to benefit the user through structured use to achieve specific outcomes, distinguishing it from activities that might incidentally provide benefits. It traces the historical roots of using photography in a therapeutic context back to Hugh Welch Diamond’s work at Surrey County Lunatic Asylum in the mid-19th century, noting his efforts to document mental illness and use photographs as part of treatment. The chapter also discusses how photography has been employed post-WWII for recreational purposes among recovering soldiers and later adopted by civilian hospitals. More recent applications include its use with clients facing various challenges such as disabilities or terminal illnesses. Key points highlight the democratic nature of photography, making it accessible to individuals without formal training in creative arts or therapy, thus broadening its therapeutic reach. The chapter concludes with considerations about how visual images can communicate significant information and foster connections within communities.
