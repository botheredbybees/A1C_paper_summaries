## The Impact of Professionally Conducted Cultural Programs on the Physical Health, Mental Health, and Social Functioning of Older Adults

This study by Cohen et al. (2006) examines the impact of professionally conducted cultural programs on physical health, mental health, and social functioning among older adults aged 65 and over in Washington D.C. Participants were assigned to either an intervention group engaging in chorale activities or a comparison group maintaining usual activities for one year. The results indicated that participants in the intervention group reported better overall physical health, fewer doctor visits, less medication use, reduced instances of falls, and lower levels of loneliness compared to the control group. Additionally, there was a trend toward increased activity levels among those involved in cultural programs. These findings suggest significant benefits for health promotion and risk reduction associated with participation in art-based activities, aligning with theoretical frameworks emphasizing sense of control and social engagement.
