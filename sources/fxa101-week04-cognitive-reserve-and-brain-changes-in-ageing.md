## Cognitive Reserve and Brain Changes in Ageing

The lecture discusses the concept of cognitive reserve and its implications in aging. It highlights how higher levels of education and engagement in activities that require creativity can help the brain find alternative pathways to maintain function despite cell deterioration or damage. The lecture also delves into structural changes in the brain with age, such as decreases in grey matter volume and increases in ventricle size, noting individual variability in these effects. Research indicates a substantial benefit of engaging in cognitive activities later in life, even accounting for socioeconomic status and depression. Specific examples include musical activities which enhance neuroplasticity and establish new neurons. The lecture also covers the role of education as a protective factor against cognitive decline by stimulating brain cell growth and maintaining neural activity to prevent atrophy. Additional topics include cognitive resilience, brain training apps improving memory and executive function, and creative arts therapies that benefit older adults' wellbeing. Finally, it examines developmental patterns of creativity throughout life, noting peaks in mid-twenties and mid-fifties, with evidence suggesting higher levels of engagement correlate with delayed brain aging.
