## FXA100 Week 6 Lecture: Visual Arts, the Brain, and Dementia Care

Week 6 of the FXA100 course delves into how visual arts, particularly painting and drawing, are perceived and processed by the brain and how these functions can be affected by dementia. The lecture discusses research findings from neuroaesthetics, including studies that show art triggers dopamine release in the orbito-frontal cortex and activates mirror neurons similar to performing an action. This engagement with art is tied to positive outcomes for individuals living with dementia through enhanced well-being, self-esteem, and cognitive processing. Art therapy is presented as a form of psychotherapy focusing on the therapeutic value of creation and self-expression, which can significantly improve quality of life for those with dementia. The lecture also explores case studies such as William Utermohlen’s artwork reflecting his experience of Alzheimer's disease, highlighting how art produced by people living with dementia provides insight into their perceptions and experiences. This underscores the broader relevance of incorporating creative arts in dementia care to enhance emotional well-being and cognitive engagement.
