## Brain Connectivity–Based Prediction of Real-Life Creativity is Mediated by Semantic Memory Structure

This study investigates the relationship between brain connectivity patterns and real-life creativity, focusing on how semantic memory structure influences creative behavior. The research builds upon associative theories of creativity, which posit that creative thinking involves generating remote associations within one's semantic memory. By employing multi-echo functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data during a semantic relatedness judgment task, the authors estimated individual semantic memory networks and found their properties significantly predicted participants' real-life creativity scores. Using connectome predictive modeling (CPM), they identified specific patterns of task-based functional connectivity that correlate with these semantic network properties. These findings suggest that brain connectivity supports creative behavior through its influence on semantic memory structure, particularly involving control, default mode, and salience networks. The study highlights the utility of computational network science in integrating behavioral, cognitive, and neural levels to understand creativity.
