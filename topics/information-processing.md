## Information-Processing and Cognitive Load

The concept of information-processing theory encompasses the cognitive mechanisms through which the human mind manages and processes information. Source 1 focuses on the role of working memory in everyday life, highlighting the capacity of working memory as a critical component in the cognitive load theory. This theory posits that the amount of information an individual can process at any given time is finite, and this capacity can be taxed by various cognitive demands. In contrast, Source 2 delves into the relationship between creativity and psychopathology, suggesting an inverted-U relationship where moderate levels of cognitive load or psychological distress can enhance creative thinking. This source introduces the idea of top-down control, which refers to the ability of the higher cognitive functions to regulate lower-level processes, potentially influencing how individuals manage cognitive load and navigate creative problem-solving. While both sources underscore the importance of cognitive mechanisms in managing information, they diverge in their specific focus on working memory versus the broader influence of cognitive load and psychological state on creativity.

## Key Findings

- Working memory capacity is a critical component in cognitive load theory.
- An inverted-U relationship exists between creativity and psychological distress.
- Top-down control plays a role in managing cognitive load and enhancing creativity.

## Open Questions

- How does top-down control interact with working memory capacity to manage cognitive load and enhance creativity?
