## Musical Performance Anxiety

Musical performance anxiety (MPA) is a pervasive issue affecting musicians across various training stages and performance contexts. Sources indicate that MPA can be influenced by factors such as instrument type, academic year, and the number of solo performance opportunities. Studies also highlight the impact of performance conditions, such as the presence of an audience or the formality of the setting, which exacerbate anxiety levels. Interventions ranging from preventive programs to psychological treatments, including exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring, are proposed to address MPA. However, while systematic desensitization and relaxation training show promise, their effectiveness may vary depending on the individual's training conditions and career prospects. There is a consensus that MPA significantly impacts performance quality and mental health, but the best approach to mitigating its effects remains an area of active research.

## Key Findings

- Musical performance anxiety (MPA) is influenced by various factors including instrument type, training stage, and performance context.
- Interventions such as exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring are proposed to alleviate MPA.
- Performance conditions like the presence of an audience and formal study impact the severity of MPA.

## Open Questions

- What are the most effective intervention strategies for reducing musical performance anxiety, considering individual differences in training conditions and career prospects?
