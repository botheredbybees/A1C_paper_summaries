## Research Design

The sources collectively highlight the foundational aspects and nuances of designing robust research studies. Source 3 introduces the importance of the scientific method in forming a clear hypothesis, setting the stage for understanding different types of research designs as detailed in Sources 2 and 4. Source 2 distinguishes between experimental and quasi-experimental designs, emphasizing key elements such as random assignment versus pre-existing groups, which can significantly impact study outcomes. This contrasts with case-study analysis noted in Source 1, a method less reliant on control variables but more focused on detailed examination of specific instances. These discussions converge in the recognition that feasibility assessment (Source 4) is crucial for choosing an appropriate methodology based not only on theoretical considerations but also practical constraints and ethical considerations.

## Key Findings

- The scientific method underpins all research designs by guiding hypothesis formulation and testing
- Random assignment in experimental studies helps isolate variables to identify causation
- Feasibility assessments are crucial for selecting a viable research design

## Open Questions

- How do researchers balance the need for rigorous causal inference with the practical limitations of conducting randomized controlled trials?
