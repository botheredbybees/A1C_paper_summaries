## Music Therapy for Resident Wellbeing

The use of music therapy as an intervention to improve the wellbeing of residents, particularly those with dementia, is discussed in both sources. Source 1 highlights how music therapy can reduce agitation and manage depression among dementia patients through targeted activities such as singing familiar songs. This approach enhances socialization by encouraging participation from individuals who may otherwise be isolated due to their condition. In contrast, Source 2 introduces a more structured observational method for assessing resident mood during informal music-making activities. While the manual does not specifically focus on therapeutic interventions, it underscores the importance of evaluating emotional and behavioral responses to arts-based interactions, including music therapy sessions. The integration of these methodologies suggests that both proactive treatment through music therapy programs and systematic observation can contribute effectively towards understanding and improving resident wellbeing.

## Key Findings

- Music therapy reduces agitation and manages depression in dementia patients
- Structured observations enhance the evaluation of mood during arts-based interactions
- Informal music-making activities promote socialization among residents

## Open Questions

- How can structured observation scales be integrated into existing music therapy programs to assess their effectiveness?
