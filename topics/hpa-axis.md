## Hpa-Axis In Stress And Sleep

The HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis is a critical physiological stress response mechanism that integrates the neuroendocrine and immune systems. It plays a central role in the body's adaptation to both acute and chronic stress through the release of cortisol, a key hormone involved in modulating inflammation and other biological processes. In Source 1, the HPA axis is discussed as part of the General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS), highlighting its importance in coping with prolonged stress. However, excessive activation of this axis can lead to negative health outcomes due to sustained high levels of cortisol, which disrupt sleep patterns and contribute to neuroplasticity changes that affect cognitive functions. Source 2 expands on these ideas by linking the HPA axis to the sympathetic nervous system's role in regulating wakefulness and vigilance during periods of stress or arousal, emphasizing its dual impact on both stress response regulation and sleep quality.

## Key Findings

- The HPA axis is a central component in biological responses to acute and chronic stress.
- Excessive activation of the HPA axis can lead to negative health outcomes through sustained high levels of cortisol.
- The HPA axis interacts with the sympathetic nervous system, influencing sleep quality and neuroplasticity.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term implications of prolonged HPA axis dysregulation on overall health and cognitive function?
