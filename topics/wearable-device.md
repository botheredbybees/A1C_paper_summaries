## Wearable Devices for Sensory Enhancement

The exploration of wearable devices as sensory enhancement tools is highlighted in two studies. One study focuses on developing a device to improve high-frequency hearing through cross-modal sensory substitution, utilizing haptic vibrations triggered by a phoneme-detection algorithm to assist individuals with impaired hearing. The other study examines the use of wearable technology for enhancing musical experiences via a haptic music player that employs multi-feature extraction techniques such as spectral flux and YIN algorithms. Both studies demonstrate the potential of wearable devices in sensory augmentation, either by compensating for auditory deficiencies or by providing an enriched perceptual experience through tactile feedback. However, there is a gap in research connecting these technologies to long-term user satisfaction and practical integration into daily life.

## Key Findings

- Wearable technology can compensate for high-frequency hearing loss using haptic vibrations and phoneme-detection algorithms.
- Haptic music players utilizing multi-feature extraction techniques enhance the listening experience through tactile feedback.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of relying on wearable sensory enhancement devices, and how do users integrate these technologies into their daily routines?
