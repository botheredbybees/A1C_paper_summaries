## Meaning in Life

Meaning in life is a multifaceted construct that has been explored through various theoretical lenses. Sources reveal that the concept of meaning in life can be approached through existentialism, positive psychology, and creative problem-solving. Existential thinkers such as Viktor Frankl have emphasized the importance of 'will to meaning' and logotherapy, which aims to help individuals find meaning in life despite suffering, proposing that this meaning can be derived through creative values, experiential values, and attitudinal values. Positive psychologists contribute to the understanding of meaning by introducing frameworks like the PERMA model and Steger's three-element model, which highlight the importance of positive emotions, engagement, relationships, meaning, and accomplishment for a fulfilling life. Additionally, the notion that engaging in creative problem-solving can alleviate existential debt, thereby enhancing meaning, underscores the dynamic interplay between creativity and existential concerns. These theories collectively illustrate the diverse pathways through which individuals can seek and derive meaning, emphasizing both the subjective experience and objective factors that contribute to a sense of purpose.

## Key Findings

- Meaning in life is a multifaceted concept that can be approached through existentialism, positive psychology, and creative problem-solving.
- Existential theories, such as logotherapy and the concept of existential vacuum, emphasize the importance of finding meaning in life despite adversity.
- Positive psychology approaches to meaning in life focus on positive emotions, engagement, relationships, meaning, and accomplishment (PERMA model) as key components.
