## Publication Bias in Scientific Research

The sources discuss the concept of publication bias within different scientific studies. Source 1 delves into the 'Mozart effect,' where initial reports suggested that listening to classical music could improve spatial reasoning skills, but subsequent research did not support these findings, indicating a potential case of publication bias. Meanwhile, Source 2 examines action video games' impact on perceptual, attentional, and cognitive skills through meta-analysis. It highlights similar issues related to the selective reporting of positive outcomes over null or negative results due to publication bias. Both sources underscore how this phenomenon can distort our understanding in various fields by inflating effect sizes and perpetuating false positives.

## Key Findings

- Publication bias is a significant issue across different domains of research, affecting both claims about the Mozart effect and studies on action video games' cognitive impacts.
- Selective publication of positive results leads to inflated effect sizes and distorted understanding in various fields.

## Open Questions

- How can we mitigate publication bias to ensure more accurate representation of scientific findings across different disciplines?
