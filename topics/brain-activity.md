## Brain Activities in Expertise and Nature Experience

This synthesis explores the neural mechanisms underpinning expertise and cognitive processes through brain activity. Source 1 employs functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to contrast brain activities between skilled and novice artists, underscoring the neurological basis of expertise and visual information processing. In comparison, Source 2 uses fMRI to investigate how nature exposure influences rumination by reducing subgenual prefrontal cortex activation. Both studies highlight the importance of understanding neural activity in relation to specific cognitive states or skills but diverge in their focus areas - one on skill acquisition and artistic expertise versus another on cognitive state regulation through environmental interaction.

## Key Findings

- Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) is a key method for studying brain activities linked with different types of tasks or mental states
- Neural activity patterns differ significantly between skilled individuals and novices, reflecting the neurological basis of expertise in specific domains such as visual processing in artistry

## Open Questions

- How do various environmental factors beyond nature exposure influence brain activity and cognitive processes?
