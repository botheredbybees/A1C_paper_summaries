## Narrative Theory

Narrative theory in the context of cinema and humanities explores how individuals construct and share their life stories. Source 1 delves into Julian Schnabel's film adaptation of 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly,' focusing on how the subjective camera technique transforms the literary 'I' into a cinematic eye. This transformation highlights the role of narrative in representing the locked-in syndrome experience, emphasizing the subjective nature of personal narrative through visual storytelling. Source 2 broadens the scope to the biopsychosocial model and values-based practice, integrating narrative theory within a broader framework of mental health and well-being. It underscores the importance of narrative in therapeutic contexts, suggesting that stories help individuals articulate their experiences and emotions. Both sources illustrate the significance of narrative in understanding and expressing personal experiences, whether through cinema or therapeutic dialogue.

## Key Findings

- Narrative theory plays a crucial role in representing personal experiences, both in cinema and therapeutic settings.
- Subjective camera techniques can effectively translate literary first-person narratives into visual storytelling.
- Narrative approaches in mental health facilitate the expression and understanding of complex emotional and psychological experiences.
