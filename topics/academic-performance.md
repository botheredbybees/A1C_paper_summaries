## Metacognitive Interventions and Academic Performance

The integration of metacognitive interventions and structured music education emerges as a promising pathway to enhance academic performance in children. Both sources highlight the positive effects on executive functions and metacognitive awareness, which are critical for learning and academic success. The longitudinal analysis underscores how structured music education can bolster executive function skills, crucial components that underpin cognitive regulation necessary for academic tasks such as planning, working memory, and inhibitory control. In parallel, the reflection on designing metacognitive interventions emphasizes the importance of enhancing self-regulatory strategies to foster motivation and performance in educational contexts. While both sources suggest complementary approaches—music education improving executive function while metacognitive training boosts awareness and goal orientation—they also point towards distinct intervention mechanisms: music’s indirect influence through cognitive enhancement versus direct instruction aimed at strategic learning processes.

## Key Findings

- Structured music education positively impacts children's executive functions, a foundational element for academic performance.
- Metacognitive interventions improve students' self-regulatory skills, such as awareness and goal orientation, which are linked to better motivation and performance in school settings.
- Both metacognitive training and structured music education contribute uniquely but synergistically towards enhancing various aspects of academic performance.

## Open Questions

- What is the optimal combination and sequencing of metacognitive interventions and structured music education for maximal impact on academic outcomes?
