## Communication Barriers Across Dementia and Hearing Loss

The studies on communication barriers in dementia patients and individuals with hearing loss highlight the complex interplay between non-verbal communication and social stigma. In the context of dementia, facilitated creativity is seen as a method to enhance positive participation and promote effective non-verbal communication among sufferers. This contrasts with the experience of individuals with hearing loss who often face negative self-perception due to societal ageism and health care disparities, which compound their challenges in communicating effectively. Despite differing contexts, both studies underscore the critical need for tailored interventions that address individual needs while reducing broader social stigmas associated with these conditions.

## Key Findings

- Non-verbal communication techniques can significantly improve interaction among dementia patients.
- Social stigma and ageism exacerbate the challenges faced by individuals with hearing loss in communicating effectively.
- Facilitating creativity aids positive participation for those with dementia.

## Open Questions

- How can interventions be designed to simultaneously address individual communication needs and broader societal stigmas?
