## Locked-In Syndrome: Quality of Life and Brain-Computer Interfaces

Research on Locked-In Syndrome (LIS) emphasizes the significant role of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) in enhancing quality of life and restoring communication abilities for individuals suffering from LIS. Studies indicate that BCIs, when augmented with functional electrical stimulation, can significantly improve the functionality and usability of communication tools for LIS patients. However, ethical considerations such as maintaining equipoise and nonexploitation are paramount in BCI research, underscoring the importance of user-centered design and commercial viability in the technology transfer of BCIs as assistive technologies. The concept of personhood in LIS highlights the importance of interpersonal recognition and self-identity, which are crucial for psychological adjustment and the overall quality of life for LIS patients. The complexity of LIS challenges traditional views of cognition and embodiment, requiring a reevaluation of the sensorimotor approach and extended cognition theories. Literature and films, such as 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly,' offer unique perspectives on the lived experiences of LIS, emphasizing the resilience and subjective experiences of individuals with LIS.

## Key Findings

- Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) significantly enhance the quality of life and communication abilities for individuals with Locked-In Syndrome (LIS).
- Ethical considerations such as maintaining equipoise and nonexploitation are critical in BCI research involving LIS patients.
- Interpersonal recognition and self-identity are key aspects of personhood in LIS, impacting psychological adjustment and quality of life.

## Open Questions

- What are the barriers to the commercial viability and usability of BCIs as assistive technologies for LIS patients?
