## Rhetoric

The study of rhetoric spans diverse fields and contexts, as evidenced by two sources. Source one explores rhetoric through the lens of circuit-bending, a practice where individuals manipulate electronic devices to create new sounds, intertwining human creativity with technological malfunction. This source emphasizes collaboration between humans and nonhumans (technology), suggesting that rhetoric is not solely human-centric but also involves interaction with machines and their inherent unpredictability. In contrast, Source two delves into the historical performance context of Commedia dell'Arte, a form of improvisational theatre characterized by masked characters engaging in humorous scenarios. This source illustrates how rhetoric operates within specific literary contexts, highlighting its adaptability to different cultural and artistic frameworks. Despite these divergent approaches, both sources converge on the idea that rhetoric is fundamentally about dynamic interaction—whether it's between humans and technology or among performers and their audience.

## Key Findings

- Rhetoric encompasses human-nonhuman interactions, including technology.
- Improvisational theatre practices like Commedia dell'Arte exemplify rhetorical adaptability within specific contexts.
- The study of rhetoric requires an understanding of both technological and historical-cultural dynamics.

## Open Questions

- How does the integration of non-human entities (like technology) alter traditional human-centric views of rhetoric?
