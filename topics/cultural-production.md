## Cultural Production in Music and International Migration

The synthesis of cultural production theories presented in these sources highlights the interdisciplinary nature of understanding music's health benefits (Source 1) and international migration studies (Source 2). Source 1 focuses on how music, when viewed through a health perspective, is perceived to have intrinsic 'goodness' that can positively impact individuals. This perception is influenced by commodification processes which situate music as both an economic commodity and a medium for communication within various social contexts. In contrast, Source 2 explores the complexities of researching arts and culture in relation to migration studies from a multi-disciplinary perspective. While these sources differ in their primary focus areas, they both underscore the importance of cultural production theories in explaining how artistic expressions such as music contribute to broader societal issues like health and international migration. The intersection between these fields reveals that music's role extends beyond entertainment; it is an integral part of social processes involved in migration and can serve as a tool for community building among migrant populations.

## Key Findings

- Music is perceived to have intrinsic 'goodness' from a health perspective, influencing individual well-being.
- Cultural production theories provide a framework to understand the commodification and situational use of music.
- A multi-disciplinary approach is necessary for comprehensive research on arts, culture, and international migration.

## Open Questions

- How can cultural production theories better integrate health perspectives with interdisciplinary studies of migration?
