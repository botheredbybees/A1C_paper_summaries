## Perceptual Decision-Making

The study of perceptual decision-making integrates insights from cognitive neuroscience, focusing on the neural mechanisms underlying value-based and perceptual decisions. Key findings indicate the involvement of the medial frontal cortex in value estimation, highlighting its role in the conversion of diverse sensory inputs into a common currency for decision-making. Neuroeconomic research underscores the importance of this common currency concept, which allows for the integration of various sensory modalities to estimate values accurately. Additionally, studies on lateral intraparietal neurons reveal their critical role in perceptual decision-making, suggesting a specific mapping of perceptual information onto saccade value. This indicates a hierarchical processing pathway where initial perceptual information is transformed and reinterpreted for decision-making purposes, with the medial frontal cortex playing a pivotal role in the final valuation of these perceptions. However, while substantial progress has been made in delineating the neural correlates of perceptual decision-making, there remain gaps in understanding the precise mechanisms of how perceptual information is transformed into actionable decisions within the brain's valuation circuits.

## Key Findings

- The medial frontal cortex is crucial for value estimation in decision-making processes.
- Perceptual information is transformed into a common currency for decision-making.
- Lateral intraparietal neurons are involved in mapping perceptual information onto saccade value.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific neural mechanisms by which perceptual information is transformed into the common currency of value estimation?
