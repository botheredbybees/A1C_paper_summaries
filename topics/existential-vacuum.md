## Existential Vacuum

The concept of the 'existential vacuum' emerges prominently in the existential and logotherapeutic literature as a state where an individual lacks a sense of direction or purpose, feeling disconnected from creative, experiential, and attitudinal values. This vacuum is described as a void in human existence that arises when the individual fails to find or create meaning in life, often due to a lack of engagement with the world through creative, experiential, or attitudinal values. Viktor Frankl's logotherapy emphasizes the 'will to meaning' as a fundamental human motivation, contrasting with the more commonly discussed 'will to power' or 'will to pleasure.' This theory posits that the existential vacuum is a critical issue in modern societies, where individuals may struggle to find significance amidst a plethora of choices and lack of clear cultural direction.

## Key Findings

- The existential vacuum is a state where individuals experience a lack of meaning and purpose in life.
- Logotherapy posits the 'will to meaning' as a fundamental human drive, opposing the 'will to power' and 'will to pleasure.'
- Engagement with creative, experiential, and attitudinal values is crucial for avoiding the existential vacuum.

## Open Questions

- How do modern cultural and societal factors contribute to the emergence of the existential vacuum, and how can they be addressed to foster a sense of meaning and purpose in individuals?
