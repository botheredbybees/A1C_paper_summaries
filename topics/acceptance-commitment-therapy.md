## Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) emerges as a significant intervention for managing psychological distress across different contexts, as evidenced by both sources. Source 1 highlights ACT's effectiveness in addressing music performance anxiety, aligning with its broader application in treating various psychological conditions. The integration of mindfulness practices, as detailed in Source 2, underscores the therapeutic benefits of ACT's mindfulness components. Relational Frame Theory (RFT), mentioned in both sources, provides a theoretical framework for understanding the functional contextual approach underlying ACT. The narrative synthesis of therapeutic interventions for music performance anxiety, alongside the exploration of mindfulness in psychotherapy, suggests ACT's versatility in therapeutic settings. However, while these sources illustrate ACT's efficacy, they also highlight the need for further research on its application and adaptability across diverse populations and settings.

## Key Findings

- ACT is effective for managing psychological distress, including music performance anxiety.
- Mindfulness-based practices are integral to ACT's therapeutic approach.
- RFT provides a theoretical foundation for understanding ACT's functional contextual approach.

## Open Questions
