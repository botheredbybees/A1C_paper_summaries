## Re-enactment Therapy

Re-enactment therapy emerges as a therapeutic modality that uses photography to facilitate the process of healing and personal growth. Both sources emphasize the role of re-enactment phototherapy in fostering self-understanding through the creation and analysis of photographic images. The first source highlights how self-portraiture can serve as a tool for individuals to revisit their past experiences, while also integrating family therapy to enhance familial bonds. In contrast, the second source delves deeper into the theoretical underpinnings of re-enactment phototherapy by linking it with gaze theory and the concept of embodiment. This approach underscores how photography serves not only as a medium for expression but also for experiencing and transforming one's own identity and relational dynamics.

## Key Findings

- Re-enactment therapy utilizes photography to aid in therapeutic processes such as self-reflection and family bonding.
- Gaze theory is integral to understanding the psychological impact of re-enactment phototherapy on personal transformation.

## Open Questions

- How does re-enactment therapy adapt to contemporary digital media environments, considering changes in how images are created and shared?
