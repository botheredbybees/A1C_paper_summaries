## Neurobiological Effects of Creative Arts Therapies

The neurobiological effects of creative arts therapies, as evidenced in two studies, highlight the profound impact these interventions can have on physiological and psychological states. One study emphasizes the role of singing in modulating mood, stress, cortisol levels, cytokine activity, and neuropeptide activity in cancer patients and carers, suggesting a direct link between the creative arts and physiological regulation. This modulation is particularly notable for its potential to influence the immune response and overall health. In contrast, the second study focuses on trauma-informed care within creative arts therapies, introducing sensory-based interventions that address developmental trauma disorder. While these studies differ in their specific focus areas, they collectively underscore the importance of creative arts therapies in providing holistic interventions that address both the psychological and physiological aspects of well-being. This integration of sensory and creative elements in therapeutic settings not only enhances mood and emotional regulation but also supports the broader goals of trauma recovery and stress reduction.

## Key Findings

- Creative arts therapies, including singing, can significantly modulate mood, stress, and physiological parameters such as cortisol and cytokine levels.
- Sensory-based interventions within creative arts therapies are effective in addressing developmental trauma disorder and supporting trauma recovery.
- These therapies offer a comprehensive approach to health and well-being by simultaneously addressing psychological and physiological needs.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term neurobiological impacts of sustained engagement in creative arts therapies for individuals with chronic conditions or trauma?
