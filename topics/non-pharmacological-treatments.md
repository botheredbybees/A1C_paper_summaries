## Non-Pharmacological Treatments for Dementia

The integration of person-centered care (PCC) principles with non-pharmacological treatments, such as creative arts interventions, represents a significant approach in dementia care. Source 1 provides a systematic review highlighting key components of PCC that include respect for individual preferences and decision-making models tailored to the needs of individuals living with dementia. This emphasizes the importance of autonomy in treatment planning. Similarly, source 2 discusses comprehensive reviews of art-based interventions from 2010-2020, underscoring engagement as a critical aspect of these non-pharmacological methods. Both sources indicate that engaging patients through their personal interests and choices can lead to improved quality of life and symptom management without relying on medication. However, source 3 cautions about the methodological weaknesses present in studies examining cultural arts interventions for dementia care, suggesting a need for rigorous evaluation methodologies moving forward.

## Key Findings

- Person-centered care is an effective framework for non-pharmacological interventions in dementia treatment.
- Autonomy and personalized engagement are critical components of successful non-pharmacological approaches to dementia.
- There is a growing body of evidence supporting the use of creative arts in enhancing quality of life for individuals with dementia.

## Open Questions

- What are the methodological standards needed to strengthen research on cultural arts interventions for dementia care?
