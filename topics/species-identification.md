## Automatic Species Identification Using Computer Vision

The integration of automatic species identification through computer vision presents both promising advancements and significant challenges. Source 1 focuses on the application of EfficientNetV2, a neural network architecture designed for image classification tasks, in identifying New Zealand (Aotearoa) flora with an additional temperature scaling technique to improve accuracy. Meanwhile, Source 2 delves into specific identification issues within Castilleja species using iNaturalist's platform, highlighting that even state-of-the-art computer vision algorithms struggle with certain taxonomic challenges, particularly when dealing with closely related species or insufficient data. Both sources emphasize the importance of accurate and reliable identifications for effective biodiversity management but also point to the need for continuous refinement and validation of machine learning models used in this context.

## Key Findings

- EfficientNetV2 demonstrates potential for automatic species identification, particularly when augmented with techniques like temperature scaling.
- Computer vision algorithms face difficulties with closely related species or insufficient data.
- Research-Grade identifications on platforms such as iNaturalist are crucial for validating and improving machine learning models.

## Open Questions

- How can the accuracy of automatic species identification be improved in scenarios where taxonomic groups are highly similar or underrepresented in training datasets?
