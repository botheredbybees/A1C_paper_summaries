## Green Spaces and Mental Health

The integration of findings from the sources highlights both the positive impacts of green spaces on mental health and the potential detriments introduced by technology interference. Source 1 discusses how portable electronic devices can negate some cognitive benefits one would gain from time spent in nature, emphasizing that while green spaces offer substantial restorative effects for attentional fatigue and stress reduction (Source 2), these benefits are not universally beneficial if individuals use their smartphones or other portable electronics during such periods. Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and Stress Reduction Theory underscore the psychological benefits of natural settings, yet Source 1 reveals a significant caveat: constant engagement with technology can undermine these restorative properties by maintaining high levels of cognitive load and attentional demand. The interplay between the restorative effects of nature and the disruptive influence of digital devices presents a nuanced picture where green spaces remain beneficial but require mindful use to maximize their therapeutic potential.

## Key Findings

- Green spaces provide substantial mental health benefits according to Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and Stress Reduction Theory, including reduced stress and improved cognitive function.
- The presence of portable electronic devices can interfere with the restorative effects of green spaces by maintaining high levels of cognitive load and attentional demand.
- Mindful engagement in nature without technology interference is critical for fully experiencing its mental health benefits.

## Open Questions

- How can individuals optimize their use of green spaces to maximize mental health benefits while minimizing the potential negative impacts of technology?
