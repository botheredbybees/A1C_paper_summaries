## Spiritual Well-Being in Cancer Patients

The topic of spiritual well-being in cancer patients encompasses various therapeutic approaches designed to enhance quality of life and mitigate psychological distress. Source 1 explores the effectiveness of a collage activity based on life review, revealing improvements in spiritual well-being, reduced anxiety and depression, and increased self-efficacy among elderly cancer patients. This non-invasive activity suggests a practical method for integrating personal history and spirituality. Source 2, a systematic review and meta-analysis, examines the efficacy of Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy (MCP) for adult patients with advanced cancer, highlighting its significant impact on spiritual well-being and overall quality of life. MCP not only enhances spiritual well-being but also reduces psychological distress, offering a robust therapeutic framework. Source 3 elaborates on the concept of finding meaning in suffering through MCP, addressing existential distress and hopelessness. The sources collectively underscore the importance of therapeutic interventions that integrate spiritual and existential dimensions, providing relief and meaning in the context of serious illness. However, while these interventions show promise, there is a need for further research to understand the long-term efficacy and applicability across diverse populations and settings.

## Key Findings

- Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy (MCP) significantly improves spiritual well-being and quality of life in cancer patients.
- Collage activities based on life review enhance spiritual well-being and reduce anxiety and depression in elderly cancer patients.
- Existential distress and hopelessness are addressed effectively through MCP, reducing psychological distress.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of collage activities and MCP on spiritual well-being and quality of life in cancer patients?
