## Puppet-Assisted Play Therapy for Children

Puppet-assisted play therapy is a versatile intervention that leverages the imaginative and symbolic nature of puppetry to facilitate emotional expression and conflict resolution in children. The integration of creative play, such as using puppets to externalize problems or engage in role-playing scenarios, supports children in addressing traumatic experiences, managing pain during medical procedures, and enhancing social communication skills, particularly for those with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). Sources emphasize the theoretical underpinnings of this approach rooted in object relations theory and narrative therapy, highlighting its potential to provide a non-verbal avenue for emotional expression and psychological catharsis. Therapists use puppets not only as tools for creative engagement but also for developing character identities that symbolize internal conflicts or externalized issues. The therapeutic benefits include reduced fear and pain, improved coping mechanisms, enhanced attention to social cues, and better emotional regulation.

## Key Findings

- Puppet-assisted play therapy enhances children's ability to express emotions non-verbally through symbolic engagement with puppets.
- The approach is effective in reducing fear and pain associated with medical procedures such as blood collection in young children.
- Puppets serve as a diagnostic tool, allowing therapists to better understand internal conflicts and externalize problems for therapeutic purposes.

## Open Questions

- How does the effectiveness of puppet-assisted play therapy vary across different cultural contexts where attitudes towards puppets may differ?
