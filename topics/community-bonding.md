## Community Bonding Through Repair and Singing

This synthesis explores two distinct yet interconnected interventions aimed at fostering community bonding. Source 1 highlights the role of collaborative repair practices in building communities around shared material engagement, emphasizing circular design strategies that promote sustainability and social cohesion. In contrast, Source 2 focuses on singing as a communal activity that enhances individual wellbeing while also strengthening social bonds through its cultural significance and neuroplasticity benefits. Both sources underscore the importance of collective activities in fostering community spirit and personal growth. However, there is a tension between the tangible outcomes of repair work versus the intangible but profound impact of singing on mental health.

## Key Findings

- Collaborative repair practices contribute to social cohesion by engaging communities around shared material goals.
- Singing enhances individual wellbeing through neuroplasticity and cultural connection, thereby reinforcing community bonds.
- Both repair activities and singing can serve as effective interventions for building stronger, more resilient communities.

## Open Questions

- How do the tangible outcomes of collaborative repair compare with the intangible benefits of communal singing in terms of long-term impact on community wellbeing?
