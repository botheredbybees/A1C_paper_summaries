## Non-Communicable Diseases

The analysis of global trends in morbidity and contributing diseases, as well as the health impacts of dietary risks, highlights significant challenges posed by non-communicable diseases (NCDs). Source 1 discusses how NCDs are a major contributor to the morbidity gap, particularly affecting low socio-demographic index regions. It also notes the compression-of-morbidity hypothesis, suggesting an improvement in reducing the duration of illness before death as life expectancy increases. However, the trend is not uniform across all regions, indicating varied effectiveness and accessibility to healthcare measures. Source 2 emphasizes the role of suboptimal diet in NCD mortality and morbidity globally, integrating findings from the Global Burden of Disease Study that underscores dietary risks' substantial contribution to NCDs. This source aligns with Source 3’s focus on the direct link between poor nutritional quality and increased disease incidence among populations worldwide. Together, these sources highlight systemic issues such as diet-related lifestyle choices and socioeconomic inequalities driving NCD prevalence.

## Key Findings

- Non-communicable diseases are major contributors to global morbidity gaps.
- Suboptimal dietary habits significantly impact non-communicable disease mortality and morbidity globally.
- Poor nutritional quality is directly linked with higher incidence of non-communicable diseases.

## Open Questions

- How can socio-demographic inequalities be addressed to improve access to healthy diets and reduce the burden of non-communicable diseases?
