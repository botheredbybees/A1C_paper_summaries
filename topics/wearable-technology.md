## Wearable Technology for Sensory Substitution

The integration of wearable technology in sensory substitution interventions showcases a promising avenue for enhancing the lives of individuals with sensory impairments. Across several studies, devices such as vests and gloves equipped with vibratory feedback have been developed to substitute auditory or visual inputs through tactile sensations. These technologies leverage neural plasticity, enabling users to perceive sound patterns or spatial information through vibration on the skin. The versatility of these tools is evident in their ability not only to assist those who are hard-of-hearing but also to potentially expand human sensory capabilities beyond traditional senses like sight and hearing. However, while initial results suggest significant potential, challenges remain regarding personalization and long-term efficacy.

## Key Findings

- Wearable technology can substitute auditory or visual inputs through tactile sensations.
- Neural plasticity enables the brain to adapt to new sensory information provided by wearable devices.
- There is potential for these technologies to enhance human sensory capabilities beyond traditional senses.

## Open Questions

- How can personalized vibrotactile patterns be developed and optimized to improve long-term efficacy of sensory substitution?
