## Neurologic Music Therapy for Upper Limb Function

Neurologic music therapy (NMT) emerges as a promising intervention for enhancing upper limb function in stroke patients, particularly in the chronic stage. Both sources emphasize the feasibility and potential benefits of NMT, specifically employing rhythmic auditory stimulation and audio-motor coupling techniques. Source 1 outlines a feasibility study that uses rhythmic auditory stimulation for upper limb rehabilitation in a home setting, highlighting the practicality of NMT for community rehabilitation. Meanwhile, Source 2 presents two contrasting case studies that utilize therapeutic instrumental music performance (TIMP) and audio-motor-synchronization to address upper limb rehabilitation. These case studies underscore the importance of tailored treatment approaches and patient suitability. The integration of musical elements such as rhythm and timing enhances motor control and coordination, offering a novel therapeutic approach to complement traditional rehabilitation methods. However, the variability in patient responses and the need for individualized treatment planning are noted as areas requiring further exploration.

## Key Findings

- Neurologic music therapy is a feasible and promising intervention for upper limb rehabilitation in stroke patients, particularly in the chronic stage.
- The use of rhythmic auditory stimulation and audio-motor coupling techniques in neurologic music therapy enhances motor control and coordination in upper limb rehabilitation.
- Patient suitability and individualized treatment planning are critical factors in the successful application of neurologic music therapy.

## Open Questions

- What are the optimal conditions for the application of neurologic music therapy, and how can treatment plans be tailored to individual patient needs?
