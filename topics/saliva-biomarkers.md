## Saliva Biomarkers in Music Interventions

Saliva biomarkers, specifically salivary cortisol and alpha-amylase, have been extensively used to evaluate the physiological impact of music interventions on stress and pain. Studies indicate that music interventions can significantly reduce stress levels and alleviate pain in various populations. For instance, in care home residents with dementia, group music therapy and recreational choir singing were found to lower salivary cortisol and alpha-amylase levels, suggesting a reduction in stress. Similarly, in patients with fibromyalgia syndrome, listening to music was associated with increased perceived control over pain, which is often linked to reduced stress levels as indicated by saliva biomarkers. However, while both studies underscore the positive impact of music interventions on stress and pain, there is a notable gap in understanding the long-term effects of these interventions, particularly in diverse populations and across varying durations.

## Key Findings

- Music interventions can reduce stress levels as measured by salivary cortisol and alpha-amylase.
- Music listening enhances perceived control over pain in patients with fibromyalgia.
- Group music therapy and recreational singing lower stress biomarkers in dementia patients.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of music interventions on stress and pain as measured by saliva biomarkers?
