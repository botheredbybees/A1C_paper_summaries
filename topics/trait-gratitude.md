## Trait Gratitude

Trait gratitude, a dispositional tendency to feel grateful regularly, is distinguished from state gratitude, which is a transient emotional response to a specific event. Studies emphasize the role of trait gratitude in enhancing subjective well-being and overall health. While state gratitude can be induced through various interventions, such as mental subtraction, trait gratitude appears more stable and predictive of long-term positive outcomes. Research suggests a causal relationship between trait gratitude and increased well-being, though the exact mechanisms linking gratitude to health benefits are still under investigation. The review highlights the potential for positive interventions to cultivate trait gratitude, thereby promoting sustained health benefits. However, there is a need for further research to delineate the distinct roles of state and trait gratitude in psychological health.

## Key Findings

- Trait gratitude is a stable personality trait associated with higher levels of subjective well-being and better health outcomes.
- Interventions aimed at increasing state gratitude may contribute to the development of trait gratitude over time.
- State and trait gratitude are distinct but complementary constructs in understanding the benefits of gratitude.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms through which trait gratitude translates into health benefits, and how do these differ from the mechanisms of state gratitude?
