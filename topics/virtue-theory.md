## Virtue Theory

Virtue theory, an influential ethical framework, is explored in two sources, each contributing distinct perspectives. Source 1 focuses on the philosophical distinction between hedonia and eudaimonia, illustrating the contrast between seeking pleasure and pursuing a life of virtue and excellence. This source emphasizes the role of virtue theory in promoting subjective well-being and the realization of human potential. In contrast, Source 2 delves into the application of Aristotelian and Thomistic virtues within political communities, engaging with the VIA classification of character strengths. It highlights the importance of virtues in shaping individuals' character and the role of political institutions in fostering virtuous citizens. Both sources underscore the relevance of virtue theory in contemporary psychological and political discussions, showcasing its enduring influence in understanding human flourishing.

## Key Findings

- Virtue theory is central to understanding both individual well-being and the cultivation of virtuous communities.
- Hedonia and eudaimonia represent contrasting approaches to achieving a good life, with the latter aligning more closely with virtue theory's emphasis on virtue and human potential.
- The VIA classification of character strengths provides a modern framework for understanding and promoting virtues within political and social contexts.

## Open Questions

- How can the Aristotelian and Thomistic virtues be effectively integrated into modern political systems to promote virtuous behavior and community well-being?
