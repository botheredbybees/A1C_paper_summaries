## Will To Meaning

The concept of the 'will to meaning' emerged within the framework of logotherapy, a psychotherapeutic approach developed by Viktor Frankl. This theory underscores the human search for meaning and purpose in life, which is distinct from the 'will to power' posited by Nietzsche or the 'will to pleasure' proposed by Freud. The 'will to meaning' asserts that finding a sense of purpose is a fundamental human drive, which contrasts with the existential vacuum—a state of aimlessness and lack of purpose. Logotherapy emphasizes the importance of discovering one's unique life meaning, which can be found through creative, experiential, and attitudinal values. The theory also introduces the concept of dereflection, encouraging individuals to move beyond reflection and into action to find meaning. Across both sources, there is a consistent emphasis on the existential imperative to find meaning, which can be a challenge when faced with an existential vacuum, thereby highlighting the importance of logotherapy in addressing this issue.

## Key Findings

- The 'will to meaning' is a central tenet of logotherapy, distinct from Freud's 'will to pleasure' and Nietzsche's 'will to power'.
- Finding meaning through creative, experiential, and attitudinal values is essential according to logotherapy.
- The concept of the existential vacuum denotes a state of purposelessness, contrasting with the 'will to meaning'.

## Open Questions

- How can logotherapy effectively address the existential vacuum in individuals who struggle to find meaning?
