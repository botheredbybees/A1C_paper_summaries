## Attention Restoration Theory

Attention Restoration Theory (ART) posits that exposure to natural environments can reduce mental fatigue by providing a restorative setting that decreases cognitive load. Both sources highlight ART's role in facilitating attention recovery, with Source 1 focusing on the application of this theory through group nature-based mindfulness interventions for college students experiencing anxiety. These interventions aim to leverage the restorative benefits of nature and mindfulness practice to alleviate symptoms associated with mental fatigue. In contrast, Source 2 presents a broader framework that includes ART alongside other theories such as Stress Reduction Theory and the Biophilia Hypothesis. This source underscores how nature contact can contribute positively to well-being by reducing stress and enhancing emotional connection with natural settings.

## Key Findings

- Attention Restoration Theory highlights nature's capacity for cognitive recovery from mental fatigue.
- Nature-based mindfulness interventions are effective tools in applying ART principles to reduce anxiety among college students.

## Open Questions

- How do the benefits of nature exposure as described by Attention Restoration Theory compare with those proposed by Stress Reduction Theory and Biophilia Hypothesis?
