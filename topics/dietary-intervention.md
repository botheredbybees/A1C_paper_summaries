## Dietary Intervention

The integration of findings from the sources highlights the significant role dietary intervention plays in managing both physical and mental health conditions. Source 1 emphasizes that dietary interventions are a modifiable risk factor for cognitive decline and dementia, suggesting that diet quality influences brain health over an individual's lifespan. Sources 2 and 3 focus on specific dietary approaches to treat depression, with Source 2 advocating for a Mediterranean-style diet supplemented with fish oil, while Source 3 focuses on the SMILES trial which tested dietary improvement for adults diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD). Both trials indicate that dietary changes can lead to improvements in mental health status as measured by scales like the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale and quality-of-life scores. However, there is variability in the specific dietary recommendations, with Source 2 suggesting a Mediterranean diet enriched with fish oil versus a more general improvement in diet suggested by Source 3.

## Key Findings

- Dietary intervention can be used to manage modifiable risk factors for cognitive decline and dementia.
- Mediterranean-style diets supplemented with fish oil may improve mental health in individuals with depression.
- Improvements in dietary habits have positive effects on quality-of-life scores and symptoms of depression as measured by the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific components of a Mediterranean diet that contribute to improved mental health outcomes, especially when supplemented with fish oil?
