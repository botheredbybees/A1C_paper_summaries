## Adult Musical Preferences and Psychosocial Development

The studies on adult musical preferences and psychosocial development reveal a complex interplay between personal choices and social influences. The Music Preferences in Adulthood Model (MPAM) highlights how conformity and the desire to fit into certain age groups or cultural norms shape individuals' music tastes as they mature. This model suggests that adults often adjust their musical preferences based on peer influence, societal expectations, and personal identity formation processes. Simultaneously, Erikson's Stages of Psychosocial Development offer a broader framework for understanding how adults navigate life challenges and form identities through various developmental crises across different age periods. While MPAM focuses more narrowly on the role of music in adult life stages, Erikson's theory provides a comprehensive view of psychosocial development that can include but is not limited to musical preferences.

## Key Findings

- Adults adjust their musical preferences based on social influences and personal identity formation processes.
- Erikson's Stages of Psychosocial Development provide a broader framework for understanding adult life challenges, including aspects related to cultural and artistic choices.
