## Music and Memory

The interplay between music and memory is a complex field with various theories attempting to elucidate how auditory experiences shape our recollection. Source 1 explores the concept of auditory memory functions, underscoring its distinct role in recalling specific musical pieces or moments from one's life, such as during the 'reminiscence bump' period when early adulthood memories are particularly vivid. This source highlights procedural memory, which involves the recall of how to perform tasks without conscious thought, a mechanism crucial for remembering songs and melodies.

Source 2 introduces a theoretical framework focusing on scale and contour in melodic memory. It posits that these two components—musical-scale (the structure or key within which music operates) and melodic-contour (the rise and fall of pitches in a melody)—are foundational to how we remember musical pieces. This perspective shifts the focus from individual notes to broader structural elements, suggesting an alternative approach to understanding auditory memory.

While both sources emphasize distinct aspects of memory associated with music—auditory functions versus scale and contour—they collectively underscore the nuanced nature of musical recollection that integrates procedural knowledge with structural and tonal patterns.

## Key Findings

- Auditory memory is essential for recalling specific musical pieces.
- Procedural memory plays a significant role in remembering how to perform tasks related to music.
- Scale and melodic-contour are critical components in the theory of melody recall.

## Open Questions

- How do auditory memory functions interact with procedural memory to influence musical recollection?
