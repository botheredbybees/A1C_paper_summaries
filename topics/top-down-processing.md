## Top-Down Processing

Across the provided sources, top-down processing is consistently depicted as a higher-level cognitive function in which pre-existing knowledge and expectations significantly influence perception. In contrast to bottom-up processing, where sensory input drives perception directly, top-down processes utilize stored information and contextual cues to interpret incoming stimuli. This interplay is critical for understanding how music is processed in the brain, as it involves not just auditory feature extraction but also incorporates learned structures and schemas to categorize and understand musical elements. The ALE meta-analytic review provides empirical evidence linking specific brain regions with top-down processing activities related to music perception. However, these sources highlight that while there is substantial theoretical work on the dichotomy between top-down and bottom-up processes, identifying precise neural correlates for top-down influence remains challenging.

## Key Findings

- Top-down processing relies heavily on prior knowledge and context to interpret sensory input.
- The integration of bottom-up and top-down processes is crucial for comprehensive music perception.
- Specific brain regions are associated with the implementation of top-down processing in music.

## Open Questions

- What are the exact neural mechanisms through which pre-existing knowledge influences auditory perception?
