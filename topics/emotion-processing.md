## Emotion Processing

The study of emotion processing encompasses the neural underpinnings and psychological impacts of emotional responses. Source 1, 'Telling me not to worry…' Hyperscanning and Neural Dynamics of Emotion Processing During Guided Imagery and Music, explores how emotional markers manifest through dual-EEG technology during Guided Imagery and Music (GIM) sessions, indicating the physiological and neural activity involved in emotion regulation. Meanwhile, Source 2, 'Emotion Processing and Mental Health,' delves into the implicit-emotional-reactions and neural-bases-of-emotions, highlighting the amygdala's critical role in processing emotional stimuli and its implications for mental health conditions such as anxiety and depression. Both sources agree on the importance of understanding the neural dynamics of emotion processing, yet they differ in their approaches, with Source 1 focusing on the therapeutic application of GIM and Source 2 on the broader implications of emotional regulation for mental health.

## Key Findings

- The amygdala plays a central role in emotion processing and regulation.
- Guided Imagery and Music (GIM) sessions can provide insights into the neural dynamics of emotion processing.
- Emotional markers and implicit emotional reactions are crucial for understanding the neural bases of emotions.
