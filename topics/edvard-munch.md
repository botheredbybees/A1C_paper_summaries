## Edvard Munch and the Representation of Illness

Edvard Munch's work, particularly his self-portraits and expressions of illness, serves as a profound commentary on the human condition and societal anxieties during the early 20th century. Source 1 focuses on Munch's self-portrait following the Spanish Flu, highlighting how illness and the pandemic culture were deeply embedded in his art. This reflects broader societal concerns and personal experiences of suffering. Source 2 examines the broader context of mental illness in visual art, linking Munch's work to the tortured artist archetype and the therapeutic potential of artistic expression. Both sources underscore Munch's role in the expressionist movement, where art becomes a medium for grappling with existential and psychological turmoil. The connection between personal and collective suffering is evident, with Munch's work serving not only as a personal catharsis but also as a societal mirror. The therapeutic value of art, as seen through Munch's practice, suggests a broader application of artistic expression in dealing with mental health issues and societal crises.

## Key Findings

- Edvard Munch's self-portraits and works reflect the societal anxieties and personal experiences of illness during the early 20th century.
- Munch's art is deeply rooted in the expressionist movement, which seeks to convey emotional and psychological states through vivid and often disturbing imagery.
- There is a therapeutic aspect to Munch's artistic practice, where creating art serves as a means of dealing with personal and societal issues.

## Open Questions

- How does Munch's work during the Spanish Flu period compare to contemporary artists addressing the current pandemic and its societal impact?
