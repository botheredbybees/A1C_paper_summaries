## Music and Dental Care

The integration of music into dental care is an emerging therapeutic intervention with implications for pain management and patient anxiety. Source 1 provides a meta-analysis highlighting that music can serve as a non-pharmacological analgesic, effectively reducing pain during dental procedures and minimizing patient anxiety. The study concludes that the soothing qualities of music activate the parasympathetic nervous system, which in turn helps to calm the body and reduce physiological stress responses. However, the application of music in dental settings should be tailored to individual patient preferences to maximize its efficacy. Source 2 shifts focus to the unique dental challenges faced by professional wind musicians, detailing issues such as lip area diseases and temporomandibular joint disorders associated with the prolonged and intense use of wind instruments. This source reveals that musicians often need specialized dental care, including adjustments to dental impressions to accommodate their embouchure, which can be a significant factor in the design and fitting of dental restorations. Despite the benefits of integrating music into dental care, there remains a need to address the specific oral health concerns of professional musicians, who may face higher risks of certain dental conditions due to their unique occupational demands.

## Key Findings

- Music can effectively reduce pain and anxiety during dental procedures.
- Professional wind musicians face unique dental health challenges.
- Tailoring music interventions to individual patient preferences enhances their effectiveness.

## Open Questions
