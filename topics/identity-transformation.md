## Identity Transformation in Healthcare

The concept of identity transformation in healthcare is explored through two distinct but interconnected lenses: portraiture as a method of research and care, and narrative medicine focusing on the 'It, Thee, and We' of disease and illness. Portraiture serves to highlight the human dimensions of care and foster empathy in healthcare by providing a visual and emotional perspective of patients' lived experiences. This approach is instrumental in combating social alienation and the existential crises patients often face due to illness. Narrative medicine, on the other hand, addresses the ways in which disease impacts an individual's sense of self and relationships, encapsulating the complex interplay between the individual's experience of illness ('It'), their personal identity ('Thee'), and their social networks and communities ('We'). Both methodologies emphasize the transformative potential of storytelling and visual arts in healthcare, aiding in the healing process by acknowledging and validating patients' emotional and psychological journeys.

## Key Findings

- Portraiture in healthcare enhances empathy and understanding of patients' experiences, addressing social alienation.
- Narrative medicine recognizes the multifaceted impact of illness on identity and social relationships.
- Both portraiture and narrative medicine facilitate identity transformation, aiding in the healing process.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of portraiture and narrative medicine be optimized to maximize identity transformation in patients?
