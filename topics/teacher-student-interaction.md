## Teacher-Student Interaction

The concept of teacher-student interaction is explored through two distinct but complementary approaches. Source 1 introduces 'Reach, Touch, and Teach', a three-step approach to education that emphasizes the importance of understanding students' concerns ('What'), reflecting on these concerns ('So What'), and acting on this reflection ('Now What'). This method fosters a dynamic and reflective learning process. Source 2 argues for the necessity of portraiture in medical education, advocating for a deep understanding of the perceptual skills and empathetic understanding required for clinical decision-making. While these sources differ in their specific focus—reflective practice versus clinical empathy—they both highlight the importance of a personalized and reflective approach in teacher-student interactions. Both approaches underscore the significance of building a relationship that is both respectful and understanding, thereby enhancing the educational experience.

## Key Findings

- Teacher-student interactions should be personalized and reflective.
- Reflective practice ('Reach, Touch, and Teach') enhances understanding and action.
- Empathetic understanding ('Perceptual-skills') is crucial for clinical decision-making.

## Open Questions

- How can the three-step reflective practice approach be integrated with clinical empathy in medical education?
