## Intangible Cultural Heritage

The concept of Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) is explored in both sources through the lenses of embroidery and craft practices. Source 1 focuses on Achara embroidery, a traditional practice in Georgia that embodies cultural values and skills passed down generations. This form of ICH serves as an identity marker for communities while also providing economic benefits. Similarly, Source 2 examines Lahthi production in Muzaffarpur, India, which is deeply rooted in local traditions and collective knowledge systems. Both sources highlight the importance of community-based practices in preserving cultural heritage and emphasize that such intangible elements are crucial to understanding a society's historical continuity and social fabric.

## Key Findings

- Intangible Cultural Heritage includes practices such as embroidery and craft production which have economic, social, and identity functions within communities.
- Community-based initiatives play a vital role in the preservation and promotion of ICH.

## Open Questions

- How can contemporary societies balance modernization with the need to preserve traditional crafts and their associated cultural heritage?
