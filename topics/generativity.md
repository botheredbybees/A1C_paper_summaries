## Generativity in Intergenerational Programs

The concept of generativity is explored across two intergenerational programs—one focusing on dance/movement and the other on music-making. Both emphasize creative activities that foster mutual learning, enhance social connections, and promote active aging among participants of different age groups. The dance/movement program in Melbourne highlights how structured yet flexible creative movement sessions can improve physical and emotional well-being for older adults. Meanwhile, the music-making program underscores the role of generativity in intergenerational engagement, where younger generations learn from older adults' life experiences while also contributing their youthful energy and knowledge. A common theme is the positive impact on social capital and individual wellbeing through these cross-generational interactions.

## Key Findings

- Intergenerational programs using creative activities like dance/movement and music-making promote mutual learning and enhance intergenerational relationships.
- Active aging is supported by such interventions, contributing to physical and emotional well-being across age groups.

## Open Questions

- How can the principles of generativity in these programs be effectively scaled or adapted for diverse cultural contexts?
