## Indigenous Cultural Healing

The topic of Indigenous cultural healing encompasses a range of practices aimed at addressing historical trauma and promoting mental well-being within indigenous communities. Source 1 discusses the 'Threads of Hope' project, which uses quilting as a therapeutic medium to address issues such as biopolitical assimilation and fractured homes among indigenous individuals. This approach not only provides a space for emotional expression but also reinforces cultural identity through traditional practices like sewing and storytelling embedded in the craft. Meanwhile, Source 2 highlights the importance of creative arts and play therapy in facilitating cultural adjustment and enhancing mental health outcomes. While both sources emphasize the role of creative interventions to foster healing from historical trauma, they differ slightly in their specific methods—quilt making versus broader creative therapies such as arts and play activities.

## Key Findings

- Creative arts provide a therapeutic medium for addressing historical trauma among indigenous populations
- Cultural identity reinforcement through traditional practices is crucial in healing processes
- Both quilting and creative play have positive impacts on mental well-being

## Open Questions

- How do specific cultural interventions differ in their effectiveness across various indigenous communities?
