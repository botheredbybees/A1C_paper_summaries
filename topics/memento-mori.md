## Memento Mori and the Therapeutic Benefits of Reflecting on Mortality

The concept of memento mori, a Latin phrase meaning 'remember that you must die,' is explored in both sources as a means of reflecting on the cycle of life and death. Source 1 examines the beauty found in decomposition within nature, highlighting the interconnectedness of life and death, while Source 2 delves into the therapeutic benefits of engaging with the concept of mortality through creativity. Both sources emphasize the importance of acknowledging and embracing the impermanence of life, suggesting that reflection on mortality can lead to a deeper appreciation for life's fleeting moments and the inevitability of death. However, there is a tension in the sources regarding the approach to contemplating mortality: Source 1 takes a more naturalistic and philosophical perspective, focusing on the beauty and inevitability of decomposition in nature, while Source 2 focuses on the therapeutic and creative processes associated with accepting one's mortality.

## Key Findings

- The concept of memento mori is linked to an appreciation of life's impermanence.
- Interconnectedness between life and death is highlighted in the context of nature's cycles.
- Engaging with the idea of mortality through creative means can have therapeutic benefits.

## Open Questions

- How do differing cultural and philosophical perspectives on mortality influence the effectiveness of memento mori practices?
