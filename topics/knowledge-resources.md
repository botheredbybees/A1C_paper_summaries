## The General Theory Of Verbal Humor

The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) is a foundational concept in humor studies, explored across multiple sources. GTVH emphasizes the role of incongruity in joke structures and comprehension. Working comedians demonstrate sensitivity to synonymy in jokes, highlighting the intricate relationship between word choice and comedic impact. The theory posits that humor arises from resolving cognitive dissonance or 'incongruity,' which can manifest through script oppositions or other linguistic mechanisms. GTVH's application extends beyond English language contexts, as seen in its relevance to translation studies where cultural nuances affect the transmission of humorous content. Despite its broad applicability, tensions arise regarding how strictly incongruity is defined and whether it fully accounts for all humor types.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) emphasizes the role of incongruity in joke structures and comprehension.
- Script oppositions play a significant role in GTVH, contributing to comedic effect through cognitive dissonance resolution.
- GTVH is applicable across different languages and cultures but faces challenges with precise definition and universality.

## Open Questions

- Does the General Theory of Verbal Humor adequately explain humor types that do not rely on incongruity?
