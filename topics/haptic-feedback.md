## Haptic Feedback in Assistive Technologies

The integration of haptic feedback into assistive technologies for sensory disabilities demonstrates a robust approach to enhancing perceptual capabilities across different sensory modalities. Source 1 highlights the use of vibrotactile gloves to improve pitch perception and melodic reproduction in hard-of-hearing individuals, emphasizing the role of multisensory integration. In contrast, Sources 2 and 3 explore haptic-assistive technologies for both auditory and visual disabilities, introducing concepts such as tactile-icons and sign-language adaptations. The Unfolding Space Glove exemplifies sensory substitution devices that transform visual information into tactile feedback, promoting brain plasticity and spatial awareness in the blind. Source 4 discusses a wearable vibrotactile system designed to offer personalized patterns for improved tactile perception, suggesting customization as a critical factor in effective haptic intervention. These studies collectively underscore the versatility of haptic feedback in addressing diverse sensory impairments but also hint at the complexities involved in translating these interventions into practical applications.

## Key Findings

- Haptic feedback technologies effectively enhance perceptual capabilities for individuals with auditory or visual disabilities through sensory substitution and integration techniques.
- Customized vibrotactile patterns are crucial for tailoring haptic feedback to individual user needs, improving the efficacy of assistive devices.
- Brain plasticity is positively influenced by repeated exposure to multisensory information delivered via haptic interfaces.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects and implications of sustained use of haptic-assistive technologies on brain structure and function?
