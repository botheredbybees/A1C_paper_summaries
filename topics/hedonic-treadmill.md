## Hedonic Treadmill

The concept of the hedonic treadmill is central to discussions on happiness and well-being. Sources consistently highlight that individuals tend to return to a baseline level of subjective well-being after experiencing positive or negative life changes, despite their initial heightened emotional responses (affect). This theory suggests that adaptation occurs quickly following significant life events, leading people back to a stable state of happiness or unhappiness regardless of external circumstances. Affective forecasting, the tendency to inaccurately predict how future events will impact one's emotions, complicates this process by often overestimating the lasting effect of transient experiences on long-term satisfaction.

## Key Findings

- The hedonic treadmill describes the phenomenon where individuals adapt quickly to changes in their lives and return to a stable level of happiness
- Affective forecasting plays a crucial role in misjudging future emotional states, which can lead to poor decision-making regarding life choices that are expected to enhance long-term well-being

## Open Questions

- How do individual differences such as personality traits or genetic predispositions influence the speed and extent of adaptation on the hedonic treadmill?
