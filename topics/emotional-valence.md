## Emotional Valence in Musical Experiences

The exploration of emotional valence through music and tactile stimulation highlights the multifaceted nature of human response to auditory stimuli. Source 1 examines how a commercial wearable tactile bass system influences emotions expressed by users, focusing on the audio-tactile substitution as an intervention to enhance emotional engagement with music. The study suggests that tactile experiences can alter perceived emotional valence, making positive feelings more pronounced and negative ones less impactful. In contrast, Source 2 delves into involuntary musical imagery repetition (IMIR), uncovering spontaneous cognitive processes associated with novel tunes. This source offers insights into the natural generation of music within the mind, revealing how unconscious processes contribute to emotional experiences through music without direct tactile intervention. Despite differences in methodology—Source 1 employs experimental manipulation while Source 2 uses qualitative interview analysis—both sources agree on the profound influence of musical stimuli on emotional valence and cognition.

## Key Findings

- Tactile stimulation can alter perceived emotional valence when listening to music, enhancing positive emotions and reducing negative ones.
- Involuntary musical imagery repetition (IMIR) provides insights into spontaneous cognitive processes that contribute to emotional experiences through music.
- Both sources highlight the significant role of musical stimuli in shaping emotional responses.

## Open Questions

- How do combined auditory and tactile interventions affect emotional valence compared to individual sensory input?
