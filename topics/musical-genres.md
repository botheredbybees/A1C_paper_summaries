## Musical Genres and Emotional Responses

The study of musical genres intersects with theories on intergenerational continuity of taste, the structure of music preferences, and emotional responses to sound. The intergenerational transmission of culture, particularly in relation to music (Source 1), highlights how parental influence shapes adolescent preferences, suggesting a continuity between generations through specific genres. This is further elaborated by the five-factor model which identifies key latent factors influencing musical appreciation across various genres (Source 2). Additionally, emotional responses to music are central to understanding individual differences and similarities in genre preference, with the Geneva Emotional Music Scale offering a comprehensive framework for measuring these emotions (Source 3). The interplay between cultural transmission and individual emotional preferences suggests that while there is continuity in taste influenced by parents, personal emotional experiences play a significant role in shaping an individual’s musical tastes beyond simple generational trends.

## Key Findings

- Parental influence significantly shapes adolescent music preferences, reflecting intergenerational continuity in taste.
- The five-factor model provides a framework for understanding the structure of music preferences across different genres.
- Emotional responses to music are pivotal in shaping individual musical tastes and can override generational influences.

## Open Questions

- How do emotional responses to music vary within specific cultural contexts, particularly where intergenerational transmission is strong?
