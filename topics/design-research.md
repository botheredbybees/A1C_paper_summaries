## Research Through Design

The concept of Research Through Design (RTD) is explored across sources as a unique methodology where the act of designing itself serves as both an inquiry and knowledge generation process. RTD emphasizes the 'designerly way of knowing' that goes beyond conventional research methods, engaging deeply with materials and communities through repair practices to build theories from practice. Source 1 discusses how such engagement fosters community bonding while employing circular design strategies for everyday artifacts, highlighting practical applications within a framework of sustainability and collective reflection. Together, these sources underscore the importance of material engagement as a key aspect of RTD, bridging theoretical frameworks with hands-on practices in communities focused on repairing and reusing items.

## Key Findings

- Research Through Design is a method that integrates design practice directly into research inquiry
- Material engagement and community bonding are crucial elements for effective Research Through Design
- Circular design strategies can be employed through repair practices to foster sustainability

## Open Questions

- How does the integration of traditional research methods enhance or detract from the unique insights generated by Research Through Design?
