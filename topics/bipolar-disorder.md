## Bipolar Disorder in Historical Context

The analysis of Vincent van Gogh's mental health across multiple sources reveals a complex interplay of potential diagnoses that includes bipolar mood disorder and borderline personality disorder. Both sources explore the retrospective diagnosis of van Gogh, with the first source emphasizing the use of modern diagnostic tools and the second focusing on a broader spectrum of potential conditions. The first source utilizes semi-structured diagnostic interviews to propose a diagnosis of bipolar disorder, while the second delves into other possibilities such as reactive depression and temporal lobe epilepsy, highlighting the challenges of retrospective diagnosis. Despite the differing approaches and proposed diagnoses, both sources agree on the need for a thorough re-evaluation of historical figures' mental health through contemporary medical knowledge. This synthesis underscores the evolving nature of mental health diagnosis and the importance of interdisciplinary approaches in understanding historical cases.

## Key Findings

- Retrospective diagnosis of historical figures' mental health is complex and often controversial.
- Modern diagnostic tools and approaches can provide new insights into historical cases, such as those of Vincent van Gogh.
- Potential diagnoses for van Gogh include bipolar disorder and other conditions like borderline personality disorder and temporal lobe epilepsy.

## Open Questions

- How can retrospective diagnosis be improved to account for the limitations of historical medical knowledge and the biases of contemporary mental health understanding?
