## Collaborative Creativity

Collaborative creativity, as explored in both sources, involves a collective effort to generate innovative ideas and solutions, often employing a democratic process. Source 1 highlights the practice through the lens of a haiku poetry session, emphasizing the importance of a democratic scoring system to ensure equitable participation. This approach aligns with the concept of 'primary imagination' from English Romanticism, which values individual expression and intuition. Source 2 expands the scope to organizational creativity, introducing the notion of 'secondary imagination', which involves the systematic application of imagination within structured contexts. Both sources underscore the potential of collaborative creativity to foster unique and valuable outcomes, yet highlight the challenges associated with managing diverse perceptual differences and ensuring democratic participation.

## Key Findings

- Collaborative creativity enhances innovation through collective effort and democratic processes.
- Primary and secondary imagination are pivotal for nurturing creativity in both artistic and organizational settings.
- Effective management of perceptual differences is crucial for successful collaborative creativity.

## Open Questions

- How can the balance between individual and collective creativity be optimized in collaborative settings?
