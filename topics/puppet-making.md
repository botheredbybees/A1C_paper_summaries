## Puppet Making in Art Therapy

The use of puppet making as an intervention within art therapy for promoting emotional empathy is explored in the provided sources. Source 1 details a specific case where puppet making was utilized to enhance both cognitive and affective empathy, particularly focusing on adolescents with autism. The Expressive Therapies Continuum (ETC) model underpins this approach, emphasizing how physical manipulations of objects can facilitate emotional expression and understanding. In contrast, Source 2 provides a broader context for the integration of puppet making in therapeutic settings but lacks direct engagement with empathy development. Both sources highlight the potential of puppet making as an intervention tool yet differ in their focus; one on clinical application and theoretical underpinnings while the other hints at its wider therapeutic utility.

## Key Findings

- Puppet making can be a valuable art therapy technique for fostering emotional empathy, especially in adolescents with autism.
- The Expressive Therapies Continuum (ETC) model offers a framework to understand how puppet manipulation contributes to emotional expression and understanding.

## Open Questions

- What are the broader therapeutic applications of puppet making beyond enhancing empathy, as suggested by Source 2?
