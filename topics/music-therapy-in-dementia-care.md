## Music Therapy

Music therapy stands out as a non-pharmacological intervention for people with dementia. Sources indicate that music engages regions of the brain spared by Alzheimer's disease, enhancing emotional memory and activating neural pathways involved in salience network functions. The practice leverages personalized musical interventions to comfort and soothe individuals at varying stages of dementia. Research highlights the role of rhythmic entrainment and dopamine release in improving mental well-being through neuroaesthetics and participatory arts practices. Music therapy can also aid in speech recovery, emotional expression, and social engagement. However, while music's potential is widely acknowledged, there remains a need for more precise understanding of its mechanisms and personalized approaches to maximize therapeutic benefits.

## Key Findings

- Music engages regions of the brain spared by Alzheimer's disease, enhancing neural pathways involved in salience network functions.
- Personalized musical interventions can comfort and soothe individuals at varying stages of dementia.
- Rhythmic entrainment and dopamine release contribute to improved mental well-being.
