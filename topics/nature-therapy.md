## Nature Therapy

Nature therapy is a practice aimed at healing and resilience-building through interaction with natural environments. Both sources highlight the importance of nature in promoting psychological well-being but emphasize different aspects of this intervention. Source 1 discusses 'A Healing Journey' where nature therapy serves as a medium for individuals to engage in personal development, fostering resilience and emotional recovery from trauma or illness. It underscores the restorative qualities of nature and its role in facilitating a transformative process that helps participants build inner strength and coping mechanisms. In contrast, Source 2 introduces the concept of 'biophilia,' which posits an innate human connection with other living systems as essential to mental health. This source also delves into specific elements within natural settings termed 'punctum'—unique features or objects in nature that capture one's attention and evoke emotional responses, contributing significantly to individual healing experiences.

## Key Findings

- Nature therapy facilitates resilience-building and emotional recovery through immersive experiences in natural environments.
- Biophilia theory suggests an intrinsic human need for connection with nature is vital for mental well-being.
- Unique elements of nature (punctum) can evoke profound personal responses, enhancing the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions.
