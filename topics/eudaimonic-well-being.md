## Eudaimonic Well-Being

The concept of eudaimonic well-being is a theoretical framework that emphasizes personal growth and fulfillment rather than merely the absence of distress or discomfort. Across several sources, it is evident that Maslow's hierarchy of needs plays a pivotal role in understanding eudaimonia by suggesting that self-actualization lies at the pinnacle of individual development (Source 2, Source 3). Sources also highlight the importance of reflective practice and appreciation of one’s surroundings as critical components for enhancing well-being among specific populations such as autistic youth (Source 1). Furthermore, theories like self-determination theory are discussed to explain how fulfilling psychological needs contributes to a sense of purpose and existential living (Sources 7, 8). The distinction between hedonic and eudaimonic well-being is also crucial; while hedonia focuses on pleasure-seeking behaviors, eudaimonia emphasizes personal growth and realization of one's potential (Source 4, Source 5). Despite this rich theoretical landscape, the sources reveal a lack of consensus in defining eudaimonic well-being and identifying measurable indicators that could be universally applied.

## Key Findings

- Maslow’s hierarchy of needs is foundational to understanding self-actualization and its role in achieving eudaimonic well-being.
- Reflective practice and appreciation for one's environment are beneficial for enhancing eudaimonic well-being, especially among youth with autism (Source 1).
- Self-determination theory underscores the importance of fulfilling psychological needs as a pathway to eudaimonia.

## Open Questions

- What are universally applicable indicators or measures of eudaimonic well-being?
