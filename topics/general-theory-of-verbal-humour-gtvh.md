## General Theory Of Verbal Humour

The General Theory of Verbal Humour (GTVH) serves as a foundational framework for understanding the mechanisms behind verbal humor, applicable across various cultural and linguistic contexts. Both sources emphasize the GTVH's role in dissecting the structural and semantic elements that contribute to humorous expressions. In 'Silence is the best answer for a bully: an exploration of humour techniques in selected Nigerian newspaper political cartoons', the theory is applied through semiotic analysis of political satire, highlighting how symbolic representations function within the context of humor. Conversely, 'Translation and Humour' explores GTVH's utility in understanding humor across different languages by focusing on knowledge resources necessary for effective translation of humorous content. While both sources leverage GTVH as a cornerstone theory, they demonstrate its versatility through distinct applications: one in semiotic analysis and the other in linguistic considerations.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humour (GTVH) provides a robust framework for analyzing humor across different contexts and cultures.
- Semiotic techniques are integral to understanding how symbolic representations contribute to humor in political satire.
- Translation of humor is facilitated by the GTVH through consideration of knowledge resources essential for comprehension.
