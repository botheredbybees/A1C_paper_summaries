## Declarative Memory

Declarative memory, a form of long-term memory, involves the storage and retrieval of facts and events that can be consciously recalled. The hippocampus is crucial in the formation and consolidation of declarative memories, as highlighted in 'Essentials of Cognitive Neuroscience.' Additionally, hippocampal activity and multivariate pattern analysis have been used to study this memory type, providing insights into its neural mechanisms. The role of the amygdala and its interactions with the prefrontal cortex, as mentioned in 'Chapter 17: Emotion,' are significant as they influence emotional memory processing and regulation. The amygdala's involvement in conditioned fear and the release of norepinephrine, modulated by the locus coeruleus, underscores the interplay between emotion and declarative memory. 'Neural Bases of Memory' further elucidates the cellular and molecular basis of memory, emphasizing Hebbian learning and long-term potentiation as mechanisms underlying memory consolidation and retrieval.

## Key Findings

- The hippocampus is central to the formation and consolidation of declarative memory.
- The amygdala plays a critical role in emotional memory processing, influencing declarative memory.
- Techniques such as multivariate pattern analysis provide new insights into the neural basis of declarative memory.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms through which the amygdala and prefrontal cortex interact to modulate emotional declarative memory?
