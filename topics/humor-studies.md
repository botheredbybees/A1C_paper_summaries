## Theoretical Frameworks Of Humor

The provided sources emphasize the theoretical underpinnings of humor, particularly focusing on Victor Raskin's General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) and Script Opposition Theory. The GTVH is highlighted across all texts for its foundational role in understanding verbal humor, which involves analyzing jokes through a structured script-based approach that includes both semantic scripts and metaknowledge resources to identify the incongruity necessary for humor. Source 2 applies this theory to analyze humorous elements within literary texts using time series analysis, suggesting that humor is strategically placed rather than randomly distributed. Meanwhile, Source 3 introduces Script Opposition Theory as a complementary model that focuses on the cognitive complexity involved in understanding opposition between scripts, further enriching our comprehension of how jokes create humor through semantic contrasts.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) is foundational for analyzing verbal humor through a script-based framework focusing on incongruity and metaknowledge.
- Script Opposition Theory complements GTVH by emphasizing cognitive complexity and the opposition between scripts to understand how humor arises from semantic contrasts.
- Humorous elements in literary texts are not randomly distributed but strategically placed, as indicated by time series analysis.

## Open Questions

- How can Script Opposition Theory be integrated with GTVH to provide a more comprehensive explanation of humor creation and understanding?
