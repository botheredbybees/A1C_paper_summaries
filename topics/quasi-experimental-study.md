## Quasi-Experimental Studies

Quasi-experimental studies, as discussed in both sources, are a type of research methodology that falls between observational and experimental studies. They are used when random assignment is not feasible or ethical, but researchers still wish to make causal inferences. The first source emphasizes the importance of pre-existing groups in these studies, highlighting the lack of random assignment as a key characteristic. The second source provides an example of a quasi-experimental study, focusing on the design and evaluation of a preventive program for musical performance anxiety. This program, aimed at improving mental health outcomes, utilized pre-existing groups of musicians, demonstrating the practical application of quasi-experimental design in the real world. While both sources agree on the methodological framework of quasi-experimental studies, there is a gap in the detailed application and generalizability of such studies across different fields.

## Key Findings

- Quasi-experimental studies lack random assignment due to practical or ethical constraints.
- Pre-existing groups are a defining feature of quasi-experimental studies.
- Quasi-experimental designs can be applied to evaluate preventive programs for mental health issues.

## Open Questions

- How do the limitations of quasi-experimental studies, such as the lack of random assignment, affect the generalizability and validity of their findings?
