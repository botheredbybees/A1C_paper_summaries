## Advanced Cancer Psychotherapy and Support

Studies on psychotherapeutic interventions for advanced cancer patients highlight the importance of culturally sensitive approaches and the focus on quality of life and spiritual well-being. A qualitative study emphasizes the centrality of family in modified dignity therapy, suggesting that cultural context and family relationships significantly influence patient experiences. On the other hand, a systematic review and meta-analysis of Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy (MCP) in adult patients with advanced cancer underscores the efficacy of MCP in enhancing spiritual well-being and reducing psychological distress. While both studies point to the importance of addressing existential concerns and providing emotional support, they differ in their emphasis on cultural and familial contexts versus a more broadly applicable approach to psychological and spiritual coping.

## Key Findings

- Culturally sensitive and family-centered therapy approaches are crucial for advanced cancer patients.
- Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy is effective in improving spiritual well-being and reducing psychological distress in advanced cancer patients.
- Existential and spiritual issues are central to the therapeutic needs of advanced cancer patients.

## Open Questions

- How can Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy be adapted to incorporate cultural and familial influences for more comprehensive support?
