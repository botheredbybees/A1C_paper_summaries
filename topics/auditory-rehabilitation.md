## Auditory Rehabilitation

The studies highlight the efficacy of auditory rehabilitation techniques that incorporate multisensory approaches. Both sources underscore the potential benefits of using sensory substitution devices for individuals experiencing hearing difficulties, whether it be through improved speech-in-noise understanding via audio-tactile sensory integration or enhanced pitch perception and melodic reproduction facilitated by vibrotactile gloves. These interventions leverage the Inverse Effectiveness Principle to maximize perceptual outcomes when auditory information is compromised. Despite promising results in controlled settings, there remains a need for further exploration of how these technologies can be integrated into daily life without cognitive overload.

## Key Findings

- Multisensory integration using sensory substitution devices shows promise in enhancing speech-in-noise understanding and pitch perception among individuals with hearing impairment.
- The Inverse Effectiveness Principle is critical to the success of auditory rehabilitation through multisensory approaches, as it allows for enhanced perceptual outcomes when direct sensory information is limited.

## Open Questions

- How can sensory substitution devices be effectively integrated into daily routines without causing cognitive overload or reducing spontaneous use?
