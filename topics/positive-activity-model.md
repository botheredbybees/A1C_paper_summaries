## Positive Activity Model

The Positive Activity Model (PAM) is a framework within positive psychology that emphasizes the role of activities in enhancing well-being. Both sources highlight the importance of engaging in activities that align with one's character strengths and values, known as self-concordant goals, to foster positive emotions and overall life satisfaction. The model suggests that such activities prevent hedonic adaptation and negate the negativity bias inherent in human cognition, thereby promoting sustained well-being. While Source 1 focuses on the theoretical underpinnings and mechanisms of PAM, Source 2 explores practical applications through the use of signature strengths in various activities. The interplay between theoretical insights and practical applications underscores the multifaceted nature of PAM in enhancing psychological well-being.

## Key Findings

- The Positive Activity Model (PAM) emphasizes the importance of engaging in activities aligned with one's character strengths and values to enhance well-being.
- PAM helps prevent hedonic adaptation and counters the negativity bias in human cognition.
- Signature strengths and self-concordant goals are critical components in the practical application of PAM.
