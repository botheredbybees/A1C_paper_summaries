## Constraints on Creativity

The study of creativity under constraints highlights a nuanced relationship where moderate levels of constraint can enhance creative output. Both sources explore this phenomenon but from different theoretical lenses: Source 1 presents an inverted U-shaped relationship between the degree of constraints and innovation, indicating that too few or too many constraints can stifle creativity. In contrast, Source 2 introduces the scarcity salience model, which explains how perceived resource limitations (constraints) influence cognitive processes leading to innovative problem-solving strategies. Both sources agree on the existence of mediating mechanisms such as mental blocks and psychological responses that either facilitate or inhibit creative outcomes.

## Key Findings

- Constraints can foster creativity when they are neither too restrictive nor too loose.
- Mediating mechanisms, such as mental blocks and psychological responses, play a significant role in how constraints affect creativity.
- The scarcity salience model offers insight into the cognitive processes involved in perceiving and responding to constraints.

## Open Questions

- How do different types of constraints (e.g., temporal vs. resource) uniquely impact creativity?
