## Therapeutic Clowning

Therapeutic clowning represents an innovative intervention in healthcare settings that leverages humor and play to alleviate psychological symptoms and enhance emotional well-being. Sources converge on the beneficial effects of therapeutic clowns, particularly for children undergoing medical procedures and older adults living in nursing homes. Therapeutic clowns are employed in various forms such as clown doctor rounds, which have been shown to reduce fear and anxiety among pediatric patients and their parents during anesthesia induction. Similarly, studies indicate that therapeutic clowns can improve social interactions and provide emotional support to elderly residents, contributing significantly to their sense of belonging and well-being. The intervention is also noted for its cultural relevance and ability to engage participants creatively, enhancing patient-care experiences through humor therapy and play therapy techniques. However, the effectiveness of online therapeutic clowning remains a less explored area, suggesting potential opportunities in virtual healthcare.

## Key Findings

- Therapeutic clowns can significantly reduce fear and anxiety among pediatric patients and their parents during medical procedures.
- Therapeutic clowns provide emotional support and improve social interactions for older adults in nursing homes, contributing to a sense of belonging.
- Creative engagement through clowning activities has cultural relevance and contributes positively to the psychological well-being of participants.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects and sustainability of therapeutic clowning interventions on patient outcomes?
