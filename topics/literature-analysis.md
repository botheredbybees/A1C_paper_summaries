## Dementia Representation in Literature and Film

The representation of dementia in literature and film highlights the intersection of personal identity and societal perception. Source 1 explores how literature and film construct Alzheimer's disease, emphasizing the subjective experience of dementia and the perception of a patient's core self. This source indicates that the portrayal of dementia often revolves around the narrative of loss and the fading of identity, which raises questions about how society perceives individuals with dementia and the ethics of representing their experiences. Source 2 delves into the narrative structure of 'Frankenstein,' exploring themes of knowledge pursuit and moral restraint. While this source does not directly address dementia, its exploration of ethical challenges and narrative structure provides a theoretical framework for analyzing the ethical considerations in representing individuals with dementia. Both sources contribute to the theoretical understanding of how literature and film engage with complex ethical issues related to dementia and the core self, highlighting the importance of ethical and moral considerations in the portrayal of such conditions.

## Key Findings

- The portrayal of dementia in literature and film often focuses on the narrative of loss and identity fading.
- The subjective experience of dementia is a central theme in literature and film.
- Ethical considerations are crucial when representing individuals with dementia.
