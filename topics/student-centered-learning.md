## Student-Centered Learning

Student-centered learning emphasizes the importance of the student in the educational process, engaging them actively in their own learning journey. The source on 'Reach, Touch, and Teach' by Terry Borton introduces a three-step approach to process education ('What, So What, Now What') that encourages students to think critically about their experiences and understand their learning processes deeply. This aligns with the philosophy of student-centered learning which values the learner's voice and perspectives. Another source discusses Visual Thinking Strategies, a methodology that further promotes student-centered learning by using visual arts to foster deeper understanding and critical thinking skills. These methodologies highlight the versatility and effectiveness of student-centered approaches in various educational settings, from traditional classroom environments to more innovative, arts-based learning contexts.

## Key Findings

- Student-centered learning involves empowering students in their learning process through critical reflection and active engagement.
- The three-step approach ('What, So What, Now What') is an effective method for fostering student-centered learning.
- Visual Thinking Strategies is a methodology that enhances student-centered learning through visual arts.
