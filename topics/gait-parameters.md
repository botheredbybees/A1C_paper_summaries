## Gait Parameters in Parkinson's Disease Rehabilitation

Gait parameters in Parkinson's disease (PD) are significantly influenced by various therapeutic interventions, including neurologic music therapy (NMT) and dance-based programs. NMT, as explored in the pilot study on Neurologic Music Therapy Training for Mobility and Stability Rehabilitation with Parkinson's Disease, utilizes techniques such as Rhythmic Auditory Stimulation (RAS), Patterned Sensory Enhancement (PSE), and Therapeutic Instrumental Music Performance (TIMP) to improve gait and mobility. These interventions are designed to enhance the rhythmic aspects of movement, which are often impaired in PD patients. The study highlights that NMT can positively impact gait parameters, suggesting a role for rhythmic cues in motor function rehabilitation. Meanwhile, the research on the effects of dance on gait and dual-task gait in PD demonstrates that dance-based programs can also significantly improve gait parameters and reduce dual-task interference, a common challenge for PD patients. The integration of rhythmic and musical elements in both NMT and dance therapies suggests a common mechanism involving the entrainment of motor rhythms, which could be a key factor in the rehabilitation of gait parameters. However, while both studies show promise, further research is needed to understand the long-term effects and comparative efficacy of these interventions. Additionally, the studies highlight the importance of individualized treatment approaches tailored to the unique needs and preferences of each PD patient.

## Key Findings

- Rhythmic auditory stimulation and dance-based programs both show promise in improving gait parameters in Parkinson's disease.
- Therapeutic interventions that incorporate rhythmic elements, such as music and dance, can enhance motor function in PD patients.
- Dual-task performance is improved with both NMT and dance interventions, indicating broader benefits beyond simple gait improvement.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects and comparative efficacy of rhythmic auditory stimulation and dance-based interventions on gait parameters in Parkinson's disease?
