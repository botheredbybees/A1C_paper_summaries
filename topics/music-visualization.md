## Music Visualization Through Sensory Substitution

MUSTEM is an innovative intervention that translates music into vibrotactile and visual signals to assist individuals with hearing impairments. The system employs psychoacoustic principles, such as loudness scaling, to accurately represent the auditory dimensions of music in a tactile format. This sensory substitution technique aims to preserve musical harmony mapping and enhance human-computer interaction. Despite being grounded in rigorous psychoacoustics and HCI design, MUSTEM faces challenges in achieving universal accessibility due to individual differences in perception and preference.

## Key Findings

- MUSTEM uses dual-modality (vibrotactile and visual) for sensory substitution of music.
- Psychoacoustic principles underpin the accurate translation of auditory signals into tactile representations.
- Enhanced human-computer interaction is a key goal in the design and implementation of MUSTEM.

## Open Questions

- How do individual differences in perception impact the effectiveness of sensory substitution systems like MUSTEM?
