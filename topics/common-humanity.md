## Common Humanity in Compassion and Self-Compassion

The concept of common humanity is a critical element in both compassion and self-compassion theories, emphasizing the shared human experience of suffering and imperfection. Across the provided sources, common humanity is discussed as a component of self-compassion, which is often practiced alongside mindfulness, aimed at fostering understanding and kindness towards oneself and others. Source 1 outlines the theoretical framework of self-compassion, emphasizing the role of common humanity in recognizing that everyone experiences pain and struggles, which can mitigate feelings of isolation and self-criticism. Source 2 further explores the measurement and application of self-compassion, reiterating the importance of common humanity in building a non-judgmental stance towards oneself and others. Source 3 introduces models of compassion and self-compassion, highlighting common humanity as a foundational principle in cultivating a broader sense of connection and empathy, beyond just self-directed kindness. Despite these shared themes, there is a need for further exploration into how common humanity can be effectively communicated and integrated into therapeutic and educational practices to enhance its impact.

## Key Findings

- Common humanity is a core component of both compassion and self-compassion theories, promoting understanding and acceptance of shared human experiences.
- Self-compassion and mindfulness practices are often used in tandem to foster greater kindness and understanding towards oneself and others.
- The measurement and application of self-compassion underscore the role of common humanity in mitigating self-criticism and fostering connection.

## Open Questions

- How can the concept of common humanity be effectively integrated into therapeutic and educational practices to maximize its benefits?
