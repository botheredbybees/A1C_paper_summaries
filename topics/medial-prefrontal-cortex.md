## Medial Prefrontal Cortex in Spontaneous Creativity

The medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) plays a significant role in spontaneous creativity and self-monitoring processes, as highlighted in both sources. The mPFC is juxtaposed with the lateral prefrontal cortex (lPFC), where the lPFC is involved in cognitive control and the mPFC is implicated in more social and emotional processing, including aspects of self-representation and self-awareness. Both sources emphasize the importance of the mPFC in the context of improvisational activities, suggesting that reduced activity in the mPFC might facilitate more spontaneous creative behavior by decreasing self-consciousness and inhibitions. However, the relationship between mPFC activity and creativity remains nuanced, with different contexts potentially modulating its role.

## Key Findings

- The medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) is crucial for spontaneous creativity and self-monitoring.
- Reduced activity in the mPFC is associated with increased spontaneous creative behavior.
- The mPFC is contrasted with the lateral prefrontal cortex (lPFC), which is involved in cognitive control.

## Open Questions

- How does the dynamic interplay between the medial prefrontal cortex and lateral prefrontal cortex influence creative processes in different contexts?
