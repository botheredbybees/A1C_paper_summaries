## Therapeutic Self-Portraiture

Therapeutic self-portraiture emerges as a powerful intervention for individuals to express and process complex emotional experiences, especially trauma. Both sources emphasize its effectiveness in digital contexts, suggesting adaptability and broad applicability. Source one highlights the use of re-enactment therapy and family therapy through photography, indicating that therapeutic self-portraits can be integrated into various clinical frameworks. In contrast, source two focuses on Jungian archetypes and color psychology to enhance emotional expression and narrative construction within the portraits. The integration of diverse psychological theories enriches the therapeutic potential of self-portraiture, making it a versatile intervention for mental health practitioners.

## Key Findings

- Therapeutic self-portraiture is an effective digital-age practice for individuals dealing with trauma and complex emotions.
- Re-enactment therapy and family therapy can utilize therapeutic photography to deepen the healing process.
- Incorporating Jungian archetypes and color psychology enhances the expressive potential of self-portraits.

## Open Questions
