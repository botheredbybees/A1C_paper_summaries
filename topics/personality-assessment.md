## Personality Assessment Through Well-being and Character Strengths

The exploration of personality assessment through the lens of well-being and character strengths reveals a multifaceted approach to understanding human personality. Source 1 discusses the six-factor model of well-being, which emphasizes a construct-oriented approach to psychological well-being through the Ryff Scales. This model highlights the multidimensionality of well-being, focusing on self-acceptance, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations with others, purpose in life, and autonomy. Conversely, Source 2 introduces the concept of character strengths, a framework that emphasizes virtues and values-in-action as key components of personality. This approach diverges from traditional personality theories by focusing on positive traits and their impact on personal and communal well-being. Despite the different theoretical orientations, both sources underscore the importance of multidimensional assessment in capturing the complexity of human personality. They highlight the need for holistic assessments that consider both positive and negative aspects of an individual's character and well-being. However, the integration of these approaches raises questions about the practical application and measurement of these constructs in clinical and research settings.

## Key Findings

- The six-factor model of well-being offers a comprehensive approach to assessing psychological well-being.
- Character strengths theory focuses on positive traits and their impact on personal and communal well-being.
- Both approaches emphasize the multidimensionality of human personality.

## Open Questions

- How can these theoretical approaches be effectively integrated into practical assessments and interventions in clinical settings?
