## Music and Arts Interventions for Serious Mental Illness

The integration of music and arts interventions as therapeutic modalities for individuals with serious mental illness highlights their potential in enhancing recovery and well-being. Source 1 presents a global scoping review emphasizing the role of music interventions, indicating that while these interventions show promise, conducting rigorous RCT studies to establish their efficacy remains challenging due to the complexity and variability of serious mental illness. In contrast, Source 2 offers a realist review focusing on community-based arts activities, aligning with the Social Identity Approach to Health and Transformative Learning Theory. It suggests that such activities facilitate identity change and recovery through transformative learning experiences. Both sources underscore the need for more robust empirical evidence to validate the effectiveness of these interventions, while also highlighting their potential to address social and psychological needs of individuals with serious mental illness.

## Key Findings

- Music and arts interventions offer promising avenues for supporting recovery in individuals with serious mental illness.
- Community-based arts activities can facilitate identity change and recovery through transformative learning.
- The efficacy of music interventions is challenging to establish due to the complexity of serious mental illness.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms through which music and arts interventions facilitate recovery and identity change in individuals with serious mental illness?
