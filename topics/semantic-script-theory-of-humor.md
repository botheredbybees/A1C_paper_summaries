## General Theory Of Verbal Humor

The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) is a comprehensive framework that explores the mechanisms behind verbal humor. Both sources emphasize the critical role of 'script opposition' and 'appropriate incongruity'. Script opposition refers to the contrast between two scripts or sets of expectations, creating unexpected shifts in perspective which often trigger humorous responses. Appropriate incongruity suggests that humor arises when an incongruous element is perceived as fitting within a particular context due to its relevance or cultural appropriateness. Despite these shared foundations, there are nuances regarding how each concept is applied and interpreted across different scenarios of humor.

## Key Findings

- Script opposition and appropriate incongruity are foundational concepts in the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH).
- Both sources highlight that humor emerges from the interplay between expectations and unexpected elements within a conversational or narrative context.

## Open Questions

- How do cultural differences affect the perception and application of script opposition and appropriate incongruity in GTVH?
