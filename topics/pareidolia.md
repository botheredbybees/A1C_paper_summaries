## Pareidolia

Pareidolia is a psychological phenomenon where the brain perceives significant images or patterns in random visual stimuli. It has been observed in various contexts, including art and occult practices during the period of 1857 to 1915 as noted by inkblot books (Source 1). These books suggest that pareidolia can be influenced by creative constraints and cultural expectations, leading viewers to see meaningful forms where none exist. In more contemporary settings, such as creativity and ageing activities (Source 2), pareidolia is explored as a tool for breaking down conventional boundaries of perception and encouraging innovative thinking within set parameters.

## Key Findings

- Pareidolia occurs when the brain interprets random patterns as significant images.
- Inkblot books from 1857 to 1915 highlight cultural influences on pareidolic perceptions.
- Creative constraints can enhance pareidolia, encouraging innovative and unconventional interpretations.

## Open Questions

- How do different cultures influence pareidolic experiences in visual stimuli?
