## Ethical Conduct in Indigenous Research

The ethical conduct in research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples is a multifaceted issue that encompasses informed consent, cultural protocols, and broader principles such as cultural sovereignty and community-led research. The guidelines underscore the necessity of adhering to ethical standards that respect the unique cultural and historical contexts of Indigenous communities. Both sources emphasize the importance of engaging with these communities in a manner that upholds their cultural protocols and ensures they have sovereignty over the research process. They highlight the need for researchers to approach their work with sensitivity and respect for Indigenous knowledge systems and methodologies, which may differ significantly from conventional Western research practices. These guidelines serve as a framework to ensure that research involving Indigenous communities is both ethically sound and respectful of cultural differences.

## Key Findings

- Ethical research with Indigenous communities requires adherence to cultural protocols and informed consent.
- Guidelines emphasize the importance of cultural sovereignty and community-led research.
- Respect for Indigenous knowledge systems and methodologies is crucial for ethical research.

## Open Questions

- How can researchers effectively navigate and incorporate cultural sovereignty into their research practices?
