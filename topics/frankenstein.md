## The Ethical Pursuit of Knowledge in 'Frankenstein'

Both sources engage with the theme of knowledge and its ethical implications, but approach it from different angles. Source 1 introduces the concept of disability within the narrative of 'Frankenstein', highlighting the societal and medical paradigms that marginalize the 'monster'. Source 2, on the other hand, focuses on Victor Frankenstein's moral and ethical challenges in his pursuit of scientific knowledge. While both sources do not directly intersect, they both underline the ethical and social consequences of unchecked knowledge and scientific exploration. This dual perspective enriches the understanding of the narrative's critique of the Enlightenment ideal of progress through science, emphasizing the importance of moral responsibility and social implications in scientific endeavors.

## Key Findings

- The narrative of 'Frankenstein' critiques the ethical implications of scientific knowledge.
- Disability within the story serves as a metaphor for the societal and ethical issues stemming from scientific progress.
- The pursuit of knowledge without ethical consideration can lead to moral and social consequences.

## Open Questions

- How can the social and medical models of disability be reconciled in the context of scientific and ethical challenges presented by 'Frankenstein'?
