## Cultural Resilience

The concept of cultural resilience is a critical framework for understanding how indigenous communities in the U.S. maintain and rejuvenate their cultural identities amidst ongoing challenges such as colonization, systemic oppression, and societal pressures to assimilate. This theory emphasizes the adaptive capacity of native cultures to persist through historical and contemporary adversities. Indigenous methodologies underscore the ethical imperatives of engaging with traditional knowledge systems respectfully while fostering community-led research initiatives that prioritize indigenous voices and values. The approach highlights the importance of decolonization efforts in promoting cultural resilience, aiming to dismantle oppressive structures and revive ancestral practices.

## Key Findings

- Cultural resilience is a crucial theoretical framework for understanding how indigenous communities maintain their cultural identities
- Indigenous methodologies are ethical approaches that prioritize respect for traditional knowledge systems
- Decolonization efforts play a vital role in enhancing cultural resilience by dismantling oppressive structures

## Open Questions

- How can contemporary research and practice better support the decolonization process and promote cultural resilience among indigenous communities?
