## Melodic Contour in Music

The concept of melodic contour is central to the theory and understanding of melodies. Both sources emphasize that melodic contours are integral to how listeners perceive and remember musical structures, with varying emphasis on their role relative to other elements like scales or short-term statistical properties. Source one highlights structural saliency in expressive accentuation as a key factor over short-term statistical learning, suggesting that the shape and direction of melodies (their contour) are more influential than immediate pattern recognition for aesthetic preference. In contrast, source two delves into how melodic contours and musical scales work together within a theory of memory for melodies, proposing that these components independently contribute to the cognitive process of recalling music. While both sources recognize the significance of melodic contours in shaping listeners' experiences and memories, they differ slightly in their focus: one on perceptual salience in expressive contexts, and the other on theoretical frameworks supporting memory.

## Key Findings

- Melodic contour plays a significant role in how musical structures are perceived and remembered.
- Structural saliency of melodic contours is emphasized over short-term statistical learning for aesthetic preferences.
- Musical scales and melodic contours independently contribute to theories about the recall of melodies.

## Open Questions

- How do structural salience and short-term statistical properties interact in shaping aesthetic responses to music?
