## Nordoff-Robbins Music Therapy

Nordoff-Robbins Music Therapy is distinguished by its emphasis on spontaneous musical interaction to foster creative potential and self-actualization in participants. This approach is rooted in the belief that intrinsic motivation and engagement in music-making can lead to therapeutic outcomes. The model, which is a core approach in music therapy, is compared and contrasted with other models, highlighting its unique contributions and principles. Key aspects include the concept of the 'music child,' which underscores the idea of innate musicality and the role of the music therapist in facilitating this aspect of the participant's personality. Feminist perspectives also influence the approach, advocating for a holistic view of the individual and the community. Despite its distinctiveness, Nordoff-Robbins Music Therapy is acknowledged as one of the foundational models in music therapy practice, yet it faces questions regarding its applicability across diverse populations and contexts.

## Key Findings

- Nordoff-Robbins Music Therapy emphasizes spontaneous musical interaction and the concept of the 'music child'.
- The approach is influenced by feminist perspectives, promoting a holistic view of the individual.
- Intrinsic motivation and engagement in music-making are central to the therapeutic process.

## Open Questions

- How does Nordoff-Robbins Music Therapy adapt to and benefit diverse populations beyond its traditional contexts?
