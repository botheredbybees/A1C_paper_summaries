## Cognitive Linguistic Approaches to Humor and Creativity

The provided sources explore various theoretical aspects of humor and creativity through the lens of cognitive linguistics. Source 1 delves into the construction of humor in stand-up comedy, emphasizing the role of metaphorical thinking and blending theory to create humorous effects. It highlights how comedians use conceptual mappings and blending techniques to generate unexpected juxtapositions that are perceived as humorous by an audience. Meanwhile, Source 2 introduces broader cognitive linguistic theories related to humor, such as script theory (SSTH) and the graded salience hypothesis, which explain why certain incongruities within scripts or schemas can elicit laughter. It discusses how these theoretical frameworks help in understanding the psychological processes that underpin humorous experiences. Lastly, Source 3 shifts focus to synaesthesia but still employs metaphorical thinking as a key concept, illustrating its importance across different cognitive phenomena beyond just humor. This source underscores the role of metaphors in shaping unique sensory and perceptual experiences, suggesting parallels between humor’s use of unexpected mappings and the cross-modal associations seen in synaesthetic creativity.

## Key Findings

- Metaphorical thinking plays a crucial role in both the creation and perception of humor and creative expressions like synaesthesia.
- Cognitive linguistic theories such as Script Theory of Humor (SSTH) and Conceptual Blending Theory provide frameworks for understanding how cognitive processes lead to humorous experiences or unconventional perceptions.
- Humor mechanisms involve recontextualizing familiar concepts in novel ways, often through the use of unexpected mappings that challenge standard script expectations.

## Open Questions

- How do different forms of synaesthesia manifest and are there specific types more amenable to cognitive linguistic analysis similar to humor theories?
