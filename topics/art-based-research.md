## Art-Based Research on Identity and Place

The provided sources illustrate the diverse ways in which art-based research engages with identity and place. Source one discusses 'sense-of-place' through drawing practices that often superficially explore cultural landscapes, emphasizing a need for more reflective practice to deepen understanding of one's connection to these places. In contrast, source two introduces walking as a critical embodied method to engage deeply with the concept of place, suggesting that movement and direct experience are crucial for meaningful inquiry into identity and environment. Both sources highlight the importance of embodiment in artistic research but differ in their approach—drawing versus walking—to explore personal and collective connections with place.

## Key Findings

- Drawing can be a superficial practice unless accompanied by reflective analysis to deepen understanding of cultural landscapes.
- Walking as an embodied practice offers profound insights into the interconnectedness between identity, embodiment, and place.

## Open Questions

- How do different art-based practices balance direct experiential engagement with theoretical reflection for more nuanced explorations of identity and place?
