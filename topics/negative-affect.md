## Negative Affect

Negative affect, encompassing distressing emotions such as anxiety, anger, and sadness, is a critical dimension of mental health studied across various interventions. Two interventions, loving-kindness and mindfulness, have shown particular promise in addressing negative affect. The systematic review and meta-analysis of loving-kindness interventions reveal significant reductions in negative affect, alongside improvements in positive affect and compassion. Meanwhile, informal or everyday mindfulness emphasizes the regular practice of being present and non-judgmentally aware of one’s experiences. This approach also aims to alleviate negative affect and enhance hedonic well-being. Both interventions highlight the importance of regular practice in managing and reducing negative affect, suggesting that the consistent application of these practices can lead to sustained improvements in mental health. However, while loving-kindness interventions focus on generating positive emotions and compassion towards oneself and others, everyday mindfulness emphasizes awareness and acceptance of momentary experiences. These interventions share a common goal of reducing negative affect, yet differ in their mechanisms and outcomes.

## Key Findings

- Loving-kindness interventions significantly reduce negative affect.
- Informal mindfulness practices also contribute to reducing negative affect.
- Both interventions enhance positive affect and well-being.

## Open Questions

- How do the mechanisms of loving-kindness and mindfulness differ in their impact on negative affect?
