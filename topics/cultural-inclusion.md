## Cultural Inclusion in Arts

The concept of cultural inclusion in arts is explored through two distinct but related perspectives: institutional programs aimed at broadening access and inclusion for diverse audiences, particularly those with specific needs like dementia or visual impairment, and historical analyses of cultural exclusion faced by immigrant artists. The Art Gallery of NSW's initiatives demonstrate a proactive approach to making cultural experiences accessible to all segments of the population. In contrast, the examination of Amsterdam's cultural policy reveals systemic barriers and gatekeeping mechanisms that historically excluded immigrant artists. Both sources highlight the persistent tension between inclusive and exclusive practices within the arts sector. While institutional efforts aim to bridge gaps and support underrepresented groups, historical trends suggest that such efforts are often met with resistance or systemic obstacles that challenge true inclusivity.

## Key Findings

- Institutional programs are pivotal in enhancing cultural accessibility for diverse groups.
- Historical context reveals systemic barriers to inclusion faced by immigrant artists.
- Cultural gatekeeping practices often hinder the full inclusion of marginalized communities.

## Open Questions

- How can institutions effectively dismantle systemic barriers to cultural inclusion for marginalized groups?
