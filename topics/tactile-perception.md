## Tactile Perception in Sensory Substitution

The exploration of tactile perception across the two sources reveals insights into how individuals adapt to sensory deprivation and leverage alternative sensory modalities. Source 1 highlights the development and application of a wearable vibrotactile system designed as an assistive technology for haptic feedback, emphasizing the potential for personalized vibrotactile patterns in enhancing user experience through touch-based interaction. This aligns with broader trends towards sensorimotor integration to support individuals with sensory impairments. In contrast, Source 2 delves into the neural mechanisms underlying tactile perception by examining the activation of primary visual cortex during Braille reading among blind subjects, indicating a remarkable degree of cortical plasticity and functional reorganization in response to sensory deprivation. Both studies underscore the adaptability of human sensory systems, particularly in compensatory roles that cross traditional sensory boundaries.

## Key Findings

- Wearable vibrotactile systems can serve as effective assistive technologies for enhancing tactile perception through personalized feedback patterns.
- The primary visual cortex of blind individuals shows activation during Braille reading, demonstrating cortical plasticity and functional reorganization in response to sensory deprivation.

## Open Questions

- How do individual differences influence the effectiveness and personalization of vibrotactile systems as assistive technologies?
