## Client Autonomy

The concept of client autonomy is integral to both the practice of art therapy for adults with dementia and person-centered (Rogerian) therapy. Both sources emphasize a client-focused approach, highlighting the importance of respecting and enhancing clients' independence and decision-making capabilities. Art therapy in healthcare settings aims to balance ethical principles such as nonmaleficence, beneficence, justice, and fidelity while supporting autonomy. Similarly, Rogerian therapy promotes empathy and unconditional positive regard to foster self-actualization and personal growth. Both interventions stress the importance of client-centered care that respects individuality and empowers clients to take an active role in their therapeutic process.

## Key Findings

- Client-centered approaches are crucial for respecting autonomy in therapy
- Ethical considerations such as nonmaleficence, beneficence, justice, and fidelity support the promotion of client autonomy in healthcare settings
- Empathy and unconditional positive regard enhance self-actualization and personal growth

## Open Questions

- How can therapeutic practices effectively balance ethical obligations with the promotion of client autonomy?
