## Directed Attention Fatigue

Directed attention fatigue (DAF) is a phenomenon observed when the prefrontal cortex, the part of the brain responsible for cognitive functions such as attention and decision-making, is overtaxed by constant demands for executive function and problem-solving skills. Sources indicate that DAF can be mitigated by engaging in activities that promote cognitive reserve, thereby enhancing one's ability to cope with cognitive demands. Reflexivity, the process of reflecting upon one's own thought processes, is highlighted as a critical component in understanding and managing DAF. Both sources emphasize the importance of cognitive reserve and executive functions in managing DAF, suggesting that interventions aimed at building cognitive reserve and enhancing executive functions can be beneficial in reducing the impact of DAF.

## Key Findings

- Directed attention fatigue (DAF) is exacerbated by prolonged engagement in activities requiring sustained executive function and problem-solving.
- Cognitive reserve and executive functions play a crucial role in mitigating the effects of DAF.
- Reflexivity is an important tool for managing and understanding DAF.
