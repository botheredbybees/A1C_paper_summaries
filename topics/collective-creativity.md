## Collective Creativity

The concept of collective creativity is explored in two distinct contexts. In the first source, 'Conceptualising and Understanding Artistic Creativity in the Dementias,' the notion of redefining creativity to encompass group efforts within dementia care settings is highlighted. This approach emphasizes a broadened understanding of creative expression that includes not just individual but also communal forms of artistry, which can be particularly beneficial for those with cognitive impairments. The second source, 'Collective Creativity and Wellbeing Dispositions: Children's Perceptions of Learning through Drama,' delves into the role of drama in fostering collective creativity among children. It suggests that activities like worldbuilding within a dramatic context not only enhance creative engagement but also promote emotional intelligence. Both studies underscore the importance of collaborative environments for nurturing creativity, albeit in different demographic and age groups.

## Key Findings

- Collective creativity is beneficial across various demographics, from elderly dementia patients to children.
- Creative activities that involve group interaction can enhance both cognitive and emotional health.
- Redefining creativity to include communal efforts offers new opportunities for therapeutic interventions.

## Open Questions

- How do different age groups or populations benefit uniquely from collective creative activities?
