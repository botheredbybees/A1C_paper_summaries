## Musical Composition and Hearing

The sources highlight the therapeutic benefits of musical composition for individuals with hearing impairments. Source 1 focuses on how composing music can serve as a therapeutic tool, aiding in personal identity formation and fostering collaborative dispositions among participants. In contrast, Source 2 delves into specific historical cases of Beethoven and Smetana to illustrate the concept of 'inner hearing' as a crucial aspect for composers with sensorineural deafness or other auditory impairments, emphasizing the role of auditory imagery in their creative process. Both sources underscore that despite physical challenges, individuals can harness their unique experiences to compose music, suggesting that musical composition serves not only an artistic but also a therapeutic purpose.

## Key Findings

- Musical composition has therapeutic benefits for personal identity and collaborative skills.
- Inner hearing is crucial for composers with auditory impairments.
- Auditory imagery plays a significant role in the creative process of deaf or hard-of-hearing composers.

## Open Questions

- How does musical composition impact individuals without obvious hearing issues, and can it serve as a universal therapeutic intervention?
