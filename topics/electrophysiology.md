## Electrophysiology in Cognitive Neuroscience

Electrophysiology, particularly electroencephalography (EEG), plays a pivotal role in understanding cognitive processes such as creativity and language. In jazz musicians, EEG studies reveal the importance of expectancy in human creativity, showing how anticipation and pattern recognition influence divergent thinking. Similarly, EEG studies on language processing support the Wernicke-Lichtheim model, which suggests that language comprehension and production involve distinct neural pathways. Predictive coding, a key concept in cognitive neuroscience, underpins both creativity and language comprehension by predicting future sensory inputs and adjusting predictions based on new information. However, while EEG provides valuable insights, it also presents challenges in isolating specific neural correlates due to its low spatial resolution. The integration of EEG with other methods, such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), is crucial for a comprehensive understanding of electrophysiological processes in cognitive functions.

## Key Findings

- Electroencephalography (EEG) is instrumental in studying cognitive processes like creativity and language.
- Expectancy and predictive coding are central to both creative thinking and language processing.
- EEG studies provide evidence supporting the Wernicke-Lichtheim model of language.

## Open Questions

- How can EEG be integrated with other neuroimaging techniques to better isolate specific neural correlates of cognitive processes?
