## The Bubonic Plague

The bubonic plague, caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, had a profound impact on medieval Europe, including demographic collapse and significant social upheavals. Both sources emphasize the devastation wrought by this disease, with Source 1 focusing on religious reforms and population shifts as consequences of the Black Death's aftermath. Meanwhile, Source 2 highlights the empirical approach to medicine that emerged in response to the plague’s spread, illustrating how practical experiences shaped medical understanding. Despite both sources agreeing on the catastrophic effects of the bubonic plague, they differ slightly in their emphases: one on social and religious changes while the other focuses more on medical responses.

## Key Findings

- The bubonic plague caused significant demographic collapse in medieval Europe
- Social upheaval was a common consequence of the Black Death's spread
- Religious reforms were initiated as a response to the pandemic

## Open Questions

- How did the shift towards an empirical approach to medicine during and after the bubonic plague influence long-term medical practices and theories?
