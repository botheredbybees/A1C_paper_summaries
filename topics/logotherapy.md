## Logotherapy

Logotherapy, an existentialist theory developed by Viktor Frankl, centers around the idea that the primary motivational force of an individual is the 'will to meaning,' which contrasts with the Freudian 'will to pleasure' and Adlerian 'will to power.' This theory posits that individuals are driven by a desire to find meaning in their lives, which can act as a counterbalance to the 'existential vacuum,' a state of emptiness and despair arising from a lack of meaning. Logotherapy emphasizes the importance of identifying and engaging in creative, experiential, and attitudinal values as means to achieve a sense of fulfillment and purpose. The concept of 'dereflection,' or stepping back from one's own thoughts to focus on external reality, is also integral to the practice of logotherapy. Across sources, the focus remains on the individual's quest for meaning as a pathway to psychological well-being, highlighting the role of existential values in personal growth and resilience.

## Key Findings

- Logotherapy emphasizes the 'will to meaning' as the primary motivational force in human behavior.
- The concept of 'existential vacuum' describes a state of emptiness resulting from a lack of meaning in life.
- Creative, experiential, and attitudinal values are critical pathways to finding meaning and achieving psychological well-being.

## Open Questions

- How does logotherapy address the potential for conflict between individual and societal values in the pursuit of meaning?
