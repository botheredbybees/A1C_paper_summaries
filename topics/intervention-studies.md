## Music Therapy as an Intervention for Dementia Care

Music therapy is increasingly recognized as a viable intervention for managing symptoms associated with dementia, including agitation. The Homeside trial, a significant example discussed, demonstrates the potential of embedding music therapy into care pathways for people with dementia to improve relationships and reduce agitation. This aligns with broader findings that interventions aimed at enhancing positive affect can effectively reduce stress and promote emotional balance. However, while music therapy offers clear benefits, there is a tension between the specific application to dementia and the broader efficacy of positive affect interventions in different contexts. The position paper on dementia care pathways emphasizes the need for targeted, personalized care, which contrasts with the more generalized approach of positive affect interventions aimed at a wider population.

## Key Findings

- Music therapy is effective in reducing agitation for people with dementia.
- Interventions that enhance positive affect can reduce stress and promote emotional balance.
- Embedding music therapy into care pathways improves relationships for dementia patients.

## Open Questions

- How can the principles of positive affect interventions be adapted for targeted use in dementia care pathways?
