## Humor and Cognitive Linguistics

The study of humor involves understanding its cognitive processes and linguistic structures. Source one introduces the Script Theory of Humor (SSTH) and explores how conceptualization processes shape our perception of humorous situations, while also touching upon the graded salience hypothesis which explains why certain aspects within a script stand out as more salient than others in creating humor. In contrast, source two delves into practical applications of humor through an analysis of Nigerian political cartoons, applying the General Theory of Verbal Humour (GTVH) to examine semiotic techniques and their use in political satire. While these sources differ in their theoretical frameworks—one focusing on cognitive linguistic approaches and the other on practical implementation—the underlying theme is consistent: both emphasize the multifaceted nature of humor that involves cultural context, language play, and social commentary.

## Key Findings

- Humor can be analyzed through cognitive linguistic theories such as SSTH and GTVH which highlight script-based understanding and salience in humor perception
- Cultural context significantly influences how humor is constructed and perceived, evident from the use of political satire in Nigerian cartoons

## Open Questions

- How do different cultural contexts impact the effectiveness and reception of specific humor theories like SSTH or GTVH?
