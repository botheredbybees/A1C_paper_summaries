## Verbal Humor

The studies collectively examine the 'General Theory of Verbal Humor' (GTVH) as a theoretical framework to understand various forms and contexts of verbal humor. GTVH serves as an overarching model in all sources, providing a structured approach to analyze joke synonymy among comedians, self-deprecating humor in political speeches, metaphorical humor in satirical news shows, and verbal humor in stand-up comedy routines. The theory is particularly useful for identifying the mechanisms of humor creation and appreciation, such as through the knowledge-resources concept that explains how audiences interpret jokes based on shared cultural or personal knowledge. However, while GTVH offers a robust structure for analyzing humor, there are limitations when applied to specific contexts like political satire or stand-up comedy performances, where additional pragmatic considerations such as audience dynamics and social norms significantly influence humor's reception.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) serves as a foundational framework for analyzing various forms of verbal humor across different contexts.
- Understanding the nuances of GTVH requires consideration of additional pragmatic factors like audience dynamics and social norms in specific comedic settings.
- Metaphorical humor and self-deprecating humor are analyzed through GTVH, highlighting its applicability to diverse types of humorous content.

## Open Questions
