## Cognitive Health in Aging

The concept of cognitive reserve and neural compensation emerges as a critical theoretical framework for understanding how older adults maintain cognitive function despite neurodegenerative conditions like Alzheimer's disease. This theory posits that individuals with higher cognitive reserves can exhibit fewer symptoms due to brain adaptability, compensating for neuronal loss. Enrichment programs aimed at enhancing fluid intelligence through cognitive training and bolstering executive functions are proposed as effective interventions to preserve or even enhance the functional capacity of older adults. These enrichment effects highlight the importance of life experiences and continued engagement in activities that promote creativity and mental health, underscoring the dynamic nature of cognitive aging beyond mere neurodegeneration.

## Key Findings

- Cognitive reserve is a protective factor against symptoms associated with Alzheimer's disease.
- Enrichment programs can improve executive functions and fluid intelligence in older adults.
- Creative engagement and continuous mental activity contribute to better cognitive health.

## Open Questions

- How do different life experiences uniquely influence the development of cognitive reserve?
