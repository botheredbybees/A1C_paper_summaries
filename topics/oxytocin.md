## Oxytocin in Singing and Well-Being

Studies focusing on the effects of singing on physiological and psychological well-being reveal a consistent pattern regarding the role of oxytocin. The first study explores the impact of singing in a choir versus solo singing on mood and levels of oxytocin and cortisol. It indicates that social interaction during choir singing significantly increases oxytocin levels, which are linked to positive mood changes. In contrast, solo singing does not produce the same increase in oxytocin. The second study further emphasizes the positive effects of singing on well-being, including reduced stress and improved mood, which are linked to higher oxytocin levels. Both studies highlight the importance of social interaction and the communal aspect of singing in enhancing oxytocin secretion, thereby contributing to well-being. However, there is a gap in understanding the precise mechanisms through which singing triggers oxytocin release and the extent to which this effect translates across different populations.

## Key Findings

- Social interaction during choir singing increases oxytocin levels more than solo singing.
- Singing contributes to well-being by reducing stress and improving mood.
- Higher oxytocin levels are associated with enhanced well-being during singing activities.

## Open Questions

- What are the precise physiological mechanisms through which singing increases oxytocin levels?
