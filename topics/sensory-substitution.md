## Sensory Substitution

The concept of sensory substitution encompasses various techniques to aid individuals with specific sensory impairments by translating information from one sense into another. MUSTEM exemplifies this approach by utilizing vibrotactile and visual feedback as a dual-modality system for music translation, enhancing the experience for those who are hearing impaired through psychoacoustic principles such as loudness scaling and musical harmony mapping. Another critical aspect of sensory substitution is its potential to engage neural plasticity—the brain's ability to reorganize itself in response to new information or changes in behavior—facilitating adaptation and enhancement of sensory perception capabilities. This adaptability is crucial for the development of assistive technologies that can significantly improve quality of life, as seen in applications like hearing loss management where sensory substitution aids facilitate more inclusive musical performance education.

## Key Findings

- Sensory substitution enhances perceptual experiences through cross-modal translation.
- Psychoacoustic principles such as loudness scaling and harmony mapping are applied to enhance vibrotactile feedback.
- Neural plasticity supports the integration of new sensory information, enabling adaptability in individuals using sensory substitution technologies.
