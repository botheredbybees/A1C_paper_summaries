## Default Mode Network (Dmn)

The Default Mode Network (DMN) is a key brain network active when individuals are not focused on the outside world and the brain is at rest, such as during daydreaming, mind wandering, or remembering past events. Across the three sources, DMN is explored in relation to brain development, cognitive reserve, sensory perception, and its role in Alzheimer's disease. Source 1 emphasizes the DMN's involvement in the Cognitive Reserve Theory, which suggests the brain's ability to compensate for aging and pathology through neural networks. Source 2 highlights the DMN's role in creative focus and sensory perception, indicating its importance in various cognitive processes beyond just rest. Source 3 delves into the impact of the APOEε4 allele on the DMN's functional connectivity, linking this to cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease. While each source approaches the DMN from a distinct angle, they collectively paint a picture of a complex network critical to both healthy and diseased brain function, particularly in aging and neurodegenerative conditions. The DMN's role in cognitive reserve, creativity, and its susceptibility to genetic risk factors underscores its significance in understanding brain health and disease.

## Key Findings

- The DMN plays a crucial role in both rest and active cognitive processes, such as creativity and memory.
- The DMN's functional connectivity is affected by genetic factors like the APOEε4 allele in Alzheimer's disease.
- The DMN is integral to the concept of cognitive reserve, which allows the brain to compensate for aging and pathology.

## Open Questions

- How does the DMN's role in cognitive reserve and creativity interplay with its susceptibility to genetic and environmental factors in neurodegenerative diseases?
