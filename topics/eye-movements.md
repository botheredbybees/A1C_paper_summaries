## Eye Movements in Cognitive Processing

The study of eye movements intersects with the broader field of cognitive neuroscience, focusing on how visual perception and attention are managed. Source 1 introduces the concept that perceiving bodily actions is an embodied experience, aligning with the enactivist theory, which suggests that perception and cognition are grounded in the interaction between the brain and the body. Source 2, however, delves into the specific neural mechanisms governing eye movements, such as the superior colliculus and the frontal eye field, which are involved in directing attention and controlling gaze. Together, these sources highlight the complexity of eye movement control and its role in cognitive processes, bridging the gap between theoretical frameworks and physiological mechanisms. However, there is a tension between the broad, holistic perspective offered by the PBA theory and the detailed neural analysis provided by source 2.

## Key Findings

- Eye movements are integral to cognitive processing, involving both high-level brain structures and specific neural pathways.
- The superior colliculus and frontal eye field play crucial roles in controlling eye movements and directing attention.
- Perceiving bodily actions involves a bidirectional relationship between perception and action, supporting an embodied cognition perspective.

## Open Questions

- How do the detailed neural mechanisms of eye movement control integrate with the broader theoretical framework of embodied cognition?
