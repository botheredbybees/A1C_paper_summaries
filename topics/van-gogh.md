## Vincent Van Gogh's Mental Health

The study of Vincent Van Gogh's mental health through artistic and medical lenses highlights the complexity of his condition and its influence on his artwork. Source 1 discusses Van Gogh as a 'tortured artist', linking his mental distress to his expressionist style, which reflects the internal turmoil he experienced. This aligns with Source 2's medical perspective, which uses diagnostic interviews to theorize that Van Gogh may have suffered from bipolar mood disorder and borderline personality disorder. Both sources agree on the importance of retrospective analysis and the need for a holistic approach to understanding the relationship between mental health and artistic expression. However, while Source 1 emphasizes the therapeutic aspects of art-making for mental health, Source 2 focuses more on the diagnostic and clinical analysis of Van Gogh's condition, suggesting a divergence in the application of mental health theories in different contexts.

## Key Findings

- Vincent Van Gogh's mental health is crucial to understanding his artistic expression.
- Diagnostic interviews and retrospective analysis provide insights into Van Gogh's potential mental health conditions.
- There is a therapeutic value in the act of creating art for individuals with mental health issues.

## Open Questions

- How do contemporary clinical theories about mental health conditions compare with the artistic interpretations of those conditions, as seen in Van Gogh's work?
