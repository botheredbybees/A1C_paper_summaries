## Material Engagement Theory

Material Engagement Theory (MET) is a conceptual framework that explores the interplay between human cognition and material engagement. Sources discuss MET's applicability in various contexts such as therapeutic practices, uncertainty of agency, and postphenomenological perspectives. The theory posits that cognitive processes are fundamentally intertwined with physical activities involving objects, suggesting that material interactions shape mental functions. One source highlights pottery making as a form of 'Handmade Therapy', emphasizing the hedonic impacts of engaging with materials like clay. Another delves into MET's relationship to postphenomenology and the concept of 'Homo faber'—a term denoting humanity’s inherent drive to create through material engagement. A third explores how playing with clay can challenge notions of agency, proposing that such activities illustrate enactive signification where meaning arises from interaction rather than pre-existing mental constructs.

## Key Findings

- Material Engagement Theory posits a reciprocal relationship between human cognition and physical engagement with objects.
- Engaging in manual activities like pottery making can have significant hedonic benefits for wellbeing.
- MET intersects with postphenomenology to explore the role of material engagement in shaping human identity.

## Open Questions

- How does Material Engagement Theory account for individual differences in how people engage with and are impacted by materials?
