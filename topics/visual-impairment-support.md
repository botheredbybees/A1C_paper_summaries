## Visual Impairment Support in Cultural Institutions

Visual impairment support in cultural institutions, such as museums and art galleries, emphasizes the importance of accessibility and inclusivity. Both the Art Gallery of NSW and the Louvre Abu Dhabi, through their initiatives like Access Programs and Tactile Studio, respectively, focus on providing sensory exploration and tactile engagement to enhance the visitor experience for individuals with visual impairments. These interventions not only cater to those with visual impairments but also extend to supporting individuals with dementia. Both sources highlight the need for inclusive designs that transcend traditional visual-centric approaches, embracing multi-sensory experiences to ensure broader accessibility and enjoyment of cultural heritage.

## Key Findings

- Cultural institutions are enhancing accessibility for visually impaired individuals through tactile and sensory engagement.
- Supporting measures extend beyond visual impairment to include sensory and cognitive challenges, such as dementia care.
- Inclusive designs in cultural institutions promote broader access and enjoyment for diverse audiences, enhancing the overall visitor experience.

## Open Questions

- How can cultural institutions further integrate multi-sensory experiences to cater to a wider range of disabilities and sensory needs?
