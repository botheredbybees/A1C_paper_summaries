## Recovery-Oriented Therapies

The synthesis of recovery-oriented therapies, particularly music therapy and art therapy, reveals a convergence in therapeutic goals and methodologies aimed at fostering recovery in individuals with mental health challenges. Both interventions emphasize the role of self-determination and motivational interviewing to enhance self-efficacy and manage ambivalence, crucial factors in initiating and maintaining recovery. The stages-of-change model is a recurring theme, indicating that these therapies are adaptable across different phases of recovery, from precontemplation to maintenance. However, while music therapy focuses on illness management and recovery-oriented care, art therapy places more emphasis on the stages-of-change model and the management of ambivalence inherent in substance abuse treatment. This indicates a nuanced approach where the specific therapeutic intervention may be tailored to the unique challenges faced by the individual in their recovery journey.

## Key Findings

- Recovery-oriented care benefits from self-determination theory and motivational interviewing to enhance self-efficacy.
- Both music therapy and art therapy utilize the stages-of-change model to address different phases of recovery.
- Art therapy specifically addresses ambivalence in substance abuse treatment, highlighting its unique role in recovery.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of music therapy and art therapy be optimized to address the full spectrum of recovery challenges?
