## General Theory Of Verbal Humor

The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) is a foundational concept explored across the sources, aiming to provide a comprehensive understanding of humor through various linguistic and semiotic techniques. GTVH examines the logical mechanism underlying humor creation and appreciation, emphasizing the role of scripts in humorous contexts. The theory suggests that humor arises from unexpected violations or distortions of expected patterns within established scripts and contextual frameworks. In political satire, such as in Nigerian newspaper cartoons, this theory is applied to dissect how semiotic techniques are employed to create humorous yet critical commentary on societal issues.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) provides a framework for understanding humor through logical mechanisms and script-based violations
- Semiotic techniques play a crucial role in the creation and interpretation of political satire in media contexts such as Nigerian newspaper cartoons

## Open Questions

- How can GTVH be further refined to account for cultural and linguistic differences in humor comprehension?
