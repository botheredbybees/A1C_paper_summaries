## Effects of Singing on Stress Hormones

The integration of findings from multiple sources highlights singing as an intervention that influences stress hormone levels and emotional states. Group singing, particularly in the context of dementia care programs, has shown to reduce stress hormone levels, including cortisol, and increase melatonin levels, which are associated with better sleep quality. The effects of singing extend beyond stress hormones to include an enhancement of emotional closeness between mother and infant, suggesting a broader impact on emotional well-being. Choir singing or listening also positively affects secretory immunoglobulin A levels and emotional state, as measured by the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule. This intervention appears to be beneficial across different populations, including dementia patients and mothers with infants, suggesting its potential utility in various care settings. However, the mechanisms underlying these benefits and the long-term effects of singing interventions require further investigation.

## Key Findings

- Singing reduces stress hormone levels, including cortisol, in various populations.
- Group singing enhances emotional closeness and well-being.
- Singing has positive effects on immune function, as indicated by increased secretory immunoglobulin A levels.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of singing interventions on stress hormone levels and emotional well-being?
