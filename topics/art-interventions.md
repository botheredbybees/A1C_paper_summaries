## Art Interventions

Art interventions are increasingly recognized as valuable therapeutic tools for diverse populations, including those with dementia and autism spectrum disorder. The MoMA Alzheimer's Project highlights the importance of cognitive engagement through educational tours and access to iconic artworks for people with dementia, suggesting that art can serve as a powerful medium for emotional and intellectual stimulation even in advanced stages of cognitive decline. In contrast, a scoping review on art interventions for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) emphasizes their potential to enhance performance skills and social interaction, integrating creative arts within the Occupational Therapy Practice Framework. While both sources underscore the therapeutic benefits of engaging with art, they diverge slightly in application: one focuses on passive engagement through museum tours while the other advocates for active creation as part of occupational therapy.

## Key Findings

- Art interventions can significantly improve cognitive and emotional well-being among individuals with dementia.
- Creative arts serve as effective tools within occupational therapy frameworks to enhance social interaction and performance skills in children with ASD.
- There is a growing body of evidence supporting the therapeutic benefits of art for various neurological conditions.

## Open Questions

- What are the comparative long-term outcomes of passive versus active engagement with art for individuals with cognitive impairments?
