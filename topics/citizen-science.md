## Citizen Science

The field of citizen science leverages the contributions of volunteers to gather large datasets for ecological research. Sources highlight diverse dimensions of this practice including data quality, participation factors, and technological enhancements. iNaturalist emerges as a key platform used across studies; it supports volunteer identification efforts (Research-Grade identifications) while facing challenges such as taxonomic accuracy and geographic precision. Studies reveal that participation is influenced by theoretical frameworks like the Capability Opportunity Motivation-Behaviour model, indicating complex interplays between personal motivations and structural opportunities. Technological advancements, particularly AI-driven detection systems, promise to enhance data quality and efficiency but require rigorous validation through human expertise. Open questions remain regarding the balance between technological support and traditional biological identification methods in ensuring reliable ecological studies.

## Key Findings

- iNaturalist is a pivotal platform for citizen science, supporting both volunteer contributions and scientific research.
- Participation in citizen science projects varies based on individual motivations and perceived capabilities as described by theoretical models such as the Capability Opportunity Motivation-Behaviour framework.
- Technological advancements like AI-based detection systems offer promising solutions to improve data accuracy and efficiency but also introduce challenges related to validation.

## Open Questions

- How can technological enhancements be effectively integrated with traditional methods of species identification to ensure reliable ecological research outputs?
