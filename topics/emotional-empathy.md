## Emotional Empathy

Both sources highlight the multifaceted approach to fostering emotional empathy through arts-based interventions. Source 1 discusses a randomized-controlled trial focusing on arts education as a means to improve disciplinary infractions and writing achievement among students, which indirectly suggests an improvement in emotional empathy due to enhanced social skills and understanding of others' emotions. Conversely, Source 2 delves into the specific application of art therapy, particularly through puppet-making activities tailored for adolescents with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). This intervention aims directly at promoting both cognitive and affective empathy within the framework of the Expressive Therapies Continuum (ETC), which integrates therapeutic approaches across various modalities. While Source 1 provides a broader perspective on educational benefits, including indirect emotional empathy enhancement, Source 2 offers a detailed case study focusing explicitly on targeted therapy for neurodivergent individuals.

## Key Findings

- Arts education and art therapy can positively influence the development of emotional empathy in different populations.
- Randomized controlled trials support the use of arts education as an intervention that leads to improvements in disciplinary infractions and writing achievement, which are indicators of social-emotional growth.
- Puppet-making within the context of art therapy can serve as a therapeutic tool for enhancing both cognitive and affective empathy in adolescents with autism.

## Open Questions

- How can the principles from educational arts programs be adapted to provide targeted interventions for neurodivergent populations, such as those mentioned in Source 2?
