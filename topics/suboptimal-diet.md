## Suboptimal Diet

The sources emphasize the significant impact of suboptimal dietary habits on public health, particularly in contributing to non-communicable diseases and mortality. A systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study reveals that poor diet is a leading risk factor globally. The studies highlight the disparity in dietary risks across different countries, with sub-Saharan Africa facing the highest mortality burden due to inadequate consumption of healthy foods such as fruits, vegetables, and milk. In contrast, high intake of sodium and processed meats contributes significantly to health issues in other regions. Despite these findings, there is a need for more targeted interventions that address specific dietary challenges faced by different populations.

## Key Findings

- Suboptimal diet is a leading risk factor for mortality globally.
- Disparities exist in dietary risks across countries.
- Specific food groups like fruits and vegetables play critical roles in mitigating health risks.

## Open Questions

- What are the most effective interventions to improve diets in regions with high suboptimal diet risks?
