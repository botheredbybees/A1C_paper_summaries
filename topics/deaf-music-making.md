## Music and Deafness

The exploration of music-making among individuals who are deaf emphasizes a shift from traditional medical models to more holistic social and cultural perspectives. Both sources highlight the importance of viewing disability as diversity rather than an impairment that needs fixing, focusing on how people with disabilities can engage in creative activities like music independently or collaboratively within their communities. The cognitive styles unique to deaf musicians underscored in these summaries suggest a rich tapestry of musical expression beyond auditory perception alone, challenging conventional notions about the prerequisites for engaging in music-making.

## Key Findings

- Disability is increasingly understood from a social and cultural perspective rather than through a medical lens.
- Music-making among individuals who are deaf can be viewed as a form of creativity and self-expression that transcends traditional auditory-centric approaches.
- Engaging with music provides cognitive benefits unique to the experiences of people who are deaf, enhancing their creative expression within communities.

## Open Questions

- What specific methodologies can better support the integration of deaf individuals into musical practices traditionally dominated by hearing participants?
