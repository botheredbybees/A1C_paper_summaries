## Little-c Creativity

The concept of 'little-c creativity' emphasizes everyday creative activities that are common in daily life rather than extraordinary or novel achievements. Source one introduces the four C model, including little-c creativity as a form of spontaneous and unstructured creativity not bound to professional contexts. This contrasts with big-C creativity, which is more about making significant contributions to fields such as science, literature, and art. In source two, the experience-sampling study highlights how personality traits influence everyday creative experiences, suggesting that certain individuals might engage in 'little-c' activities more frequently due to their dispositional tendencies towards openness or originality. The inventory of Creative Activities and Achievements (ICAA) from Source three provides a structured approach to measure real-life creativity, capturing both little-C and pro-C (creative professionals) aspects, indicating the importance of understanding everyday creativity within broader frameworks.

## Key Findings

- Little-c creativity involves spontaneous creative activities in daily life that are not confined to professional contexts.
- Personality traits such as openness play a significant role in engaging more frequently with 'little-c' creative behaviors.
- The ICAA offers a tool for assessing both little-C and pro-C creativity, underscoring the significance of everyday creativity.

## Open Questions

- How can measures like the ICAA be adapted to better capture cross-cultural differences in everyday creativity?
