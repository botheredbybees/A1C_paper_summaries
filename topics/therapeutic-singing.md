## Therapeutic Singing

Therapeutic singing is an intervention that leverages the neurological integration of speech, breathing, and swallowing to enhance cognitive functions and improve quality of life for individuals with Parkinson's Disease. Both sources emphasize the role of therapeutic singing as a means to improve swallowing, a critical yet challenging area for individuals with Parkinson's. The first source discusses the physiological benefits of singing on the vocal folds and cognitive enhancement through neurological integration. The second source introduces specific tools like the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) and swallowing quality of life (SWAL-QOL) to measure the efficacy of therapeutic singing. Both sources suggest that singing can serve as an early intervention to maintain or improve swallowing abilities, though they differ in the level of detail provided regarding the specific neurological mechanisms involved.

## Key Findings

- Therapeutic singing can enhance cognitive functions and improve quality of life for individuals with Parkinson's Disease.
- Therapeutic singing is a viable early intervention to maintain or improve swallowing abilities in individuals with Parkinson's.
- Specific tools like UPDRS and SWAL-QOL can be used to measure the efficacy of therapeutic singing in patients with Parkinson's.

## Open Questions

- What are the exact neurological mechanisms through which therapeutic singing improves swallowing and cognitive functions in Parkinson's patients?
