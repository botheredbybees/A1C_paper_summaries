## Perma Model

The PERMA model of well-being, introduced by Martin Seligman, is a framework for understanding psychological flourishing that includes five elements: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment. Both sources highlight the PERMA model's significance in contemporary positive psychology but also introduce critiques and extensions. Source 1 compares PERMA with the complete mental health model, noting cultural biases in well-being research that may affect the universal applicability of PERMA. Source 2 emphasizes the model's third element, Meaning, and discusses Steger's three-element model of meaning, which aligns with the PERMA model but adds depth to the concept of meaning. This integration suggests that while PERMA provides a foundational understanding of well-being, it may require cultural and theoretical refinements for broader applicability.

## Key Findings

- The PERMA model is a foundational framework for understanding well-being in positive psychology.
- The PERMA model's element of meaning aligns with Steger's three-element model of meaning.
- Cultural biases in well-being research may impact the universal applicability of the PERMA model.

## Open Questions
