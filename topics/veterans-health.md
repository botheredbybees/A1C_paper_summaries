## Veterans' Health Through Art and Non-Clinical Programs

The sources collectively highlight the multifaceted approach to enhancing veterans' health through creative arts interventions. Source 1 employs a Grounded Theory framework to delve into how Gulf War veterans represent their 'toxic wounds' visually, uncovering deep emotional and psychological impacts of military service through an Art Elicitation Technique. This work underscores the therapeutic value of artistic expression as a means for self-representation and healing from trauma that is not easily articulated verbally. In contrast, Source 2 shifts focus to institutional approaches by examining non-clinical arts programming within the Veterans Health Administration (VHA). It reveals a broader picture of how creative activities are integrated into holistic health care, engaging multiple stakeholders such as veterans, staff, and community partners in promoting well-being beyond traditional medical interventions. Both sources emphasize the importance of art-based therapeutic practices in addressing complex veteran issues, suggesting a convergence between individual artistic exploration and systemic health programming.

## Key Findings

- Artistic expression serves as an effective tool for self-representation and healing among veterans dealing with 'toxic wounds' from military service
- Non-clinical arts programs within the VHA contribute to holistic healthcare by engaging multiple stakeholders in well-being initiatives
- There is a growing recognition of creative therapies as integral components of veteran health care, complementing traditional medical approaches

## Open Questions

- How can individual-level therapeutic benefits from artistic expression be scaled up and integrated into broader institutional frameworks like the VHA's non-clinical arts programs?
