## Nature Exposure

The synthesis of sources on nature exposure reveals a consistent theme that interactions with natural environments offer psychological and physiological benefits. Both studies suggest that engaging in activities within green spaces can mitigate mental health issues such as rumination, which is linked to depression (Source 1). Additionally, Source 2 highlights that spending time outdoors not only provides a restorative environment but also contributes to vitamin D absorption and cognitive enhancement. The subgenual prefrontal cortex activation noted by Source 1 correlates with emotional regulation processes, indicating a neurological basis for the observed psychological benefits from nature engagement.

## Key Findings

- Nature exposure can reduce rumination, which is associated with decreased risk of depression.
- Spending time in natural environments offers physiological benefits such as increased vitamin D levels and cognitive improvements.
- There is evidence that outdoor activities stimulate a restorative environment beneficial for mental health.
