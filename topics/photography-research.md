## Photography as a Tool for Storytelling and Healing

The use of photography as an intervention to facilitate storytelling and healing is explored in both sources. Source 1 emphasizes the role of creative expression through photography as a therapeutic mechanism, specifically addressing mental health concerns and employing non-verbal communication to express complex emotions that words might not convey effectively. In contrast, Source 2 applies this concept to end-of-life care scenarios within intensive care units (ICUs), focusing on Meleis's Transitions Theory to understand the experiences of bereaved families undergoing significant life transitions due to loss. Both sources highlight photography's capacity as a medium for connecting deeply personal narratives with broader emotional and psychological contexts, underscoring its potential across various therapeutic and supportive environments.

## Key Findings

- Photography serves as an effective tool for non-verbal communication in expressing emotions related to mental health and life transitions.
- The application of photography can provide a unique perspective on end-of-life care experiences from both the caregiver's and patient's viewpoints.

## Open Questions

- What are the implications for expanding the use of photographic storytelling techniques in other therapeutic or support settings beyond mental health and end-of-life care?
