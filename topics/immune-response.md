## Immune Response in Mental Health Interventions

The integration of mental health interventions such as group drumming and choir singing shows promising effects on the immune response and mental well-being. Drumming interventions demonstrate reductions in inflammatory immune responses among individuals with mental health conditions, indicative of a positive impact on immune health. Choir singing and listening activities also influence the immune system, specifically by increasing levels of secretory immunoglobulin A (s-IgA) and reducing cortisol, which indicates an enhanced immune response and reduced stress. Additionally, singing as a therapeutic intervention has been shown to modulate mood, stress, and levels of cortisol and cytokines in both cancer patients and their caregivers. These interventions not only provide mental health benefits but also positively influence the immune system, suggesting a synergistic relationship between mental well-being and immune function. However, the mechanisms through which these activities influence the immune system are not entirely clear, and further research is needed to understand the underlying biological pathways.

## Key Findings

- Group drumming and choir singing interventions can reduce inflammation and stress levels, respectively, positively impacting the immune system.
- Therapeutic singing and drumming have broadened our understanding of the interplay between mental health and immune function.
- Activities like singing and drumming can significantly improve mood and stress, leading to better immune responses.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific biological mechanisms through which singing and drumming interventions improve immune response?
