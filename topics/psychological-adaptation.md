## Psychological Adaptation to Extreme Circumstances

Psychological adaptation in extreme circumstances, such as locked-in syndrome, illustrates the resilience and capacity of individuals to maintain a positive quality of life, despite severe physical limitations. Source 1 highlights the importance of psychological adjustment and social support in enhancing the quality of life for individuals with locked-in syndrome. This resilience is paralleled by the concept of happiness adaptation, or the 'hedonic treadmill', discussed in Source 2, which explains how humans adapt to changes in their life circumstances, maintaining a relatively stable level of happiness over time. However, the hedonic treadmill also suggests that continuous high levels of happiness might be unsustainable. Thus, while individuals can maintain a sense of well-being in challenging conditions, the mechanisms that support this adaptation may also limit the extent of sustained high levels of happiness or quality of life.

## Key Findings

- Individuals can maintain quality of life and psychological well-being in extreme conditions through social support and psychological adjustment.
- The concept of the hedonic treadmill explains how individuals adapt to changes in their circumstances, maintaining a stable level of happiness over time.
- Psychological adaptation mechanisms, such as the hedonic treadmill, may also limit the extent to which individuals can sustain high levels of happiness or quality of life.

## Open Questions

- How do the limitations of the hedonic treadmill impact the long-term psychological adaptation and quality of life of individuals in extreme circumstances?
