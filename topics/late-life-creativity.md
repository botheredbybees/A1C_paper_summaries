## Late-Life Creativity

The concept of late-life creativity encompasses the idea that older adults can maintain and even enhance their creative capacities throughout aging. Sources highlight various aspects such as cognitive resilience, community engagement, and social practices fostering creativity. The 'creative ager' emerges as a new cultural figure, challenging traditional views on aging by promoting anti-aging attitudes through continuous involvement in artistic activities. Studies also emphasize the importance of sustainable writing practices for poets, illustrating how late-life creativity involves negotiating one's place within established literary contexts. These findings collectively suggest that engaging in creative pursuits can significantly benefit older adults' quality of life and contribute to redefining cultural perceptions of aging.

## Key Findings

- Late-life creativity challenges traditional negative stereotypes about aging by promoting active engagement in artistic activities.
- Creative practices among the elderly are associated with cognitive resilience, enhancing mental health and well-being.
- The concept of 'creative ager' signifies a shift towards more positive cultural attitudes towards older adults.

## Open Questions

- How do societal structures and norms impact the ability of older adults to engage in creative activities?
