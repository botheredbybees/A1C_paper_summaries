## Stigma Reduction in Dementia

The documents emphasize the importance of reducing stigma surrounding dementia through various interventions such as language guidelines and dance therapy. Source one presents a set of language guidelines aimed at promoting cultural sensitivity, person-centered care, and ultimately reducing the social isolation often associated with dementia diagnoses. These guidelines advocate for respectful terminologies that focus on the individual's experience rather than their condition alone. Meanwhile, source two highlights an initiative by Canada's National Ballet School called 'Dancer Not Dementia,' which uses dance as a therapeutic intervention to challenge stereotypes about people living with dementia. The program aims to foster inclusivity and reduce stigma through art and movement therapy sessions open to both dancers and non-dancers who have been affected by dementia in any capacity. While these sources share the common goal of mitigating societal stigmas, they approach this objective from different angles: one through linguistic norms and the other via creative arts.

## Key Findings

- Language guidelines promote cultural sensitivity and person-centered care to reduce stigma
- Dance therapy can be used as an intervention to challenge stereotypes about dementia

## Open Questions

- How effective are language guidelines in changing public perceptions compared to experiential programs like dance therapy?
