## Emotional Integration

Emotional integration refers to the process of assimilating and expressing one's emotions in a coherent manner. Two key sources offer complementary insights on emotional integration: Necker's Smile, which focuses on the immediate affective consequences of early perceptual processes, highlights the intrinsic link between perception and affect. It suggests that emotions are an integral component of perceptual processes, thereby laying the groundwork for emotional integration as an ongoing process embedded in the fabric of human perception. On the other hand, Vocal Psychotherapy emphasizes the role of the voice in therapeutic settings for emotional integration, particularly through the concept of transitional objects. This method uses the voice as a medium to explore and integrate emotions, fostering a deeper understanding of oneself. Both sources converge on the importance of integrating emotional experiences seamlessly with cognitive and perceptual processes, thereby facilitating a more holistic understanding of emotional well-being.

## Key Findings

- Emotional integration is a fundamental process that involves the coherent assimilation of emotional experiences.
- Perception and affect are intertwined, with affect playing a crucial role in early perceptual processes.
- Vocal psychotherapy can serve as an effective method for emotional integration, using the voice as a transitional object.
