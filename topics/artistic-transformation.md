## Artistic Transformation

Artistic transformation, as explored through the lens of emerging makers and health and wellbeing perspectives, highlights the profound impact of creative engagement on individuals. Alice Walton's work emphasizes the use of surface texture and handbuilding techniques to transform not just materials but also the perception of art itself. This transformative approach is mirrored in the broader context of health and wellbeing, where artistic transformation is seen as a potent intervention that helps individuals engage with their disabilities in a creative and empowering manner. The iconography of disability, as discussed in the context of health and wellbeing, illustrates how artistic practices can serve as a means for individuals to express their experiences and transform societal perceptions of disability. Despite the shared emphasis on transformation, the sources highlight different aspects of this process, with Walton focusing on the tactile and material aspects of art, while the health and wellbeing perspective emphasizes the symbolic and societal impact of artistic practices.

## Key Findings

- Artistic transformation involves both material and symbolic aspects, reflecting a multifaceted approach to creative expression and engagement.
- Creative engagement can serve as a powerful intervention for individuals with disabilities, facilitating a redefinition of their experiences and societal perceptions.
- Surface texture and handbuilding techniques are key elements in the material aspect of artistic transformation.

## Open Questions

- How do the material and symbolic aspects of artistic transformation intersect and influence each other in the context of health and wellbeing?
