## Statistical Learning in Music

The concept of statistical learning in music is explored through two distinct but interconnected lenses. The first source delves into how listeners develop musical expectancies based on past experiences, which shape their emotional responses to music. This process hinges on the accumulation and analysis of patterns in auditory information over time. Conversely, the second source investigates the role of melodic contour in influencing expressive accentuation, finding that structural saliency plays a more significant role than short-term statistical properties in guiding aesthetic preferences. Despite this distinction, both sources emphasize the importance of statistical learning as a fundamental mechanism for processing musical elements, albeit with varying degrees of emphasis on either long-term or immediate pattern recognition.

## Key Findings

- Statistical learning is crucial in developing emotional responses to music based on accumulated patterns and expectations.
- Melodic contours can override short-term statistical properties in guiding expressive accentuation and aesthetic preferences.

## Open Questions

- How do long-term versus short-term statistical properties interact in influencing listeners' musical experiences?
