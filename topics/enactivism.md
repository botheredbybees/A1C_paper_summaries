## Enactivism

Enactivism, a theoretical framework within embodied cognition, posits that perception and cognition are inherently linked to bodily action and interaction with the environment. Both sources emphasize the dynamic, interactive nature of cognition, where the body and its actions are fundamental components. Source 1 underscores the concept that 'perceiving is bodily action' (PBA), suggesting that perception and action are inseparable and mutually constitutive. Source 2, however, challenges this view by examining the implications of locked-in syndrome, where cognitive functions persist despite the inability to engage in typical bodily actions. This raises questions about the extent to which enactivism can account for cognition in severely restricted bodily conditions. The debate highlights the need to refine enactivist theory to address the limitations and potential contradictions inherent in scenarios where bodily interaction is severely constrained.

## Key Findings

- Enactivism posits that perception and cognition are inseparable from bodily action and interaction with the environment.
- The sensorimotor approach within enactivism emphasizes the importance of the body in cognition.
- Locked-in syndrome poses a significant challenge to traditional enactivist views, questioning the necessity of bodily interaction for cognition.

## Open Questions

- How can enactivist theory be adapted to account for cognition in conditions where bodily interaction is severely restricted or absent?
