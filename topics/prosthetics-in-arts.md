## Prosthetics in the Arts

The integration of prosthetics in the arts, as explored in various creative works, reveals a rich interplay between physical adaptation and artistic expression. In 'Creative Adaptations in Performance,' the role of music therapy and rhythmic entrainment is highlighted, showcasing how therapeutic practices can be translated into artistic expressions that enhance health and wellbeing. The use of prosthetics in these performances not only aids in overcoming physical limitations but also serves as a medium for conveying deeper personal and social messages. Meanwhile, 'Five Open Mouths' delves into the concept of unwinding gesture, reflecting on the resilience and narrative of disability in art. This piece underscores the transformative power of dance in articulating the challenges and triumphs of living with prosthetics. Lastly, Lisa Bufano's work demonstrates the boundary-pushing nature of disability art through her suspension performances, where adapted stilts serve as an extension of her body, exploring themes of identity and agency. Despite varying artistic expressions, a common thread is the empowerment and self-representation that prosthetics bring to the artistic narrative. However, there is a noticeable tension between the therapeutic and performative aspects of prosthetic use, with each source highlighting different facets of how prosthetics can be integrated into artistic practices.

## Key Findings

- Prosthetics in the arts serve as a medium for personal and social narratives.
- Rhythmic entrainment and therapeutic practices can be creatively integrated into performances.
- Disability art often focuses on themes of resilience, identity, and agency.

## Open Questions

- How does the therapeutic use of prosthetics differ from its artistic expression in the context of disability art?
