## Stress Reactivity

The concept of stress reactivity is central in understanding the psychological and physiological responses to stressors in both habitual music listening and mindfulness practices. Both sources highlight the role of emotion regulation as a mediator in these contexts. The first source, 'Listening to music and physiological and psychological functioning,' emphasizes the impact of habitual music listening on emotion regulation and stress reactivity, suggesting that regular engagement with music can serve as a buffer against the negative effects of stress. The second source, 'How Mindfulness Works,' delves into the mechanisms by which mindfulness practices affect stress reactivity through emotional regulation, particularly through the technique of urge surfing, which helps in managing stress and emotional reactions without reacting impulsively. Both sources suggest that effective stress management involves strategies that enhance emotion regulation, leading to reduced stress reactivity. However, while both sources indicate the importance of stress reactivity, they offer different frameworks (music listening vs. mindfulness) to manage it, highlighting the need for a broader understanding of how various interventions can interact to mitigate stress.

## Key Findings

- Stress reactivity is influenced by emotion regulation strategies.
- Habitual music listening and mindfulness practices both serve to reduce stress reactivity.
- The technique of urge surfing in mindfulness helps in managing stress without reacting impulsively.

## Open Questions

- How do different interventions (music listening vs. mindfulness) interact to mitigate stress reactivity?
