## Altered States of Consciousness

The exploration of altered states of consciousness (ASC) reveals a complex interplay between psychological and neurological dimensions. Source 1 focuses on the role of music in inducing and understanding ASC, particularly in the context of absorption and flow states, suggesting that music can facilitate a state where cognitive and emotional processes are redirected in a focused, yet creative manner. This narrative review highlights the broader significance of ASC for understanding phenomena such as mind-wandering and creative thought, indicating that ASC can provide insights into the nature of consciousness beyond the normal waking state. Conversely, Source 2 delves into the neural correlates of consciousness, examining how different brain states manifest in altered consciousness. Utilizing functional neuroimaging, this source identifies specific neural signatures that distinguish ASC from normal wakefulness, emphasizing the importance of neural activity patterns in understanding ASC. Together, these sources underscore the multidisciplinary nature of ASC research, bridging psychological theories with neuroscientific evidence to provide a comprehensive understanding of how ASC is both experienced and processed within the brain.

## Key Findings

- Music can induce altered states of consciousness and facilitate psychological processes such as absorption and flow.
- Functional neuroimaging techniques are crucial for identifying the neural correlates of altered states of consciousness.
- Understanding altered states of consciousness provides insights into cognitive processes such as mind-wandering and creativity.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific neural mechanisms that underlie the psychological experience of altered states of consciousness induced by music?
