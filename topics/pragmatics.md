## Pragmatics in Stand-Up Comedy

The application of pragmatics to analyze humor in stand-up comedy highlights the role of maxim violations as a primary mechanism for generating laughter. Source 1, 'Maxim Violations and Humor in Trevor Noah's Stand-Up Comedy,' uses Grice's Cooperative Principle to examine how breaking conversational maxims (such as quality, quantity, relation, and manner) can lead to humorous effects when expectations are subverted. In contrast, Source 2 introduces the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH), which categorizes verbal humor into five types but focuses specifically on implicature and metaphorical language, suggesting that GTVH complements rather than contradicts the Cooperative Principle by providing a more comprehensive framework for understanding verbal humor's complexity. Both sources concur that violations of conversational norms are crucial to humor generation; however, Source 2 offers a broader classification system that considers additional linguistic elements beyond simple maxim violation.

## Key Findings

- Maxim violations play a significant role in generating humor in stand-up comedy.
- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) provides a more detailed framework for understanding the mechanisms behind verbal humor, including implicature and metaphorical language.
- Both theories of humor coexist by highlighting different aspects of how conversational norms are challenged to produce laughter.

## Open Questions

- How do different cultural contexts influence the effectiveness of maxim violations in generating humor?
