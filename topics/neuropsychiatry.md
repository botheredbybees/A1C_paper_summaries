## Nonpharmacological Interventions in Neuropsychiatry

The provided sources highlight the use of nonpharmacological interventions such as music-based and art therapies to address neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS) in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment. Source 1 reports on a specific trial that utilizes individualized music therapy for hospitalized older adults, noting its effectiveness as a nonpharmacological treatment. Similarly, source 2 emphasizes the importance of neurologically informed, evidence-based art therapy to improve quality of life and manage NPS in dementia care. A systematic review presented in Source 4 supports the efficacy of such interventions on cognitive function and QoL among older adults with dementia. These studies collectively demonstrate the therapeutic potential of individualized approaches that cater to person-centered care, an idea underscored by the concept of neuroaesthetics (Source 3). However, while these sources affirm the benefits of nonpharmacological treatments, there is a noted gap in understanding their mechanisms and long-term effects.

## Key Findings

- Nonpharmacological interventions such as music and art therapy are effective in managing neuropsychiatric symptoms.
- Individualized approaches tailored to person-centered care show promise in improving quality of life for patients with dementia or cognitive impairment.
- Neurologically informed practices can enhance the effectiveness of nonpharmacological treatments, suggesting a need for more targeted therapies based on neuroscience principles.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects and underlying mechanisms of nonpharmacological interventions in neuropsychiatry?
