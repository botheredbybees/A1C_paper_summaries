## Subjective Experience in Dementia and Consciousness

The exploration of subjective experience through the lens of dementia and consciousness highlights the intricate relationship between the mind and body in understanding personal and collective meaning. Source 1 emphasizes the importance of the body as a medium for articulating meaning in dementia care, focusing on the collective subjective experiences and situational awareness of patients and caregivers. This perspective underscores the role of physical and social interactions in conveying and interpreting subjective experiences. In contrast, Source 2 delves into the neuroscientific foundations of consciousness, proposing the global neuronal workspace theory to explain how consciousness emerges from the interaction of various cognitive processes. This theory suggests that unconscious processing and attentional mechanisms play critical roles in shaping subjective experiences. The interplay between these theories reveals the complexity of understanding subjective experience, necessitating a multifaceted approach that bridges the gap between neurological and experiential perspectives.

## Key Findings

- The body and physical interaction play a crucial role in the articulation of subjective experience, particularly in the context of dementia care.
- Unconscious processing and attentional mechanisms are fundamental to the neuroscientific understanding of subjective experience.
- A holistic approach that integrates neuroscientific evidence and experiential understanding is essential for comprehending subjective experience.

## Open Questions

- How can the global neuronal workspace theory be applied to better understand and support the subjective experiences of individuals with dementia?
