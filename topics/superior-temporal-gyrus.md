## Superior Temporal Gyrus in Music and Speech Processing

The superior temporal gyrus (STG) plays a central role in both music and speech processing, as highlighted by multiple sources. Source 1 presents a case study demonstrating the critical role of the STG in maintaining musical function, even in the context of brain tumor surgery. This aligns with the findings in Source 2, which identifies the STG as a key region for the neural overlap of singing and speaking, indicating its importance in auditory processing and vocal production. Source 3 further confirms the STG's significance, highlighting its interaction with the anterior planum polare and superior temporal sulcus in the neural representation of music and voice. These sources collectively underscore the multifaceted role of the STG in cognitive functions related to auditory perception and production.

## Key Findings

- The superior temporal gyrus (STG) is crucial for auditory processing and vocal production.
- STG is involved in both music and speech processing, showing neural overlap between singing and speaking.
- The STG interacts with other brain regions, such as the anterior planum polare and superior temporal sulcus, to represent music and voice.

## Open Questions
