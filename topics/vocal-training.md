## Vocal Training and Neural Adaptations

Vocal training, as explored in the context of singing and wellbeing, and the specialized practice of opera singing, showcases significant neural adaptations that impact both motor-sensory integration and interoception. The integration of vocal fold oscillation during singing not only enhances motor skills but also improves sensory integration, highlighting the role of singing in overall wellbeing. Studies on opera singers demonstrate experience-dependent changes in brain function, including neural reorganization, which are observed through the use of functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). These findings suggest that vocal training can lead to profound changes in the brain, enhancing both physical and cognitive capabilities. However, the extent to which these adaptations can be generalized to non-professional singing contexts remains an open question.

## Key Findings

- Vocal training, particularly singing, enhances motor-sensory integration and promotes overall wellbeing.
- Professional opera singing leads to experience-dependent changes in brain function, as evidenced by neural reorganization and increased interoception.
- Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) is a valuable tool for understanding neural adaptations in vocal training.

## Open Questions

- To what extent do the neural adaptations observed in professional opera singers translate to amateur or recreational singing contexts?
