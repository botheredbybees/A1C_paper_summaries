## Consonance and Dissonance

The concept of consonance and dissonance plays a central role in understanding musical perception and memory. Source 1 explores the relationship between consonant intervals, which are perceived as more harmonious, and dissonant intervals, seen as less so, based on their frequency relations. This source also emphasizes the importance of long-term memory templates, indicating that listeners have stored patterns for what constitutes consonance and dissonance. In contrast, Source 2 broadens this understanding to include tonal hierarchies, suggesting a structured way in which consonance and dissonance are organized within musical contexts. It draws on linguistic theories applied to music to explain how these structures influence perception and memory processes, highlighting the cognitive basis of such auditory experiences.

## Key Findings

- Consonance-dissonance is fundamentally linked with frequency relations between tones.
- Long-term memory templates play a crucial role in recognizing consonant intervals as more harmonious than dissonant ones.
- Tonal hierarchies provide structured organization for understanding consonance and dissonance within musical contexts.
