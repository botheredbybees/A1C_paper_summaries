## Music Consumption

The sociology of music consumption, as explored by Bourdieu, emphasizes the role of cultural capital and class inequality in shaping musical tastes and consumption patterns. This perspective highlights how individuals use music as a means of social distinction, reinforcing societal hierarchies. In contrast, the social and applied psychology of music consumption focuses on understanding music consumption within its social and behavioral contexts, employing interdisciplinary methodologies to study the intricate relationship between musical behavior and social interactions. While both fields acknowledge the influence of social context on music consumption, they diverge in their primary emphasis: Bourdieu's work focuses on the sociological implications of cultural capital and class structure, whereas social and applied psychology approaches highlight the dynamic interplay between personal and social factors in musical behavior.

## Key Findings

- Music consumption is influenced by cultural capital and class inequality.
- Social and applied psychology offers a framework for understanding the behavioral aspects of music consumption within social contexts.

## Open Questions

- How do the sociological and psychological approaches to music consumption complement each other in understanding the complex dynamics of music in society?
