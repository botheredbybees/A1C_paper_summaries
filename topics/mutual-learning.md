## Mutual Learning Across Generations and Abilities

The concept of mutual learning emerges as a significant theme in both sources, indicating its broad applicability across different contexts. Source 1 focuses on intergenerational music-making as an intervention promoting active ageing among children and older adults through generativity, emphasizing the exchange of skills and experiences between generations. In contrast, Source 2 describes an exhibition initiative that brings artists together to foster a better understanding of intellectual disability, highlighting mutual learning in transcending power dynamics within artistic communities. Both sources underscore the transformative potential of reciprocal educational processes where participants are both teachers and learners, leading to personal growth and societal enrichment.

## Key Findings

- Intergenerational music-making fosters mutual learning by enabling skill exchange between different age groups.
- Exhibitions can serve as platforms for artists with diverse backgrounds to engage in mutual learning experiences.
- Mutual learning initiatives promote active ageing through generativity and personal development.

## Open Questions

- How do transcending power dynamics influence the effectiveness of mutual learning initiatives?
