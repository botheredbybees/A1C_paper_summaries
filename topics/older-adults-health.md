## Health Benefits of Creative Activities for Older Adults

Research highlights the diverse health benefits derived from creative activities such as dance movement therapy and arts engagement among older adults. Source one reports on a randomized controlled trial focusing on mild dementia patients, revealing that both dance movement therapy and physical exercise positively influence diurnal cortisol slopes—a physiological marker of stress—indicating a potential nonpharmacological intervention for psychological wellbeing. Meanwhile, source two, a systematic review, underscores the broader impact of arts and creativity in later life, linking these activities to improvements in cognitive function and social connectedness, alongside enhancements in physical balance. Both sources collectively indicate that creative engagement can serve as an effective supplementary treatment option for older adults experiencing mild cognitive decline or those seeking to maintain their overall health.

## Key Findings

- Creative interventions such as dance movement therapy and arts participation can positively impact the physiological stress markers in older adults with mild dementia.
- Engaging in artistic activities enhances physical balance, cognitive function, and social connectedness among older people, contributing significantly to their overall wellbeing.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of creative interventions on health outcomes for older adults without pre-existing conditions?
