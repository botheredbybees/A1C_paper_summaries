## Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy

Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy (MCP) emerges as a therapeutic intervention aimed at addressing existential distress and enhancing spiritual well-being in individuals facing severe illness, particularly those with advanced cancer. Both sources emphasize the critical role of finding meaning in the face of suffering, which is central to MCP's efficacy. The systematic review and meta-analysis in Source 1 highlight significant improvements in patients' spiritual well-being and quality of life, alongside reductions in psychological distress. Source 2 elaborates on the concept of existential distress, indicating that MCP helps individuals confront and find meaning in their suffering, thereby alleviating hopelessness. However, while both sources affirm the intervention's positive impacts, they also hint at the need for more detailed exploration into the specific mechanisms through which MCP brings about these changes.

## Key Findings

- Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy improves spiritual well-being and quality of life in patients with advanced cancer.
- MCP reduces psychological distress and existential suffering in patients.
- Finding meaning in suffering is a key component of MCP's efficacy.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms through which Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy enhances spiritual well-being and reduces existential distress?
