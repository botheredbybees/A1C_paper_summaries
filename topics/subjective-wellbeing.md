## Subjective Wellbeing

The concept of subjective wellbeing (SWB) is explored through two distinct but related therapeutic approaches: knitting as implicit spirituality and positive expressive writing. Both methods aim to enhance SWB by engaging individuals in meaningful activities that foster a sense of connection, purpose, and self-reflection. Knitting, described as an 'implicit religion,' provides a tangible form of spiritual expression and community engagement, which can contribute positively to mental health and overall life satisfaction (Source 1). Positive expressive writing interventions, on the other hand, involve techniques such as best possible self interventions that encourage individuals to imagine their ideal future selves and write about it. This practice has been shown to improve SWB by reducing symptoms of depression and anxiety, enhancing mood, and increasing feelings of positivity (Source 2). Despite differences in methodology, both approaches highlight the importance of creative expression and mindfulness practices as key components in achieving higher levels of subjective wellbeing.

## Key Findings

- Knitting as an implicit spiritual practice can enhance mental health and life satisfaction by fostering a sense of community and purpose.
- Positive expressive writing interventions, such as best possible self exercises, are effective in improving mood and reducing symptoms of depression.
- Both knitting and positive expressive writing share common goals in promoting subjective wellbeing through the mechanisms of creative expression and mindfulness.

## Open Questions

- How do cultural contexts influence the effectiveness of knitting or positive expressive writing interventions on subjective wellbeing?
