## Neuropsychiatric Symptoms in Dementia Management

The studies reviewed indicate that both art-based interventions and dance-specific activities demonstrate effectiveness in managing neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS) among older adults with dementia. Art-based interventions, such as visual arts therapy, improve not only cognitive function but also the overall quality of life (QoL). Dance-Specific Activity (DSA), on the other hand, is specifically tailored to address NPS, suggesting a more targeted approach for managing symptoms like agitation and depression. Both methods highlight the importance of non-pharmacological interventions in dementia care, emphasizing their role beyond symptom management by enhancing QoL and providing meaningful social engagement opportunities.

## Key Findings

- Art-based interventions can improve cognitive function and quality of life among older adults with dementia.
- Dance-specific activity is effective in managing neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS) in people living with dementia, offering a targeted approach to symptom management.
- Non-pharmacological interventions such as art therapy and dance offer meaningful engagement opportunities that enhance the overall well-being of individuals with dementia.

## Open Questions

- What are the comparative effectiveness levels of different types of non-pharmacological interventions (e.g., art-based versus dance-specific activities) in managing specific neuropsychiatric symptoms among people with dementia?
