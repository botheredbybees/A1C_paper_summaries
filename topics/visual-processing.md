## Visual Processing Adaptation

Visual processing, particularly in the context of color perception and affective responses, is explored across two sources. The first source delves into human color perception, focusing on the role of cone photoreceptors and the concept of color adaptation, which refers to how the visual system adjusts to different lighting conditions. The second source introduces the immediate affective consequences of early perceptual processes through the lens of Necker's smile, emphasizing that affect is an integral part of perception. Both sources highlight the complexity and adaptability of human visual processing, suggesting that visual stimuli not only influence perception but also have immediate emotional impacts. The integration of gestalt theory in the latter source complements the understanding of visual processing by adding a layer of holistic interpretation, indicating that perception involves more than just sensory input but also cognitive and emotional processing.

## Key Findings

- Color adaptation involves changes in the visual system's sensitivity to adjust to varying lighting conditions.
- Affective responses are an inherent part of early perceptual processes, not just a subsequent reaction.
- The integration of gestalt theory provides a broader framework for understanding visual processing as a holistic experience.
