## Justice in Healthcare and Research Ethics

The concept of justice is a fundamental ethical principle in both healthcare and research, addressing issues of fairness, equity, and rights. In the context of art therapy for adults with dementia, justice is concerned with ensuring fair treatment and respect for the autonomy of patients. This includes considerations of beneficence, nonmaleficence, and fidelity, ensuring that therapy is beneficial, avoids harm, and maintains trust. In psychological research, justice pertains to the broader principles of research merit and integrity, emphasizing the importance of fair selection and treatment of participants. Both contexts highlight the necessity of equitable treatment and the need to balance individual rights with societal benefits, while acknowledging the complexities and nuances involved in applying justice to diverse populations and settings. The synthesis reveals a consistent theme of justice as a crucial ethical pillar, though it also underscores the challenges in its practical application across different settings and patient groups.

## Key Findings

- Justice is a central ethical principle in both healthcare and research, emphasizing fairness and equity.
- In healthcare, justice is concerned with respecting patient autonomy and ensuring fair treatment.
- In research, justice pertains to the integrity and merit of the research, including fair participant treatment.

## Open Questions

- How can the principle of justice be effectively balanced with other ethical principles like beneficence and nonmaleficence in the complex setting of dementia care?
