## Singing-Based Therapies for Respiratory Diseases

Singing-based therapies present a promising intervention for individuals suffering from respiratory diseases, particularly chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Sources highlight the potential of these therapies to improve both physical and mental wellbeing. The interventions involve group singing activities which are shown to enhance social bonding among participants, contributing positively to their quality of life. Additionally, singing-based therapies are noted for their ability to trigger neurochemical releases that can alleviate symptoms and improve overall wellbeing. However, while these therapies show promise, their effectiveness varies across different patient populations, and more research is needed to standardize protocols and measure outcomes accurately.

## Key Findings

- Singing-based therapies improve the quality of life for COPD patients.
- Group singing enhances social bonding and emotional wellbeing.
- Singing triggers neurochemical releases that can alleviate symptoms of respiratory diseases.

## Open Questions

- How do singing-based therapies impact different subpopulations of respiratory disease patients?
