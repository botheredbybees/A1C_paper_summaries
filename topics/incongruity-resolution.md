## Incongruity-Resolution in Humor

The theory of incongruity resolution underpins both the understanding and appreciation of absurdist humour and the broader mechanics of humor processing. Source one delves into how absurdist humour deliberately disrupts expectations, creating a sense of confusion or absurdity that is then resolved through mental processes to integrate these disruptions into coherent narratives. This process of resolving incongruity is posited as central to triggering laughter and enjoyment from such humour. In source two, the neural basis for humor processing is discussed, revealing that the resolution of incongruities activates specific regions in the brain associated with reward systems, indicating a physiological basis for why resolving unexpected situations leads to pleasure or amusement. Both sources highlight the cognitive effort required to perceive and process humour as a form of problem-solving activity within the mind. However, while there is agreement on the importance of incongruity resolution for humor appreciation, there remains some ambiguity regarding how different types of resolutions (e.g., those in absurdist versus conventional jokes) might be processed differently by the brain.

## Key Findings

- Incongruity resolution plays a crucial role in triggering laughter and amusement.
- The resolution of incongruities activates the reward system in the brain, suggesting a physiological basis for humor appreciation.
- Absurdist humour relies on deliberate disruption followed by mental processing to integrate unexpected elements into coherent narratives.

## Open Questions

- How do different types of incongruity resolutions (e.g., absurdist vs. conventional) influence neural activity patterns and humor perception?
