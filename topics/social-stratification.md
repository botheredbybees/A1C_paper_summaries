## Social Stratification

Social stratification, as explored in the sources, revolves around the hierarchical ordering of individuals or groups into social categories with unequal access to resources and opportunities. Cultural capital, as discussed in the cultural capital transcript, plays a critical role in social stratification. It refers to non-financial social assets, such as education, intellectual taste, and knowledge, that promote social mobility beyond economic means alone. The introduction to sociology text further elaborates on sociological theories that explain how social stratification is perpetuated through the socialization process and cultural phenomena. Both sources highlight the multifaceted nature of social stratification, indicating that it is not merely economic but also involves cultural and social elements that influence an individual's social status and mobility.

## Key Findings

- Social stratification involves more than just economic factors; it includes cultural and social elements that contribute to an individual's status.
- Cultural capital is a significant non-economic resource that influences social mobility.
- The socialization process and cultural phenomena are critical in perpetuating social stratification.

## Open Questions

- How do the interplay of cultural and economic factors influence the perpetuation and transformation of social stratification?
