## Endorphins and Health

Endorphins, neurotransmitters often associated with pain relief and pleasure, are discussed in both music therapy and physical activity contexts. In music therapy, endorphins are seen as a critical biological factor in mitigating pain and enhancing quality of life. The biopsychosocial model, explored in the context of physical activity, integrates endorphins as a key physiological component in health, emphasizing the broader environmental and psychological factors that influence endorphin production. Both sources highlight the significance of endorphins in enhancing well-being but differ in their focus—music therapy specifically on pain and mortality, while physical activity emphasizes a holistic approach including environmental influences. This divergence suggests varied applications of endorphins in health-related practices, underscoring the multifaceted role of endorphins in maintaining and enhancing human health.

## Key Findings

- Endorphins play a crucial role in pain management and enhancing well-being.
- Music therapy and physical activity are distinct practices that leverage the benefits of endorphins.
- The biopsychosocial model provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the impact of endorphins on health.

## Open Questions

- How do different activities or therapies, such as music therapy and physical exercise, uniquely influence endorphin levels and health outcomes?
