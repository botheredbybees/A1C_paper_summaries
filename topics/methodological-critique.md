## Methodological Critique Of Arts-Based Interventions

The two sources engage deeply with the concept of methodological critique from different angles. Source 1 critiques arts-based interventions by highlighting challenges such as randomization and confounding factors, emphasizing the importance of a clear definition of 'art' in research design. In contrast, Source 2 introduces 'Research Through Design,' which advocates for a more fluid and iterative methodology that embraces the designerly way of knowing to build theories through practice rather than adhering strictly to standard research methods. Both sources underscore the need for rigorous yet flexible methodologies in exploring complex phenomena like arts-based interventions.

## Key Findings

- Randomization is challenging in studies involving subjective experiences such as those in arts-based interventions.
- The definition of 'art' can significantly influence the outcomes and interpretation of arts-based intervention research.
- Research through design offers a methodological approach that incorporates iterative practice to build theories, moving beyond traditional experimental methods.

## Open Questions

- How can researchers reconcile the need for rigor in methodology with the inherent flexibility required when studying subjective experiences like those involved in arts-based interventions?
