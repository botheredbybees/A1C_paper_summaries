## Philosophical Critique Of Health And Wellbeing Interventions

The two sources engage with the philosophical underpinnings of health and wellbeing interventions, offering critical perspectives that emphasize the importance of methodological rigor and value-driven approaches. Source 1 critiques the 'Creative Health' report for its emphasis on evidence-based approaches, advocating for greater transparency in methodological processes to ensure the validity of claims made about arts-based interventions. In contrast, Source 2 explores the Aristotelian-Thomistic philosophy as a framework to critique contemporary psychology's values-in-action (VIA) classification, suggesting a need for a more nuanced understanding of virtues and strengths within the context of political communities. Both sources highlight the necessity of considering philosophical foundations in the development and implementation of health and wellbeing interventions, underscoring the importance of ethical and methodological considerations.

## Key Findings

- The value of arts-based interventions in health and wellbeing is increasingly recognized, but their effectiveness can only be assessed through transparent and rigorous methodologies.
- The Aristotelian-Thomistic philosophical framework offers a valuable critique of contemporary psychology's approach to character strengths and virtues, highlighting the need for a more holistic understanding of human flourishing.
- Both sources underscore the importance of considering philosophical underpinnings when developing and implementing health and wellbeing interventions.

## Open Questions

- How can philosophical frameworks like Aristotelian-Thomism be effectively integrated into evidence-based approaches to ensure both methodological rigor and ethical considerations in health and wellbeing interventions?
