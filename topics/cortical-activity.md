## Cortical Activity in Cognitive Neuroscience

Cortical activity, a central theme in cognitive neuroscience, involves the dynamic processes occurring in the cerebral cortex that underpin higher cognitive functions. Sources converge on the role of cortical activity in consciousness and its regulation through neural networks. The reticular activating system, a network of neurons in the brainstem, modulates cortical activity, influencing states of consciousness. This activity is further elaborated by theoretical frameworks such as Global Workspace Theory, Recurrent Processing Theory, and Integrated Information Theory. These theories propose that cortical activity is crucial for integrating information across different brain regions, thereby facilitating cognitive processes and consciousness. However, while these theories provide valuable insights, they also present conceptual challenges, such as the exact mechanisms of information integration and the operational definition of consciousness.

## Key Findings

- Cortical activity is central to understanding the neural basis of consciousness.
- The reticular activating system plays a critical role in regulating cortical activity and consciousness.
- Theoretical frameworks such as Global Workspace Theory, Recurrent Processing Theory, and Integrated Information Theory explain the role of cortical activity in information integration and cognitive function.

## Open Questions

- What are the exact mechanisms by which cortical activity integrates information across brain regions to facilitate consciousness?
