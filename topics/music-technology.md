## Music Technology in Feminist HCI and Immersive Experiences

The exploration of music technology through feminist Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) principles and the concept of immersion reveals a rich tapestry of methodological approaches. Source 1 delves into DIY music hardware development from a feminist perspective, emphasizing design semantics and the narrative construction that challenges traditional HCI practices. This approach highlights how feminist HCI can serve as a lens to critique and redesign technology to better address social inequalities, particularly in the context of music creation tools where gendered narratives are often perpetuated by existing technologies. In contrast, Source 2 introduces the concept of material drift within the framework of 4E cognition theory, which posits that cognitive processes are embedded in the body, environment, and extended artifacts such as cameras used to create audiovisual compositions from found objects. This source underscores how technology itself can be a catalyst for creative expression when users engage with it beyond its initial design intent, suggesting an intersection between feminist HCI's focus on empowering marginalized voices through technology and the fluid nature of material engagement. Source 3 transitions the discussion into virtual reality (VR) spaces, where music technologies are utilized to foster immersive experiences that enhance inclusivity in musical practices. This source bridges the gap between physical DIY hardware hacking and digital environments by emphasizing how VR can democratize access to sophisticated music production tools, thereby echoing feminist HCI's aim of reducing barriers to entry for marginalized groups through technology. However, despite the thematic convergence towards more inclusive technological design, there remains a tension between ensuring that immersive technologies truly serve diverse user needs without perpetuating new forms of exclusion based on digital literacy or physical capability.

## Key Findings

- Feminist HCI principles challenge traditional narratives in DIY music hardware design by emphasizing inclusivity and empowerment of marginalized voices.
- 4E cognition theory highlights the fluid relationship between users, their environment, and technology in creative processes like composing with found objects.
- VR technologies have the potential to democratize access to sophisticated music production tools but face challenges related to inclusivity.

## Open Questions

- How can feminist HCI principles be effectively integrated into VR design practices to ensure that immersive musical experiences are truly inclusive?
