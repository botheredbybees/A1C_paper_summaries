## Subjectivity in Film and Literature

The examination of subjectivity in both film and literature reveals a rich tapestry of narrative techniques and thematic explorations. Source 1 delves into the adaptation of the memoir 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly' by Julian Schnabel, focusing on the transformation of a first-person literary narrative into a cinematic experience. This source highlights the use of a subjective camera to emulate the locked-in syndrome experience, emphasizing the unique perspective of the protagonist. Meanwhile, Source 2 investigates the representation of Alzheimer's disease in literature and film, exploring how the disease alters the core self and personal identity through the lens of subjectivity. Both sources underscore the complexity of representing subjective experiences, with the former emphasizing visual and narrative techniques to capture internal perspectives, and the latter discussing the impact of disease on identity and perception. The convergence of these perspectives provides a nuanced understanding of how subjectivity is constructed and communicated through various artistic mediums.

## Key Findings

- Subjective camera techniques in film can effectively convey the internal perspective of characters, especially in narratives about locked-in syndrome.
- Literature and film representations of dementia and Alzheimer's disease emphasize the fragmentation and transformation of the core self and personal identity.
- The use of subjectivity in art forms like literature and film allows for a deeper exploration of human consciousness and perception.

## Open Questions

- How can filmmakers and writers more accurately represent the complex and evolving nature of subjectivity in conditions such as locked-in syndrome and dementia?
