## Intergenerational Choirs and Dementia Care

The concept of intergenerational choirs emerges as a therapeutic intervention for individuals with dementia, offering both social engagement and cognitive benefits. Source 1 highlights the therapeutic benefits derived from musical experiences in an intergenerational setting, which help mitigate symptoms associated with memory loss, such as enhancing recall and fostering emotional connections between different age groups. In contrast, Source 2 delves into a thematic scoping review of community-based participatory arts activities, emphasizing their role in person-centered care for people living with dementia. It underscores the importance of participation and communication, suggesting that these activities provide not just therapeutic benefits but also enriching in-the-moment experiences. Both sources align in recognizing the unique benefits of group musical or artistic engagements for individuals affected by dementia, yet they diverge slightly in their focus; Source 1 places emphasis on the intergenerational aspect and direct memory-related outcomes, while Source 2 broadens the scope to include various forms of participatory arts activities and highlights the overarching principle of person-centered care.

## Key Findings

- Intergenerational choirs offer therapeutic benefits for individuals with dementia by enhancing recall and fostering social connections.
- Community-based participatory arts activities support person-centered care approaches in dementia treatment, focusing on participation and communication.

## Open Questions

- How do intergenerational choir experiences specifically contribute to long-term memory retention or cognitive decline mitigation compared to other forms of community engagement?
