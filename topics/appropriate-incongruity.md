## Appropriate Incongruity in Humor

The concept of 'appropriate incongruity' within humor theory emphasizes the balance between predictability and unpredictability. Both sources highlight that humorous situations are often perceived when there is a deviation from expected norms (script opposition), yet this deviation must also align with certain societal or contextual expectations for it to be considered appropriate and hence, humorous (appropriate incongruity). The integration of script overlap, where elements of two scripts collide in an unexpected way, further enriches the understanding of how humor arises. Both sources agree on the necessity of balance; too much opposition without any underlying agreement can lead to confusion rather than laughter, while too little opposition results in a lack of comedic effect.

## Key Findings

- Humor is generated through a delicate balance between script opposition and appropriate incongruity.
- Script overlap plays an important role in understanding how humor arises from the interaction of two scripts.

## Open Questions

- How does cultural context influence the perception of 'appropriate incongruity' in different societies?
