## Historical Diagnosis of Vincent Van Gogh's Mental Health

The analysis of Vincent van Gogh's mental health diagnosis has been a topic of extensive historical and psychiatric interest, with recent studies offering new insights. Two primary sources focus on retrospective diagnoses through modern psychiatric frameworks. Source 1 presents a new perspective using bottom-up diagnostic interviews, concluding that van Gogh may have had bipolar mood disorder and possibly borderline personality disorder. In contrast, Source 2 explores a wider range of conditions, identifying bipolar disorder and reactive depression as likely diagnoses, while also suggesting temporal lobe epilepsy as a contributing factor. Both sources underscore the complexity of diagnosing historical figures due to the lack of direct evidence and the evolving nature of psychiatric classifications. The integration of contemporary diagnostic criteria with historical clinical descriptions reveals the dynamic interplay between past and present mental health understandings, highlighting the importance of context and methodology in psychiatric diagnosis.

## Key Findings

- Retrospective diagnosis of historical figures like Vincent van Gogh can be approached through modern psychiatric frameworks.
- Bipolar mood disorder is a commonly proposed diagnosis for van Gogh's mental health issues.
- Temporal lobe epilepsy is suggested as a potential contributing factor to van Gogh's symptoms, in addition to mood disorders.
