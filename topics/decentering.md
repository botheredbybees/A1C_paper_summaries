## Decentering in Mindfulness

Decentering, a core mechanism in mindfulness practice, involves the ability to observe one's thoughts and feelings from an objective stance, reducing the automatic influence of internal states on behavior. Across both sources, decentering is highlighted as a pivotal process that enables individuals to disengage from habitual patterns of thinking and feeling, thereby fostering greater flexibility and adaptability in cognitive and emotional processing. This ability to decenter is linked to improved emotion-regulation and attention-regulation, suggesting that it plays a crucial role in the therapeutic benefits of mindfulness interventions. Both sources also emphasize the involvement of the default-mode network and attentional networks in decentering, indicating neural mechanisms that support this process. However, while the first source focuses on the role of executive control and meta-awareness in decentering, the second source introduces the concept of reduced attentional bias, highlighting the specific ways in which decentering can alter the processing of emotionally salient stimuli. Despite the agreement on the importance of decentering, there is a noted gap in the exploration of how decentering translates into long-term behavioral changes.

## Key Findings

- Decentering involves observing thoughts and feelings objectively.
- Decentering is linked to improved emotion and attention regulation.
- Neural networks, including the default-mode and attentional networks, are involved in decentering.

## Open Questions

- How does decentering translate into long-term behavioral changes?
