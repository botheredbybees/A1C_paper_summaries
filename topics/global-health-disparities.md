## Global Health Disparities

The study of global health disparities highlights significant differences in healthcare outcomes among various populations worldwide. Source 1 discusses adolescent and young adult health, focusing on mental-health-disorders and communicable-diseases influenced by environmental-factors. In contrast, Source 2 examines the link between diet and non-communicable-diseases, emphasizing nutritional-quality as a critical factor. Both sources underscore dietary quality as pivotal in addressing disparities but diverge in their primary focus: one addresses communicable diseases prevalent among younger populations impacted by environmental stressors, while the other delves into the relationship between diet and non-communicable diseases affecting all age groups. This synthesis integrates findings that highlight nutritional quality's role in reducing health disparities through improved dietary practices across different demographics.

## Key Findings

- Dietary quality significantly influences health outcomes among various populations globally, impacting both communicable and non-communicable disease prevalence.
- Environmental factors play a crucial role in the development of mental-health-disorders and communicable diseases among adolescents and young adults.

## Open Questions

- How do environmental stressors interact with nutritional quality to exacerbate global health disparities?
