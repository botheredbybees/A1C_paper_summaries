## Human-Computer Interaction in Sensory Substitution and Assistive Technology

The integration of human-computer interaction (HCI) methodologies with sensory substitution technologies highlights the potential for assistive technology to enhance user experiences across various disabilities. Source 1 introduces MUSTEM, a dual-modality system that translates music into vibrotactile and visual signals, enabling users who have auditory impairments to perceive musical harmony through tactile feedback and visual representation. This innovative approach underscores how sensory substitution can be used effectively in the context of music appreciation, demonstrating the broader applicability of tactile and visual interfaces as assistive technologies for those with sensory limitations. In contrast, Source 2 focuses on Contextual Design (CD) methodology, which involves a series of steps such as contextual inquiry to deeply understand users' needs and contexts before designing solutions. This methodological approach can be seen as complementary to the technical innovations described in MUSTEM by providing the necessary user-centered design framework for developing effective sensory substitution systems like MUSTEM.

## Key Findings

- Sensory-substitution technologies offer new possibilities for enhancing accessibility through non-traditional sensory modalities
- Understanding users' needs and contexts is crucial for designing effective assistive technologies

## Open Questions

- How can user-centered design methodologies be integrated with technical innovations to optimize the development of sensory substitution systems?
