## Gratitude Interventions

Gratitude, as an intervention in positive psychology, encompasses both state and trait gratitude and employs techniques such as mental subtraction to enhance appreciation for life circumstances. The Positive Activity Model and the Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model provide theoretical frameworks for understanding how gratitude exercises function to mitigate negativity and foster well-being. These models highlight the role of gratitude in combating hedonic adaptation, a process where individuals return to a baseline level of happiness despite positive life changes. Source 3 introduces acts of kindness as a complementary intervention, further emphasizing the interpersonal dimension of gratitude. However, the integration of these models with specific gratitude practices reveals a need for more empirical validation on how different forms of gratitude impact long-term psychological health.

## Key Findings

- Gratitude interventions can combat hedonic adaptation.
- Mental subtraction enhances trait gratitude.
- Acts of kindness complement gratitude practices in fostering well-being.

## Open Questions

- How do state and trait gratitude influence each other over time in response to interventions?
