## Biodiversity Conservation

The intersection of design and conservation emerges through the lenses of Research Through Design (RtD) and Edward O. Wilson's 'Biophilia'. RtD emphasizes iterative research with community engagement to mitigate confirmation bias, ensuring that biodiversity efforts are grounded in user needs and local contexts. Wilson's biophilic theory posits an innate human connection to nature, underpinning the value of conserving natural diversity as a fundamental aspect of human welfare and ecological balance. While both approaches highlight the importance of understanding the intrinsic relationship between humans and their environment, they differ in their methodologies: RtD focuses on design-driven research cycles, whereas biophilia emphasizes psychological and evolutionary connections to biodiversity.

## Key Findings

- Community engagement is crucial for effective biodiversity conservation initiatives.
- The human connection with nature plays a significant role in motivating conservation efforts.
- Iterative approaches to researching and designing conservation strategies can help avoid biases that may otherwise hinder the success of these initiatives.

## Open Questions

- How can biophilic principles be effectively integrated into RtD methodologies for more impactful biodiversity conservation?
