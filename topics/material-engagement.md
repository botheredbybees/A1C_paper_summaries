## Material Engagement in Creative and Repair Practices

The synthesis integrates themes of material engagement from two sources that explore creative ideation through collaborative art-making processes and repair practices of everyday artefacts. Source 1, 'Collage Co-Collaborator: Creative Ideation for Divergent Thinking in Art Making,' highlights the role of materials in facilitating creative agency and divergent thinking. It underscores how engaging with tangible materials fosters innovative ideas by encouraging collaboration among participants to ideate freely. In contrast, Source 2, 'The communities and materials of repair collectively reflecting on repair practices of everyday artefacts,' examines material engagement within a circular-design framework where repairing everyday objects not only extends their lifecycle but also strengthens community ties through shared experiences. Both sources emphasize the importance of interaction with physical materials to enhance cognitive processes like ideation and problem-solving, while Source 2 further illustrates how such activities contribute to broader societal goals by promoting sustainability and social cohesion.

## Key Findings

- Material engagement facilitates both creative agency and collaborative ideation in art-making contexts.
- Repair practices involving tangible objects foster community bonding alongside environmental sustainability.
- Engaging with materials can support cognitive processes, such as divergent thinking and problem-solving.

## Open Questions

- How does the nature of material interaction differ between creative ideation and repair activities, and what are the implications for user experience and outcome?
