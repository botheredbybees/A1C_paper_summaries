## Virtue Ethics in Psychology

Virtue ethics, a philosophical framework emphasizing the development of moral character through the cultivation of virtues, finds significant application in contemporary psychological research. Sources converge on the importance of concepts like hedonia and eudaimonia, where hedonia focuses on pleasure and subjective well-being, while eudaimonia underscores the importance of fulfilling one's potential and living a meaningful life. Positive psychology's engagement with virtue ethics, particularly through the Values in Action (VIA) classification, aims to understand and enhance character strengths and virtues. The VIA approach introduces a three-dimensional model of virtues that includes practical wisdom, suggesting that virtues are interrelated and work together in a unified manner, a concept referred to as the unity of virtue. This framework challenges psychologists to consider virtues not merely as isolated traits but as integral components of a holistic approach to well-being and flourishing.

## Key Findings

- Virtue ethics emphasizes the development of moral character through virtues.
- Hedonia and eudaimonia are distinct yet interrelated concepts in well-being theories.
- The VIA Classification offers a structured approach to understanding and cultivating virtues.

## Open Questions

- How can the unity of virtue concept be empirically tested and integrated into psychological practice?
