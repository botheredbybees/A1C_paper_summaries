## Inclusive Culture

The concept of an inclusive culture is explored through two distinct yet interconnected lenses: Australia's cultural policy as outlined in 'Revive: a place for every story, a story for every place,' and the realm of Disability Arts as presented in 'Disability Arts Now.' Both sources emphasize the importance of representation and the integration of diverse narratives within the cultural landscape. 'Revive' advocates for a creative economy that honors and integrates First Nations stories and perspectives, aiming to enrich cultural infrastructure through the recognition of these narratives. On the other hand, 'Disability Arts Now' focuses on the importance of first-person narratives and disability justice within the arts, challenging existing cultural norms and advocating for the visibility and inclusion of disabled voices. While these sources differ in their specific focus—Indigenous representation versus disability rights—they both underscore the broader theme of creating cultural spaces that are inclusive and reflective of the diverse experiences within society. The synthesis of these perspectives highlights the complexity of fostering an inclusive culture that respects and integrates multiple marginalized voices.

## Key Findings

- First Nations and disability narratives are crucial for a culturally rich and inclusive society.
- Inclusive cultural policies require the recognition and integration of diverse voices.
- First-person narratives in the arts challenge and broaden cultural norms.

## Open Questions

- How can cultural policies effectively balance the inclusion of multiple marginalized voices without diluting the impact of individual narratives?
