## Autonomy in Art Therapy and Creative Interventions for Dementia Care

The concept of autonomy is critically examined within the context of art therapy and creative interventions aimed at dementia care. Source 1 emphasizes the ethical imperative to uphold principles such as nonmaleficence, beneficence, fidelity, and justice when engaging in art therapy practices with individuals suffering from dementia, highlighting that these principles must be balanced against the need for beneficent actions even when they may not align with an individual's expressed wishes. The challenge here is maintaining respect for autonomy while ensuring patient safety and well-being. Source 2 reviews creative interventions in dementia care and underscores the importance of engagement as a key component. This highlights how interventions should cater to individual preferences and abilities, thus supporting autonomy but also requiring careful consideration of each participant’s capacity to make informed decisions about their involvement.

## Key Findings

- Autonomy is a central ethical principle in art therapy practices for dementia care
- Balancing respect for patients' choices with the need for protective measures is challenging
- Engagement and individual preferences are key aspects of effective creative interventions

## Open Questions

- How can practitioners ensure that autonomy is respected while also implementing necessary protections?
