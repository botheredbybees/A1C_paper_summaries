## Singing for Respiratory Rehabilitation

Singing as a therapeutic intervention for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) has garnered attention due to its potential to enhance both physical and mental health outcomes. The Singing for Better Breathing Project demonstrates that singing can be an engaging and supportive activity for individuals with COPD, potentially improving their respiratory function assessment. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that singing interventions led to significant improvements in symptoms and quality of life for stable COPD patients, indicating that singing may serve as an effective non-pharmacological treatment option. Additionally, research suggests that singing can enhance the physical quality of life and maximal expiratory pressure, contributing to better management of the disease. However, the specific mechanisms by which singing impacts COPD symptoms remain underexplored, suggesting a need for further research to elucidate the underlying physiological and psychological benefits.

## Key Findings

- Singing interventions can improve respiratory function assessment in COPD patients.
- Singing can lead to significant improvements in physical quality of life for patients with stable COPD.
- Singing enhances maximal expiratory pressure in COPD patients, indicating improved respiratory muscle strength.

## Open Questions

- What are the underlying physiological and psychological mechanisms by which singing impacts COPD symptoms?
