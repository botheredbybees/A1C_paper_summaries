## Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy

Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a widely recognized psychological intervention aimed at modifying dysfunctional emotions, behaviors, and thoughts through a goal-oriented, systematic process. The sources provided offer insights into the cognitive and neural mechanisms involved in such therapies. One source highlights the role of the temporoparietal cortex and dorsal stream in spatial cognition, which are crucial for understanding how CBT can influence neural processes underlying spatial awareness and cognitive restructuring. The other source introduces mindfulness meditation as a technique involving present moment awareness and repeated attention redirection, which aligns with CBT principles by focusing on the present and altering maladaptive thought patterns. However, while both sources discuss neural and cognitive aspects relevant to CBT, they do not directly address therapeutic interventions or their efficacy.

## Key Findings

- CBT involves modifying dysfunctional emotions, behaviors, and thoughts.
- Mindfulness meditation, a technique that promotes present moment awareness and repeated attention redirection, aligns with CBT principles.
- The temporoparietal cortex and dorsal stream are involved in spatial cognition and cognitive restructuring.
