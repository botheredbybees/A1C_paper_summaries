## Music Performance Anxiety

Music performance anxiety (MPA) is a pervasive issue among musicians that encompasses both psychological and physical dimensions. Source 1 introduces a short performance anxiety scale for musicians, highlighting the importance of assessing MPA in a concise and reliable manner. Sources 2 and 3 delve into the broader context of MPA, identifying various personality traits and teaching experiences as potential contributors to the condition. These studies also delineate the subtypes of MPA, underscoring the multifaceted nature of the disorder. Source 4's scoping review emphasizes the relationship between flow experience and MPA, suggesting that emotional self-regulation and self-efficacy play crucial roles in mitigating anxiety during performances. Source 5 discusses the broader occupational health context, integrating physical health issues such as performance-related musculoskeletal disorders with mental health concerns, thus providing a holistic view of the challenges faced by professional musicians. Finally, Sources 6 and 7 focus on therapeutic interventions, such as cognitive behavioral therapy and acceptance and commitment therapy, as well as mental training techniques like visualization, demonstrating the variety of approaches available to address MPA.

## Key Findings

- Music performance anxiety encompasses psychological and physical dimensions and varies among musicians based on personality traits and experience.
- Flow experience and self-regulation are critical factors in managing MPA.
- Cognitive-behavioral therapy and acceptance and commitment therapy are effective therapeutic approaches to treating MPA.
