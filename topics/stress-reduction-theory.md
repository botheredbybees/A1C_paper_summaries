## Stress Reduction Theory

The synthesis of the provided sources reveals a consistent emphasis on Stress Reduction Theory as an explanatory framework for understanding the mental health benefits derived from spending time in nature. Each source underscores the role of natural environments in mitigating stress, supported by related theories such as Attention Restoration Theory (ART), which posits that contact with nature allows individuals to recover from attention fatigue and thereby reduces stress. Additionally, concepts like Biophilia Hypothesis and Nature Connectedness are invoked to explain a deeper human instinctual attraction towards nature and the resultant positive mental health outcomes. Despite these convergences, there is variability in how each concept interacts within Stress Reduction Theory, indicating a multifaceted understanding of natural environments' therapeutic benefits.

## Key Findings

- Stress Reduction Theory underpins the idea that contact with nature can alleviate stress-related symptoms.
- Attention Restoration Theory (ART) complements Stress Reduction Theory by explaining recovery from attention fatigue through engagement in natural settings.
- Biophilia Hypothesis and Nature Connectedness enhance understanding of intrinsic human-nature relationships as a source for well-being.

## Open Questions
