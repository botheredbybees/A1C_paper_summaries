## Child-Centered Play Therapy

The topic of Child-Centered Play Therapy (CCPT) emerges as a promising intervention for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), particularly in contexts involving adverse childhood experiences and psychological well-being. Sources consistently highlight CCPT's potential to foster reflective practice and improve both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being among autistic youth, suggesting its multifaceted benefits beyond mere symptom management. One study utilizes brain EEG activity as an objective measure of CCPT’s impact, providing empirical evidence for the therapy's effectiveness in altering neural patterns associated with autism. However, while multiple sources emphasize the positive outcomes of CCPT, there remains a gap in understanding how these interventions specifically influence different aspects of well-being and neural functioning.

## Key Findings

- Child-Centered Play Therapy (CCPT) is beneficial for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), enhancing both their hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.
- Reflective practice through CCPT can foster a greater appreciation for personal surroundings among autistic youth, indicating potential improvements in quality of life.
- Brain EEG activity studies suggest that CCPT may alter neural patterns associated with autism, providing objective evidence of its effectiveness.

## Open Questions

- How do specific aspects of child-centered play therapy contribute to both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being among children with ASD?
