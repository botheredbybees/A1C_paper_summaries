## Music Therapy in Aphasia Rehabilitation

Music therapy, particularly singing interventions, has been explored as a potential rehabilitation method for individuals with aphasia, a language disorder often resulting from stroke. However, the effectiveness of singing, especially for non-fluent aphasia, has been questioned due to factors such as the reliance on motor automaticity and the familiarity of lyrics, which may not effectively engage language recovery processes. In contrast, combining traditional methods like repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation with music therapy appears to offer promising outcomes in randomized controlled trials, suggesting that integrating diverse therapeutic approaches could be more beneficial than singular music-based interventions. This synthesis highlights the need for a nuanced understanding of music therapy's application in aphasia rehabilitation, emphasizing the importance of evidence-based practices that consider both the strengths and limitations of different therapeutic modalities.

## Key Findings

- Singing interventions in music therapy may not effectively engage language recovery processes for non-fluent aphasia.
- Combining repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation with music therapy shows promising results in aphasia rehabilitation.
- The effectiveness of music therapy in aphasia rehabilitation may depend on the specific type and severity of aphasia.

## Open Questions

- How can music therapy be optimized to better engage language recovery processes in non-fluent aphasia?
