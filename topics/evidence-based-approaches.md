## Evidence-Based Approaches in Arts and Health

The sources discussed the application of evidence-based approaches in arts and health, highlighting both methodological transparency and the rehabilitation of incarcerated individuals through creative interventions. Source one critiques 'Creative Health: The Arts for Health and Wellbeing' by emphasizing the importance of methodological transparency to ensure that claims made about the benefits of arts for health are substantiated with rigorous research methods. It argues against a prescriptive approach to arts in healthcare, suggesting instead that evidence should guide practice while allowing room for creativity and flexibility. Source two addresses the use of artistic programs within criminal justice systems as rehabilitation tools for incarcerated individuals. This source points towards the necessity of evidence-based approaches to effectively integrate arts into corrections settings, promoting social reintegration through creative engagement.

## Key Findings

- The importance of methodological transparency in validating claims about the benefits of arts interventions
- Evidence-based practices should be applied flexibly within creative contexts to maximize their effectiveness for rehabilitation and health outcomes
- Arts programs can play a crucial role in supporting social reintegration and psychological well-being among incarcerated individuals

## Open Questions

- How can evidence-based methodologies effectively balance rigorous research standards with the necessity of adapting creative practices to diverse populations?
