## Connectedness To Nature

The concept of connectedness to nature is a central theme in both sources. Source one introduces the idea through Attention Restoration Theory and Stress Reduction Theory, emphasizing how time spent in nature can have restorative effects on mental health by alleviating stress and enhancing cognitive functions. This theory posits that natural environments provide conditions under which direct attention processes are replenished, thereby reducing mental fatigue and promoting psychological well-being. In contrast, Source two delves into the measurement of connectedness to nature using the Connectedness to Nature Scale (CNS), focusing on how a sense of interconnectedness with the environment influences subjective well-being and environmental responsibility. Despite their different approaches, both sources converge in highlighting that a stronger connection to nature is associated with better mental health outcomes and a more proactive stance towards environmental issues.

## Key Findings

- Connectedness to nature enhances psychological well-being by reducing stress and restoring cognitive functions.
- Time spent in natural environments can improve attentional capacity, thereby benefiting overall mental health.
- A strong connection to nature is positively correlated with higher levels of environmental responsibility.
