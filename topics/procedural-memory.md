## Procedural Memory

Procedural memory, a type of long-term memory that stores information related to how to perform certain tasks, is explored in both sources as a critical component of cognitive function, particularly in relation to the effects of neurodegenerative diseases. Source 1 focuses on the interplay between music and memory, highlighting how auditory cues can influence procedural memory through the lens of the 'reminiscence bump,' a phenomenon where individuals recall more events from their teens and early twenties. In contrast, Source 2 examines the impact of Alzheimer's disease on the procedural memory of renowned painters, using the dual-stream framework and predictive coding to illustrate the brain's capacity to maintain certain creative processes despite significant cognitive decline. Both sources emphasize the resilience and adaptability of procedural memory, even in the face of neurological disorders.

## Key Findings

- Procedural memory is crucial for the execution of learned skills and behaviors.
- Auditory cues and music can enhance procedural memory recall.
- The dual-stream framework and predictive coding are useful models for understanding the preservation of procedural memory in individuals with Alzheimer's disease.

## Open Questions

- How do auditory cues specifically influence procedural memory in individuals with neurodegenerative diseases?
