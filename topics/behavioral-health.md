## Music Therapy and Behavioral Health

The relationship between music and behavioral health is a complex interplay between cultural attitudes, psychological benefits, and therapeutic applications. Source 1 discusses how music, often stigmatized as 'problem music,' is used by youth to signal identity and gain psychological gratification, highlighting the cultural and social impact of music in shaping behavior and identity. Source 2 focuses on the therapeutic efficacy of music-based interventions, specifically in treating substance use disorders, through a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. While Source 1 addresses the cultural and social implications of music within youth subcultures, Source 2 emphasizes the clinical effectiveness of music as a therapeutic tool. Both sources illustrate how music can influence behavior, either through cultural and social identity signaling or as an intervention for behavioral health issues.

## Key Findings

- Music can be used by youth as a means of identity signaling and psychological gratification
- Music-based interventions are effective in treating substance use disorders according to clinical trials
- The therapeutic use of music spans beyond conventional therapeutic settings into cultural and social contexts

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of using music-based interventions for treating substance use disorders?
