## Arts Therapies for Autism Spectrum

The integration of arts therapies for individuals on the autism spectrum highlights both theoretical and practical approaches to enhancing social and emotional well-being. Source 1 explores the cognitive mechanisms underlying social cognition, such as the Theory of Mind (ToM) and the neural substrates responsible for empathy and social understanding, including the anterior paracingulate cortex and temporoparietal junction. Source 2 builds on these theoretical foundations by discussing the application of expressive arts therapies, specifically tele-dance movement psychotherapy, which aims to facilitate empathy mirroring and emotional expression. These sources collectively demonstrate a shift from understanding the neurocognitive basis of social cognition to applying this knowledge in therapeutic contexts, emphasizing the role of arts therapies in improving ToM and social interaction skills in individuals with autism.

## Key Findings

- Expressive arts therapies enhance social and emotional engagement in individuals with autism.
- Empathy mirroring is a critical component of therapeutic interventions for improving social cognition in autism.
- The anterior paracingulate cortex and temporoparietal junction are neural regions involved in social cognition that may benefit from targeted interventions.

## Open Questions

- How do arts therapies specifically target and improve the neural substrates involved in social cognition for individuals with autism?
