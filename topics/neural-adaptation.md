## Neural Adaptation

Neural adaptation, a fundamental concept in neuroscience, refers to the brain's ability to adjust and modify its responses to stimuli over time. This adaptive process is observed in both sensory and cognitive domains, as evidenced by two sources. Source 1 examines how human colour perception adapts through cone photoreceptors, showing the brain's capacity to adjust to changes in visual input. Meanwhile, Source 2 discusses how auditory experiences influence speech comprehension, indicating that the brain also adapts to auditory stimuli. These findings highlight neural adaptation's role in sensory perception and cognitive processing, underscoring the brain's dynamic nature in responding to environmental stimuli. However, there is a tension between the specific sensory focus in Source 1 and the broader cognitive implications in Source 2, suggesting that further research is needed to integrate the sensory and cognitive aspects of neural adaptation into a comprehensive theory.

## Key Findings

- Neural adaptation is a fundamental process in sensory perception and cognitive function.
- Cone photoreceptors and auditory experience are critical in the adaptation process.
- Adaptation influences both sensory perception and cognitive processing.

## Open Questions

- How can the sensory-specific findings be integrated with cognitive processing insights to form a cohesive theory of neural adaptation?
