## Illness Narrative as Therapeutic Practice

The concept of illness narrative as a therapeutic practice is explored in both sources, highlighting the transformational power of storytelling in health care. Source 1 emphasizes narrative therapy as a means to provide humanistic care, illustrating how patients can gain agency through the construction and re-telling of their illness stories. Source 2 extends this by addressing the 'It, Thee, and We' of disease and illness, pointing to the identity transformation, social alienation, and existential crises patients may face. Both sources agree that engaging with illness narratives can offer therapeutic benefits, fostering a deeper understanding of the patient’s experience and facilitating healing. However, there is a noted tension in Source 2 regarding the complexity of these narratives and the challenges they present in the therapeutic process.

## Key Findings

- Illness narratives can provide therapeutic benefits by offering patients a sense of agency and control over their experiences.
- Engaging with illness narratives can help address identity transformation, social alienation, and existential crises.
- Narrative medicine and narrative therapy both emphasize the importance of storytelling in healthcare.

## Open Questions

- How can healthcare professionals effectively engage with and utilize the complexity of illness narratives in therapeutic practice?
