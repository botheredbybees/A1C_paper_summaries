## Intersectionality in Creative Arts Programs

Intersectionality, as a theoretical framework, is integral to both the development of creative arts programs and inclusive governance policies. In the context of arts and disability, the use of identity-first language reflects the importance of recognizing individuals' self-identification and the unique intersection of their identities, including disability status. Source 1 highlights the necessity of inclusive governance that respects the autonomy and distinct needs of various identity groups. In contrast, Source 2 underscores the application of intersectionality in practice through co-tinkering and anti-oppressive practices, focusing on the experiences of trans, non-binary, and gender creative youth. This approach emphasizes the collaborative process between facilitators and participants in creating arts programs that are sensitive to the diverse identities and needs of its members. Both sources illustrate how intersectionality is crucial for understanding and addressing the complexities of social identities within the framework of creative arts and governance.

## Key Findings

- Intersectionality is fundamental for understanding and addressing the unique needs of diverse identity groups in creative arts and governance.
- Identity-first language acknowledges the autonomy and self-identification of individuals, especially those with disabilities.
- Co-tinkering and anti-oppressive practices are effective methods for ensuring arts programs are inclusive and responsive to the intersectional needs of participants.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific challenges in implementing intersectionality in creative arts programs and inclusive governance policies, and how can these be addressed?
