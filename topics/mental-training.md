## Mental Training

Mental training emerges as a versatile intervention with applications across different contexts, including cognitive enhancement in aging populations and psychological support for performance anxiety in musicians. In the context of healthy aging, mental training alongside aerobic exercise enhances cognitive functions, suggesting that cognitive activities can complement physical exercise in promoting brain health. In contrast, the application of mental training in the realm of music performance anxiety focuses on techniques such as activation-regulation, motivation-regulation, and visualization. While both studies highlight the potential of mental training, they differ in their specific methodologies and application areas, emphasizing its adaptability but also the need for tailored approaches.

## Key Findings

- Mental training can enhance cognitive functions in healthy elderly subjects, when combined with aerobic exercise.
- Specific mental training techniques such as activation-regulation, motivation-regulation, and visualization can effectively address music performance anxiety.
- The versatility of mental training allows for its adaptation to various contexts, including both health and performance enhancement.

## Open Questions

- What are the optimal combinations of mental and physical exercises for maximizing cognitive benefits in aging populations?
