## Cognitive Therapy

Cognitive therapy is a widely recognized psychological intervention that focuses on identifying and altering negative thought patterns and beliefs. The first source highlights its application in treating musical performance anxiety, where exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring are employed to manage anxiety symptoms effectively. Cognitive restructuring involves the identification and modification of dysfunctional beliefs, which is crucial in the context of performance anxiety, helping musicians to challenge and change their negative thoughts about performing. The second source introduces dispositional mindfulness and mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) as complementary approaches to cognitive therapy. MBSR encourages individuals to focus on present-moment experiences, reducing the impact of anxious thoughts by promoting a non-judgmental awareness of current emotions and sensations. While both cognitive therapy and MBSR aim to address maladaptive thinking patterns, they differ in their approach, with cognitive therapy focusing on the identification and modification of specific thoughts, whereas MBSR emphasizes present-moment awareness and acceptance.

## Key Findings

- Cognitive therapy is an effective intervention for managing performance anxiety by altering negative thought patterns.
- MBSR complements cognitive therapy by promoting present-moment awareness and acceptance.
- Cognitive therapy involves techniques such as exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring to address dysfunctional beliefs.
