## Self-Determined Motivation in Music Listening

Research on music listening motivation and its impact on subjective well-being highlights the importance of self-determined motivations over external pressures. In Canadian late adolescents, higher levels of intrinsic motivation for music listening are positively correlated with greater happiness (Source 1). This suggests that engaging in activities like music listening autonomously enhances one's sense of fulfillment and life satisfaction. The concept of hedonia from Source 2 further supports this by indicating the pursuit of pleasure as a component of subjective well-being, aligning closely with the autonomy-driven enjoyment derived from self-determined motivation towards music. Additionally, the broaden-and-build theory and undo hypothesis presented in Source 2 offer explanations for how positive emotional experiences, such as those facilitated through autonomous music listening, can help individuals build resources (e.g., attentional control) and subsequently better manage stressors (Source 2). These theories suggest that self-determined motivation not only contributes to immediate happiness but also has long-term benefits by enhancing psychological resilience.

## Key Findings

- Self-determined motivations for music listening are positively associated with global happiness in late adolescents.
- The pursuit of pleasure through autonomous activities like music listening is a key component of subjective well-being (hedonia).
- Autonomous engagement in music listening can contribute to building psychological resources and resilience against stress.

## Open Questions

- How do different cultural contexts influence the relationship between self-determined motivation for music listening and subjective well-being?
