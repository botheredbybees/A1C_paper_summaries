## Mixed-Methods in Mental Health Research

The integration of biopsychosocial approaches with mixed-method research strategies is evident in both sources. Source 1 highlights the use of a 'creative green prescription' museum program to assess its impact on psychological well-being, emphasizing the importance of combining various disciplines such as horticulture and artmaking within a community setting. This holistic approach aligns with the biopsychosocial model, which considers biological, psychological, and social factors in understanding health and illness. Source 2 underscores the value of mixed-method research methods to provide comprehensive insights into music, mind, and body studies. It suggests that integrating quantitative and qualitative methodologies can offer a more nuanced understanding of mental health phenomena than either approach could alone.

## Key Findings

- The biopsychosocial model offers a holistic framework for assessing the impact of therapeutic interventions on mental health.
- Mixed-methods research is valuable in providing a comprehensive view by integrating quantitative and qualitative data to understand complex phenomena related to music, mind, and body.

## Open Questions

- How can mixed-method approaches be further developed or refined to better capture and analyze the intricate relationships between art, nature, and psychological well-being?
