## Script Opposition in Discourse Analysis

Discourse analysis has been enriched through the examination of humor within various contexts, such as political rhetoric and cognitive functions. Source 1 delves into the use of self-deprecating humor by US presidents at state dinner speeches to foster a sense of affiliation with their audience, drawing on the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH). Conversely, Source 2 introduces Script Opposition Theory within the GTVH framework, emphasizing the role of cognitive complexity in understanding how humor operates through the juxtaposition and resolution of conflicting scripts. Both sources highlight the multifaceted nature of humor's communicative function but diverge slightly in their focus—whereas one examines humorous speech acts for social cohesion, the other considers humor from a more cognitively complex perspective to explain its diverse impact on discourse.

## Key Findings

- The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) serves as a foundational framework for understanding various types of humor across different contexts and cultures.
- Self-deprecating humor is used by political figures to establish rapport with their audience, suggesting its affiliative function in discourse analysis.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of Script Opposition Theory and cognitive complexity enhance our understanding of self-deprecating humor's social functions beyond simple affiliation?
