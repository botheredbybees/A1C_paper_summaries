## Acculturation in Mental Health and Social Inclusion

The concept of acculturation is pivotal in both music therapy for diverse service users in mental health settings and social inclusion among ethnic communities. Both sources emphasize the importance of cultural humility and engaging service users through culturally relevant practices. In mental health, music therapy is seen as a medium to bridge cultural gaps and enhance therapeutic outcomes for individuals from diverse backgrounds. Meanwhile, social inclusion initiatives highlight the role of cultural engagement as a tool for fostering social connections and reducing isolation among ethnic minorities. However, while both sources acknowledge the benefits of cultural engagement, they differ in their primary focus: one on therapeutic interventions and the other on community-wide social inclusion efforts. This highlights the need for a more integrated approach that leverages therapeutic practices to support broader social inclusion goals.

## Key Findings

- Cultural humility and engagement are critical in both therapeutic and social inclusion contexts.
- Music therapy can serve as a tool to enhance mental health outcomes for diverse populations.
- Cultural engagement initiatives are essential for reducing social isolation among ethnic communities.

## Open Questions

- How can therapeutic practices, such as music therapy, be effectively integrated into broader social inclusion initiatives?
