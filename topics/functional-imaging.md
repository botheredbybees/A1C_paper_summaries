## Functional Imaging in Music Neuroscience

Functional imaging studies in music neuroscience provide evidence for brain plasticity and morphological changes in musicians compared to non-musicians, as well as distinct neural correlates for singing and speaking. These studies highlight the role of specific brain regions, such as the inferior precentral gyrus and superior temporal gyrus, in musical activities. While the studies show clear differences in brain structure and function, they also underscore the importance of understanding both shared and unique neural mechanisms involved in musical and linguistic processing. The integration of functional imaging techniques allows researchers to explore the complex interplay between brain structure and function in the context of music.

## Key Findings

- Functional imaging studies reveal morphological changes in the brains of musicians compared to non-musicians.
- Specific brain regions, such as the inferior precentral gyrus and superior temporal gyrus, are crucial for musical activities.
- There are both shared and distinct neural correlates for singing and speaking, highlighting the complexity of neural processing in music.

## Open Questions

- How do the neural correlates of singing and speaking differ in non-musicians compared to musicians, and what implications does this have for understanding language and music processing?
