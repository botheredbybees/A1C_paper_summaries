## Music and Singing in Cancer Care

The integration of music and singing as therapeutic interventions in cancer care reveals a significant impact on the quality of life for patients and their families. Source 1 highlights the physiological benefits of singing, including modulation of mood, reduction of stress, and regulation of cortisol, cytokines, and neuropeptides, which collectively contribute to an enhanced immune response. In contrast, Source 2 focuses on the psychological and emotional benefits of music therapy, emphasizing its role in providing psychosocial support and improving the quality of life for cancer patients and their families. Both sources underscore the multifaceted benefits of these interventions, addressing both the physical and emotional needs of individuals undergoing cancer treatment. However, while Source 1 delves into the specific physiological mechanisms of singing, Source 2 provides a broader overview of music therapy, including its applications in various settings and its benefits for different stakeholders. The interplay between these interventions suggests a comprehensive approach to cancer care, addressing both the biological and psychological dimensions of the illness.

## Key Findings

- Singing and music therapy both improve quality of life for cancer patients and their families.
- Singing modulates physiological responses such as cortisol and cytokine levels.
- Music therapy offers psychosocial support and emotional relief to cancer patients.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of singing and music therapy on cancer patients' health outcomes?
