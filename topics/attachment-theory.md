## Attachment Theory

Attachment theory, a foundational framework in developmental psychology, explores the emotional bonds formed between children and their primary caregivers. Sources highlight the application of attachment theory across diverse fields. Source 1 illustrates how Transactional Analysis (TA) Developmental Collage Therapy employs visualization techniques to bring internal working models (IWMs) to light, emphasizing the role of ego states in understanding these mental representations crucial for therapeutic interventions. This source underscores the complexity and subjective nature of IWMs as they relate to attachment experiences. Source 2 focuses on creative methods—such as imagination, illustration, and storytelling—to address social, emotional, and mental health issues, particularly those stemming from Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs). It demonstrates how these creative approaches can facilitate healing by engaging with the narrative aspects of attachment theory, thereby offering a practical dimension to theoretical constructs.

## Key Findings

- Internal working models play a critical role in understanding and addressing attachment-related issues through therapeutic interventions such as TA Developmental Collage Therapy.
- Creative methods, including imagination and storytelling, are effective tools for enhancing social and emotional well-being by engaging with the narratives of adverse childhood experiences.
- Attachment theory provides a conceptual basis for both clinical and educational applications aimed at improving mental health outcomes.

## Open Questions

- How can the subjective nature of internal working models be effectively addressed in large-scale, quantitative research methodologies?
