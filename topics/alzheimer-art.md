## Art and Alzheimer's Disease

The exploration of art as a therapeutic and expressive tool for individuals suffering from Alzheimer's disease reveals both the subjective experience of the disease and the potential for artistic expression as a form of resilience and self-representation. Sources highlight the case of William Utermohlen, whose later paintings serve as a visual diary reflecting the progression of his Alzheimer's disease, providing a unique perspective on the disease's impact on cognitive and creative faculties. Other sources suggest that while the disease may cause significant changes in artistic output, such changes can also be seen as a form of creative resilience, where the individual continues to express themselves despite the debilitating effects of the disease. This synthesis underscores the importance of subjective experience in understanding Alzheimer's disease and the potential for art to serve both as a therapeutic and a communication tool for individuals with the disease.

## Key Findings

- Artistic expression can serve as a form of resilience and self-representation for individuals with Alzheimer's disease.
- The progression of Alzheimer's disease can be visually documented through changes in an artist's work.
- Art offers a unique perspective on the subjective experience of living with Alzheimer's disease.

## Open Questions

- How can art-based interventions be tailored to maximize therapeutic benefits for individuals at different stages of Alzheimer's disease?
