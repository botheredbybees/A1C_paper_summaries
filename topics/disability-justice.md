## Disability Justice

The concept of disability justice is central to both sources, emphasizing the importance of inclusive cultural practices and the use of first-person narratives in the disability rights movement. Both sources highlight the principle that any discourse or action concerning disability should not exclude disabled people from the conversation or decision-making processes ('nothing about us without us'). This principle is foundational to the disability justice movement, which seeks to create a more equitable society that includes people with disabilities. The use of first-person narratives is seen as a powerful tool for empowering individuals and communities within the disability justice movement, allowing them to share their experiences and advocate for their rights. Both sources underscore the necessity of inclusive practices in fostering a culture where disabled voices are heard and valued, contributing to a broader societal understanding of disability justice.

## Key Findings

- The disability justice movement emphasizes the principle of 'nothing about us without us'.
- First-person narratives play a crucial role in empowering individuals within the disability justice movement.
- Inclusive cultural practices are essential for ensuring that disabled voices are central to discussions and decisions regarding disability rights.

## Open Questions

- How can the disability justice movement better incorporate the perspectives of marginalized groups within the disabled community?
