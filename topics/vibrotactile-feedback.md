## Vibrotactile Feedback

The integration of vibrotactile feedback into music and hearing assistance technologies highlights a promising avenue for sensory substitution and augmentation. Studies such as the 'Enhancing Pitch Perception and Melodic Reproduction' paper underscore the effectiveness of vibrotactile gloves in improving pitch perception among hard-of-hearing individuals, while 'Sensory Substitution in Music' emphasizes the role of head-related transfer functions (HRTF) and sinusoidal modeling synthesis (SMS) to enhance deaf perception through tactile feedback. MUSTEM's dual-modality system further advances this concept by combining vibrotactile with visual information to assist hearing-impaired users, illustrating a trend towards multi-sensory integration for more comprehensive support. The 'Recent Developments in Haptic Devices' review provides an overview of how these systems are evolving, focusing on sensory augmentation and hybrid biology. However, there is variability across studies regarding the optimal design of vibrotactile feedback devices, with some advocating for personalized patterns while others stress cross-modal enhancement strategies.

## Key Findings

- Vibrotactile gloves improve pitch perception in hard-of-hearing individuals.
- Head-related transfer functions (HRTF) and sinusoidal modeling synthesis (SMS) enhance deaf perception through tactile feedback.
- MUSTEM's dual-modality system integrates vibrotactile and visual cues for better assistive technology solutions.
