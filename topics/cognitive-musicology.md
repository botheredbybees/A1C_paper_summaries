## Cognitive Musicology

The field of cognitive musicology integrates theories from psychology and linguistics to understand the perception and memory of music. Source 1 explores Sweet Anticipation, a theory centered around Bayesian probability and temporal predictability in musical experience, suggesting that anticipation plays a critical role in human engagement with music. This aligns closely with concepts like consonance-dissonance discussed in Source 2, which focuses on tonal structures and hierarchies as foundational to memory and perception of musical sounds. Both sources highlight the importance of predictive mechanisms in cognitive processing of music, yet differ in their approach, with one emphasizing temporal predictability and the other focusing more on structural expectations within tonal frameworks.

## Key Findings

- Cognitive musicology integrates psychological and linguistic theories to understand human perception and memory related to music.
- Bayesian probability models are used to explain anticipatory responses in musical contexts.
- Tonal structures, including consonance-dissonance hierarchies, influence how humans perceive and remember music.

## Open Questions

- How do temporal predictability and structural expectations within tonal frameworks interact in cognitive processing of music?
