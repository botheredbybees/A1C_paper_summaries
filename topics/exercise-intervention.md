## Exercise Intervention

Both sources highlight the critical relationship between exercise and cognitive health. Source 1 focuses on the impact of physical activity on brain morphology and biomarkers related to Alzheimer's disease, emphasizing changes in hippocampal subfield volumes and neurofilament light chain levels as potential indicators of cognitive function improvement due to regular exercise. In contrast, Source 2 delves into how acute exercise influences mood and cognition through its effects on executive functions and the modulation of neural activity patterns and neurochemical pathways. Both sources suggest that exercise is not just beneficial for physical health but also has significant implications for brain health and cognitive performance.

## Key Findings

- Regular physical activity can improve hippocampal subfield volumes, potentially enhancing cognitive function.
- Exercise influences mood and cognition through multiple mechanisms including neurochemical pathways and neural activity patterns.
- Acute exercise has immediate effects on executive functions and mood.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term benefits of sustained physical activity for brain health in comparison to short-term acute exercise?
