## Fusiform Face Area

The Fusiform Face Area (FFA) is a specialized brain region critical for face recognition, as evidenced by studies of perceptual metamorphoses and prosopagnosia following direct electrical stimulation. The FFA is part of a modular system in the brain that is distinct from other visual processing pathways, specifically those involved in visual agnosia. This modularity is supported by findings from multivariate pattern analysis which show that the FFA responds selectively to faces. However, the extent of its modularity and the degree to which it operates independently from other brain regions remain subjects of debate, suggesting a need for further research into the exact functional and anatomical boundaries of the FFA.

## Key Findings

- The Fusiform Face Area is a specialized brain region crucial for face recognition.
- Studies of prosopagnosia and perceptual metamorphoses support the FFA's role in face processing.
- Multivariate pattern analysis confirms the FFA's selective response to faces.

## Open Questions

- The degree of modularity and independence of the Fusiform Face Area from other brain regions remains unclear.
