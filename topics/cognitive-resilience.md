## Cognitive Resilience in Older Adults

The concept of cognitive resilience is explored across multiple sources as a crucial mechanism for maintaining mental health and cognitive function in older adults. The brain-development-theory and the hormesis-hypothesis suggest that engaging in creative activities can enhance neural plasticity, thereby contributing to better cognitive outcomes in later life. Sources highlight how gene-cohen-research supports these theories by demonstrating the positive effects of various creative pursuits on brain health. Additionally, combinatorial activities and community engagement are seen as effective strategies for fostering late-life creativity and promoting quality-of-life. However, while there is agreement that creative activities enhance cognitive resilience, sources differ in their specific recommendations regarding which types of activities are most beneficial.

## Key Findings

- Creative activities contribute to brain health and cognitive resilience in older adults through neuroplasticity.
- Community engagement and combinatorial activities can foster late-life creativity and improve quality-of-life.

## Open Questions

- What specific types of creative activities are most effective for enhancing cognitive resilience?
