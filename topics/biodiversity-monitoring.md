## Biodiversity Monitoring Through Citizen Science

The integration of citizen science platforms such as iNaturalist into biodiversity monitoring offers a robust method for collecting large-scale environmental data. Sources highlight the importance of data quality and user engagement in these initiatives, noting that projects like iNaturalist not only contribute to scientific research but also serve as educational tools for participants. Challenges such as accurate identification, particularly with species like Castilleja (Orobanchaceae), are addressed through the application of computer vision algorithms, enhancing the reliability of data inputs. The continuum-of-engagement model underscores that sustained participation in citizen science projects can be fostered by framing these activities as leisure-based and project-oriented, thereby increasing both the quantity and quality of biodiversity monitoring contributions.

## Key Findings

- Citizen science platforms like iNaturalist are valuable tools for large-scale biodiversity monitoring and contribute to scientific research while providing educational opportunities.
- The accuracy of species identification on citizen science platforms is a critical challenge that can be addressed through technological advancements such as computer vision algorithms.
- Sustaining participant engagement in long-term projects relies heavily on framing activities as leisure-based, thereby enhancing the quality and consistency of data contributions.

## Open Questions

- How can we effectively bridge gaps between citizen science platforms and traditional scientific methodologies to ensure that user-generated data is seamlessly integrated into broader ecological research?
