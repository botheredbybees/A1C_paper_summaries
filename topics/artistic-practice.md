## Research Through Design and Artistic Practice in Healthcare

The integration of artistic practices within research methodologies, particularly through the designerly way of knowing, highlights a shift from traditional standard methods towards more innovative approaches like Research Through Design (RTD). Source 1 emphasizes RTD as an essential means to build theories directly from practice rather than merely applying existing ones. This approach is characterized by its hands-on and iterative nature where prototypes are developed through design thinking processes that include critical reflection, thereby embedding theory-building within the creative process itself.

Source 2 delves into the practical applications of such theoretical frameworks in healthcare settings. It discusses the necessity for clear project contracts to ensure clarity on roles, intellectual property rights, and ethical considerations when artists engage with medical institutions or patients. This highlights not only the importance of establishing robust contractual agreements but also underscores the unique challenges faced by arts-based research within a clinical context. The synthesis reveals a convergence between RTD's theoretical underpinnings and its practical implications in healthcare projects where the artist's role is both creative and collaborative.

Overall, while RTD offers a powerful framework for integrating artistic practice into medical research, it necessitates careful consideration of ethical guidelines and legal frameworks to safeguard the interests of all parties involved.

## Key Findings

- Research Through Design (RTD) emphasizes building theories directly from practice within an iterative design process.
- In healthcare settings, clear project contracts are essential for managing intellectual property and ensuring ethical conduct in arts-based projects.
- The designerly way of knowing facilitates a shift towards more innovative research methods that bridge theory and practical application.

## Open Questions

- How can Research Through Design be effectively integrated into existing medical research frameworks while addressing ethical considerations?
