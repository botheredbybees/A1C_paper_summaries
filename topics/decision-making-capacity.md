## Decision-Making Capacity

The concept of decision-making capacity in individuals with early-stage dementia is explored across several sources. Key to this discussion is the balance between maintaining personhood and enabling autonomy while recognizing cognitive limitations. Sources emphasize interventions that help maximize personhood, such as engaging patients in care planning through simplified methods like video diaries or photo albums, which can maintain a sense of control and self-expression for individuals with dementia. However, there are tensions around how to best support decision-making capacity without overwhelming the individual or overly paternalizing their care decisions.

## Key Findings

- Interventions aimed at maximizing personhood in early-stage dementia also enhance decision-making capacity by allowing patients to participate actively in planning and making choices about their care.
- Techniques like video diaries are effective in maintaining a sense of autonomy for individuals with cognitive decline, thereby supporting their overall well-being and quality of life.

## Open Questions

- How can healthcare providers best balance the need to protect decision-making capacity in dementia patients while also respecting personal autonomy and reducing stress or confusion?
