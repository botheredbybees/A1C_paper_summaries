## Interdisciplinary Education

The concept of interdisciplinary education is explored in two distinct contexts. Source 1 discusses a programming platform designed for children to create and explore hybrid musical instruments, emphasizing the integration of computational thinking with creative arts, thus promoting an early introduction to interdisciplinary skills essential for future technological innovation. In contrast, Source 2 focuses on medical students who are trained through formal art observation sessions aimed at improving their diagnostic competencies by enhancing visual literacy. This approach underscores the importance of integrating fine arts into medical training to refine observational and analytical skills necessary in clinical practice. Both sources highlight the effectiveness of interdisciplinary education in developing cognitive and practical abilities across diverse fields, suggesting a broader applicability of such educational methodologies.

## Key Findings

- Interdisciplinary education can enhance computational thinking and creative problem-solving skills among children through interactive platforms like programming for hybrid musical instruments.
- Formal art training improves diagnostic competencies by enhancing visual literacy in medical students, indicating the value of integrating arts into science-based curricula.

## Open Questions

- How scalable are these interdisciplinary educational approaches across various disciplines and age groups?
