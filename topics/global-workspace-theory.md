## Global Workspace Theory

Global Workspace Theory (GWT) is a prominent theoretical framework in cognitive neuroscience that aims to explain consciousness by proposing a workspace mechanism in the brain. GWT posits that the human brain contains a 'global workspace' that integrates and broadcasts information from various specialized processing modules, making it accessible to the entire brain. This theory contrasts with unconscious processing, which operates in isolation. Source 1 introduces the basic evidence supporting GWT and its role in understanding conscious awareness. Source 2 elaborates on GWT and compares it with Integrated Information Theory, another prominent theory of consciousness. Source 3 further discusses GWT and its implications, alongside the Recurrent Processing Theory, which offers a different perspective on how the brain processes information. Despite GWT's explanatory power in addressing consciousness, it faces challenges in fully capturing the complexity of conscious experience and the integration of information.

## Key Findings

- Global Workspace Theory explains conscious awareness as a result of information integration and broadcasting within the brain.
- GWT contrasts with unconscious processing, highlighting the role of attention in bringing information to the global workspace.
- GWT is compared to Integrated Information Theory and Recurrent Processing Theory, showing diverse theoretical approaches to consciousness.

## Open Questions

- How does Global Workspace Theory account for the integration of information across different brain modules without losing specificity and functional localization?
