## Disability in Performing Arts and Cinema

The exploration of disability through performing arts and cinema offers unique perspectives on the experiences and representations of disabled individuals. Source 1 delves into the application of brain-computer interfaces (BCI) for individuals suffering from locked-in syndrome (LIS), highlighting their creative expression through technological means. This source underscores the potential of BCI to serve as a conduit for artistic expression for individuals who are severely physically restricted. Conversely, Source 2 focuses on the cinematic portrayal of disability, particularly the concept of 'haptic vision' and its implications on the narrative and emotional engagement of the audience. It critiques the traditional cinematic contract, emphasizing the importance of contact and sensory engagement, which can be disrupted or redefined in narratives about disability. Both sources illuminate the complexities of representing disability, whether through innovative technologies that enable creative expression or through narratives that challenge conventional cinematic conventions.

## Key Findings

- Brain-computer interfaces (BCI) offer disabled individuals new avenues for creative expression and communication.
- Cinematic representations of disability often challenge traditional notions of sensory engagement and narrative structure.
- The use of technology and narrative in representing disability can serve to empower and highlight the human experience.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of brain-computer interfaces in performing arts and cinema be further developed to enhance the representation and experience of disability?
