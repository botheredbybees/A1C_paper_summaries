## Singing for Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

Singing is emerging as a promising intervention for managing chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Both sources highlight the potential of singing to improve physical quality of life and reduce symptoms such as dyspnea in COPD patients. Source 1 emphasizes the systematic review and meta-analysis indicating that singing can enhance maximal expiratory pressure, a measure of respiratory strength, thereby alleviating symptoms. Source 2 corroborates these findings, underscoring the positive impact on overall quality of life for COPD patients through singing. However, while both sources affirm the benefits, they do not provide a comprehensive understanding of the long-term effects and mechanisms underlying the therapeutic impact of singing on COPD.

## Key Findings

- Singing can improve physical quality of life in COPD patients.
- Enhancement of maximal expiratory pressure is observed in COPD patients who sing.
- Singing reduces dyspnea and enhances respiratory strength in COPD patients.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects of singing on COPD patients' respiratory health and quality of life?
