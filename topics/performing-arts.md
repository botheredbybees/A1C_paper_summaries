## Performing Arts and Embodied Creativity

The integration of embodied creativity theories and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) in the performing arts offers a rich landscape for both theoretical exploration and practical application. Source 1 delves into the 4E theory and embodied cognition, which emphasizes the importance of bodily engagement in the creative process. This theory suggests that creativity in performing arts is not just mental but deeply tied to physical experiences and interactions. In contrast, Source 2 explores how BCI technology can serve as an enabling tool for individuals suffering from locked-in syndrome (LIS), allowing them to engage in creative expression despite physical immobility. The use of BCI in this context highlights the potential for technological intervention to support embodied creativity principles, especially for those who might otherwise be excluded from physical participation in the arts.

## Key Findings

- The performing arts benefit from an embodied approach to creativity, integrating physical and mental processes.
- Brain-computer interfaces can enable creative expression in individuals with LIS, aligning with principles of embodied cognition.
- Technological interventions can support and enhance traditional embodied creativity practices.

## Open Questions

- How can the principles of embodied creativity be integrated with technological tools to enhance creative expression for all performers, including those with physical limitations?
