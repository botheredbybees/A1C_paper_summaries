## Social Upheaval

The Black Death of the 14th century serves as a pivotal case study in understanding social upheaval. Both sources highlight the profound impact of Yersinia pestis on European society, fundamentally altering the feudal system and catalyzing significant changes through social upheaval. Source 1 underscores the role of this bacterium in causing widespread mortality, leading to labor shortages which weakened the rigid structure of the feudal system. The empirical approach to medicine introduced during this period further complicated traditional societal roles, fostering skepticism towards established authority figures such as church officials and lords. Similarly, Source 2 emphasizes how the Black Death disrupted the social contract underpinning the feudal system by significantly reducing the population, leading to demands for better working conditions and wages among survivors. This era marked a shift in power dynamics from landowners to laborers due to increased bargaining leverage of the latter group.

## Key Findings

- Yersinia pestis caused widespread mortality that led to significant social upheaval.
- The Black Death disrupted the feudal system, shifting power from landowners to laborers.
- Empirical approaches to medicine introduced during this period challenged traditional societal norms.
