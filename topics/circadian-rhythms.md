## Circadian Rhythms

Circadian rhythms are internal biological processes that regulate the timing of various physiological and behavioral functions over a roughly 24-hour cycle. These rhythms, primarily influenced by light exposure through the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the brain, play a critical role in sleep-wake cycles, hormone release, body temperature regulation, and other important bodily functions. Disruption to these rhythms due to factors such as irregular screen use at night can lead to decreased melatonin production and adverse health consequences. Across sources, there is an emphasis on how environmental cues like the light-dark cycle influence circadian rhythms, underscoring their importance for sleep hygiene practices aimed at mitigating negative impacts of disrupted biological clocks.

## Key Findings

- Circadian rhythms regulate a wide range of physiological functions over approximately 24 hours.
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) plays a crucial role in setting and maintaining circadian rhythms based on light exposure.
- Disruption to natural circadian rhythms due to factors like screen use can adversely affect health through mechanisms such as decreased melatonin production.
