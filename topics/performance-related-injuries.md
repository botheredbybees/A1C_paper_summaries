## Performance-Related Injuries in Musicians

Performance-related injuries, particularly upper musculoskeletal disorders, are prevalent among musicians, necessitating a musician-centered approach to treatment and prevention. Both sources highlight the importance of understanding the unique demands of musical performance on the body, which often differ significantly from other forms of physical activity. The musician-centered approach emphasizes the integration of instrument-specific considerations into injury management strategies. Source 1 advocates for a holistic treatment that not only addresses physical symptoms but also the psychological and social impacts of injuries on musicians' careers and well-being. Source 2 provides empirical evidence from a study of university jazz students, illustrating the high prevalence of performance-related medical problems and the need for a health-focused musicianship curriculum. The synthesis reveals a consensus on the necessity of tailored interventions that consider the specific requirements of each instrument and the overall well-being of the musician. However, there is a tension regarding the balance between injury prevention and the maintenance of artistic integrity and performance standards.

## Key Findings

- Performance-related injuries are common among musicians, especially those involving the upper musculoskeletal system.
- A musician-centered approach to treatment and prevention is essential, incorporating instrument-specific considerations.
- Incorporating health-focused education in musicianship curricula can help mitigate the occurrence and impact of performance-related injuries.

## Open Questions

- How can injury prevention strategies be integrated into the rigorous demands of musical performance without compromising artistic standards and performance quality?
