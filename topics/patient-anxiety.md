## Patient Anxiety in Medical Settings

The provided sources explore various interventions aimed at reducing patient anxiety in medical settings, with a focus on both pediatric and general populations. Source 1 highlights the application of music as a therapeutic intervention for pain management and anxiety reduction during medical procedures. This meta-analysis underscores the role of audioanalgesia in mitigating anxiety and enhancing patient comfort. In contrast, Source 2 discusses the use of therapeutic clowning as a non-pharmacological method to distract and calm children undergoing medical procedures, particularly during anesthesia induction. Additionally, therapeutic clowning is noted for its ability to reduce parental anxiety, thereby creating a more supportive environment for pediatric patients. Both sources emphasize the importance of patient-centered approaches in managing anxiety and pain, suggesting that interventions can vary based on the specific needs and circumstances of the patient.

## Key Findings

- Music can effectively reduce patient anxiety and pain during medical procedures.
- Therapeutic clowning is an effective method to distract and calm children during medical procedures.
- Non-pharmacological interventions such as music and clown therapy can also alleviate parental anxiety.

## Open Questions

- How do the effects of music therapy and therapeutic clowning compare in reducing anxiety for adult versus pediatric patients?
