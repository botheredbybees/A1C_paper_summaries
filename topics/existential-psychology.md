## Existential Psychology

Existential psychology centers on the human quest for meaning and purpose in life, delving into the psychological impact of existential crises such as identity transformation and social alienation. Sources illuminate the interconnectedness of individual experiences ('thee'), societal contexts ('the we'), and the abstract nature of illness ('the it'), through narratives that highlight the transformative power of identity crises and the challenges they pose to one's sense of self. Logotherapy, introduced in the historical context, emphasizes the search for meaning as a pivotal therapeutic approach, underscoring the significance of the 'will-to-meaning' in overcoming existential vacuums. Both sources highlight the importance of narrative medicine and dereflection in bridging the gap between existential theories and practical therapeutic applications, emphasizing the narrative construction of identity and the search for meaning.

## Key Findings

- Existential psychology addresses the human quest for meaning and purpose, especially during identity transformations and existential crises.
- Narrative medicine and logotherapy are pivotal in therapeutic approaches, focusing on the 'will-to-meaning' and existential vacuums.
- The interplay between individual narratives ('thee'), societal contexts ('the we'), and abstract concepts ('the it') is central to understanding existential challenges.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of narrative medicine and logotherapy be further developed to address the complexities of individual and societal existential challenges?
