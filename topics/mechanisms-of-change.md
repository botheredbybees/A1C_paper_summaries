## Mechanisms of Change in Therapy

The investigation of mechanisms of change in therapeutic settings, particularly in somatic modalities such as dance/movement therapy, reveals a rich intersection between psychological theories and neurobiological processes. Source 1 outlines a theoretical framework that elucidates the neural correlates underpinning therapeutic interventions, emphasizing the importance of understanding how somatic experiences facilitate psychological transformation. This framework underscores the significance of neurobiological processes in the efficacy of dance/movement therapy. However, Source 2 shifts the focus to empirical research methods in music therapy, highlighting the importance of randomized-controlled-trials, grounded-theory, and phenomenological approaches. While both sources explore different aspects of therapeutic efficacy, they share an underlying theme: the necessity of integrating theoretical understanding with rigorous empirical investigation to enhance therapeutic practices.

## Key Findings

- Mechanisms of change in somatic therapies can be explained through neurobiological processes.
- Theoretical frameworks are essential for understanding therapeutic efficacy.
- Empirical research methods are crucial for validating theoretical models.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of neurobiological insights with empirical research methods improve our understanding of mechanisms of change in therapy?
