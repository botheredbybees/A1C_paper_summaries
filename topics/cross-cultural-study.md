## Cross-Cultural Variation in Musical Perception and Preference

Research exploring cross-cultural musical perception highlights both cultural specificity and universal elements. Source one investigates native Amazonians' lack of aversion to dissonance, contrasting with Western music preferences, emphasizing the role of aesthetic preference shaped by cultural exposure rather than innate biological predisposition. In contrast, source two uncovers latent factors in global musical preference that are consistent across diverse cultures, suggesting the existence of universal structures and personality-music correlations. The studies collectively underscore the complexity of human musical behavior as a blend of culturally specific norms and underlying, possibly biologically-rooted traits.

## Key Findings

- Cultural exposure significantly influences aesthetic preferences in music.
- There are latent factors in musical preference that are universally present across different cultures.
- Dissonance perception varies greatly between Western and indigenous populations.

## Open Questions

- How do innate biological traits interact with cultural upbringing to shape individual musical preferences?
