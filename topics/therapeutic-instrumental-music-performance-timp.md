## Therapeutic Instrumental Music Performance (Timp)

Therapeutic Instrumental Music Performance (TIMP) is a specialized form of neurologic music therapy aimed at enhancing motor functions, particularly in individuals with Parkinson's disease and chronic stroke. Both sources highlight the potential benefits of TIMP in rehabilitation, emphasizing its use in rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS) and patterned sensory enhancement (PSE) to improve mobility and stability. However, the application varies, with Source 1 focusing on mobility and stability in Parkinson's patients, while Source 2 delves into upper limb rehabilitation for chronic stroke survivors. Both studies suggest the importance of audio-motor-synchronization, indicating that TIMP's effectiveness may hinge on the precise timing and synchronization of music with motor activities. Despite these commonalities, the methodologies and patient outcomes differ, suggesting that TIMP's application might be tailored to specific rehabilitation needs and patient contexts.

## Key Findings

- TIMP enhances motor functions in both Parkinson's disease and chronic stroke patients.
- Rhythmic auditory stimulation and patterned sensory enhancement are key components of TIMP.
- Audio-motor-synchronization is crucial for the effectiveness of TIMP in rehabilitation.
