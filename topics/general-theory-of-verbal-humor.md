## General Theory Of Verbal Humor

The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) is a comprehensive framework for understanding the mechanisms and structure of verbal humor. Across multiple sources, GTVH's foundational elements—such as script opposition, appropriate incongruity, and logical mechanism—are discussed in detail. Script opposition involves the juxtaposition of incompatible scripts or scenarios to create humorous effects, while appropriate incongruity refers to the placement of unexpected elements within a familiar context that aligns with audience expectations for humor. Logical mechanisms explore how jokes are constructed and interpreted through language and cultural norms. GTVH also considers meta-communicative signals in conversation and their role in humor, such as false analogies or parodies used by Woody Allen's writing style. However, there is a tension between the theoretical foundations of GTVH and its practical application in analyzing conversational data for social identity construction.

## Key Findings

- Script opposition and appropriate incongruity are central concepts within General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH).
- Logical mechanisms play a critical role in parsing how humor is structured and conveyed through language.
- Meta-communicative signals like parodies or false analogies contribute to the richness of verbal humor in social interactions.

## Open Questions

- How does GTVH account for cultural specificity when analyzing script oppositions and logical mechanisms in conversational data?
