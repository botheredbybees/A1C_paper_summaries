## Laughter Prescription In Lifestyle Medicine

The concept of the Laughter Prescription as an intervention in lifestyle medicine is explored through two sources which converge on laughter's therapeutic potential. Both emphasize the distinction between spontaneous and simulated (or self-induced) laughter, highlighting that the act of laughing can be beneficial even when not naturally occurring. The 'motion creates emotion' theory supports this idea by suggesting physical actions can trigger emotional responses, indicating that inducing laughter may have psychological benefits similar to those derived from genuine laughter. Both sources also recognize simulated or self-induced laughter as a tool for therapeutic intervention, applicable in various contexts such as stress reduction and mood enhancement.

## Key Findings

- Simulated laughter is recognized as beneficial, even when not naturally occurring.
- 'Motion creates emotion' theory supports the efficacy of induced laughter.
- Self-induced laughter can serve as a therapeutic tool.

## Open Questions

- What are the long-term effects and sustainability of integrating self-induced laughter into regular health routines?
