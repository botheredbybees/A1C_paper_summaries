## Brain-Computer Interface

The reviewed studies highlight the multifaceted impact and potential of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) in enhancing the quality of life for individuals suffering from locked-in syndrome (LIS) and other disorders of consciousness. BCIs are seen as tools for communication restoration, aiding in the empowerment of patients who are voiceless due to their condition. However, the integration of BCIs with other technologies, such as functional electrical stimulation, is suggested to enhance their efficacy. Ethical considerations are paramount, especially regarding the responsibilities of researchers towards participants, including the maintenance of equipoise and non-exploitation. The ethical framework must also address the complex scenarios where patients might be in a covert or functional locked-in state, necessitating careful protocols for decision-making and communication. Despite the promise, the review also highlights the need for ethical guidance and the importance of understanding the full spectrum of patient conditions to ensure the benefits of BCIs are realized responsibly.

## Key Findings

- Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) can significantly improve communication for individuals with locked-in syndrome (LIS).
- Combining BCIs with functional electrical stimulation (FES) may enhance their effectiveness for LIS patients.
- Ethical considerations, including the maintenance of equipoise and non-exploitation, are crucial for BCI research.

## Open Questions

- What are the ethical implications of using BCIs in patients with covert consciousness, where the full state of awareness is not clear?
