## Humoral Theory

Humoral theory, a cornerstone of ancient and medieval medical thought, was deeply influenced by Galen's interpretations of Hippocratic medicine. This framework posits that the body maintains health through balance among four bodily fluids: blood, phlegm, yellow bile (choler), and black bile (melancholy). In medieval Europe, this theory not only shaped medical practice but also explained spiritual phenomena such as visionary experiences during times of plague. During the Renaissance, the concept was further integrated into various aspects of life, including artistic innovations like linear perspective, which reflects a broader societal interest in order and balance—values central to humoral theory. However, despite its pervasive influence, challenges arose regarding the adaptability of humoral principles to rapidly changing social and environmental contexts.

## Key Findings

- Humoral theory was extensively influenced by Galen's interpretations of Hippocratic medicine
- In medieval Europe, humoral theory explained both physical health and spiritual phenomena like visionary experiences
- During the Renaissance, humoral theory had a broad impact beyond healthcare, influencing art and societal beliefs

## Open Questions

- How adaptable is humoral theory to modern medical understanding?
