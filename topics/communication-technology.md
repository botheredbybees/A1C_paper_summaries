## Communication Technology for Neurological Disorders

The integration of communication technology with neurological disabilities highlights both the adaptability and social connectivity benefits for affected individuals. Source 1 focuses on brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) combined with functional electrical stimulation (FES) to enhance communication capabilities for people with locked-in syndrome. This approach demonstrates significant potential in assisting patients who are completely or partially paralyzed and unable to speak, by enabling them to communicate through brain signals. Conversely, Source 2 explores the use of eye-gaze technology, specifically the Tobii PCEye, which enables individuals like artist Francis Tsai to interact with their environment and engage in social activities despite physical limitations. Both sources underscore the transformative impact of technology on communication for individuals with severe disabilities, facilitating a more connected and expressive existence.

## Key Findings

- Brain-computer interfaces combined with functional electrical stimulation offer a promising pathway for enhancing communication in people with locked-in syndrome.
- Eye-gaze technology enables individuals with physical disabilities to interact socially and express themselves through non-verbal means.
- Communication technologies such as BCIs and eye-gaze systems significantly improve the quality of life for individuals with severe neurological disabilities.

## Open Questions

- What are the limitations and challenges associated with long-term use of brain-computer interfaces and eye-gaze technology in clinical settings?
