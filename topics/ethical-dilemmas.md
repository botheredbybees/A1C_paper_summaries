## Ethical Dilemmas

The concept of ethical dilemmas is explored in both sources, highlighting the complex interplay between knowledge pursuit and moral restraint. The first source, 'Knowing and Seeing: Reconstructing Frankenstein,' delves into the narrative structure and ethical challenges that arise from the pursuit of knowledge, reflecting on the moral implications of scientific advancement and the creation of life. In contrast, the second source, 'Erasing Gender Diversity,' focuses on the ethical challenges faced in the context of social work and human rights, specifically in protecting personal autonomy and fundamental human rights. Both sources underscore the necessity of moral restraint and ethical considerations in the face of societal and scientific progress. However, they differ in their context and scope, with the former addressing the ethical implications of scientific inquiry and creation, while the latter emphasizes the protection of rights and autonomy in the social and political sphere. These sources collectively highlight the pervasive nature of ethical dilemmas across various domains, necessitating a nuanced understanding of moral principles and their application in different contexts.

## Key Findings

- Ethical dilemmas arise from the pursuit of knowledge and the advancement of science.
- The protection of fundamental human rights and personal autonomy is a critical ethical concern.
- Moral restraint is essential in navigating complex ethical challenges in both scientific and social contexts.

## Open Questions

- How can ethical principles be effectively applied and balanced in contexts where they conflict with scientific advancement or societal norms?
