## Material Engagement Theory

Material Engagement Theory (MET) is a framework that emphasizes the role of material objects in cognitive processes. Sources highlight MET's application in understanding therapeutic benefits of pottery making, exploring postphenomenological perspectives on human-material relationships, and examining how engaging with materials like clay affects agency and signification. While all sources converge on MET’s foundational principles, they diverge in their specific applications: one explores hedonic impacts, another considers philosophical implications through the lens of 'Homo faber', and the last delves into agency and enactive signification. These varied perspectives underscore MET's versatility as a theoretical tool for analyzing human engagement with material culture.

## Key Findings

- Material Engagement Theory (MET) is fundamental in understanding cognitive processes intertwined with physical objects.
- MET has applications across therapeutic contexts, philosophical explorations, and studies of agency and signification.
- Each source contributes unique insights into the multifaceted nature of human-material interactions.
