## Voice Quality in Singing

The research on voice quality in singing, particularly in individuals with quadriplegia, highlights both the benefits and challenges associated with singing as a therapeutic tool. Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) present a biopsychosocial model that complicates the relationship between physical ailments and vocal performance. Conversely, singing training offers rehabilitation benefits for those with quadriplegia, suggesting a positive impact on voice quality. The interplay between these conditions and their treatments underscores the need for a nuanced understanding of how physical health influences vocal abilities. While singing can be beneficial for vocal rehabilitation, there is a need to consider the potential exacerbation of TMD symptoms. This interplay suggests that while singing can be a valuable therapeutic intervention, its application must be tailored to individual needs to ensure optimal outcomes.

## Key Findings

- Singing training improves voice quality for individuals with quadriplegia.
- Temporomandibular disorders impact voice quality through a biopsychosocial framework.
- Physical conditions such as TMD can complicate vocal rehabilitation efforts.

## Open Questions

- How can singing training be adapted to accommodate individuals with temporomandibular disorders without exacerbating their condition?
