## Transformative Learning Theory

Transformative Learning Theory (TLT), introduced by Jack Mezirow, emphasizes the role of critical reflection in transforming one's perspective and understanding of the world. Source 1 delves into the operationalization of TLT, focusing on the processes of reflection and the conditions necessary for transformative learning to occur. Source 2 applies TLT in the context of community-based arts activities, suggesting that such activities facilitate identity change and recovery from serious mental illness through transformative learning processes. Both sources underscore the importance of critical reflection in transformative learning but differ in their applications: Source 1 emphasizes the theoretical underpinnings and operational aspects, while Source 2 applies the theory to a specific community context. This dual approach highlights the versatility and applicability of TLT across diverse settings, from theoretical analysis to practical interventions.

## Key Findings

- Critical reflection is a central component of transformative learning.
- Transformative learning theory is applicable across various fields, from theoretical analysis to practical interventions.
- Community-based arts activities can facilitate identity change and recovery through transformative learning processes.

## Open Questions

- How can transformative learning theory be further operationalized in diverse community contexts to enhance its applicability and impact?
