## Brain-Computer Interfaces

Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) represent a transformative intervention for individuals with severe motor disabilities, such as locked-in syndrome (LIS), enabling them to communicate and express themselves through technology. Across the sources, BCIs are highlighted for their potential to enhance sensory perception through neural plasticity and expand creative expression in performing arts. However, the transfer of BCI technology from research to practical use faces significant barriers, including usability and user-centered design considerations. Ethical concerns also emerge regarding the quality of life and human rights for those utilizing BCIs, particularly in LIS contexts. The integration of BCIs in assistive technology highlights the importance of addressing these challenges to ensure effective and responsible deployment.

## Key Findings

- BCIs enable individuals with LIS to communicate and express themselves through technological means.
- BCI technology can enhance sensory perception through neural plasticity and expand creative expression in performing arts.
- Usability and user-centered design are critical for the effective transfer of BCI technology from research to practical use.

## Open Questions

- How can ethical considerations be integrated into the development and deployment of BCIs to ensure the quality of life and human rights for individuals with LIS?
