## Mindfulness-Based Psychotherapy

Mindfulness-based interventions have emerged as a pivotal component in contemporary psychotherapy, offering a structured approach to mental health treatment. Source 2 emphasizes the role of mindfulness in regulating cognitive and affective processes, thereby mitigating symptoms of mental illness. This aligns with the application of mindfulness within therapeutic frameworks, as discussed in Source 3, where specific therapies like Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), and Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) are highlighted. These therapies integrate mindfulness practices to enhance self-awareness and emotional regulation, fostering resilience and reducing distress. Despite this, the integration of arts in health, as explored in Source 1, presents an alternative continuum model that challenges the traditional boundaries of mindfulness-based psychotherapy by suggesting a more inclusive approach to mental health treatment. This model proposes a broader spectrum of therapeutic modalities, potentially enriching the mindfulness paradigm by incorporating artistic expression.

## Key Findings

- Mindfulness-based interventions such as MBCT, ACT, and DBT are integral to contemporary psychotherapy.
- Mindfulness practices aid in regulating cognitive and affective responses, contributing to mental health improvement.
- The arts in health model suggests a broader therapeutic approach, potentially complementing mindfulness-based practices.

## Open Questions

- How can the continua model of arts in health be integrated into mindfulness-based psychotherapy to enhance therapeutic outcomes?
