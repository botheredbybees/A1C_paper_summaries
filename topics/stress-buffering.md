## Stress Buffering Potential

The concept of stress buffering encompasses the ability of positive social interactions and emotions to mitigate the adverse effects of stress. Source 1 examines how social presence can serve as a stress buffer, but this potential is influenced by individual differences in social anxiety. Individuals with higher social anxiety may not benefit from social presence as a stress buffer, suggesting that the effectiveness of social interactions as stress buffers varies based on individual characteristics. In contrast, Source 2 focuses on the broaden-and-build theory and the undo hypothesis, which propose that positive emotions and emotional broadening can counteract the negative impacts of stress, thereby enhancing well-being. Both sources highlight the importance of emotional and social resources in managing stress, yet they underscore the critical role of individual differences in how these resources are utilized and experienced.

## Key Findings

- Positive social interactions and emotional states can serve as stress buffers to mitigate the negative impacts of stress.
- The effectiveness of social presence as a stress buffer may be compromised in individuals with higher social anxiety.
- The broaden-and-build theory posits that positive emotions can broaden an individual's momentary thought-action repertoire and build enduring personal resources.

## Open Questions

- How do individual differences in social anxiety impact the effectiveness of positive emotions and social interactions as stress buffers?
