## Primary Progressive Aphasia

Primary Progressive Aphasia (PPA) is a neurodegenerative condition impacting cognitive function, particularly within the realm of language and memory. Both sources emphasize the pivotal role of semantic memory in the progression and diagnosis of PPA. Semantic memory refers to the memory system for storing conceptual knowledge about the world, distinct from personal episodic memory. The condition is often associated with semantic dementia, indicating a decline in the ability to understand and use words and concepts, which is closely tied to the deterioration of semantic memory structures in the brain. Embodied cognition, a theoretical framework, is also discussed in relation to PPA, suggesting that language comprehension and production are grounded in sensory and motor experiences. This framework offers a unique perspective on how language deficits in PPA might manifest and evolve over time. Despite the insights provided, there remains a need for more research to understand the full extent of how embodied cognition can inform therapeutic interventions for individuals with PPA.

## Key Findings

- Primary Progressive Aphasia is characterized by a decline in semantic memory.
- Semantic dementia is commonly associated with PPA, impacting the ability to understand and use language.
- Embodied cognition offers a theoretical framework to understand language deficits in PPA.

## Open Questions

- How can embodied cognition inform therapeutic approaches for individuals with PPA?
