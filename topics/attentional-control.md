## Neurophysiology of Sensory Attention

The neurophysiology of sensory attention involves the understanding of neural mechanisms that enable selective attention. Source 1 delves into the role of the frontal eye field (FEF) and gain-modulated rates in the context of cognitive neuroscience. It emphasizes the use of multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) to uncover complex neural interactions. Source 2 expands on the neural mechanisms of attention, focusing on sensory-attention, a fundamental aspect of cognitive processing that allows for the filtering of sensory information. Both sources highlight the importance of attentional control mechanisms in directing and focusing cognitive resources towards relevant stimuli, with neural correlates providing insights into how attention is modulated and maintained in various cognitive tasks.

## Key Findings

- The frontal eye field (FEF) plays a crucial role in attentional control.
- Neural mechanisms of attention involve selective attention to filter sensory information.
- Multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) aids in understanding complex neural interactions related to attention.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific neural pathways involved in the transfer of attentional control from initial sensory processing to higher cognitive functions?
