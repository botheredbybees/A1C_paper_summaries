## Functional Independence

Functional independence interventions aim to enhance the self-sufficiency of individuals with various disabilities, including autism and spinal cord injuries. Source 1 presents a randomized controlled trial examining the impact of dance practice on autistic children and adolescents, finding improvements in functionality and social behavior. Source 2 explores therapeutic recreation interventions post-spinal cord injury, focusing on their relationship to functional independence and quality of life. Both studies highlight the importance of tailored interventions to meet the diverse needs of individuals, emphasizing the role of non-verbal communication skills in enhancing independence for those who may struggle with verbal expression. However, there is a gap in understanding how these interventions can be effectively scaled and integrated into broader therapeutic frameworks.

## Key Findings

- Dance practice can improve functionality and social behavior in autistic children and adolescents.
- Therapeutic recreation interventions positively impact functional independence and quality of life in individuals with spinal cord injuries.
- Non-verbal communication skills are crucial for enhancing functional independence in individuals with communication challenges.

## Open Questions

- How can dance practice and therapeutic recreation interventions be effectively scaled and integrated into broader therapeutic frameworks?
