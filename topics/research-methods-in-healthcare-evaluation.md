## Evaluation Methods in Healthcare

The evaluation methods discussed across these sources emphasize the importance of both quantitative and qualitative approaches to assessing healthcare interventions. Source 1 contrasts research with evaluation, highlighting the need for comprehensive evaluations that consider complex intervention factors beyond simple RCTs. Meanwhile, Source 2 delves into process evaluations and ethnographic studies as essential tools for understanding how interventions are implemented in real-world settings. This source underscores the value of outcome research alongside these qualitative methods to provide a holistic view of healthcare program effectiveness. Lastly, Source 3 introduces ROBINS-I as an assessment tool designed specifically for non-randomized studies, addressing the need for robust methodological rigor when evaluating interventions that cannot be tested via RCTs due to ethical or practical constraints.

## Key Findings

- Evaluation methods are crucial for assessing complex healthcare interventions beyond simple RCT designs
- ROBINS-I provides a structured approach to assess risk of bias in non-randomized studies, enhancing methodological rigor
- Qualitative approaches like ethnographic studies and process evaluations complement outcome research in understanding intervention implementation
