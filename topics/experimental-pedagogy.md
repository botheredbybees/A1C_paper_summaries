## Experimental Pedagogy in Architectural Design Education

The integration of experimental pedagogical methods within architectural design education has been explored through two distinct yet complementary approaches. The first source emphasizes Gilles Deleuze's philosophy as a foundational framework for encouraging creative experimentation and interdisciplinary connections in the design studio. This approach seeks to transcend traditional boundaries by integrating craft mediums and fostering an environment that values material exploration and innovation. The second source, 'The PopUp Workshop: Learning with Material,' advocates for hands-on learning experiences where students engage directly with materials to develop a deeper understanding of form and function. It underscores the importance of 'Learning by Doing' and 'Material Thinking,' highlighting how working closely with physical materials can lead to novel insights and design solutions. Both sources converge in their emphasis on interdisciplinary collaboration, suggesting that integrating diverse fields such as art, science, and technology into architectural education enhances creativity and problem-solving skills among students.

## Key Findings

- Creative experimentation and hands-on learning are integral components of effective pedagogical practices in architectural design education.
- Interdisciplinary connections foster innovative thinking and provide a richer educational experience for architecture students.
- Material-focused workshops and studio environments facilitate deeper engagement with the physical aspects of design.

## Open Questions

- How can traditional architectural education frameworks be adapted to fully incorporate experimental methods without sacrificing technical rigor?
