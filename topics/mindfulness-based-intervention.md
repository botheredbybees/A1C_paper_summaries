## Mindfulness-Based Intervention

The integration of mindfulness practices across various interventions highlights their versatility in addressing a wide range of health issues. Source 1 explores the feasibility of using online mindfulness-based dance/movement therapy to address cognitive decline, loneliness, and mental health among older adults during the COVID-19 pandemic. It emphasizes the importance of physical activity alongside psychological support for this population. In contrast, Source 2 delves into a randomized controlled trial assessing the effectiveness of a Mindfulness-Based Social Work and Self-Care (MBSWSC) program in reducing workplace stress and improving emotion regulation skills among social work professionals. Both studies highlight the significant benefits of mindfulness-based interventions, demonstrating improvements in mental health and well-being across different demographic groups and contexts. However, there is variability in how these practices are implemented—Source 1 focuses on physical movement within a therapeutic context whereas Source 2 emphasizes cognitive and emotional aspects through structured self-care programs.

## Key Findings

- Mindfulness-based interventions can significantly improve mental health outcomes in diverse populations including older adults and social work professionals.
- The integration of physical activity into mindfulness practices appears beneficial for addressing loneliness and cognitive decline in older adults.
- Structured self-care programs like MBSWSC are effective in reducing workplace stress and enhancing emotion regulation among professionals.

## Open Questions

- How can the benefits observed in younger, more flexible working populations be extended to older or less mobile groups?
