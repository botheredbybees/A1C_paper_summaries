## Medical Diagnosis in Renaissance Art

Renaissance art offers a unique window into the medical practices and understandings of its time. Two studies highlight how historical paintings can be analyzed to diagnose diseases, revealing insights into both artistic intent and contemporaneous medical knowledge. The first source identifies malignant breast cancer in Renaissance depictions, while the second examines Caravaggio's self-portraits for signs of jaundice. Both sources demonstrate the potential for retrospective diagnosis through visual analysis, suggesting that art can serve as a historical record of disease symptoms and treatments. However, these studies also reveal the limitations of such an approach, as medical knowledge and diagnostic criteria have evolved since the Renaissance era.

## Key Findings

- Renaissance paintings provide evidence of diseases like breast cancer and jaundice.
- Retrospective diagnosis through art analysis can shed light on historical medical practices.
- Art offers a visual record of disease symptoms that can be studied by modern researchers.

## Open Questions

- How reliable are diagnoses based solely on artistic depictions when compared to contemporaneous medical records?
