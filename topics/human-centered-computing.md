## Human-Centered Computing

The concept of Human-Centered Computing (HCC) emerges as a central theme in all provided sources, emphasizing a shift towards collaborative survival and post-anthropocentric worldviews. Source 1 initiates the discussion on HCC by exploring observational sketching to enhance noticing skills, fostering an understanding beyond traditional anthropocentrism. This approach encourages collaboration between humans and their environment for mutual benefit. Sources 2 and 3 delve into specific applications of HCC: DIY culture among older men (Source 2) driven by altruistic motivations and the material-driven design in textile interfaces (Source 3), both rooted in a user-centric methodology that prioritizes understanding human needs through creative and contextual engagement with materials and processes. Despite the diverse contexts, all sources converge on the importance of empathy, collaboration, and an integrated approach to technology and society.

## Key Findings

- Human-Centered Computing emphasizes a collaborative approach between humans and their environment for mutual benefit.
- DIY culture among older men is driven by altruistic motivations and sustained through human-centered design principles.
- Material-driven design in textile interfaces exemplifies an integrated method of understanding user needs through direct engagement with materials.
