## Person-Centered Care

The concept of person-centered care in dementia management is underscored across multiple sources as a holistic approach emphasizing dignity, individualized treatment plans, and the preservation of personal identity. This model integrates various therapeutic interventions such as reminiscence therapy, music-based approaches like personalized playlists or the Veder method, and theatrical activities that stimulate memory and emotional engagement. Reminiscence therapy, particularly through life history work, is highlighted for its role in enhancing quality of life by reinforcing individual identity and improving social interactions. Creative communication methods, including theater-based interventions, are noted to foster positive affect and reduce challenging behaviors associated with dementia. Additionally, the use of music as a therapeutic tool is shown to evoke autobiographical memories, improve cognitive function, and enhance overall well-being. Sources also emphasize the importance of environmental modifications in care facilities that support person-centered practices, facilitating an environment where residents can maintain their selfhood and dignity.

## Key Findings

- Person-centered care emphasizes individualized treatment plans tailored to preserve personal identity and dignity.
- Reminiscence therapy through life history work enhances quality of life by reinforcing individual identity and social interactions.
- The use of creative communication methods, such as theater-based interventions, promotes positive affect and reduces challenging behaviors in dementia patients.

## Open Questions
