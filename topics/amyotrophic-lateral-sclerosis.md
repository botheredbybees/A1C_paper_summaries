## Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) is a motor neuron disease characterized by the progressive degeneration and death of motor neurons, leading to muscle weakness and atrophy, and ultimately affecting a patient's ability to walk, talk, eat, and breathe. The disease's diagnostic criteria and genetic factors are critical elements in understanding ALS, as highlighted in the background chapter. Epidemiological studies using capture-recapture methodology reveal the incidence and prevalence of ALS, providing insights into the disease's distribution and impact on populations. Additionally, ALS's pathophysiology involves TDP-43 pathology, which is also observed in other polyglutamine diseases, suggesting common pathways in neurodegeneration. Neurorehabilitation approaches, including music and dance therapy, have been explored for their potential benefits in managing symptoms and improving quality of life for individuals with ALS and other neurological conditions.

## Key Findings

- ALS is a motor neuron disease with significant genetic and environmental factors.
- Capture-recapture methodology enhances the accuracy of epidemiological studies on ALS incidence and prevalence.
- TDP-43 pathology is a common feature in ALS and other polyglutamine diseases, suggesting shared pathogenic mechanisms.
- Neurorehabilitation approaches, such as music and dance therapy, may offer symptomatic relief and quality of life improvement for individuals with ALS.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms by which TDP-43 pathology contributes to the progression of ALS and how can these mechanisms be targeted for therapeutic intervention?
