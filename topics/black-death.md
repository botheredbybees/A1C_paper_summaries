## The Black Death and Its Impact

The Black Death, caused by the Yersinia pestis bacterium and spread through fleas on rats, had profound impacts across Europe in the 14th century. Sources highlight demographic collapse due to high mortality rates, particularly affecting the feudal system's structure and stability. Social upheaval was rampant as traditional societal roles changed and a labor shortage led to increased wages for peasants and decreased power for landowners. A notable development was an empirical approach to medicine arising from the plague's spread, challenging existing religious explanations of disease causality. Religious institutions also faced challenges during this period, leading to reforms and shifts in spiritual practices.

## Key Findings

- The Black Death caused significant demographic collapse across Europe due to high mortality rates.
- Feudal systems were destabilized by the plague-induced labor shortages that increased peasants' bargaining power.
- Social upheaval led to religious reforms as traditional explanations of disease causality faced challenges.

## Open Questions

- How did long-term demographic changes after the Black Death influence European society beyond immediate social and economic shifts?
