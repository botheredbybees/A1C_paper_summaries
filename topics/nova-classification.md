## Nova Classification

The NOVA classification theory categorizes foods based on the extent and purpose of industrial processing. The classification distinguishes between unprocessed or minimally processed foods (such as fresh produce, grains, milk), processed culinary ingredients (like oils, butter, cheese), processed foods (foods that have been altered to increase their shelf life but not for taste enhancement), and ultra-processed foods, which are designed to be hyper-palatable and convenient. This system aims to inform dietary choices by highlighting the health impacts of different food categories, advocating for a 'Eat Food' principle that prioritizes unprocessed or minimally processed items over those heavily industrially treated.

## Key Findings

- The NOVA classification divides foods into distinct categories based on processing level and purpose.
- Ultra-processed foods are characterized by their industrial formulation aimed at creating hyper-palatable products.
- Promoting the consumption of unprocessed or minimally processed foods is a central recommendation of this theory.
