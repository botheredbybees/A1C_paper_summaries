## Musculoskeletal Pain in Musicians

Musculoskeletal pain among musicians is a significant health concern influenced by both physiological and psychological factors. Sources reveal that repetitive strain injuries (RSIs) and focal dystonia are common physical ailments, highlighting the repetitive nature of musical practice as a risk factor. Additionally, performance anxiety exacerbates these conditions, as increased stress levels can lead to heightened pain perception. The Yerkes-Dodson law explains the relationship between arousal and performance, suggesting that optimal performance and pain management require a balance of stress. Furthermore, the presence of tinnitus in musicians adds another layer of complexity to the management of musculoskeletal pain, indicating a multifaceted approach is necessary to address both physical and auditory symptoms.

## Key Findings

- Repetitive strain injuries and focal dystonia are prevalent among musicians.
- Performance anxiety can worsen musculoskeletal pain due to the Yerkes-Dodson law.
- Tinnitus complicates the experience of musculoskeletal pain in musicians.

## Open Questions

- How can the multifaceted approach to managing musculoskeletal pain in musicians be effectively integrated into existing healthcare practices?
