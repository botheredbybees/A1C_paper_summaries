## Auditory Imagery

Auditory imagery is a method of mental rehearsal that involves creating a mental representation of sound without the presence of an external stimulus. Both sources emphasize its importance in understanding perceptual processes and neural coding, especially as it pertains to musical expectations and working memory. Source 1 focuses on empirical findings related to auditory imagery, highlighting how it can be used to study brain activity during mental listening tasks. Meanwhile, Source 2 discusses the application of auditory imagery within activities designed to enhance musical comprehension and recall, suggesting a practical methodology for studying this phenomenon in various contexts.

## Key Findings

- Auditory imagery is crucial for understanding neural coding in the absence of actual sound stimuli
- Working memory plays a significant role in auditory imagery processes
- Musical expectations influence the way auditory imagery is formed and perceived

## Open Questions

- What are the specific neural mechanisms underlying the formation and recall of auditory imagery?
