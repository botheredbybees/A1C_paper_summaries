## Acts of Kindness

Acts of kindness, whether spontaneous or planned, form a cornerstone of positive psychology interventions aimed at enhancing well-being. The Positive Activity Model (PAM) and the Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model (HAPM) offer frameworks to understand how these acts contribute to long-term happiness by preventing the human tendency to adapt to positive experiences, thus sustaining their positive effects. These models highlight the importance of both random and targeted acts of kindness in mitigating negativity biases, which can otherwise overshadow positive experiences. Sources indicate that while spontaneous acts of kindness may provide immediate gratification, planned acts of kindness can offer sustained benefits by fostering a habitual practice of kindness.

## Key Findings

- Acts of kindness are effective interventions in positive psychology aimed at enhancing well-being.
- The Positive Activity Model and the Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model explain how acts of kindness sustain positive effects.
- Random and targeted acts of kindness can mitigate negativity biases.

## Open Questions

- What is the optimal frequency and type of acts of kindness to maximize their long-term benefits?
