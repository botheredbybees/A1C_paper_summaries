## Spirituality

The concept of spirituality is explored through two distinct yet complementary lenses. In 'Knitting as Implicit Spirituality,' spirituality is examined as an implicit religion within the context of knitting, suggesting that such activities can contribute to subjective wellbeing through a subtle, often unacknowledged spiritual practice. This source highlights the less explicit forms of spirituality embedded in everyday activities, which can have profound impacts on individuals' sense of fulfillment and connection. Conversely, 'The Decoding of the Human Spirit: A Synergy of Spirituality and Character Strengths Toward Wholeness' delves into the explicit dimensions of spirituality, emphasizing its integration with character strengths as defined by the VIA Classification. This source suggests that spirituality can be a path to wholeness through the grounding path (practicing spirituality in everyday life) and the sanctification path (dedication to spiritual practices). These two sources illustrate both the implicit and explicit dimensions of spirituality and their varied impacts on individuals' wellbeing and character.

## Key Findings

- Spirituality can manifest in both implicit and explicit ways.
- Knitting and other everyday activities can serve as implicit spiritual practices.
- Explicit spirituality, when integrated with character strengths, contributes to wholeness and wellbeing.

## Open Questions

- How do implicit spiritual practices compare to explicit spiritual practices in terms of impact on mental health and wellbeing?
