## Imagination and Causation in Scientific Inquiry

The sources explore foundational aspects of scientific inquiry, focusing on the roles of imagination and causation. The first source delves into the necessity of longitudinal data for establishing causation, highlighting the importance of temporal sequence and the complexities of inferring causation from observational data. In contrast, the second source investigates the role of imagination, particularly from an embodied perspective, emphasizing the importance of experiential knowledge and mental simulation in scientific discovery. Both sources underscore the significance of background knowledge, with the first source focusing on its role in causal inference and the second on its importance for imaginative thinking. The integration of these perspectives reveals a dual approach to scientific inquiry: one that relies heavily on empirical evidence over time and another that leverages the creativity and insight derived from embodied experiences.

## Key Findings

- Background knowledge is crucial for both causal inference and imaginative thinking in science.
- Temporal data is essential for establishing causation, but it is not the only means of scientific discovery.
- Embodied imagination, or the use of experiential knowledge and mental simulation, plays a vital role in scientific creativity.

## Open Questions

- How can the integration of longitudinal data and embodied imagination enhance our understanding of causation in complex systems?
