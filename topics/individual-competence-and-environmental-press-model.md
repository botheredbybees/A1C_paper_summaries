## Individual Competence and Environmental Press Model

The Individual Competence and Environmental Press Model posits that the interaction between an individual's abilities and the demands placed by their environment determines outcomes. In dementia care, this model emphasizes how physical environments can either facilitate or hinder a person's functioning based on their residual capabilities. While some sources highlight the design of spaces to support declining cognitive capacities (progressively lowered threshold model), others underscore the importance of creating adaptable settings that align with individual needs and preserve dignity. Despite these variations in approach, there is consensus around the necessity for environments that are responsive to both current and changing needs of individuals with dementia.

## Key Findings

- The interaction between an individual's capabilities and environmental demands significantly influences outcomes in dementia care
- Physical environment design plays a crucial role in supporting or hindering functioning among individuals with declining cognitive abilities
- There is a need for adaptable environments that respond to both current and changing needs of individuals with dementia

## Open Questions

- How can the individual competence and environmental press model be effectively applied across diverse settings to support different stages of dementia?
