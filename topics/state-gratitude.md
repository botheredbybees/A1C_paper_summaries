## Gratitude Interventions

The concept of gratitude, both as a state and a trait, has been extensively explored in relation to its impact on health and well-being. State gratitude, the momentary feeling of thankfulness, is often elicited by specific events or circumstances, whereas trait gratitude is a more stable personality trait characterized by a general inclination to be grateful (Source 1, Source 2). Research indicates that fostering state gratitude can lead to improvements in subjective well-being and has the potential to mitigate stress and enhance resilience (Source 1). Source 2 further elaborates on the use of positive interventions that focus on cultivating state gratitude, including the mental subtraction technique, which encourages individuals to imagine a life without certain positive events or people to appreciate them more. Despite the promising effects, the causal relationship between state gratitude and health outcomes remains under scrutiny, with ongoing debates about the directionality and mechanisms involved in these relationships (Source 1).

## Key Findings

- Fostering state gratitude can improve subjective well-being and resilience.
- Positive interventions, such as mental subtraction, are effective in cultivating state gratitude.
- Research is still needed to clarify the causal relationship between state gratitude and health outcomes.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific mechanisms through which state gratitude influences health and well-being?
